Changhua has a long history. There is an ancient story about the origin of Changhu of the Yao people:
Long ago, Pan Wang, the leader of the Yao ethnic group, had a young and beautiful wife named Liu Sanmei. She was hardworking and kind, and could sing and dance well. Since then, the annual Panwang Festival has been set, which is October 16. At this festival, the Yao people imitate the shape of shaking a hammer to make a big long drum, compose 72 sets of movements, and beat them rhythmically continuously to encourage and promote the heroic and tenacious fighting spirit of the Yao people. Later, this kind of dance appeared at the Yao family's festive festivals. The Yao family celebrated traditional festivals such as “New Year” and “Song Hall” on October 16 of the lunar calendar, or on festive occasions such as celebrating a good harvest and congratulating the newlyweds. Young men and women improvised and danced to each other. The dragon and dragon leaped. It was imposing and spectacular. In particular, the Yao 'Panwang Festival' is most popular on October 16.
Long inspirations are divided into 'single dances', 'duo dances', and 'group dances'. It has 72 performance programs, and each set is divided into several movement details such as 'starting the course' and 'moving to the course'.
The dancers are strapped, dressed in short clothes, drums on their shoulders, and shot with both hands. Sometimes they walk and dance lightly, sometimes jumping rapidly. The drums accompanied the dance posture, making people dazzled and excited. Most of the drumming movements of long drums express production and lifestyle content, such as building houses, plowing fields and cultivating land, imitating the movements of animals, etc., and have a vivid image and a rich atmosphere of life. Their movements are rough, brave, unrestrained, strong, powerful, aggressive, and liberal. Whether they are jumping, leaping, squatting, falling or spinning, jumping, jumping, etc., they all show the personality characteristics of the Yao people who are passionate, unrestrained, strong and brave.
Today, the Yao people play long drums in villages, and are not limited to traditional festivals; they can play long drums during the National Day, Spring Festival, or when celebrating a good harvest. Its magical appeal has been recognized by the world.

As a result, on May 29 of the lunar calendar, on the mother's birthday, the third son rushed a fat pig to the mother's house to slaughter. At that time, the Yao people didn't have any dishes. After the meat was cooked, the third son cut a large piece with a knife, placed it in the palm of his hand, ordered some chili soup as a seasoning, and paid tribute to his mother and two older brothers, to express filial piety and gratitude. Since then, holding a large piece of meat in the palm of your hand to honor the venerable, the elderly, and family and friends has become a form of etiquette.”
