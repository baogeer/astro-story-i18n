 Preserving the year is the custom of not sleeping on the last night of the old year and staying up late to welcome the arrival of the new year. It is also called New Year's Eve, commonly known as “staying old.” Exploring the origin of this custom, there is an interesting story circulating in the folklore: in ancient times, there was a type of ferocious monster scattered deep in the mountains and jungle, and people called them “years.” It has a hideous appearance and is ferocious. It feeds exclusively on birds, animals, and scaly insects, and changes its flavor every day. From being eaten by gnats to large living people, people talk about the color of the “New Year.” Later, people slowly grasped the “New Year” activity rules. They traveled to crowded places every 365 days to taste delicious food, and they only appeared after dark. When the chickens sounded, they went back to the mountains and forests. Having calculated the date of the “New Year's Eve” raging, people saw this terrible night as “New Year's Eve,” and came up with a complete set of ways to celebrate the New Year's end: every night of the year, every household prepares dinner in advance, turns off the flames and cleans, then locks up all the front and back doors of the house, and hid in the house to eat the “New Year's Eve Dinner”. Since this dinner had an uncertain meaning, it was very rich. Apart from having the whole family eat a small meal together In addition to being in harmony and reunion, you must also sacrifice your ancestors before eating, ask for the blessings of their gods and spirits, and spend this night in peace and have eaten After dinner, no one dared to sleep, so they huddled together and chatted boldly. The habit of staying old on New Year's Eve gradually developed. The custom of keeping the year began in the North and South Dynasties, and many writers in the Liang dynasty had poems about keeping the year. “Two years in one night, five years more than two years.” People light candles or oil lanterns and stay up all night, as a symbol of driving away all evil plagues and looking forward to a happy and happy new year. This custom has been passed down to this day.
