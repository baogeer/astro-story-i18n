Choi Ji wasn't Helen, but she started a war. Of course, this wasn't her fault; it was just because she was young and invincible, crazy and cute, but she married a little old man who couldn't stand tall.
Tsai Ji's marriage to an old man was not voluntary; it was determined by national strength. As Princess Cai Guo, she was treated by her older brother Tsai Mu-hou as a gift from a small country to show goodwill to a big country, and married Qi Huan Gong. At the time, Choi Ji was just a teenage girl, and Qi Huan Gong was an old man who could be her father.
According to “Historical Records,” “Huan Gong is good at home and has many internal pets, such as his wife's six.” It can be seen that Qi Huan Gong was a person with good luck. An old husband and young wife, Qi Huan Gong also had sympathy for Choi Ji at first. Tsai Ji has a lively personality, loves to laugh and make fun, and is not one of the ladies.
Once, she went boating and paddling with Qi Huan Gong, and while having fun, she poured water on Qi Huan Gong. Qi Huan Gong, who is old and has always maintained his dignity, is a bit unaccustomed to this kind of gameplay, but he's not mad; he just laughs. But the more she played, the crazier Tsai Ji became. Suddenly, the boat rocked. This frightened Qi Huan Gong. He got upset: “Don't shake, don't shake.” The frightened appearance of Qi Huan Gong was probably very funny. Tsai Ji had no intention of stopping, and continued to wander around. As a result, Qi Huan Gong was furious and sent Choi Ji back to Cai Guo the next day.
Whoever wanted to have fun on a whim has made such a big deal. After Choi Ji was deported to Cai Guo, Tsai Mu-hou was very angry and felt that Qi Huan Gong really didn't give Cai Guo any face. As a result, he took the initiative to marry Tsai Ji to King Chu Cheng of Chu. King Chu Cheng didn't know that he married Qi Huan Gong's divorced wife. He loved Tsai Ji, and named her his wife. Originally, it was considered a bad thing to become a good thing. The dust settled, but Qi Huan Gong knew about this.
Qi Huan Gong heard that Tsai Ji, who had been deported by himself, had married someone else. Consciously lost her face and was extremely annoyed, she decided to attack Cai Guo and Chu Guo in order to regain her face. This spring and autumn hegemon is certainly overbearing. He actually doesn't allow divorced wives to remarry; for this reason, he also beat their newlywed husbands.
After all, there was a huge disparity in national strength, and Cai Guo quickly lost the war. Tsai Mu-hou fled to Chu in a hurry. Qi Huan Gong was still unwilling to rest and directly targeted Tsai Ji's new husband, King Chu Cheng. When the Qi soldiers arrived in the city, according to convention, Qi had to explain “in the name of starting a career,” because “having gone out of business without a name” is often made fun of, and it also seemed immoral. The people of Chu were very surprised. They thought that the attack on Chu by Qi was simply “out of place”: even if Qi's horny horses and cows compete, they won't be able to get to Chu. Qi Huan Gong, why have you been here all this time?
Faced with this kind of question, Qi Huangong was of course embarrassed to say that it wasn't for anything else, only that Chu Chengwang married my ex-wife. He had no choice but to say that it was because “Chu did not give Western Zhou Shanggong” because “Zhou Zhao and Wang died inexplicably in Chu country when they visited Chu” (it was Chu people who missed his boat and drowned him). People from Chu fought back and said, “Zhou Zhaowang died. Ask us what to do? You should ask Han Shui!”
Later, Qi sent Prime Minister Guan Zhong, and Chu sent out Qu Wan to make a mess. In the end, the class returned to North Korea because Qi's logic was poor and the reasons were insufficient. A bloody battle was spared. As you can imagine, if this war starts, the final responsibility will probably fall on Choi Ji. People will judge this innocent woman the same way they would judge Helen, satirize her for “turning all over the country,” and scold her for being “red-faced.”
Actually, speaking of it, this is nothing more than Qi Huan Gong's farce. When young women marry older men, in addition to their status and financial strength, they are also often because they have gone through many vicissitudes and are peaceful and tolerant, know how to cherish jade, and accept their immaturity. However, Qi Huan Gong, an old man, doesn't have a high level of emotional intelligence. The slightest matter causes a divorce. It's really ridiculous.
