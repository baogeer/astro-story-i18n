 There is a wealthy family who lives on the east side of Agutomba village. Although he has plenty of fertile land and countless gold and silver treasures, his greed is still growing. In order to pursue good profits, they actually became fraudulent merchants.
One evening, Agutomba didn't know where he came from. I saw him leisurely chasing two yaks, carrying four packs full of food on his back. When he quickly entered the village, it was strange that he didn't take the usual route home, but instead went around the east end of the village and deliberately beat this rich man by passing by. It turns out that he was trying to make big money owners greedy for big work, and evil thoughts were rampant. Unsurprisingly, the rich man saw it and immediately asked, “Agutomba, look at your bag full. What are those things?”
“Food.” Agton Barts answered very simply.
“Yo, you're not easy! Where did you get it?”
“Too poor to do anything, so I had to do a little trade! Yesterday, I brought some sharpening stones to Lhasa. This amount of food was exchanged for sharpening stones.”
Wealthy owners heard that “carrying some sharpening stones” was exchanged for so much food. They felt that this was a way to make money and was profitable; so they asked Agutomba in detail about the market situation in Lhasa. Agutomba had already guessed his mind and gave him a further explanation, saying, “Now, sharpening stones are really tasty in Lhasa! As soon as I got there, they sold it out. However, the market and price in this regard are also a bit strange: each large sharpening stone costs five or two silver; each small sharpening stone sells for five or two silver. I was unlucky this time; all of the sharpening stones I brought were big pieces. If they were small, that would be a great deal! ... Hearing this, the rich owners seemed to be in a hurry. Perhaps he thought he had heard everything he wanted to ask. At this point, he hadn't waited until Agutomba was introduced, so he hurriedly turned around, got rid of Agutomba who was talking, and went back to his dirty life.
That night, he ordered all his family's servants to smash all the sharpening stones that were already ready and used into small pieces. Faced with these small stones piled up like mountains, the puzzled wife of the rich man couldn't help but wonder, “You're crazy, why should you smash a good sharpening stone like this?” “You know damn it! When you arrive in Lhasa tomorrow, you'll know!” The rich man said with an arrogant expression, and his face was full of pride and a very mysterious smile.
Early the next morning, the rich owner himself set out and marched to Lhasa city with a long horse gang full of smashed small sharpening stones. As soon as I arrived in Lhasa, it certainly attracted quite a few “customers.” However, as soon as people saw that they were small pieces of gravel that were not in shape or used, they all became customers. The rich owner's sharpening stones were all day long, and not even a single piece was sold.
Later, he learned that he had been fooled. He hurriedly came back to “settle the accounts” and asked, “You said the sharpening stones were very tasty. I shipped them. Why didn't I buy any?”
Agton Barry bluntly answered, “Everyone bought mine yesterday. After only one night, why would they buy yours? Whetstones can't be eaten as food!”
