 The Xiongnu Empire once dominated Europe
In the middle of the 1st century AD, the Huns active on the Mongolian plateau were repeatedly defeated by the Eastern Han dynasty of China, and were also squeezed by the emerging Xianbei people on the Mongolian plateau, and had to migrate west. Since then, there are few references to the whereabouts of the Huns in history books over the next 300 years. In the 4th century AD, this mysterious people appeared in Europe again and went nomadic to the grassland of the Don River on the north bank of the Caspian Sea.
In 375 AD, the Huns began large-scale expansion. They destroyed the country established by the Alans and Ostrogoths west along the Danube River, conquered Armenia to the south, and invaded Persia and Syria. The Huns are famous for their cruelty. Where they passed, they often left ruins and empty bones. After taking over the Hungarian steppes, the Huns settled temporarily. In 433 AD, the Hun Dazan became the head of various departments in Attila and established a strong centralization of power. A huge empire emerged in the vast land from the Volga in the east to the Rhine in the west and the Danube in the south. During Attila's reign for 20 years, Buda, the capital of the Huns, became the political center of Europe. Messengers from various countries gathered here and scrambled to offer their own offerings and show surrender. The Xiongnu Empire is at its peak.
Star of the Huns share the same love with the king of the Huns
In the 5th century AD, the Roman Empire had its ups and downs. It became increasingly unable to cope in the conflict with various barbarian regimes and had to acquiesce to their independent status. At this point, a great figure in the history of the Roman Empire appeared. His name was Arches. Arches was born to a prominent family in Gaul. His father, Gordon Gius, had many battles in the Western Roman army, and finally became the cavalry commander of the Western Roman Empire and was crowned a count. Aches' teenage years were spent with the Goths and the Huns. During his time as a hostage by the Huns, Arches met many Hunnic aristocrats. With the support of the Huns, Arches quickly rose to prominence in Roman politics and became the Gallic governor of the Western Roman Empire. He fought against barbarians such as the Visigoths, the Franks, and the Alans in Gaul. He was famous for his repeated victories.
Arches and Attila have known each other since childhood, and the two have a close relationship. Attila once found Attila a knowledgeable and versatile personal secretary to help Attila manage diplomacy; he also sent his son to Attila to learn riding and shooting. Arches wanted peaceful coexistence with the Huns. He knew that Western Rome was already struggling to deal with the Germanic barbarians in the country and must never have bad relations with the Huns. The experience of being a hostage as a teenager gave Arches a deep understanding of the Huns. He was familiar with the tactics of the Huns, and was very aware of the weaknesses of the Huns. Archies' efforts have brought peace to the Western Roman Empire for more than 20 years. Attila attacked the Eastern Roman Empire many times during this time, but has always been at peace with the Western Roman Empire. However, a dispute over interests caused the two friends to finally pull out the sword, and Aches' rich experience made him a veritable Huns nemesis.
Asking Western Rome for women and land
Ambitious Attila has long coveted the prosperity of Gaul and Italy. In 449 AD, it was discovered that Onolia, the younger sister of the Western Roman Emperor, had an affair with the General Guard, and Emperor Valentinian placed her under house arrest in a monastery. The good-natured Honoria secretly wrote to Attila to ask for help, saying she would love to be with her. Attila immediately demanded Honoria from the Western Roman Emperor and asked the Western Roman Empire to take half of the land as a dowry. Such excessive and humiliating demands were rejected by the Western Roman emperor. So Attila used this as an excuse to launch a war against Western Rome.
In 450 AD, Attila assembled the Huns and 500,000 minions of conquered nations to attack Western Roman Gaul. As the famous Gallic cities fell one by one, Attila's army pointed directly at the famous city of Orleans. The ravages of northern Gaul by Attila's army shocked all the barbarians of the Western Roman Empire, and everyone realized that they could not fight the Huns with their own strength alone. Arches seized this opportunity to fight against the Huns and ran around, and finally joined forces with various barbarians to establish a united front against the Huns. Germanic barbarians from all over Gaul and Spain, and even the Celtic tribes of Britain sent troops to help, and Visigothic King Theodoric, who had fought with Arches for 20 years, personally led an army to help. When Attila joined forces in Orléans, the Western Roman coalition in Arches grew bigger and bigger. They had already assembled more than 500,000 people and officially went to war against the Huns. The two former friends finally met on the battlefield.

When Attila learned that the Western Roman allied forces were approaching Orléans, they immediately withdrew their encirclement north, and at the same time ordered the Huns that had been looted in various parts of Gaul to gather in the Champagne Plain. Arches led an army and followed. The two armies met near Chalons-sur-Marne and set up a decisive battle. On the side of the Western Roman Allied Forces, Archeus personally led the Western Roman Legion to the left. The Visigothic army was on the right wing, while the Alans and other barbarians were in the center. Archees' deployment was quite risky, because he placed the weakest part of the Western Roman Allied Forces in the middle, and was very easy to be broken through from the center by the Huns and cut off the Western Roman Front; however, on the other hand, the Hunnic forces that broke through the center were also at risk of being overtaken by the Western Roman Allies from both sides. Arches faced his old friend Attila and stepped out of a dangerous game. Attila went head-to-head, personally leading the elite riders of the Huns to the center, putting the East Goths on the left wing, and the other barbarian armies formed the right wing.
On September 20, 451 AD, the two armies began a decisive battle in Chalon. The two sides have invested more than 1 million troops in this battle. The Huns launched an attack first. Under the cover of a rain of arrows shrouded from the sky, the Huns rushed to the center of the Western Roman Allied Forces. The central front composed of barbarians could not resist it, and was deeply inserted in a wedge shape by the Huns cavalry. At this point, the Huns cavalry began to rotate to the left, encroaching on the Visigothic army. Attila was on the brink of war. He knew that the Western Roman Legion could not resist the fiercest attack by the Huns. However, the Visigoths are strong and strong, and they are rival opponents. If they can be annihilated, they will have the winning ticket. He organized the two wings of the Hunnian coalition to take the lead. At this point, the battle has turned into a scuffle, and the situation of the Western Roman coalition forces is critical. Although the fighting lasted only 5 hours, the dead were scattered all over the field, bloodshed, and 160,000 people died.
Strong Visigoths saved the Western Roman coalition forces. The Visigothic King Theodoric, who was in his sixties, personally led a counterattack. As a result, his arrow fell, and he was trampled and killed by the Visigothic Iron Riders who followed him. The Visigoths, who had lost their leader, showed only a moment of panic, and order was quickly restored under the command of Prince Torismon. The Visigothic cavalry fought back and crushed the Huns back, while the panicked Huns hit the shield line of the left-wing Western Roman Legion head-on and fell under heavy javelin fire one after another. At this point, the left-wing East Goths of the Huns were also unable to resist the impact of the Visigothic Iron Riders and were the first to flee. The battle of Sharon was divided between victory and defeat.
Attila was forced to lead the remnant army of the Huns back to the camp on the banks of the Marne. They were connected from beginning to end with Huns caravans, and archers were scattered all over the place, forming a very strong line of defense. Attila used a wooden saddle to build a hill and placed all his gold and silver jewels and concubines on it. He sat in the middle himself, planning to set himself on fire once the Western Roman army broke through his fortress.
Arches let Attila go at a critical moment. This prominent Western Roman leader had a long-term political vision. He believed that the greatest affliction of the Western Roman Empire was not the Huns, but the Gallic barbarians. Retaining the Huns as an external enemy made the barbarians, led by the Visigoths, afraid and had to continue cooperating with the Western Roman Empire. If Attila dies, the Huns Empire is bound to collapse, and the Gallic barbarians will definitely have to turn their heads against the Western Roman Empire.
Attila dies in a new house
Attila only lived for two years after escaping by chance during the Battle of Sharon. During these two years, he organized an army to attack the Eastern Roman Empire in an attempt to recover from the decline of the Xiongnu Empire. In 453 AD, Attila married Ildiko, a Germanic bride. He was very drunk during the wedding reception. The next day, the crowd walked into the new house and discovered that Attila's blood vessels had burst and died in a pool of blood, while his bride huddled down at the corner of her bed and shivered. At the time, some people thought Attila died of a circulatory disorder, while others suspected that Ildiko murdered Attila. At Attila's funeral, the Huns cut their hair, pierced their cheeks, and mourned their monarch with blood. Attila's coffin is divided into three layers: the outermost layer is iron, the second layer is silver, and the innermost layer is gold to symbolize his immortal exploits. The Huns stopped water from a river, buried Attila's body under a dry riverbed, and then opened the gate to release water. All slaves involved in the construction were executed so that future tomb raiders could not take advantage of it. His tomb has yet to be found.
After Attila's death, his sons fought a civil war to win over the Grand Order, and the Xiongnu Empire collapsed. In 454 AD, the East Goths and Gipids formed a coalition to defeat the Huns in Hungary. Since then, the Huns have been forced to retreat to the southern Russian steppes. In 461, one of Attila's sons tried to rebuild the Huns Empire and launched an East Goth war against the Danube River basin, which was defeated. In 468, he launched another war against the Eastern Roman Empire, and as a result, he died in battle. Since then, the Huns have been completely silent until forgotten by history.
