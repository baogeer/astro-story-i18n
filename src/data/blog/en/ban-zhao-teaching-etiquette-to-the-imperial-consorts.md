 Author: Hou Hongbin
Ban Zhao: Zi Huiban, a princess, an Eastern Han female historian and writer. Ban Biao's daughter, Bang Gu's sister, has family roots, is talented, knowledgeable, and literate. Ban Chao herself was often summoned to the Imperial Palace to teach the Empress and the nobles to recite the sutras. Gong Zhong Zong was her teacher. Famous scholars Ma Rong and Zheng Xuan were all her students. Ban Chao married Uncle Cao from the same county at the age of 14 and is known as the “Cao Family.” Ban Zhao helped her brother Bangu complete the writing of China's first chronicle history “Han Shu,” and also wrote the first complete Chinese code for women, “The Women's Commandment,” which came about
A profound impact. Ban Zhao-nian passed away, and the Empress Dowager paid tribute to her in plain clothes.
Every woman who loves fashion can't stand the shame of being backward. Especially a very smart woman like Ban Chao.
At that time, it was still in the 1st century AD, and the customs of drug use in the two Jin dynasties were not popular (mainly, Wu Shi San had not been invented yet); the street hippie with a loose head was also a famous style of celebrity after 200 years; and the wrinkled Liu Xian dress, like Zhao Feiyan's, was very Bohemian, but women all over the street have been wearing it for decades; and she, after all, is a woman from high society. She's still married to someone and can't play punk or hip-hop yet, right?
Ban Zhao looked out of the Dongguan Library window and was a little worried. What will I wear to the palace tomorrow?
Ban Chao is famous. His grandfather was Guangping Taishou Ban Zhi; his father was a historian and writer Ban Biao; his older brother, Bang Gu, wrote “Han Shu,” and his second brother is Dingyuan Hou Banchao, who wrote in the Rong, and he all have great taste. And she herself spent many years living alone in historical materials, and painstakingly became a famous historian, writer, astronomer, and mathematician. With his inheritance, dowry, royalties, and funding from the National Social Science Research Fund, Ban Zhao finally had a decent life. However, she still has a slight pain.
Early marriage was popular in Eastern Han. Although there are many benefits to getting married late, the scariest thing is that you may never get married. Someone like Meng Guang who hasn't married at the age of 28 would simply not have survived unless they met Liang Hong, who filed a crime with Qi Mei. Ban Zhao was not left behind; he married his brother Cao Shou at age 14. Unfortunately, Cao Shou died early; she was widowed at a young age. This causes a problem: dressed modernly, and afraid that people would say that they are in spring; dressed in old fashion, and afraid of neighborhood jokes. As a result, when Ban Zhao, who is in his 20s, went to the court to give lectures to the empress and concubine, he showed up wearing a lace strappy bandeau one day, a Victorian high neck robe, and another day wearing an ancient Greek ruffle dress, fluctuating between conservative and avant-garde. The concubines, who are still obsessed with Yappie and only know how to draw their eyebrows, started to imitate this little widow's attire. Next, all the women walking down the street were wearing the clothes Ban Zhao had worn yesterday; at the entrance of Xianyang City, there was a sign saying how to wear a top today and how to dress next. Refreshed daily. Ban Chao was once again in disarray. Depressed, extremely depressed.
Finally, one day, Ban Chao was open and cheerful: if you want to be at the forefront of the times, you have to play subversive; it must not be painful or itchy. Sister Feiyan of the previous generation proposed the “glass of water doctrine,” and also loved to play with multiple P's, wife swapping, and les. It was popular all over the country. At one time, she was the “Patriarch of Oriental Pop”; she did the opposite and invented a series of cool costumes. She stayed dusty when walking, and was not happy with ripples in her movements. In conjunction with this fashion press conference, she wrote a book with theoretical guidance called “The Female Commandment,” which is sold in bundles with clothing. As a result, any woman who wants to buy her costume series, or a man who wants to give this dress to a girlfriend, must memorize the contents of “The Woman's Commandment.” Anyone who cannot recollect the “Three Virtues from the Four Virtues”, “having a man is like a wolf, still afraid of their fate; having a daughter is still afraid of a tiger”, “there is no meaning for a husband to marry again, there is no right for a woman”, “being quiet and honest, being orderly, acting shameful, and acting lawfully”, etc., must not buy it. Since each edition is a limited edition, piracy of “The Woman's Commandment” was rampant for a while, and women feared they wouldn't be able to pick it up and it would go out of print next time.
Ban Chao hid behind a curtain and secretly laughed. Every set of clothes has a logo with the word “Jeong”. They always pretend to unwittingly reveal this logo, which means that the dressed woman is already familiar with “The Commandment for Women.” Just as every woman has an LV heart today, then in Xianyang, and all over the country, every woman had a “female commandment” heart. Ban Zhao achieved great commercial success and quickly replaced the Feiyan sisters as fashion pioneers. Soon, Ban Chao was called “Master Cao” by Han and Emperor, probably the same as calling Yang Jiang “Mr. Yang” and Song Meiling as “Mr. Song.”
I didn't expect being single to be this successful; Ban Chao was moved by himself. She died when she was 71, and the “Women's Commandment Collection” changed to a chief designer. As a result, the clothes sold very poorly, and were later replaced by the pop style of broad robes and large sleeves. However, books have been handed down all the time, so much so that future generations only knew their books and didn't know their clothing. Since then, a series of subsequent works such as “Women's History Prove,” “Women's Rules,” “Women's Hyo Sutra,” “The Woman's Analects,” “Internal Training,” “The Girl's School,” and “Women's Studies,” have all failed to achieve the sales miracle of “The Woman's Commandment.”
Ban Chao had his own sustenance. He had no problem; however, he made all the women of the next 2,000 years as harsh and serious as if they were in a monastery. She also has an innocent face: if you want to blame, blame your descendants for being good; I'm just selling shirts. abstinence? It's none of my business; S/M? It's none of my business.
