Before we talk about the story of Cai Yi and Xiao Fengxian, let's take a look at an old story between Liu Bannong and Sai Jinhua.
In 1934, Peking University professor Liu Bannong visited the door six times to conduct an in-depth interview with the famous prostitute Sai Jinhua. For a while, it became a major event in the cultural world. As a result, Liu Bannong contracted the disease and died that year. “Sai Jinhua Skills” was completed by his student Shang Hongkui. According to the recollection of Zhao Xiaoyi, an old Taiwanese newspaper, Liu Bannong and Sai Jinhua first met at a social dinner and talked about the past “saving the country in bed.” The train was full of words. The big guys in attendance, such as Hu Shi and Fu Si-nian, heard very loud and “all were happy.” In the end, as a result of discussions among the bosses, Liu Bannong, the translator of “The Lady in the Camellia,” decided to write an oral record of the number one prostitute in China.
Why has the story of Little Phoenix been around for so many years? Liu Bannong's interview with Sai Jinhua is a background and reference. Women's liberation is an important part of the May Fourth Movement and the evolution of the ideology of the Republic of China. However, not many women were able to step up to the front desk at the time. There were very few women in the revolutionary community itself. Female intellectuals in the new era are still growing, so it is inevitable that people look for legends in popular figures. Furthermore, the status of women is indeed rising dramatically — Peking University professors visited a generation of famous prostitutes, and the incident itself had considerable dissemination value and symbolic significance.
Sai Jinhua is very good at self-packaging and has close relationships with the “communication community”. Apart from Liu Bannong, Zeng Fan, Qi Rushan, and Zhang Renshui have all written her stories or stories. In an age where there was no television, the influence of these newspaper bullies far surpassed “Do Not Disturb”. Little Phoenix, and her legendary love affair with Cai Yi, is a completely different kind of story — there are many speculations and bystanders about her, and there are very few objective records.
More of the wonderful story of Cai Yi and Little Phoenix should come from people's imagination. In times of war and turmoil, the country was in crisis. The young general was a handsome and elegant young general, a true Qinglou woman who fell out of dust and mud; there were contrasts in identity, conflict, and grudge. This kind of story is very much in line with the Chinese aesthetic. People sincerely hope that in this troubled world, there will be not only great people, but also couples; the former saves the nation, and the latter moves China. Just as someone would write “Man Jiang Hong” under the name of Yue Fei, that phrase is so well spelled up, and it fits perfectly with what we imagine Yue Fei himself — but it really isn't real; it's just a literate role-playing game. In our culture, it's always easy to confuse the two things of goodness and truth.
A good story in the minds of the Chinese begins with a young man's dream.
After Cai Yi was 17, she went to Japan to study twice, studying business first, then attacking the military. Returned home at age 22 to supervise the new army. In 1911, at the age of 29, Cai Yi responded to the Wuchang Uprising in Yunnan and was publicly promoted as the leader, and later became the governor of the Yunnan military government. For example, the young general from Japan, China and China was greatly appreciated by Yuan Shikai, and Cai Yi also claimed in a public communication that Yuan was “brilliant and brilliant, and the hope was well received”, and gladly went to Beijing to serve as a senior official in the Beiyang government. Yuan Shikai even made Yuan Keding worship Cai Yi as his mentor — everyone understood the meaning of “First Assistant to the Prince.”
The honeymoon period ended in 1915, when Yuan Shikai decided to become emperor. Cai Yi, a revolutionary, did not tolerate Hong Xian's farce at all. He vowed to “use force to fight for the personality of 40,000 people” and go back to Yunnan to launch the famous Patriotic War. The above are all historical facts, and they are also the basic themes of emotional legends.
Meanwhile, Little Phoenix appeared at the time when Cai Yi and Yuan Shikai broke up.
The two met in the midst of ups and downs. According to some tabloids at the time, their relationship eventually developed to the point where they discussed marriage. As a high-ranking official, why did Cai Yi hang out at the Eight Great Hutong? First, sex prostitutes are entirely a classic style of famous Chinese people; second, during the liberation era, they had an open temperament, and the story of Liu Bannong can be used as a reference. Cai Yi became Liu Bei, who grew vegetables, and Yuan Shikai became Cao Cao who cooked green plum wine.

Although it is impossible to prove the truth or falsehood, the story does have some justification — Cai Yi escaped from Beijing in 1915. In the spring of the following year, he led an army to defeat Yuan Jun in Sichuan.
