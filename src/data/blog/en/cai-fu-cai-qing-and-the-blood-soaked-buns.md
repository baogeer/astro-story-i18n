 In the past, there was a custom of avoiding evil and driving away demons in northern China. Wherever someone “knocked over”, hit a ghost, or was haunted by a ghost, the witch Shenhan would eat a type of human blood steamed bun for patients. This kind of human blood bun is very careful and must be obtained from the murderer. When the executioner executed, he prepared many steamed buns in advance. After the murder, he spilled blood and stained all the steamed buns. It is said that this kind of blood-stained steamed bun can drive away demons, avoid evil, and test the spirits. As a result, this kind of blood steamed bun is very expensive.
Legend has it that this kind of human blood steamed bun originated from Cai Fu and Cai Qing.
Cai Fu, known as the “Iron Arm”, was imprisoned by the Beijing Daming Government and an executioner; Cai Qing is Cai Fu's younger brother, a minor prisoner, and an execution executioner. He was born to love a flower. For this reason, they all call him “One Branch Flower” Cai Qing.
The two brothers, Cai Fu, and Cai Qing grew up together in the government. Seeing that they were involved in all other errands, they all had a lot of fuel and water. They were all executed, and nothing was easy to catch. They were very unbalanced in their hearts, so they wanted to get some oil and water. What is the “fishing” method? Looking for an executed prisoner? No good. They are empty-handed; they have nothing but one body, and they have no right to exempt others from capital crimes. Thinking back and forth, I haven't thought about coming up with a path.
On this day, Cai Fu and Cai Qing went to death again. Outside the court, the family of the executed person knelt down. A large bundle of burning paper was piled up next to the family to burn after death.
Burning paper turned out to be a very common thing, and it was also a custom passed down from ancient times. The Cai brothers were used to it, and they didn't take it seriously at all.
One day, the two brothers visited a friend's house. A friend is burning paper for a dead loved one. During the conversation, a friend asked the two execution officers, do they know the origin of burning paper?
Let's take a look at how common the two burn paper. If we want to go back to the source and talk about the origin, they really don't know. A friend then spoke to them.
Legend has it that during the Eastern Han dynasty, Cai Lun invented the art of papermaking. But no one bought the paper that was made. The husband and wife made up a plan — pretend to die. After Cai Lun's “death,” his wife cried while burning the paper she made. Soon, Tsai Lun came “alive.” Tsai Lun told everyone that the paper for tsumayaki is money in the dark. He used this money to buy up Hades and let him go... Since then, Cai Lun's paper has become a sought-after item, and after people died, they followed suit. Over time, it became a folk custom.
This story told by a friend was originally a casual joke, but Cai Fu and Cai Qing got into his head. They think our ancestors, Tsai Lun and his wife, can use plans to promote their own goods, so why don't we write articles based on our own strengths? As a result, I mixed up my brain and came up with the idea of “human blood steamed buns.”
They can't sell human blood steamed buns themselves; they have to find someone to sell them. Who are you looking for? They naturally thought of the witch god Han. At the time, there was a shortage of medicine, and there were many patients. The witch Shenhan flourished in response to the times and became a “savior” for patients. The two quietly colluded with these people and made up a set of “human blood steamed buns to exorcise evil spirits.”
Since then, the witch god Han has used human blood steamed buns obtained from the executioner Cai Fu and Cai Qing to “cure diseases and drive away evil.” The more this law is spread, the more divine it is, and the more it is spread, the more evil it becomes. Cai Fu and Cai Qing made a fortune, and so did the witch god Han. Over time, it actually spread among the people, passed down from generation to generation, and became a “custom” in the northern region.
