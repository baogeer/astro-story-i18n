 Sun Shaozhen is a professor and doctoral supervisor at the School of Literature at Fujian Normal University, former vice chairman of the Fujian Writers Association and president of the Fujian Writing Society. His main representatives include “Theory of Literary Creation”, “Interpretation of Literary Texts”, “The Rise of New Aesthetic Principles in the East”, “Breaking Through and Constructing a Text Center”, “The Complete Interpretation of Classical Prose”, “A Microscopic Case Study of Classical Poetry”, and “Speak the Charm of Classics”.
Speaker: Sun Shaozhen Lecture Venue: South China Agricultural University Online Lecture Time: March 2022
“Seven Truths and Three Fictions” from “Romance of the Three Kingdoms”
“Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is China's classic novel, and it is also the country's earliest long novel. Judging from the history of world literature, around the 14th century AD, when “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” was written, classic long stories did not appear in other parts of the world, at least in Europe. There were only a few short stories, such as Boccaccio's “Ten Day Tales” and some knights novels.
“Romance of the Three Kingdoms” used to be out of place; it belongs to folk culture. Until the May Fourth New Cultural Movement, Hu Shi, Lu Xun, and others incorporated it into the history of orthodox literature. However, Hu Shi and Lu Xun rated “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” relatively low, or rather negative.
For example, Hu Shi believes that “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” has a big problem. It combines materials related to “The Three Kingdoms”, plus some Song Yuan operas, and some elements from “The World Speaks a New Language,” and combines these stories, etc., with no literary imagination, and very weak creativity. In Hu Shi's view, “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is only a very appealing popular historical novel. Furthermore, he also believes that the author's ideas are very corrupt. Hu Shi calls it a “bad guy,” believing that the author is too obsessed with history and lacks imagination, so it cannot be called art. Hu Shi's opinion is consistent with Lu Xun. Lu Xun was speaking on the history of Chinese novels at the Beijing Women's Teacher Training School. Hu Shi read Lu Xun's speeches, so he was influenced by Lu Xun on this issue.
Lu Xun's evaluation of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” was quite academic. He quoted the views of the Qing dynasty scholar Zhang Xuecheng. Zhang Xuecheng proposed “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” “Seven Truths and Three Fictions,” that is, the seven points are similar to the official historical records of “Three Kingdoms,” while the other three points are fictional. As a result, “mixed rhetoric is easy to confuse” and “confuse viewers.” Lu Xun also agreed with this view. On the artistic side, Lu Xun criticized two characters in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” for being very poorly written. One was Liu Bei. In order to show his generosity, he turned out to be very hypocritical. The second one is Zhuge Liang. Lu Xun said that Zhuge Liang was written as “a wise and close demon,” and is so intelligent that he is like a monster.
All in all, Hu Shi and Lu Xun have a rather negative attitude towards “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms”, from ideology to art.
I think the opinions of Hu Shi and Lu Xun on “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” are very interesting. The contributions of the two gentlemen to literature are clear, but in today's view, some of their evaluations of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” are biased. For example, with regard to criticism of Liu Bei's image, I personally think that Liu Bei in the novel is a warlord in a troubled world. He keeps half of his speech, or is indifferent to what people say. This is also normal for warlords.
In fact, from the Three Kingdoms period to the advent of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” at the end of the Yuan Dynasty, the story of the Three Kingdoms has been around for about 1,000 years, and various related folk stories, literary creations, poetry adaptations, etc. have appeared, which include very deep national cultural traditions and historical accumulation. The creation of “Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is a grand project. It describes the history of the Three Kingdoms for less than 100 years, and the author's main material when writing the novel was the biographies of hundreds of people in “Three Kingdoms”. “Romance of the Three Kingdoms” links the contents of these biographies of historical figures originally divided into different countries and turned them into an organic whole, eventually forming a great epic work.
Many of the phrases commonly used in our lives come from “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms”. For example, “Go Shirtless” comes from a description of brave general Xu Chu, and “He Shengliang” comes from a plot where Zhu Geliang and Zhou Yu fight wisely. There are also “Zhou Yu fights against Huang Gai; one wants to fight another wish”, “lost his wife and joined the army”, “said Cao Cao and Cao arrived,” “lost Jingzhou,” and “borrowed from Dongfeng.” The fact that so much content has spread to this day also shows that the artistic power of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is deeply rooted in the hearts of the people.
In particular, in terms of character formation, the images of Guan Yu, Zhu Geliang, Zhao Yun, Zhang Fei, etc. created in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” are very vivid. Not only do they have long-term and far-reaching influence in our country, but even in countries such as Japan, North Korea, South Korea, and Vietnam, they also have great influence. Mao Zonggang, who deleted “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” once commented that there are three main characters in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms”, namely Cao Cao, Zhu Geliang, and Guan Yu. Their images are immortal. Our lecture is mainly from the perspective of character formation. Let's talk about the value of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” by taking a few representative figures as examples.
Cao Cao, a character with an extremely complex personality
Reading “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is indeed easy to cause a problem, because some of its contents and history are contradictory and easy to confuse.
For example, the artistic image of Cao Cao in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” affirmed by Mao Zonggang and Cao Cao's impression from a historical perspective are quite far apart: after the Yellow Scarf Uprising at the end of the Eastern Han Dynasty until the brief reunification of Western Jin, the war continued for more than 90 years. Household registration was scattered, disaster was rampant, the population was drastically reduced, culture was destroyed, and the survival and development of the Chinese nation was greatly affected. In his poem, Cao Cao said, “White bones are in the wild; there are no chickens for a thousand miles. “People are left behind, and thinking breaks one's gut.” Tsai Wenji's poem also said, “Hang a man's head on the horse, carry a woman behind the horse.” Although the poem contains artistic exaggerations, it clearly reflects the serious lack of protection for people's lives due to the long war and turmoil at the time. The long war between warlords left the Eastern Han dynasty empty. In a troubled world, everyone with force can compete to become an emperor. Whoever has the strongest force can end this disaster will be viewed as a hero. Therefore, when future generations looked at this period of history, they thought this was an era where heroes were born. However, Cao Cao grew into the strongest power in the midst of this war. He kidnapped the emperors to make the princes very strong economically and military, relatively open in politics, and finally unified the North. Therefore, Cao Cao can also be called a hero. However, “Romance of the Three Kingdoms” apparently did not use Cao Cao as a hero. The author arranged a lot of vivid content for Cao Cao and wrote him as a ruthless, spicy, cruel, and murderous adulterer.
This difference between fiction and history can be extended to our evaluation of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” which is not the same as the evaluation criteria for “The Three Kingdoms.” “The Chronicles of the Three Kingdoms” is an official history; “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is a novel, mostly written by a writer; its value lies not in the truth but in the beauty of emotion. What the novel wants to reveal is the heart of a person. The characters it describes are warlords or peasants; it focuses on perspective and insight into people's inner feelings. In this sense, the characters in the novel may be fictional from a historical perspective, but if it is possible to vividly reveal his emotional world, inner thoughts, and perceptions by creating this character's image, then it has literary value. In the language of aesthetics, it is aesthetic value.
Therefore, let's take another look at Cao Cao in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” from the perspective of literary value. Although he is a fictional image of literature, the fiction is very wonderful, and it is still very realistic to read today.
It is worth explaining that although “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” treats Cao Cao as a ruthless villain, this does not mean that “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” simply stigmatizes Cao Cao. In fact, “Romance of the Three Kingdoms” demonizes Cao Cao on the one hand, and glorifies Cao Cao on the other. For example, as mentioned in “The Chronicles of the Three Kingdoms,” the warlord Dong Zhuo placed more emphasis on Cao Cao during his time in controlling the Eastern Han dynasty and gave Cao Cao the position of captain. This was moderately treated at the time, but Cao Cao thought there was no future with Dong Zhuo, so he slipped away. Regarding this episode, “Romance of the Three Kingdoms” dealt with Cao Cao, although he was highly regarded by Dong Zhuo, but he did not like Dong Zhuo. At this time, Situ Wang Yun felt that Dong Zhuo's dictatorship had caused the Han family to fall apart. Everyone met to discuss how to confront Dong Zhuo. Cao Cao generously and passionately took the initiative to assassinate Dong Zhuo. This plot clearly glorified Cao Cao. Then, the story of Cao Cao leaving Beijing later is described in “The Chronicles of the Three Kingdoms” that Cao Cao said goodbye to Dong Zhuo. When he changed his name and passed by Zhongmou, he was caught by the local pavilion manager. Some local people recognized Cao Cao and let him go; the matter was over. However, the corresponding period of history in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is that Cao Cao was forced to flee Beijing because he failed to assassinate Dong Zhuo and was arrested while passing by Zhongmou. When the county governor Chen Gong interrogated him, Cao Cao treated him with dignity. In the end, he was moved by Chen Gong. He abandoned the official and fled with Cao Cao. This once again glorified Cao Cao.
However, the depiction of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” began to take a sharp turn. After Cao Cao and Chen Gong escaped, they killed Lu Bosu's family because of a misunderstanding. When he later learned that he had mistakenly killed a good person, he killed Lu Boyu again in order to destroy them. This is a plot not found in “The Three Kingdoms.” This section is a demonization of Cao Cao in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms.” In this way, Cao Cao became a young righteous man who never feared life or death, to a great wrongdoer who indiscriminately killed innocent people, and also said a widely spread phrase: “I'd rather teach me to bear the world, and stop teaching the world to bear me.” The original version of this phrase was “I'd rather lose to others than anyone else”. Chen Shou didn't use it when he wrote “The Three Kingdoms,” and later, when Pei Songzhi made notes on “The Three Kingdoms,” it was recorded again. One of the original versions of this story is that when Cao Cao fled, they thought Lu Bosu's family would murder him, so they took advantage of the night to kill people, then said this in the midst of misery. Looking at it now, the original meaning of Cao Cao's statement was probably to say that he had no choice but to kill people. He was still quite sad and helpless at the time. However, in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” this sentence was adjusted, and Cao Cao's mentality became that he was not at all sad about murder, believing that he could hurt everyone as long as he was in danger. After this change, Cao Cao became a cruel and murderous person.
So, isn't the novel's portrayal of Cao Cao's character transformation too abrupt? It's not. The greatness of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is shown here. It says that Cao Cao is a good person at first. He is a strong and bloody righteous man, but Cao Cao's mentality is also very unhealthy, mainly because he has a suspicious personality. Doubts. Originally, many warlords and counselors in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” had a problem, but Cao Cao's doubtful nature was very characteristic. Other people's doubts are twofold. They think that a person may be good or bad, while Cao Cao's doubtful is very “firm”. He believes that as long as he suspects, it is someone who is bad, that is, someone who is a threat to Cao Cao; furthermore, follow-up actions that others doubt may be undecided; however, Cao Cao will act immediately after being doubtful, and even if he knows that innocent people are killed indiscriminately.
The core character of Cao Cao, portrayed in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” is suspicious, and his fate changed a series of changes because of his doubts. For example, in the Battle of Chibi, Cao Cao was hit by Zhou Yu and Huang Gai's counterplan because of his doubts, and executed his navy superintendents Cai Hao and Zhang Yun without any definitive proof, causing Cao Jun to be overwhelmed by water battles. Moreover, Cao Cao's questionable personality continued throughout his life, and even Cao Cao's death was due to his doubts. In his later years, Cao Cao suffered from a headache and sought treatment from Huayuo. The prototype for this story comes from “The Three Kingdoms.” The records say that Huayu treated Cao Cao. Later, after taking a leave of absence, Cao Cao sought him again. Huayu repeatedly deduced that his wife was sick and was late. Cao Cao then sent someone to verify the incident and said that if Huayu was true, they would distribute food to him, and take him back if Huayu lied. As a result, Huayu was arrested and eventually died in prison. However, in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” Huayu said that in order to cure Cao Cao's headache, Cao Cao felt that Huayu wanted to kill him by taking advantage of treatment, so he imprisoned Huayu. In the end, Hua Tuo died in prison, and his medical book about his life experiences also disappeared.
The great thing about “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is that it not only links Cao Cao's decades of life experiences through his questionable personality, but also shows the other side of Cao Cao's doubts, which is unquestionable. Cao Cao's doubts are of others, while his doubts are his own.
In “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” Cao Cao is very confident and even narcissistic in his own judgment. For example, when the wandering guest sent Liu Bei to go to Cao Cao, Cao Cao's mentor advised him, saying that Liu Bei is an ambitious person and is also from the Han family, and should be killed. However, Cao Cao believes that Liu Bei himself is nothing remarkable. After killing Liu Bei, his reputation may be damaged, and others will not dare to surrender to himself in the future. Afterwards, there is a famous plot in the novel, “Cooking Wine on Heroes.” Cao Cao said that the heroes of the world are you, Liu Bei and me. When Liu Bei heard this, he pretended to be very scared, saying that he was frightened by lightning in the sky. Cao Cao laughed out loud that heroes are also afraid of thunder, so he let his guard down. Later, chaos broke out in Xuzhou. Liu Bei found an excuse to claim that he wanted to attack Yuan Shu for Cao Cao, and left after borrowing 5,000 soldiers and horses from Cao Cao. After Liu Bei left, Cao Cao's mentor said he couldn't let go, and Cao Cao sent Xu Chu and 500 people to go after Liu Bei. Xu Chu is a brave general under Cao Cao, but he has a simple mind and doesn't understand strategy. He sent someone like this to go after Liu Bei, and 500 people chase 5,000 people. Obviously, he can't catch up. Cao Cao's advisors also thought the same. They felt it would be a pity not to be able to catch up with Liu Bei, but Cao Cao would never publicly admit his mistakes. He said that since he used someone, they wouldn't doubt him. “If I sent him, how can I regret it?” This is how Liu Bei successfully escaped. This is Cao Cao's unquestionable side; “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” was a great display.
In “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” Cao Cao's attitude toward his advisers was not the same as Liu Bei and Sun Quan. Compared to Liu Bei's treatment of Zhu Geliang, Sun Ce, and Sun Quan's treatment of Zhou Yu, Cao Cao's attitude towards the counselor was to listen if he wanted to, not listen to what he didn't, and if he lost the war, he would shift responsibility to the counselor. This is not to say that Cao Cao doesn't pay attention to talent; he attaches great importance to talent, but he cannot tolerate that his mentors are clearly superior to his own.
After careful analysis, there are complex causes for Cao Cao's mentality. There are three points in the world. Liu Bei can rely on his Han lineage, and Sun Quan also has his father and brother's heritage. Not only does Cao Cao not have this, but he also has a family stain. That is, his grandfather was a lieutenant. His father was the adopted son of a lieutenant. Cao Cao's suspicious and narcissistic personality is also partly due to his relatively insufficient political capital. The novel describes Cao Cao losing to Huarong in the Battle of Chibi. Seeing the dangerous terrain in the area, he still had to laugh a little bit and laugh at Zhu Geliang, who was not as good as him. Of course, this kind of behavior also encouraged the disabled soldiers, but even if it only had 5 minutes to show his wisdom and superiority, Cao Cao wouldn't let go of it.
Cao Cao in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” attaches great importance to talent. Once he discovers that someone is particularly talented, he will make every effort to dig up the corner. For example, Cao Cao was defeated by Liu Bei in the Battle of Xinye. After the war, it was discovered that Liu Bei was assisted by military commander Xu Shu to be so powerful, so he held back Xu Shu's mother as a threat, and Xu Shu was forced to go to Cao Cao's place. After that, Liu Beibing defeated Nagasakapo, and Zhao Yun clashed to rescue Ah Dou. Cao Cao saw Zhao Yun Xiaoyong and moved his mind to pull him over, so he was not allowed to fire an arrow. As a result, Zhao Yun successfully broke out of the encirclement. After Liu Beibing's defeat in Xuzhou, Guan Yu surrendered to Cao Cao with Liu Bei's two wives. Guan Yu proposed conditions during his surrender. Specifically, he surrendered to Han and did not surrender to Cao. Additionally, Liu Bei's two wives must be treated with courtesy. Cao Cao is a pragmatic person. He loves talent so much that he agreed to Guan Yu's conditions. After Guan Yu surrendered, Cao Cao made a lot of emotional investments, and gave them to Guan Yu's wealthy women. As a result, Guan Yu left on his own for thousands of miles after getting the news from Liu Bei. Cao Cao still had to be very generous on the surface. Since Guan Yu was not officially released to let Guan Yu go, Guan Yu went through five levels and six generals along the way. In the end, Cao Cao put down documents to let Guan Yu go. Lu Xun really appreciates this episode of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms”. He believes that although Cao Cao is an adulterer, he is very generous here and loves talent.
However, when Cao Cao discovered that his mentor's strategy surpassed his own, his mentality changed. In the battle between China and China described in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” Cao Cao's army was in a dilemma. At this point, Cao Cao's bookkeeper Yang Xiu showed ingenuity. He saw through Cao Cao's mentality at a glance and immediately prepared to retire from the military. Cao Cao was seen through his mind and was very unhappy. At the beginning, when Liu Bei was under Cao Cao, he paid great attention to keeping the light behind, but Yang Xiu preferred Lu Cai to promote himself. “Romance of the Three Kingdoms” specifically explains the bad relationship between Cao Cao and Yang Xiu. Among them, it was mentioned that Cao Cao passed by Cao Eu's monument. There are eight characters written by Cai Yong on the monument: “young woman with yellow silk, grandson.” These eight characters imply four characters. Cao Cao asked Yang Xiu, can you tell which four words are implied? If the question was Liu Bei, he definitely said I couldn't see it. Yang Xiu didn't care about this; he directly answered that I had already seen it. Cao Cao just said don't talk about it; let me think about it. It was only after the group walked 30 miles that Cao Cao asked Yang Xiu to answer. Yang Xiu explained that Huang Yan was talking about colored silk, with a color on the side of the silk characters, which together is the word “absolute”; “young woman” is a young girl, and when taken together, it is a “wonderful” word. Dismantled in this way, the four characters implicit in Cai Yong's eight characters are “wonderful words.” Cao Cao said, “Your answer is exactly what I want. See “The World Speaks a New Language” for the original version of this story. In the original story, Cao Cao said that my intelligence was 30 miles short of Yang Xiu. However, adjustments were made in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” and it became that Cao Cao was unwilling to admit that he was not as good as Yang Xiu. Cao Cao in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” used this sense of wisdom and superiority to maintain his confidence and the legitimacy of his rule. If his subordinate is smarter than him, this subordinate is dangerous. Yang Xiu's death was due to Cao Cao considering his heir in his later years. At first, Cao Zhi's performance was more in line with Cao Cao. As a result, Cao Cao discovered that the reason Cao Zhi performed well was because of Yang Xiu's behind-the-scenes mentality. Cao Cao worried that in the future, if Cao Zhi's ability could not hold back Yang Xiu, he would be controlled by Yang Xiu, just as Emperor Han Xian was controlled by his Cao Cao, or Cao Zhi was relatively ignorant, then the results could only be worse. So Cao Cao killed Yang Xiu.
Precisely because of this kind of thinking, we saw that when the Battle of Chibi was about to begin, Cao Cao would lead a large group of people on a boat to write the poem “A Song for Wine, Geometry of Life”. This is a very classic poem. He wanted to show in front of his subordinates that he was not only good at fighting, but also very literate.
Compared to history, “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” portrayed Cao Cao, who used talents like Cao, quite in line with the actual situation. Tang Taizong once passed by Cao Cao Temple. His inscription in the temple mentioned that Cao Cao cannot allow his subordinates to be superior to him. Such a narrow mindset is a drawback for leaders. Tang Taizong believed that the ability of a leader was not necessarily superior to that of a mentor; it was enough to be able to lead these advisors, and commented that Cao Cao “a general's wisdom is more than enough, and not enough to be an emperor.”
In addition to his core personality traits, the image of Cao Cao in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is actually very rich.
Cao Cao was very forgiving at times. For example, the famous man Neheng offended Cao many times, including scolding Cao and others. Cao Cao didn't kill him, and finally sent him to Liu Biao's subordinate Huang Zu. You Heng, under Huang Zu's subordinate, continued to curse people, and was eventually killed in anger by Huang Zu. Cao Cao used a knife to kill someone. He allowed people to scold him, but if he didn't kill him himself, he wanted someone else to kill him. Sometimes Cao Cao acted in an unfathomable manner. When he led an army to prepare for the Southern Expedition, Kong Rong firmly opposed it. If Cao Cao didn't listen, Kong Rong said, “Even if they were not benevolent, they would be invincible!” Cao Cao was furious when he heard it, and arrested Kong Rong and killed him. Cao Cao is also very insidious at times. When he attacked Yuan Shu, military rations were insufficient, so he instructed his military recruiters to withhold military rations, then the sergeants became dissatisfied. He also called the military quarters' officer and “wanted to use your head to show his ears,” promised to support his family, and then killed the military commander to calm the military chaos. Cao Cao was also sometimes very ferocious. In the Yidaizhao incident involving Liu Bei and Dong Cheng, Cao Cao caught the participant Ji Ping and interrogated him to ask him to confess who the mastermind was. Ji Ping refused to confess, and Cao Cao used extremely cruel punishment to maim Ji Ping. Cao Cao also showed ridiculously timidity at times. When Zhang Fei cut down Nagasakabashi, he led the army but was frightened away by Zhang Fei's few people.
Cao Cao's personality in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is just that; it is extremely complicated. Through the novel's wonderful description, we feel that he is very sad, very despicable, very hateful, very shameful, very valuable, and also very unfortunate, very pathetic, very happy, and very abhorrent. Taken together, these terms may not be enough to describe Cao Cao's rich personality.
The psychological relationships between characters embodied in “Polyintellect” Zhu Geliang
Now let's talk about Zhu Geliang again.
Lu Xun commented on “The Three Kingdoms” by Zhu Geliang's “Smart and Close Demon.” This makes sense. Zhu Geliang in “The Three Kingdoms” is so smart that he can forecast the weather more than 1,000 years ago. One of the classic passages in the book The Battle of Chibi is about Zhuge Liang borrowing an arrow. This story is fictional; it is not in “The Chronicles of the Three Kingdoms.” But this piece of fiction is very well made. Moreover, the fiction of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” has been built up through history. After many years of baptism, many untested contents have been eliminated. For example, in the prototype of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” Zhu Geliang was once portrayed as being very reckless, and sometimes even like Zhang Fei. However, Zhu Geliang in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” was completely different; he became the embodiment of wisdom in the eyes of readers. One reason for this is the author's full expression of the mentality of the characters in the novel.
The reason why Kusabune in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” has become a very famous literary classic is because it fully expresses people's hearts. At the time, Sun and Liu families joined forces to resist Cao Cao going south. Zhou Yu and Zhu Geliang were on the same front. Against this backdrop, the greater Zhu Geliang's ability, the more beneficial it actually was to Zhou Yu's success against Cao Cao. However, Zhou Yu described in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” has his unique mentality. Although Zhou Yu sees that Zhu Geliang is highly intelligent and beneficial to him, he cannot allow Zhu Geliang's talents to take precedence over his own, because he is the commander who fought against Cao. Therefore, every time Zhou Yu asks Zhu Ge Liang to study anti-Cao, ostensibly to defeat Cao Cao; in fact, it is to legally get rid of Zhuge Liang. Zhou Yu asked Zhu Geliang to discuss the matter. He said that in the current battle on the river, we lacked arrows, so Zhu Geliang only gave him 10 days to prepare 100,000 arrows. In fact, this is just harassing Zhu Geliang. In an age where productivity was extremely low, gathering enough craftsmen and preparing all the materials needed to make arrows in ten days was an impossible task. But Zhu Geliang answered that it doesn't take ten days; three days is enough. Zhou Yu was very happy. She said that there was no joke in the military, so Zhu Geliang issued a military warrant; if he did not complete the mission for three days, he would have to kill Ge Liang's head. Zhu Geliang signed a military warrant and left; Zhou Yu sent Lu Su to investigate the situation. Lu Su asked Zhu Geliang, can you make 100,000 arrows in three days? Zhu Geliang said that I am in danger and I need you to save me. Zhu Geliang asked Lu Su to gather 20 small boats and arrange them with green cloth and scarecrows. Lu Su then agreed to do the same. After getting ready to stop, it wasn't until the third night that Zhu Geliang took Lu Su aboard the boat. 20 small boats drove straight into Cao Jun's water village and marched into the army with drums under Cao Jun's eyes. This frightened Lu Su. But Zhu Geliang said don't be afraid. Even though there was heavy fog up until today, I'm sure Cao Cao was skeptical. Under the heavy fog, he wouldn't dare to send troops to fight; he could only shoot an arrow to repel us. As a result, Zhu Geliang made 20 small boats receive random arrows from Cao Jun. He waited until the sky was almost bright, the fog was about to dissipate, and the scarecrows on board were filled with more than 100,000 arrows. Only then did Zhu Geliang order the retreat. When he left, he made the sergeant shout “Xie Cheng's arrow.”
The entire story of Kusabune Borrowed Arrows was fictional by Luo Guanzhong. Judging from history, Zhu Geliang has never used a straw boat to borrow an arrow, but Sun Quan did something similar. He took a boat to observe Cao Jun's water village and was discovered by Cao Cao. Cao Cao ordered an arrow to drive his boat away. This incident has been circulating for hundreds of years. By the time of “Three Kingdoms Peace Tales,” it was adapted into Zhou Yu taking a boat to spy on Cao Cao's water village. Many of Cao Jun's arrows were shot at Zhou Yu's boat. If we estimate that an arrow has four or two weights, a few thousand arrows hit a boat, it weighs several hundred pounds. These arrows all hit one side of the boat, and the boat is crooked on the water. Zhou Yu (actually Sun Quan in history) ordered the boat to be dropped to the other side. When the other side of the boat was also filled with Cao Jun's bow and arrow, and the weight on both sides was almost balanced, Zhou Yu sailed off and shouted “Xie Chengxiang's arrow” when he left. “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is based on these two materials, and new elements have been added. The author first arranged for heavy fog to fall that day, and the weather conditions were calculated in advance by Zhu Geliang, just like the current weather forecast. Next, due to poor visibility under the fog, Cao Cao, who is skeptical, is afraid of being ambushed and is afraid to go out to fight. This kind of plot arrangement is scientifically unreasonable, but it is very vivid, and the art can be fictionalized, assumed, and imagined.
Moreover, it must be seen that Zhu Geliang's multiple intellect was forced out by an ally of Zhou Yu. His intellect was adventurous.
