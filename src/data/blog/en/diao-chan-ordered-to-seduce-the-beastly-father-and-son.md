 It was the responsibility of the older men in the imperial court, and Wang Yun, a disciple of the Eastern Han Dynasty, was neither afraid to commit assassination, nor was he able to carry out a military rebellion, so he had to bet on a woman's chastity youth and life, putting this major mission on a prostitute. Wang Yun raised Diao Chan from an early age just for the sake of this moment, leaving a woman to carry such a heavy burden as Jiangshan Saji on her weak shoulders. This day has finally arrived, and Diao Chan's tragic fate has actually been decided.
Wang Yun treated her as a gift, a chess piece, and a chip. Diao Chan was on the strings and had no choice but to shoot. Seducing Dong Zhuo first, then seducing Dong Zhuogan's son Lu Bu. Diao Chan became a complete accessory to men. However, when Diao Chan excellently completed her mission as a woman, the next situation was beyond her control. Dong Zhuo died and Lu Bu also became a grasshopper after the fall, so Diao Chan lost her role, and therefore had no value. She became a woman who drifted everywhere. How sad this is!
As a woman, Diao Chan has become the smartest she can do. Her ultimate failure is not a personal one, but an inevitable failure for a woman in a male society. The root cause of Diao Chan's tragedy was because the civil servants and military generals in the Three Kingdoms, including Eastern Han, never had a man who was really responsible for women.
