 Last year, I used “Poetry Theory” to write “A Brief Analysis of Shen Peng's Calligraphy with Du Fu's Poetry”. Mr. Shen Peng commented, “Jian Chunjun's interpretation of his humble work with Du Fu's poetry theory is unique and creative.” With this experience, I came up with the idea of appreciating Du Shi in reverse through “pictorial theory.”
“Two yellow lilies and green willows, and a group of egrets in the blue sky. The window contains a thousand autumn snow in Xiling, and the door is docked at the Dongwu Wanli Boat.” Because of the words “window contains”, this poem, which is familiar to everyone, has a strong sense of image, so I named it “Xiling Snow Map.”
one
There are mountains in this “frame”. Judging from conventional thinking, it is easy to conclude that it is definitely a winter mountain with snow. However, the words “green willow” in Du Shi's first sentence describe the atmosphere of early spring. Literally speaking, the first sentence seems to contradict the third sentence. Also, if you look at the outstanding painters of the Northern Song Song Dynasty, painting theorists Guo Xi, Guo Si's father and son, the description of “Winter Mountain” in “Lin Quan Gaozhi” is “Winter Mountain is bleak and sleepy,” which is far from the beauty of “the window contains a thousand autumn and snow in Xiling.”
Some people also say that this is an absolute writing technique. Going down the “beginning and transition” just means the transition from winter to spring, which is an inverse sentence. To those who held this view, Qing Dynasty Pu Qilong once refuted it in “Reading Du Xin Xing”: “He is talented and talented, consistent in his intention, and interpreting the family in four scenes is too simple.”
The poet used the words “window contains” to look to the outside world, and the “green willow” and “white snow” in front of him were reflected at the same time. Oh, but the snow in Xiling in early spring hasn't melted yet? Still not right. Lao Du's phrase “a thousand autumn snows” revealed that this snow is snow that never melts all year round. To be precise, Xiling Snow is both a winter mountain, a spring mountain, a summer mountain, and an autumn mountain, and this poem is indeed a scene of early spring.
Wang Wei's “The Secret of Shanshui” talks about the past: “Or a picture within easy reach. Write a landscape of a hundred thousand miles, east, west, north, south, north, and west. Currently, spring, summer, autumn, and winter, were born under the pen.” Lao Du captured the visual space and time space of Xiling's “A Thousand Autumn Snow” into a “picture frame” with a specific lens. Lao Du is a poet, more like a master Chinese landscape painter. “Xiling Snow Map”, with such exquisite images and ingenuity, seems to have been obtained carelessly.
Guo Xi and Guo Si also explained in “Lin Quan Is Elegant” that Chinese landscape paintings often have a “three distance” landscape: “high” makes people appreciate the majesty of the mountain, like being immersive; “profound” makes people feel that the mountain is heavy, repetitive, and unfathomable; and “calm” makes people have a broad field of vision and a sense of comfort. To achieve the “three distance” artistic effect, it is necessary to break the limitations of focusing on the perspective and use scattered perspectives such as looking up, looking down, and looking straight to depict the scene in the painting.
Outside of the early spring thatched cottage, the green willow and yellow lily singing, reflecting the tranquility of the surroundings, which is “peaceful.” The sky is high and the clouds are pale, and egrets fly freely. This is “high.” The main peak above is high, and the mountain is large, showing a deep sense of dignity; the boats anchored in the murmuring of the river below fluctuate in the wind and waves, and also show a strong sense of fluidity. Isn't this “profound”?
With the help of the “Three Yuans” panoramic composition, Lao Du painted us a picture of the landscape with real changes and clear layers. “Yellow Oriole,” “Green Willow,” and “Egret” are displayed in front of you; the “thousand miles of snow” scattered over the empty mountains filled the light fog. Coupled with the fact that the ship bound for Dongwu was ethereal in the mist of water vapor, the whole picture was dyed in an orderly manner, with clear colors and a dark mood, which was intoxicating after watching.
two
The bright color combination brings rich pictorial meaning and clear rhythm to the poem, and also depicts the poet's relaxed and relaxed state of mind. Lao Du's “Xiling Snow Map” uses “yellow oriole,” “green willow,” “white heron,” and “green sky,” and is decorated in a patchwork pattern; furthermore, it extends from point to line, towards an endless space, showing a distinct three-dimensional sense, rhythm, and patchwork, forming an elegant and bright landscape painting.
There are countless examples of using contrasting colors in poetry to increase the intensity of emotional colors. Examples include Zhou Bangyan's “Countless Greenery in the Smoke, Red Sunset on the Back of the Geese” (“Yulou Chun”), Yang Wanli's “Embracing the Lotus Leaves Infinitely Blue, Illuminating the Sun” (“Dawn Jingci Temple and Sending Lin Zifang”), Bai Juyi's “Red Flowers Win the Fire at Sunrise, and the River Is Green as Blue in Spring” (“Remembering Jiangnan · Jiangnan Is Good”). The colors used very frequently in Lao Du's poems, such as “Red in Peach Blossoms, Tender, Qinggui, Ye Xin” (“Honoring Li Duodu's Peacock in Early Spring”), “Snow Mountain Bijie Tianbai, Jincheng Dushihuang” (“Huaijin Shui Ju Zhi”, “Green Wind Folds Bamboo Shoots, Red Blossoms, and Fermented Plums” (“Traveling with Zheng Guangwen to He Jiangjun Mountain Forest”, 10 verses 5), etc. This “emotion of coloring” has the clarity and intuitiveness of the painting, and enhances the appeal of the poetic mood.
three
“Mountain love is my love, mountain nature is my nature” is the aesthetic view of Ming's Tang Zhiqi's landscape painting “Paintings, Weeks of Landscape”. It emphasizes that the artist's temperament fits perfectly with the natural personality and mood of the landscape, so that the writing is not rigid. This view applies not only to painters, but also to poets. Li Bai's “flowing straight down three thousand feet, probably nine days for the Milky Way to set” can be described as a fantasy; Su Yi's “looking at the side of the mountain as a peak, with different heights near and far” can be described as philosophical; and Lao Du's “Window Containing a Thousand Autumn and Autumn Snow in Xiling” depicts a beautiful wonderland.
In fact, behind the Italian poem “Xiling Snow Map” lies the main theme of Lao Du's poem: Wounded and Drifting, Hoping to Return. “Two Orioles” and “A Party of Egrets” both symbolize family and reunion, in stark contrast to one's own solitary wandering. The deep meaning can be seen from the image of “A Thousand Autumn Snow,” which suggests that the world is as before, and that wars continue. The emotion contained in the phrase “doorstep” is clear, and the meaning of “looking back” is highlighted. “Every time I turn to the North to see Beijing and China,” the boat that sails to the East anchored outside the gate is the boat that Du Fu always thought about returning home.
In the late Ming and early Qing dynasties, Wang Si-shi's “Du Wei” only saw Du Fu's happiness and comfort in Shudi: the thatched hall had many bamboo trees, and the area was very spacious, so the birds and herons flew, which was appropriate. The window reflected Xishan and the ancient snow, and they didn't get tired of it. This was the same fun as watching someone who was relaxed with a wobble. Mondomari Kure Chuan, the official poem “A Lifetime in the Heart of the River and the Sea, Bent Boats a Long Time Ago” is also there. Gong Ge tried Wu; if he is safe now, he can live; if he is in trouble, he can go; it's not bad to go; what is appropriate!

Chinese painting, starting with faience patterns a thousand years ago, has followed the path of “image” modeling, which has become one of the characteristics of national aesthetics. Zhang Yanyuan from the Tang Dynasty said in “Famous Paintings of the Past”, “If you have a similar shape, you lose your charm; if you have color, you lose your brushwork; isn't it a painting?” Shi Tao, a famous painter from the Qing dynasty, said, “Not similar.” However, Lu Xun has quite a few words about “freehand”: “Two points are eyes. I don't know if they are long or round; one painting is a bird; I don't know if it's an eagle or a swallow.” (“Remembering the Soviet Printmaking Exhibition”)
Du Fu's “Xiling Snow Map” poem, the phrase “Menpo Dongwu Mileboat” embodies the compositional mindset of “not similar”. Is Lao Du picturing a ship bound for Dongwu actually parked at his doorstep? In my opinion, this “close-up view” is probably writing a distant view, and it is farther away than “the window contains a thousand autumn and snow in Xiling.” Therefore, to understand the fourth sentence, the key is to clarify the meaning of the third sentence. “Xiling Snow Map” is a picture in a “frame,” which is the interesting part of the third sentence.
Su Shi tasted the story: “Poetry is inexhaustible; it overflows into books and becomes paintings; everything is beyond poetry.” This is just a general understanding of Chinese art. “Poetry is an intangible painting, painting is a tangible poem... the realm is familiar, the mind is ready, and the beginning is right and center, right and left meet the source.”
I often fantasize: would Du Fu paint a picture based on the mood of the poem and title the poem on the picture? Was it because of Du Fu Shi's greater influence that his paintings were instead overwhelmed?
