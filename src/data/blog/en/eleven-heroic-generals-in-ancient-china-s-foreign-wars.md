 1. Li Mu
Perhaps because “The Chronicles of History” were written by people from the Han dynasty, the records of many generals during the Spring and Autumn Warring States period seem to have been diluted. In fact, during the Spring and Autumn Warring States period, Li Mu was a figure that must not be overlooked. During the Warring States period, he was Zhao Guo's most successful general in fighting against Qin, and Lian Po and Zhao were not as good as him. But today I'm talking about foreign wars. There is no need to go into detail about the war against Qin.
Li Mu's great exploits were in terms of his record against the Huns. Previously, Zhao Guo had always been threatened by the Huns. Zhao Jun was repeatedly defeated by the Huns, and even when Zhao Wuling rode and shooting in a disguised manner, he was brave. Zhao Jun learned a great deal from the Huns in terms of equipment and combat methods, and established a cavalry force that shocked the Six Nations. However, after all, riding and shooting was not Han's specialty. As the cavalry of Zhao Guo rode across the Central Plains, the war against the Huns was still a series of battles and losses. After all, using cavalry against the Huns was certainly an advantage for the Han people, who were still relatively naive in cavalry tactics at the time, to use their own short strike. This situation did not change until Lee Mu-seok defended the Great Wall. Originally, Li Mu was assigned to Northern Xinjiang, as a result of Zhao Wang's counterfeiting plan against the Qin State. But thanks to the Qin State's counterplan, because of Li Mu's arrival, China has had its first successful war against the nomadic people.
Li Mu had only one war with the Huns in the true sense of the word, but this one time alone, the Huns were afraid to go south for almost ten years. In this battle, Li Mu brought the wisdom of Sun Tzu's art of war to the limit. First, he lost five straight games, abandoned the heavy weight of the sheep and cows, lured the main forces of the Huns from the prairie where they were good at to the periphery of the Great Wall, where the Han people were good at, to carry out a decisive battle, and paralyzed his opponents by showing weakness. Then they attacked quickly, gathered people and horses late at night, attacked the Huns camp at night, and killed the Huns knights in their sleep. At the same time, heavy chariots surrounded the exit of the Huns camp and shot the fugitives from the Huns with arrows as dense as locusts. The main forces of the Huns were almost completely destroyed in this battle, and it was difficult to recover their energy for a few years. More importantly, this battle is a classic example of the Han army's infantry riders fighting collaboratively, providing a model for the Han regime to learn from in future battles with the Huns.

5. Li Jing

6. Pei Xingjian
Perhaps because Wu Zetian existed, or perhaps because of the need to glorify Tang Taizong, posterity often downplayed this period of history during Tang Gaozong's reign. Pei Xingjian came from a shih family, and the Pei family has been a famous family since the two Jin dynasties. From Jin to Tang, there were 237 people in the Pei family who held the position of generals. It can be called a military family. Born into such a family and bearing the family's honor, General Pei did not dishonor his family and left a strong mark on Chinese history with his great exploits.
What is dramatic is that Pei Xingjian's great exploits came from a wrongful case. In the Gaozong era, he was framed by the Wu clan for opposing the establishment of Wu Zetian as empress. However, Tang Gaozong was talented, so he was demoted to the position of the governor of Xizhou (present-day Turpan, Xinjiang). It was this verdict that changed the fate of General Pei's life, and made him grow from an idle Beijing official to a warlord dominating China and abroad.
The first major battle that General Pei independently commanded was the Battle against Western Turks. Although the Tang dynasty had many disputes before, Tuban also extended its reach to the western region at this time. The Western Turks began to switch to Tufan and fought against the Tang dynasty. At this time, Persia, a country belonging to the Tang dynasty, was destroyed by gluttony, further worsening the Tang dynasty's diplomacy. Pei Xingjian, on the other hand, seized this opportunity and pretended to escort the Prince of Persia back home, but he killed a revolt in the middle of the road, led a heavy attack on Western Turks, and annihilated the West Turkic rebels in one fell swoop. and capture their Khans. His exploits in this battle are comparable to Li Jing's attack on Yinshan at night. Since then, the Western Turks have been forced to move west to Central Asia and have basically abandoned their attempts to compete with the Tang dynasty for the western region.
Since then, General Pei invested his whole life in fighting against the Turks, suppressing the East Turkic rebels and attacking the remaining Turkic forces. Wherever he went, all the Turks settled down. Strong consolidation defended the borders of Datang. General Pei himself died of illness while marching to conquer Western Turks at the age of 64.
