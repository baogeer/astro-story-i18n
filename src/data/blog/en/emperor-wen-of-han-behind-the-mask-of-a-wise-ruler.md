 Recently, the location of Emperor Han Wen's tomb was determined, which attracted widespread attention. Emperor Han Wenwen Liu Heng was an example of an ancient monarch. Later generations mentioned Shengshi and Ming Monarch, which must be called Wenjing. It seems that Emperor Han Wenwen was born a perfect politician with a sense of holiness, kindness, and self-discipline. However, looking at historical books, the image of Emperor Wen was not as perfect and beautiful as future generations said; he was also worried, afraid, unconfident, and even had unrestrained selfishness. It's just that he has been wearing a thick mask since the beginning of his inauguration to hide his true self. So what did the real Liu Heng look like after the mask?
A throne falls to the throne: frightening the young emperor
After the death of Emperor Han Xiao Hui, Liu Ying, was suddenly crowned as the new monarch by Zhou Bo and Chen Ping, since her son was considered an impersonation and was Liu Heng, the biggest king of Liu Bang's sons.
Liu Heng was banished when he was seven years old. He has always quietly acted as the king in Taiyuan. He has no interest in Chang'an. This kind of political modesty and laid-back desire has a lot to do with their mother's weak clan. Back then, Bo entered the Han Palace as Wei Bao's late wife. Accidentally, Liu Bang gave birth to Liu Heng, and was later snubbed. The year Liu Heng was isolated, the lonely Bo clan followed her son to Taiyuan. Bo didn't have any major goals in his life; he just wanted to stay safe and secure with his son as king in Taiyuan.
After the death of Emperor Han Gao, Mrs. Qi was hated by Lu Hou for being loved back then, and was severely punished by others. Her son, Liu Ruyi, of Zhao Wang, was also killed. Bo's humble attitude of retreat enabled the mother and son to escape the cruel court disasters of the post-Lu era. Liu Hengqian's retreating political attitude was not unrelated to this experience.
So when a Beijing messenger came to ask him to be emperor in 180 BC, his first reaction was not excitement, but fear and doubt. After all, the Zulu Rebellion has just been brought to an end. After the bloody battle, what did Chang'an really look like? What kind of feelings did old ministers like Zhou Bo and Chen Ping really feel at ease? What is the attitude of Liu's children and grandchildren? He knows nothing and is afraid to rashly accept the throne. At this point, Liu Heng was still an ordinary person. Everything he thought, said, and did was instinctive. However, it was also at this point that the packaging of Liu Heng's image began. Liu Heng changed consciously or unconsciously, getting closer to the vague image of a saint in later history books.
When Liu Heng set out from Taiyuan and arrived at the outskirts of Chang'an, a minor event quite a turning point happened. Zhou Bo and Chen Pingping arrived at Weiqiao to greet Xinjun. Taiwei Zhou Bo suddenly drove over to Liu Heng for a big gift and asked for a loan to speak. Zhou Bo's intention was simple and crude; he wanted to give the emperor's seal to Liu Heng before all the heroes, and seek the highest political qualification to support the hero. Liu Heng was only 23 years old at the time, an age where he was young, crazy, and easily carried away. If an ordinary person at this age encounters this kind of thing, they will probably personally get out of the car and pick up Taiwei Zhou, warmly greet him, thank him, and ask him.
But he's no longer an ordinary person. Lieutenant Lieutenant Song Chang immediately stopped him and said, “Do what you say, say it publicly. If I say I am, the king is not selfish.”
The classic TV drama “Yongzheng Dynasty”, which premiered in 1999, made use of this allusion. After Kangxi fell, the Seventeenth Agor asked to see the newly ascended Emperor Yongzheng. Wu Sidao stopped him and said, “If it's an official matter, ask Zhang Tingyu to deal with it; if it's a customs guard, go to Grandpa XIII. If it's a private matter, Tianzi has no private matters.” The TV drama adaptation shows that this reaction is really classic, and it pinpoints the most accurate point when the status of a feudal lord changes to a monarch.
Song Chang, not Liu Heng, gave an accurate response, but in the context of the history book, Song Chang, as Liu Heng's son, was naturally an extension of Liu Heng's political will. Song Chang was quick to respond, and Liu Heng learned quickly. Zhou Bo knelt down in public to present the Tianzi Seal, and Liu Heng said in a learned way, “Let's discuss it at the residence.” The heroes have already set the tone; do we need to talk about it again? However, in full view of the public, Liu Heng did not enter the Imperial Palace but wanted to “discuss” the old residence of the first generation, and all the heroes had to face each other.
From being overwhelmed to playing tai chi, Liu Heng officially became an emperor from this moment on.
2. Picking up Heroes: Asking Ghosts Is Just Because of Inner Worries
“Historical Chronicles: The Family of Chen Chengxiang” records a match between Emperor Han Wen and a minister. Emperor Han Wen asked Prime Minister Zhou Bo: “How about one year old in the world being jailed?” Zhou Bo replied, “I don't know.” He also asked, “How about getting in and out of the world's one-year-old money valley?” Zhou Bo didn't know; he was ashamed and sweaty.
Emperor Wen also asked Zuo's Prime Minister Chen Ping the same question. Chen Ping dealt with it very smartly. Jiei Ting's duty was to execute the prison, and Qian Gu was responsible for managing the internal history of the Sun. Let's ask them about these trivial matters. The prime minister assists Tenshi in managing important matters; just take good care of the ministers. Emperor Wen continued to praise goodness. Since then, Zhou Bo has resigned as Prime Minister Right, and Emperor Han Wen approved.
This incident happened not long after Emperor Han Wen took office. On the surface, it seemed that the monarchs were shaking their wits. The boss and Zhou Bo couldn't fight the emperor and Chen Ping, leaving them in a state of panic. But there are lots of articles behind it. Regardless of the specific affairs of the prime minister, anyone with a little bit of common sense in politics can understand this. Emperor Wen used this to confuse Zhou Bo; there is a deeper purpose behind it. Shortly after this incident, Emperor Wen made the excuse that there were too many rulers in Chang'an City, causing supply pressure on the Beijing division to increase, implying that Jiang Hou Zhou Bo took the lead in leaving Beijing and living in his feudal land. Leaving Beijing meant staying away from the supreme power. Zhou Bo was unwilling but unable to resist.
After Zhou Bo returned to Jiangxian County, he was attacked once again. Emperor Wen convicted him of the crime and put off the hat of conspiring against him. Zhou Bo spent a lot of money to get rid of his uncle Bo Zhao, and pleaded in front of Empress Bo before being released from prison. Back then, General Zhou, who killed an army of one million, could not help but lament the value of prison guards only today.
Is Zhou Bo really rebellious? Definitely not. Empress Dowager Bo once questioned Emperor Wen of Han. If he wanted to commit a rebellion, he did so long ago when he first became a lieutenant, so why did he have to wait until now. Later historians interpreted Jiang Hou's prison mostly due to Zhou Bo being the head of the Han Dynasty Military Merit Group. Emperor Han Wen's attack on him only eliminated the influence of the hero group.
This statement is certainly reasonable. However, there are plenty of reasons to attack a hero, and it's not as mean as a big boss. Emperor Wen was so ruthless, probably because Zhou Bo once killed several sons of Emperor Han Hui Liu Ying. There are many doubts in the historical community about the identities of Liu Ying's sons; we don't need to talk about the truth or falsehood. Why did Emperor Han Wen react to this incident? Whether the son of the second brother Liu Ying is real or false, Zhou Bo, as a foreign minister, actually has the power to determine the lineage and status of the royal family. This is absolutely intolerable.
If one day, the Hero Group is also dissatisfied with Liu Heng, will they also concoct, abolish, and kill him according to law? Whether they are politicians or ordinary people, their fears and concerns about an unknown fate are often the same. Emperor Han Wen categorically ordered the arrest of innocent Zhou Bo, probably out of excessive fear.
When we lift the curtain of history, we often see the most authentic human nature. Li Shangyin's “Jia Sheng” Shi Yun: “Poor sit in the middle of the night and don't ask Sang Sheng to ask the devil.” Li Yishan reversed his past admiration for Emperor Han and sarcastically satirized Emperor Wen of asking for comfort from ghosts. Actually, why isn't this a reaction of Emperor Wen's inner fears and worries?
Emperor Wen also acted similarly towards his brothers in the royal family. Emperor Wen forced his younger brother, King Liu Chang of Huainan, to death, and condoned his son Liu Qi to kill Prince Wu Wang Liu Yi. Folklore says, “One foot of cloth can still be sewn; a bucket of rice is still safe; the two brothers are not compatible.” “Historical Chronicles: The Legend of Huainan Hengshan” records the reaction of Emperor Han and Wen. He gave a rare explanation of folklore, Yao Shun banished the flesh and blood, and Zhou Gong killed Guan Cai. The scriptures seem to be well-founded, but no matter how you discern them, they cannot dispel the fact that flesh and blood go hand in hand.
Although Emperor Han Wen had a politically correct mask, that mask was just a shell, and no one took care of his inner unease. In the middle of the night, Emperor Han Wen, who was lonely and gloomy, had to seek help from ghosts.
3. Thrifty that has been exaggerated by publicity
Also misguided by historical propaganda is Emperor Han Wen's frugal style.
Bangu warmly praised Emperor Han Wen in the legend “Han Shu: The Benevolent Chronicles of Emperor Wen,” saying that in his 23 years as emperor, “there was nothing to gain from the palace, court, riders, and service.” “The exclusive duty is to transform the people with morality, and to be rich at home.”
Indeed, Emperor Han and Wen were rich in a benevolent style of government, opening up a liberal political environment in the Wenjing Era. In particular, the “30 taxes and 1” policy he ordered was implemented, which exempted land rent for 13 consecutive years, bringing unprecedented tax relief to ordinary people. This was an unquestionable achievement of his great achievement.
Historians like to go to extremes. Many people say that Emperor Han Wen often described him as perfect. Unfortunately, this is not the case.
As recorded in the “Han Book”, there was a time when Emperor Han Wen wanted to build a terrace and asked the craftsmen who built it to evaluate how much it would cost. The craftsman said he needed 100 gold. Emperor Han Wen did the math. The average family property of ten families was worth 100 gold. It was too wasteful to build a terrace, so they stopped altogether.
This incident certainly reflects Emperor Han Wen's love for his people and his unwillingness to waste it, but can it be determined from this that he was a highly self-disciplined person who was always frugal and never wasteful?
“The Chronicles of History: The Legend of Nepheung” records another incident. Emperor Han and Wen blessed Deng Tong and often rewarded Deng Tong tens of thousands of gold, and more than a dozen times before and after.
With the money given to Deng Tong, it was possible to build a thousand terraces. Comparing the two things together, Han Wen's previous frugality simply had a sense of being playful.
If Deng Tong were a meritorious minister to become a minister, it would be understandable that it would have been too much work. However, this lucky minister did not give the country half credit to the people. He was able to go from being a poor boatman in Shu County to becoming the emperor's favorite minister. It was only because Emperor Han and Wen had a dream where someone pushed him when he ascended the sky. That person's attire was similar to Deng Tong; Deng Tong achieved such a dramatic transcendence of classes.
Emperor Han Wen sought someone to meet Deng Tong. The other party said that in the end, Deng Tong would die poor and run out of money. Emperor Han Wen laughed. This wasn't easy to do. He immediately ordered Deng Tong to be given a copper mine to mine copper mines to mint copper coins on his own.
The use of national instruments to give individuals freedom of wealth is unacceptable in any era. But Emperor Han Wen was so capricious. The deep motive behind Deng Tong, a villain who favors virtue and ignorance, is Emperor Han Wen's deep unease about the legitimacy of the throne.
When he first took the throne, Emperor Gao's heir and grandson have died. The remedy for the heir to the heir to the throne was to last his brother and brother. Well, Chu Wang Liu Jiao (the younger brother of Emperor Liu Bang) and Qi Wang Liu Xiang (son of Liu Fei, the eldest son of Liu Bang) are all likely to take the throne. Emperor Han Wen refused to enter the Imperial Palace directly into Chang'an, and invited Chu Wang and others to discuss it. This is just cowardly people who don't have strong enough qualifications.
Many years later, he favored Deng Tong and promoted him to a position that could not be added. To put it bluntly, he still added a little collateral capital to the throne. In my dream, there was someone in heaven to help me; in reality, there was such a person. Then I became an emperor; it wasn't God's will!
Successive monarchs have faced a choice between personal desires and political morality. Compared to some monarchs who abandoned the bottom line, Emperor Han Wen had better control, basically maintained a good side, and became a Ming monarch in the eyes of future generations.
Under the cover of a political mask, the multifaceted nature of Emperor Han and Wen still persists. Now it seems that this is the true appeal of historical figures. (Chen Fengtao)
