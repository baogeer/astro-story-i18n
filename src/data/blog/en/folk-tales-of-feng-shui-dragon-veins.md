 For the people of the Tang dynasty, it was bound to be an eventful time: the Anshi Rebellion had not yet settled down, and Huiyu killed another strong man, Pei Luo, to first calm the western region, then break the Turks, and then dominate the prairie and desert, staring closely at the fat meat of the Central Plains. Tang Suzong was in power at the time, and he was busy enough just to clean up the mess. He was simply unable to cope with the threat from the frontier. After a few days, he decided to send his daughter out to be a wife for Gong Li Pei Luo. This also conforms to the general rule of the Han and Tang Dynasties: if men don't work, they change women; if they can't beat others, they get along with their relatives; and exchange peace by being an old father-in-law or uncle.
Before sending her daughter out, the Tang Su sect sent a group of people to Huizhou on a business trip. In fact, they were doing an inspection and trying to figure out the details of Pei Luo. There was a strange person in the research team. Looking east and west along the way, he saw a small mountain bag and climbed up. He measured the sun's shadows with a tuguey, used a compass to set the direction, and drew some inexplicable symbols on paper. The team leader praised him... saying military surveys are important. Well, that person can say, what kind of military survey, I'm looking for dragon veins. Turns out he's a Feng Shui sir.
Mr. Feng Shui returned to Chang'an and reported to Tang Suzong that after careful investigation, he finally discovered where the dragon's veins were located, just south of the desert, a small hill called Fukuyama. As long as this small hill was destroyed, the royal spirit of reverberation would dissipate, and Pei's strength would be over. Tang Suzong was overjoyed and ordered that the person sending his family must find Fukuyama and move it back. Afterwards, the family delivery team found Fukuyama, burned it, filled it with vinegar, broke it down into pieces, and loaded it back. Then, of course, Pelo died, and the return reached a low point, and the frontier threat was lifted.
Whether this story is true or not, it can be written into a Feng Shui book... as an example. Because it can prove that Feng Shui is amazing, very useful, and has very historical roots.
There are also two other cases, which are very similar to the one where Tang Suzong digs a dragon. In the late 11th century, the emperor of Liao feared that real women threatened national security, and asked Mr. Feng Shui to go to the Nu Zhen Tribe to find dragons, find a small mountain, burn it with vinegar, dig it up, and pile it up on Mennei Street in Xi'an, Beijing. It is now called Miner Mountain. In the early 13th century, Emperor Jin Guo feared that Mongolians threatened national security, and asked Mr. Feng Shui to go to the Mongolian desert to find dragon veins. He also found a small mountain, burned it with vinegar, dug it up, and piled it up in Beijing's Jingshan Park. Now it's called Wansui Mountain. Unfortunately, there are no big spirits: the emperor of Liao dug up the dragon veins of real women, and the real woman destroyed Daliao; Emperor Jin dug up the Mongolian dragon veins, and the Mongols still destroyed Daikin. By writing these two things into the book, the proof is not that Feng Shui is amazing and very useful, but that it is terrible and very intrusive. However, friends who are obsessed with Feng Shui will say, “They didn't dig the right dragon veins. If they sent me back then, I would definitely be able to find the real dragon veins, so it doesn't mean that Feng Shui science is useless; it just means they aren't good at learning.
This kind of story about the four ancestors of Huangmei also played out once. There is no way to test the story in which era. Legend has it that Huangmei Sizuo Temple has been growing all the time; if Huangmei grows to Taibai Lake, Huangmei will grow all the time, and if it grows to Taibai Lake, Huangmei will rise to heaven. It just so happened that the emperor was dizzy at the Jinluan Temple and ordered Qin Tianjian to watch the sky. After Qin Tianjian watched the sky, he reported that there was a treasure in Hubei. Tenshi was about to appear after it was formed, so His Majesty went to the palace and was dizzy. As for the exact location, it was really impossible to see clearly, so the emperor sent people to search everywhere in Hubei. When they found the area near Huangmei's Sizu Temple, they discovered that this place actually had the spirit of an emperor, so they quickly sent troops to break up the mountain range. This mountain is now Longjiao Mountain. After being tested, they unanimously decided to cut off Longjiao Mountain to destroy Huangmei's Feng Shui as an emperor. When the soldiers dug up Longjiao Mountain during the day, the excavation site automatically closed up again at night. Then the ghost shouted at a high altitude, “You have a thousand people digging during the day, I have 10,000 people to fill it at night, I'm only afraid of putting paulownia stakes on all sides”, so the sergeant leader cut down the Tongzi tree and cut it into stumps. During the digging, the ghost saw the stump and was afraid to come close. Thus, the ghost cut off Longjiao Mountain and destroyed the Feng Shui of Huangzi. To this day, the excavation site still exists, and the local people have always told this story.
