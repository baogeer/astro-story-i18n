 There is a Turkish official from the Hani tribe who has always had a grudge against Atebo and wanted to force his family to fall to death. So he said to Atebo, “Atebo, you're really smart, go do business. I'll lend you the principal amount, as much as you want to borrow.”
Atebo asked, “How can I pay back in the future?”
The Turkish official said, “When the donkey rolls over the mule, 100 is 400.” Atebo then borrowed 20 pieces of silver.
A year later, Atebo borrowed a donkey and a mule, took him to the Turkish official's house, and said, “I got my money back.”
The civil service official said, “Wow, bring it!”
Atebo said, “Slow! Let's first take a look at how the donkey rolls over and how the mule rolls over.” Speaking of which, let go of the reins of donkeys and mules. The donkey and mule just stand, don't roll over, don't roll over. Half an hour later, Atebo asked the civil service if he had seen it clearly.
A civil servant stared: “You.. you..”
Atebola pulled the donkey and mule and said, “Look, the donkey didn't roll the mule and didn't roll over!” They said they would give back 20 or two pieces of money to the civil service.
A few days passed, and the Turkish officials settled that Atebo had returned only 10 yuan in money, and he still owes 10 yuan. Atebo is limited to paying off early the next morning; otherwise, he will have to go to Atebo's home to get something to pay off the debt.
Atebo said, “Then come get your stuff early tomorrow morning. As the old saying goes, three are in the crowd, and three things are many. When you come to pick up something, you can only bring three people, you can only take three things, and whichever three you mean, take any three.”
The civil service officials heard that the Atebo family had two large red cabinets and a bright bronze mirror, so they agreed.
The next day, Atebo placed a bunch of banana peels and a few bananas at the foot of the stairs. He tied a bad dog to the door, then closed the door, plugged in wooden pins, and invited a few notaries.
At this point, the Turkish official walked to the door with his servant. The evil dog ran past. The civil servant pointed at the dog in horror and shouted, “Pull me away!”
Atebo then handed the leash to the first servant.
The civil service officer was about to walk through the door. When he saw a wooden pin on the door, he pointed at the wooden pin and shouted, “Take it for me!”
Atebo then handed the wooden pin to the second servant.
Opening the door, the civil service officer saw banana peels at the foot of the stairs. Worried that he would slip and fall, pointing at the banana peel and shouted again: “Take it for me!”
Atebo then handed the banana to the third servant, swept the banana peel aside and said, “OK. Please go!”
The civil servant was dumbfounded: “Ah, what?”
Atebo said, “Look, aren't dogs, wooden pins, and bananas the things you asked to take away?”
