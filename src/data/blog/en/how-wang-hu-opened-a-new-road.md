 Once upon a time, when people from Yankeng got down to Dongcun Ting, they had to walk a Panshan path and pass through the entrance of Longhua Temple. If you follow the trend and walk smoothly, it will take several miles, but the monk at Longhua Temple won't let you go. He said that if he cleared the mountain and paved the way, moved the dragon's veins and broke Feng Shui, the temple would fall. There were many arguments, and at one point, hundreds of monks with martial arts beat people to the bone.
After Wang Hu became sensible, he knew the wishes of his elders and wanted to benefit the next generation, so he came up with a clever plan. In this way, he spoke in detail to the family elders in the four villages of Yankeng. The brigade chiefs listened, praised each one, acted separately, and waited for orders.
On June 30 of the lunar calendar, it was a dark night of the moon, and there were scattered drizzle in the sky. Ding Zhuang, 18 to 40 years old, from four villages took the lead with hoes, iron bars, sticks, and wood knives; more than 200 women prepared their families to lift stones and pick dirt; three and 50 old and young people swam back and forth with pine firewood torches; plus more than 200 scarecrows made in advance. Each hermit tied a lantern and lined up one line in an orderly manner. With a single call, everyone shouted, “Cairo! Splitting mountains...” The sound of “climbing, climbing” cutting down trees, and “ding, ding” shook the earth. The whole mountain was about to explode.
The monk at Longhua Temple heard it. When he came out to take a look, he only saw that the mountain was full of lights, and there were a thousand people, to say the least. The head monk was terrified. He only sent a dozen monks outside to watch the limelight; the rest sat down at the main hall and read: “There is no Amitabha Buddha in the south.”
Overnight until dawn, a new flat road was opened. When rice was cooked with raw rice, the monk of Ryuka-ji Temple had no choice but to go along with the water and couldn't do it.
