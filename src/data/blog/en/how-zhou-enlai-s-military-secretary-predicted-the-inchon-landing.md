 Author: Cho Young-soo
Lei Yingfu was born in 1921 in Mengjin County, Henan Province. He enlisted in the military in 1938, became a member of the Eighth Route Army, and joined the Chinese Communist Party in the same year. During the war of resistance, he served as Deputy Director of the Data Research Office of the General Staff and Operations Department of the Military Commission and military secretary to Chief of Staff Ye Jianying. During the Liberation War, he served as acting head of the executive department of the Beiping Army Investigation Department and chief of staff of the Third Field Army Division. After the founding of the People's Republic of China, he served as Premier Zhou Enlai's military secretary and served in the General Staff of the Chinese People's Liberation Army. Promoted to the rank of Major General in 1961. Lei Yingfu is known by Mao Zedong as the “Talented Man of Luoyang,” yet little is known about his legendary experiences.
“I was born to be useful”
In August 1938, Lei Yingfu, who was only 18 years old, traveled a long distance to Yan'an, which he had long wanted, and was enrolled in the 6th Anti-War Brigade to study. On the first day, Mao Zedong came to the school to meet the students of the Anti-Chinese University. Lei Yingfu excitedly handed over a notebook and asked Chairman Mao to give him a motto. Mao Zedong immediately gladly wrote “Struggle” on it with two energetic characters. As a result, Lei Yingfu was very encouraged.
In November, in accordance with work needs, the Central Military Commission decided to set up a staff training team at the General Academy of Resistance Against Japanese Armed Forces. The cadets were selected by each squadron and composed of outstanding personnel.
In the spring of 1939, the anti-resistance training team officially started school. Mao Zedong, Chen Yun and others served as teachers. The first lesson Mao Zedong taught in a clear sense was “The Strategic Issues of the Chinese Revolution.” Mao Zedong spoke in depth and depth. Takaya Kenshou, and the students were attentive, listening and memorizing. During the break between classes, Mao Zedong had to randomly check the notebooks of several students, while the focus fell on Lei Yingfu. After reading Lei Yingfu's class record book, Mao Zedong asked Teng Daiyuan close to him about Lei Yingfu's situation. Teng Daiyuan told the truth, “He came to Yan'an and forced him to go to Liangshan!”
One sentence aroused Mao Zedong's interest, and then asked, “Why say it?” As a result, Teng Daiyuan introduced Lei Yingfu's life to Mao Zedong. Lei Yingfu originally attended middle school in Luoyang. Due to her poor family situation, she was unable to collect her tuition fees. The difference was only 16 yuan. The school paid within the time limit; otherwise, she was ordered to drop out of school. “I was so helpless that I had to drop out of school and go to the railway wrecker exam. I was shut down because I couldn't pay my deposit. Later, they wanted to take the elementary school teacher exam, but they were still denied because they didn't have a deposit. After that, they went to various places, applying for the exam, becoming an apprenticeship at a cloth shop, etc., all failed because they had no money and no insured person. Lei Yingfu had no choice but to shout at the sky and repeated Li Bai's bitter “Born to be useful” and arrived in Yan'an.
During the Juhuang Festival in 1939, when Zhou Eng returned from Chongqing to report on his work, he mentioned the current heavy work of the Southern Bureau. “Ye Jianying urgently needs two staff officers as assistants.” Mao Zedong confidently said, “OK! I recommend you a good resource: Rei Yingfu.”
The writing made waves of misfortune and fortune
In January 1941, after the “Southern Anhui Incident,” Ye Jianying was transferred back to Yan'an as Chief of Staff of the Central Military Commission, accompanied by Lei Yingfu. In Yan'an, Lei Yingfu is the director of the data room of the Eighth Route Army Headquarters Operations Department, and is also the editor of the “Jiefang Daily” military supplement.
In late June 1942, one year after the outbreak of the Soviet-German War, Lei Yingfu wrote an article entitled “One Year of the Soviet-German War”. The article is about the war situation between the Soviet Union and Germany over the past year. Comprehensive research has been carried out, which is reasonable and evidence-based. “Jiefang Daily” was published in a prominent position as a key article, and it was serialized for 2 days.
On one day, Ye Jianying went to Mao Zedong's office and saw a copy of “Liberation Daily” on Mao Zedong's desk, with the article “One Year of the Soviet-German War” published on it. Looking closely, I only saw many circles and marks on it. When Mao Zedong mentioned this article to Ye Jianying, Ye Jianying hurriedly said, “It was written by Xiao Lei.” Mao Zedong praised: “This doll is amazing!” Mao Zedong then proposed walking to Wang Jiaping to meet Lei Yingfu.
After the meeting, Mao Zedong held Lei Yingfu's hand and said, “I read “One Year of the Soviet-German War” you wrote. It is very good. It accurately reflects the situation of the war and the views of our party. The text is clear. Very persuasive, I read it all in one go.”
Lei Yingfu humbly said, “It's a task given to me by social media; it's a barely crossing over...”
Mao Zedong went on to praise, “Listen to Ye Shen's speech. He wrote his speeches against the crowd, and the Southern Administration's leaflet revealing the truth about the incident in southern Anhui. It was also written by you. It really is worthy of being a 'Luoyang Talent'!”
On August 18, 1943, an article entitled “Military Education Must Start from Reality” and signed “Zhu De” was published in the “Military Supplement” by Yan'an's “Jiefang Daily” with a large title. This article was written by Lei Yingfu, with reference to Zhu De's thoughts and opinions.
The article emphasizes that military education must start from reality, and that strong combat morale must be combined with good combat skills in order to stand invincible. After the article was published, it aroused strong reactions among senior cadres. They all praised the article for its clear banner, thorough analysis, and grasped the key points of current military education. Lin Biao was the only one who came out and sang the opposite, accusing the article of “simply military views.” “It violates the principles of our military's political establishment of the Party.” One day, Lin Biao summoned Lei Yingfu from Wang Jiaping's own residence. He said loudly, “You are the writer of the article. You should do a good job of checking, and find problems from a worldview!” Because of this, Lei Yingfu was deeply distressed and worried, and he found Mr. Zhu. Zhu De said, “Don't be afraid; history has its own public opinion.” Mao Zedong became aware of this incident. He seriously criticized Lin Biao on inappropriate occasions and arbitrarily criticized the main military leaders. The approach was wrong, and pointed out that his understanding of “building an army by politics” was one-sided. If this is the case, the “public case” initiated by Lei Yingfu will have to be put to rest.
Little staff officers have a lot to do
The Korean War broke out in June 1950, and it took only two months for the Korean People's Army to rush the US and Lee fake troops to the Nakdong River area at the southeast corner of the Korean peninsula. At this point, the Soviet Union and North Korean leaders seemed to be overly optimistic about the situation, believing that total victory was imminent.
At this time, Lei Ying-fu, who is working in the General Staff's combat room, is closely watching the Korean war situation day and night. Through analysis, Lei Ying-fu believes that the US military will not be willing to fail. They may have dispatched two strategic reserves stationed in Japan to carry out a surprise attack and land at Incheon Port, where North Korea is beating its back, in an attempt to cut off the Korean People's Army's path.
On August 23, Lei Yingfu told Zhou Enlai about this idea and expressed his views from six aspects. Zhou Enlai immediately made Lei Yingfu's predictions and judgments, made a phone call, and reported to Mao Zedong. On the phone, Mao Zedong said that the Prime Minister was invited to take Lei Yingfu on a trip immediately.
In fact, Mao Zedong, as a strategist, had long anticipated actions against the US military; he predicted that the war would change unexpectedly. Planning ahead, on August 5, he ordered the Northeast Border Service to complete preparations for war in August.
When Zhou Enlai led Lei Yingfu to the Juxiang Bookstore, Mao Zedong was carefully studying the latest developments about the US military based on a large pile of telegrams. Rei Young-fu reported his six opinions on the Korean war situation. While listening and nodding, Mao Zedong sometimes used a pencil to write a few strokes on the manuscript in front of him. Lei Yingfu worked hard to complete the report. Mao Zedong did not make too many comments; he only seriously uttered the six words “reasonable, very important.”
When Mao Zedong set his sights on Lei Yingfu for consultation, Lei Yingfu said that he also predicted the US military's landing time in the combat room, and September 15 is the most likely. Mao Zedong was very interested in the “landing time” and immediately asked “can we be more specific”?
Lei Yingfu methodically said, “We studied the sea-tide from September to November on the west coast of North Korea and found that there are three best dates to choose from: September 15, October 11, and November 3. Around the three best dates, there are 2 to 3 good times for each. The Incheon coast can only be used for docking for 3 hours every 12 hours. Assuming that the landing time is September 15, there will be two high tide times this day, once at 6:59 a.m. and the other time at 19:19, 35 minutes after sunset in the afternoon. September 15 is relatively more likely than the other two times, so it is highly likely that this day will be chosen for the US military to land.”
As expected, the US military landed in Incheon at 5 a.m. on September 15, 1950. The US military, under the command of 70-year-old five-star Admiral MacArthur, first occupied Wolmido, known as the Incheon Barrier, and then took advantage of the high tide in the afternoon of the same day to occupy a breakwater and land in the southern part of Incheon. By the afternoon of September 16, the whole of Incheon was controlled.
Afterwards, Mao Zedong said, “Don't think anything about how America is. Our junior staff can predict the time and place of MacArthur's landing, and it's that accurate. This can be said to be rare in military history. Our little staff can do a great job.” (This article was originally published in “World of Literature and History”, No. 11, 2009, by Zhao Yingxiu.
