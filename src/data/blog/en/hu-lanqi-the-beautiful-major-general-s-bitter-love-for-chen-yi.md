 Core summary: China once had a female general like this. Although she was married once, she met Chen Yi, and the two fell in love; Golgi admired her very much; Stalin personally designated her as one of the people responsible for Gorky's coffin.
In modern Chinese history, Hu Lanqi had close relationships with many famous people from both the Kuomintang and the Communist Party, and can be described as a legendary woman in modern Chinese history. “Mrs. Mei,” the new “May 4th” woman of Chengdu described in Mao Dun's novel “The Rainbow,” was modeled after Hu Lanqi. Hulan Qi served in a prison in Nazi Germany, defended Gorky, and was awarded the rank of Major General during the War of Resistance Against Japanese Aggression, becoming the first female general in modern China.
From escaping marriage to embarking on the path of revolution
Hu Lanping was born on June 22, 1901 to a family surnamed Hu, Gongsuo Street, Chaoyuan, Chengdu, Sichuan. His ancestor, Hu Dahai, was a founding hero in the Ming Dynasty. His father was Grandpa Pao. He advocated good manners and always engaged in the “repetition of the Qing dynasty and restoration of the Ming Dynasty.” In 1921, after graduating from Chengdu Yuxiu Girls' Normal School, Hu Lanqi was assigned to her cousin Yang Guzhi by her father Xu.
Yang Guzhi is a snob businessman. Hu Lanqi didn't like him, so he simply fled from Chengdu to Luzhou in southern Sichuan to become an elementary school teacher and began to earn a living independently. Hu Lanqi is very beautiful. The Sichuan warlord Yang Sen once wanted to marry her as his little wife, but was rejected. Qin Dejun, the girlfriend of the famous writer Mao Dun, told Mao Dun the story of Hu Lanqi. Mao Dun was very interested and wrote it into his novel “The Rainbow.” Soon, Hu Lanqi was admitted to South Sichuan Normal School.
In the spring of 1923, Chen Yi, who returned from work and study in France, wrote numerous poems criticizing the dark warlord politics of the time, and had a positive impact on the intellectual community of Sichuan, especially among young students. Hu Lanqi is one of the young people who were inspired by his article. She went directly to the newspaper to find Chen Yi, and they became close friends ever since.
Soon, Hu Lanqi married Chen Mengyun of the Sichuan Army Youth Corps and deputy head of the political department of the Third Division of the Sichuan Army. In the spring of 1926, Hu Lanqi went to Guangzhou, the center of the revolution at the time. He Xiangning, the wife of Liao Zhongkai, the left-wing leader of the Kuomintang and Sun Yat-sen's close aide, met in Guangzhou with Zhou Enlai and Li Fuchun. He Xiangning and Song Qingling had a major influence on Hu Lanqi's life. They can be called her guides and protectors. In the fall of 1926, at the height of the Northern Expedition War, Hu Lanqi left the Central Women's Department with He Xiangning as the minister, went to the front line of the Northern Expedition, and was admitted to the Wuhan Central Military and Political Academy, where she was formed into a girls' team. Here, Hu Lanqi met his friend Chen Yi.
One day in April 1927, Chen Yi invited Hu Lanqi to eat at a restaurant in the Yellow Crane Tower in Wuhan. During the meeting, Chen Yi asked, “It's been a few months since you arrived at the military academy. Have you joined the Party?” Hu Lanzi bowed his head and said, “Not yet. I applied to the party organization, but Li Shu-ning (that is, Zhao Yiman) asked me to divorce Chen Mengyun and cut off financial ties with him. But I once said to Chen Mengyun that as long as he is always revolutionary, I will never be separated from him. Now how can I divorce him for no reason?” Chen Yi expressed understanding. He later sought Li Shu-ning, the head of the girls' team party group, for Hu Lanqi's entry into the party, but to no avail.
Soon, Hu Lanqi's husband Chen Mengyun and Fan Yingshi, the former head of the Propaganda Section of the Political Department of the Third Division of the Sichuan Army, also arrived in Wuhan. After Chiang Kai-shek launched the “April 12” counterrevolutionary coup, Wang Jingwei also acted recklessly and blatantly disbanded the Central Military Academy in Wuhan. At the mercy of the Communist Party of China organization, the Wuhan Central Military Academy was transformed into a teaching group for Zhang Fakui's Second Front Army. On August 1, the teaching delegation was ordered to conquer Jiang. The day before preparing to exile, Chen Yi went from Wuchang to Hankou to resign to friends Chen Mengyun, Hu Lanqi, and Fan Yingshi.
After the defeat of the Great Revolution, Hu Lanqi acted as the Minister for Women in the Hankou Special Party Department and the director of the Women Workers Movement Committee of the Wuhan Federation of Trade Unions, and also served as a steering committee member for the Women's Movement in Xiang'e and Jiangxi. In 1929, she was personally named by Chiang Kai-shek and expelled from Jiangxi Province. This year, Hu Lanqi visited Europe under the name of the director of the Jiangxi Provincial Relief Hospital Orphanage Center and Women's Correctional Center to inspect social assistance services. After arriving in Germany in 1930, Hu Lanqi lived with Ho Heung-ning and Liao Cheng-chi's mother and son. In the same year, as introduced by Cheng Fangwu, Hu Lanqi joined the Chinese Language Group of the German Communist Party and became a member of the Communist Party of China.
Sitting in a Nazi-fascist prison
In 1931, Hu Lanqi accompanied Song Qingling back to China to attend Song Qingling's mother's funeral. In October of the same year, Song Qingling sent Hu Lanqi back to Germany to continue his studies through the help of American progressive journalists Shi Motley and Chen Hansheng. At that time, fascist forces were rampant in Germany, and Japan had already taken control of the three northeastern provinces of China. In order to unite more international students to fight against Japan and save their lives, overseas Chinese in Germany and students formed the Party's peripheral organization “German Overseas Chinese Anti-Imperialist League”. Hu Lanqi served as chairman. The members include Wang Bingnan, Jiang Longji, Cheng Qiying, and Liu Simu.
At the time, most of the Chinese students in Germany were controlled by the National Government's embassy in Germany. They tried to put the fight against Japan and save the country into the path of supporting Jiang and anti-communism. Among them, members of the Communist Party were influenced by Wang Ming, Kang Sheng, and others of the CCP delegation to the Communist Party. They did not fully understand the significance of the anti-fascist united front. They treated left-wing Kuomintang leaders such as Song Qingling and He Xiangning with extreme left-wing sectarian sentiments, and spread gossip. They regard Song Qingling and the Nineteenth Route Army generals as “centrists” — and all “centralists” are the most dangerous because of their progressive performance and the most “deceptive.”
For a period, they prohibited party members from having any contact with anti-Jiang generals such as Song Qingling and Feng Yuxiang. Against this backdrop, Hu Lanqi, who accompanied Song Qingling back to China for her mother's death, was expelled from her party membership when she returned to Berlin. A few years later, during the review, I learned that at the time, Hu Lanqi was falsely charged with a number of charges. The main one was that she escorted the “Kuomintang official” (referring to Song Qingling) back home, even though she had obtained prior consent from the party organization for this task.
Hu Lanqi appealed to the Communist International. The Central Committee of the Communist Party of Germany was commissioned by the Communist International to send the head of the parliamentary caucus, O Toberg, to investigate and resolve it. With the help of Toberg and Minzenberg, the German Communist Party restored Hu Lan Qi's party membership. In his memoirs, Hu Lanqi said, “Some comrades have no objections on the surface, but in their hearts they are unwilling to follow suit. Sure enough, after the meeting, they still didn't return my party card to me immediately, nor did they let me know that the meeting had taken place.” Since then, Hu Lanqi did not have a party license, and was unable to connect with an organization after returning home, and has been active as a democrat.
In December 1932, in accordance with the decision of the German Communist Party, Hu Lanqi spoke at an anti-fascist conference held at the Berlin Stadium to accuse Japan of invading China. Also speaking at the conference was German Communist Member Maria Aisse. The two have known each other ever since. In the spring of 1933, Hitler came to power, and a large number of communists and progressives were arrested. Hu Lanqi collaborated with Fitz Nitta, a staff member of the German Communist Party Central Committee newspaper “Red Flag,” to publish leaflets. As a result, she was arrested and imprisoned for women. Song Qingling, Lu Xun and others protested against the German Consulate in Shanghai under the name of the “Civil Rights Protection League.” Three months later, Hulan Qi was released.
It was only because Aisser and Clara Zetkin acted as members of parliament to guarantee that Hulan Qi was able to stay in Germany. Soon, Hulan Qi was once again deported from the country. After leaving Germany, Hulan Qi was forced to make a living and studied part time at a Jewish gardening school in Paris.
She used this time to write the memoir “In German Women's Prison”, which was published one after another in “Le Monde” edited by Henri Babisse. Hu Lanqi's article revealed the cruel persecution of progressives and innocent people by German fascists through personal experience. It also describes the hatred and witty and tenacious struggle of female prisoners from all walks of life against fascism. The article was immediately translated into the four languages of Russian, English, German, and Spanish, and attracted widespread international attention and praise.
In 1934, she attended the first Soviet Writers' Congress and gave an impromptu speech at the conference, recounting her experience of being cruelly persecuted by Hitler. Gorky was deeply moved. The Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister of the Soviet Union, who was immediately invited to attend, tried to retain Hulan. As a result, she stayed in the Soviet Union to write. When Gorky introduced Hulan Qi to other Soviet literary artists at a small family dinner, he praised her for “this is a real person”!
Positive role in the war of resistance
In 1935, Hu Lanqi was commissioned by Wang Ming and Kang Sheng of the Chinese Communist Party Delegation to the Communist Party to work in the United Front against Japan in Hong Kong. Seeing the Japanese invaders burning and fornication, Hu Lanqi was very angry. She volunteered to recruit 10 young women at a tutoring school for women set up by the Shanghai Young Women's Christian Association. On October 5, 1937, Hu Lanqi established the Shanghai Working Women's Battlefield Service Corps in Shanghai. In the afternoon of the same day, the female soldiers bid farewell to He Xiangning and went to the front line of anti-Japanese resistance. He Xiangning asked everyone, “Your life is in danger at any time when you go to the front line. Are you afraid or not?” The members of the group answered in unison: “Don't be afraid!” He Xiang Ning was very happy to see everyone's determination. She encouraged the female soldiers to do a good job in the military and serve the anti-Japanese soldiers and people.
Hu Lanqi led service teams to carry out publicity and education work and battlefield rescue work in various war zones. Every day during the war of resistance between Song and Shanghai, there are some moving stories of heroic killing of enemies. Torin-ji Temple is a high point of contention between the enemy and us, and our side has a platoon of guards here. Under enemy attacks, most of our soldiers were killed and wounded; there were only two people left: platoon leader Hu Yuzheng and squad leader Pan Yulin.
Finally, the enemy stormed into the temple, and Hu Yuzheng ran out of bullets. At this critical moment, Hu Yuzheng grabbed a shovel, hit an enemy officer in the head, and slashed it with force. The enemy officer immediately fell to the ground. As soon as the enemy officers fell, the rest of the Japanese invaders were frightened and ran back. Squad leader Pan Yulin fired several shots in a row from behind, killing several more enemy soldiers. In this way, they repelled yet another onslaught from the enemy and held on to their position at Torin-ji Temple. Hu Lanqi processed this story of heroic killing of enemies into the Peking opera “The Battle at Donglin Temple” and performed condolences on the front line, which had great results.
In November 1937, Shanghai fell, and the service corps left Shanghai with the army. Afterwards, they moved to the front lines of anti-Japanese resistance in eight provinces to promote the war of resistance, making outstanding contributions to the war of resistance. In early 1938, the service team arrived in Nanchang. At this time, leaders of the New Fourth Army, including Xiang Ying and Chen Yi, formed the New Fourth Army in Nanchang. The party organization decided to establish a party branch within the service group. The original nine members of the Communist Youth League were all converted to Communist Party members. The branch was directly led by Chen Yi, and there was no relationship with the local party.
After the defeat of the Great Revolution, Hu Lanqi and Chen Yi hadn't seen each other for ten years. It's been a while since we met again, and it's hard to express how happy they are for each other. Hu Lanqi described her experiences over the years, and also mentioned her divorce with Chen Mengyun. To resolve the issue of Hu Lanqi's party membership, Chen Yi wrote a letter to Deng Yingchao and Cai Chang. After arriving in Wuhan, Deng Yingchao forwarded Chen Yi's letter to Wang Ming, secretary of the Yangtze River Bureau of the CPC Central Committee. Wang Ming soon summoned Hu Lanwei.
Wang Ming said, “I read Chen Yi's letter. After studying it, we decided to let you continue to be active outside, don't have horizontal contact, and contact me directly in the future.” Hu Lanqi asked how to resolve his party membership issue. Wang Ming said, “The Chinese Communist Party has not issued a party certificate; your party organization relationship is directly linked to me.” Hu Lanqi was very happy to finally connect with the party. However, Wang Ming was later criticized, and the issue of Hu Lanqi's party membership was still unresolved.
In the summer of 1939, Hu Lanqi came to Chongqing, the capital. Soon, Chiang Kai-shek issued a letter of appointment to Hu Lanqi, appointing her as the mentor of Major General of the Battlefield Party and Political Committee of the National Government Military Commission, and sending her to work in the 3rd, 6th, and 9th war zones. She used this special status to do a lot of useful work for the Party.
Since leaving the New Fourth Army, Hu Lanqi has been watching Chen Yi's safety. In June 1947, the Kuomintang newspaper published “Chen Yi's Death” and “Chen Yi's Death” with shocking large headlines, and also used large pages to describe in detail the circumstances of the “Chen Yi Memorial Service.” Hu Lanqi was very sad when she learned about it. Later, when she received another letter from Chen Yi's parents and inquired, she decided to support the elder and use an orchard, field, and house outside the East Gate of Chengdu for the elderly.
