 One of the Eight Immortals of Taoism. A native of Tang Kaiyuan and Tianbao Shi. Summer clothes, snow and ice in the winter. They often sing step songs in Chang'an City. The lyrics have many fairy meanings. There are people who met him as a child, and when they are old, they are as good-looking as ever before riding a drunk crane at a restaurant. Yuan Renren used this anecdote to write a miscellaneous drama where Han Zhong left behind and made peace.
In “The Wonder Buddha”: “Lan Caihe, I don't know who is there. I often wear tattered clothes, a six-inch belt, boots on one foot, and bare feet on the other. In summer, I wear thick underwear under a long shirt; in winter, I lie in the snow and exhale like steam. Every time I ask for food on the street, I have three feet of extra length. Sing when drunk. The young and old all watched him sing, and when he sang, he seemed crazy, but he wasn't. The lyrics are random, and the song is full of fantasy and unpredictable. They put the money they got on a rope and dragged it away, but they just dropped it and ignored it. Sometimes it's given to poor people's homes, and sometimes it's spent in wine shops. Traveled all over the world, some people saw him when he was a child, and they never saw him until old age. The blue and peaceful appearance is still the same. Later, someone saw him drinking at the Houliang Restaurant, and heard a loud sound. Suddenly, he flew up into the sky on a crane, dropped his boots, shirt, and belt, and slowly climbed up. The original drama “Zhong Li Quan Du Lan Caihe” says that Lan Caihe is a stage name. His real name is Xu Jian. He sings miscellaneous drama in Gou Lan. When he was 50, he was beaten by the government for not knowing what mistakes he made, and was later turned into a fairy by Zhong Li Quan.
