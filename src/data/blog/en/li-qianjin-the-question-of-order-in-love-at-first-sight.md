Jia Mu was supposed to be among the best book reviewers in ancient times. With just a few words of “Breaking the Lies,” she succinctly and clearly assessed the cliché of talented and beautiful novels of all time: “These books are all a set. The left are nothing but talented people; they are the least interesting. They all started with Shuxiangmenden. “Right on the Wall” is clearly a blueprint for Jia Mu's criticism. Deconstructing the story of this miscellaneous drama written by a white servant in the Yuan Dynasty is a regular routine for ancient operas, and anyone with the will can watch and portray it.
First, love at first sight: Shaojun, the son of Shang Shu Pei Xingjian, went to Luoyang to buy flowers on the orders of Tang Gao Zong. He passed through Luoyang's garden one day and immediately saw his daughter standing against a wall, so he got involved in writing poetry. Li Qianjin wrote a poem and asked him to meet him in the back garden that night. Shaojun certainly jumped in from the wall and was discovered by Li Qianjin's nanny, causing the two to leave quietly.
Second, he decided to live in the back garden for life: Li Qianjin eloped and followed Pei Shaojun back to Chang'an's home and hid in the back garden. The two lived together for seven years and gave birth to two twins.
Once again, the parents broke up their good marriage: during the Ching Ming Festival, Pei Shangshu came to the garden by chance and met this young brother and sister. After questioning, he learned about the beginning and end. Pei Shangshu decided that Li Qianjin had committed adultery and ordered Shaojun to take Li Qianjin home from writing a book, but left behind two children.
The climax, the winner among the Fallen Boys: this is the only reasonable way for children to fight. Pei Shaojun, a junior high ranking official in Luoyang, hopes to get back together with Li Qianjin. Li Qianjin has a temper, resents him for taking a break from himself, and insists on refusing to accept it.
At the end of the day, the marriage was originally decided: his father, Bae Sang-shu, later learned that Li Qianjin was the daughter of his old partner, Li Shijie, and he had also discussed marriage for his children before. After some explanation and plea, Li Qianjin forgave them. The couple broke the mirror and got back together.
This pattern of falling in love at first sight is simply too addictive to death. Love dramas in Song Yuan operas all add branches and leaves to this framework. Taste is a process of accumulation. Little girls who are only 14 and 5 years old and don't leave the door alone can have such an accurate and ruthless vision of their parents picking their son-in-law! This is clearly a paranoia of a group of book readers: in the Yuan Dynasty, the place of books was low; they had no choice but to imagine that a good sister would fall from the sky, and that it was a fortune; they married themselves.
