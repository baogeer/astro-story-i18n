 During the Qingqian Carnival, calligraphy was popular, and the calligraphy of Weng Fanggang, Liang Tongshu, and Wang Wenzhi was even more popular. He was also famous at the time. He was Liu Yong, the main character in the TV series “Prime Minister Liu Luo Guan”. He was good at learning the strengths of famous artists of the past, bold and innovative, and has a special charm, and is loved by modern people.
Liu Yong is a master writer. His calligraphy is unconventional. His calligraphy did not follow the rules. He was introduced by Zhao Meng, Wei Jin, and also Yan Zhenqing, Su Shi, and the Dharma postings of various families. He was not imprisoned by the ancients in the middle and late stages. He had strong bones, rich in taste, transcendent and unique, and “famous all over the world.” At first glance, Liu Wei's calligraphy is round and smooth, like a bunch of cotton. After careful examination, the bones are clear, and they contain strength. The realm of Liu Wei's calligraphy can be summed up in the three words “quiet,” “light,” and “clear.” This is where he surpasses ordinary people. He is in stark contrast to Wang Wenzhi, who likes to use light ink, and is known as the “Prime Minister of Strong Ink.” However, he dared to break through traditional writing and was criticized by some conservative-minded calligraphers at the time. Weng Fanggang was one of them.
 
 Bao Shichen from the Qing dynasty records this interesting fact: Weng Fanggang's son-in-law is a student of Liu Wei. Once, when this student visited his father-in-law, he met Weng Fanggang who was practicing calligraphy. He also wrote the font he had practiced all his life. Every stroke and drawing was completely in accordance with the requirements of the ancients, without changing a single stroke. Influenced by teacher Liu Wei, this student was unaccustomed to his old father-in-law's adherence to the rules. “Father-in-law, you and my teacher are great contemporary calligraphers. I've never heard you comment on my teacher's calligraphy. Please talk to me today!”
 
 Weng Fanggang put down his pen, looked at his son-in-law, and said, “Go back and ask your teacher which characters he wrote from the ancients?”
 
 This student actually went back and asked the teacher. Liu Wei smiled and said, “When you went back, you also asked your father-in-law, which of the words he wrote was his own?”
 
 These interrogations reflect their different views on the art of calligraphy; one is old-fashioned and the other innovative. This story later became a great story in the history of calligraphy.
 
 It is said that Liu Yong's posture in holding a pen is also very peculiar. When he writes in front of the guests, the pen is on his wrist and uses the traditional method of holding the pen. However, when he wrote in the inner study room himself, he turned the pen tube and wrote quickly, regardless of whether he wrote large or small letters. The pen flutters back and forth with the finger, left and right, like a lion rolling a hydrangea. When he was excited to write, he even took off his pen and flew to the ground. Unfortunately, this method has not spread.
