 Legend has it that Luban was the smartest and most capable craftsman in ancient China. He was originally known as Lou Gong; because he was a native of Lu in the late Chunqiu period, Luban became the title he was called by his descendants.
Once, he came to Wu Guogu's Sicheng, paradise on earth, which certainly lives up to his name. The towers of the ancient city are arranged one by one. The teahouses and restaurants are very lively. Luban's excitement multiplied, figuring out the characteristics of Suzhou architecture, and being fascinated. Suddenly there was a loud noise. Ruban looked back and saw a newly built pagoda standing tall in the green, grassy empty field in front of him. There was a crowd of people in front of the tower, making a lot of noise, and I didn't know what they were doing. Slowly walking past the onlookers, he saw an old man dressed in satin, wearing a high crown, and a satchel around his waist, who was furious. He was furious. A middle-aged man squatting across from the old man, hugged his head with both hands and looked depressed. Luban was so strange; he only found out the truth when he asked.
The old man is a famous local rich man. He is preparing to build a pagoda in order to accumulate good and moral acts, which has been passed down through the ages.
The craftsman took over the project. The pagoda was built using timber and meticulous planning. After nearly three years of hard work, the pagoda was finally completed. However, I don't know how to do it. Even though the pagoda is built, it is always tilted regardless of whether it is viewed horizontally or vertically. According to measurements, the pagoda is indeed tilted nearly ten degrees. People are shaking their heads and pointing fingers at it. The rich man thought that the rebellious attack on the tower was very angry and would damage his full merits. Therefore, he personally found the craftsman to settle the accounts: either tear down and rebuild the pagoda, or fix the pagoda; otherwise, it must be dealt with by the government.
This made it difficult for the craftsmen. If they were to demolish and rebuild, they would sell their children and sell their children themselves, and they would not be able to withstand financial pressure; if the pagoda was fixed, this wouldn't be possible either, because even though the pagoda is wooden, it's still about 1 million pounds, so I can only watch and marvel at the tower!
Ruban took a close look around the pagoda, and also took a look at the crafters who couldn't do anything, and walked over to comfort him, “Don't worry; as long as you get me some wood, I can fix it in less than a month!”
As soon as the craftsman heard it, he was half-convinced and half-skeptical, but there was no other way. So he brought wood and waited with a glimmer of hope. Meanwhile, Luban didn't let anyone interfere with his help. He cut the wood he carried into many small wooden wedges and knocked it in from the inclined side of the tower piece by piece, causing the inclined side to slowly raise it. Ping-pong pong like this. After getting up early and working in the dark for a month, the pagoda certainly stood upright.
The craftsman gratefully asked Luban, “Gong, why can you make the pagoda stand upright with this remedy?”
Luban replied, “Since the leaning tower is wooden and has a piping structure, the pull between the parts is quite strong and can form an organic whole, so it can be fixed by hitting a wooden wedge. The wooden wedge also has an inclined surface. Not only is it easier to hit it in, and it has the effect of 'four or two kilograms'. After being driven in, it can also raise the height of the inclined surface of the tower, so that the tower is no longer tilted.”
