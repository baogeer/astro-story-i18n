 General Meng Tian, a famous general of the Qin dynasty, gave the world the impression that he was the character of a warrior who attacked the Huns and built the Great Wall. So was General Meng Tian really that kind of person in history? Is it just a Takeo?
1. Born into a eunuch family
Meng Tian came from a very noble family. He came from a family of officials and military generals. Although their ancestors were from Qi, his grandfather Meng Wei and his father, Meng Wu, were both senior Qin generals. They all contributed greatly to the six countries of Qin unification. The merits of the Meng family were few families in Qin at the time that could be compared to them. It was also such a prominent family and a family of military generals that laid a solid foundation for Meng Tian's development.
Meng Tian's grandfather, Meng Wei, became an official of the Qing dynasty as early as Qin Zhaogong, and visited other countries as a general many times. In 248 BC, they attacked Korea, occupied places such as Seonggao and Yeongyang, and established as Sanchon County. In 244 BC, they attacked Korea again, and this time they took over 13 Korean castles. In 247 BC, they attacked Zhao Guo, the same ancestor of Qin, and occupied Taiyuan. After conquering Jinyang, which was originally the country of Zhao, in 246 BC, they also attacked places such as Zhaoguo New Town, and took 37 castles, making it Taiyuan County. When Wei Guo was attacked in 246 BC, they were fought by the allied forces of Wei, Han, Zhao, Chu, and Wei, led by Xinling King Wuji, and as a result, they were defeated in battle. However, in 242 BC, Meng Wei used the time of Xinling's death to attack Wei Guo, occupy places such as Changping and Sanyang, seize a total of 20 castles and make them East County, and avenge the last defeat.
Although Meng Tian's father Meng Wu's merit was not as high as his grandfather Meng Wei, he was also quite high. For example, in the 22nd year of Qin Zhaogong, Meng Wu led an army to attack Qi (Tianshi Qi) and occupy 9 cities in the country. In 223 BC, Meng Wu attacked Chu together with General Wang Jian. As a result, Chu Wang was captured and Chu General Xiang Yan, who led an army to resist the Qin army, was killed. Xiang Yan was the father of Xiang Liang, the uncle of Xiang Yu, the later king of Western Chu.
II. Meng Tian's exploits
Sima Qian's “Historical Chronicles” is a biography for Meng Tian, not specifically for her grandfather Meng Wei and her father Meng Wu, but to a certain extent, Meng Tian's military exploits should not be as good or comparable to her grandfather Meng Wei and her father Meng Wu. In particular, he did not do much in the war to unify the Six Kingdoms.
What made Meng Tian enter the historical stage was not a remarkable success, but a defeat. In 225 BC, Qin Shi Huang disobeyed General Wang Jian's suggestions and sent Li Xin and Meng Tian to lead 200,000 troops to attack Chu. As a result, Xiang Yan fought back, and was completely defeated. When Meng Tian saw Li Xin's defeat in this battle, he resolutely and quickly withdrew his troops, reducing the losses of the Qin army. However, it can also be seen from the other side that Meng Tian was still very good at using troops. Of course, his father Meng Wu, who hated him, helped him get revenge.
Meng Tian officially came into people's eyes. In 221 BC, considering Meng Tian's family history, Qin Shi Huang sent him to attack Qi, the son of General Wang Jian. As a result, Qi Wangjian was captured and destroyed. Meng Tian was revered as an internal historian by Qin Shi Huang, thanks to the success of the war when they were destroyed the next time.
After Qin Shi Huang unified the six countries, the northern region faced threats from the Huns, an ethnic minority, which provided an opportunity for Meng Tian to stay young forever. However, it is also interesting. The direct reason Qin Shi Huang sent Meng Tian to attack the Huns was not because the Huns invaded the Qin dynasty, but in 215 BC, Lu Sheng, a native of Yan, mentioned “Hu Ye, the one who died of Qin” in a book presented to Qin Shi Huang. (“Historical Records: The Chronicles of Qin Shi Huang”) Perhaps Qin Shi Huang believed in superstition, and also had little confidence in himself or his rule. He was very angry and afraid, so Paimeng Tien led an army of 300,000 to attack the Huns north. Meng Tian lived up to Qin Shi Huang's trust and successfully recovered the area south of the Yellow River (Hetao area) and defeated the Huns. As a result, Meng Tian stood on the sidelines to fight against the Huns for more than ten years. In order to protect the Central Plains region from being plundered by the Huns, Meng Tian took many defensive measures. First, use Tianxuan of the Yellow River as a fortress barrier, and build 44 castles along the Yellow River to guard. Second, as everyone knows, Meng Tian spent a lot of manpower, material, and financial resources to build the famous Great Wall of China from east to Lintao in the west based on dangerous mountain terrain. Although this huge project prevented the Huns from going south, it was also one of the factors that caused the great uprising of the late Qin peasants. Qin Shi Huang and Meng Tian did not expect this. In order to make it easier for Qin Shi Huang to travel, Meng Tian was ordered to build a 1000-mile long road from Xianyang to Jiuyuan, which is equivalent to today's highway. On the one hand, it helped the Qin dynasty to increase troops to the north and consolidate its rule in the north; in fact, it was also one of the measures to defend the Huns; on the other hand, it also objectively promoted economic and cultural exchanges with the northern region. Meng Tian relied on the merits of fighting against the Huns and was selected to be published in the “Historical Records”. To a certain extent, it is also the result of the Han nationalist ideology.
Meng Tian has also made significant contributions to the inheritance of Chinese culture. Legend has it that the brushes we use to write calligraphy today were created by Meng Tian using rabbit hair and bamboo tubes. Of course, this is just a legend, but it should be quite likely.
3. Trusted by Qin Shi Huang
Qin Shi Huang was an emperor who would rather die of exhaustion than trust ministers. So what is the relationship between Qin Shi Huang and Meng Tian? It should be said that Meng Tian is a special case. Meng Tian is deeply trusted by Qin Shi Huang. The following evidence can prove it.
First, Qin Shi Huang made Meng Tian lead an army of 300,000 to fight against the Huns in Shanggun for more than 10 years, while Meng Tian's younger brother Meng Yi (the character played by Jackie Chan in the movie “Mythology”) was close to Qin Shi Huang. “He took the position of Shangqing, then joined the throne, and when he joined.” (“Historical Chronicles: The Biography of Meng Tian”) The two brothers have each been stationed in one place for more than ten years, and the other is a staff officer within North Korea. Had it not been for the deep trust of Qin Shi Huang, then this is unimaginable, because it is easy for a rebellion to occur in a situation where “a quiet foreign affair is always a conspirator” (“Historical Record: The Biography of Meng Tian”).
Second, in 212 BC, Qin Shi Huang killed more than 460 Confucians in Xianyang Pit. Prince Fu Su expressed opposition and attacked Emperor Qin Shi Huang several times, angering Qin Shi Huang. However, the angry Qin Shi Huang's punishment for Fusu, the future heir to the throne, was to let Fusu go to the county to serve as a guard for Meng Tian. Some people think this is Qin Shi Huang's distrust of Meng Tian. I think this is exactly Qin Shi Huang's trust in Meng Tian. Qin Shi Huang did not allow Fusu to monitor and supervise Meng Tian; in fact, he let Meng Tian cultivate and train the Soviet Union, help the Soviet Union build meritorious businesses, increase political capital, and eliminate the negative effects of Qin Shi Huang's own remarks that were not conducive to ruling, so as to better inherit the throne.
Finally, during his serious illness, Qin Shi Huang “ordered Zhao Gao to give his son a book to help him say, 'Meng Tian, a soldier, was buried with the funeral in Xianyang. '” (“Historical Chronicles: The Biography of Lee Si Lie”) This is actually an orphan for Qin Shi Huang. It allows Meng Tian to assist the Soviet Union in governing the country, fully demonstrating Qin Shi Huang's trust in Meng Tian.
Why did Qin Shi Huang trust Meng Tian so much? I think there are four reasons: first, Meng Wei and Meng Wu, the two generals of the Meng family are very loyal; second, Meng Tian and the brothers Meng Yi are very loyal and loyal to Qin Shi Huang, and work hard for Qin Shi Huang before and after; fourth, in addition to his military exploits, Meng Tian himself also has great strategy and ability.
4. The far-sighted Meng Tian
Although Meng Tian comes from a family of military generals, he is not just a warrior like most generals; Meng Tian is a military general with great strategy, talent, and wisdom. Why are you saying that?
First, we can see from his rivals Zhao Gao and Li Si's comments on him. When winning over Li Si to help Hu Hai seize power, Zhao Gao told Li Si that Li's talent, merit, ambition, public approval rating, and the level of trust supported by the Soviet Union were not as good as Meng Tian; this was also acknowledged by Li Si himself.
Second, after Hu Hai took power, when Fu Su and Meng Tian were given death in the name of Emperor Qin Shi Huang, it can also be seen from the reactions of Fu Su and Meng Tian to the royal decree. When Fu Su received the Edict of Death, she cried and wanted to commit suicide, yet Meng Tian dissuaded Fu Su from committing suicide, and expressed doubts about the authenticity of the decree of death, and proposed to re-verify the authenticity before committing suicide. However, Fusu did not listen to dissuasion and committed suicide, while Meng Tian refused to commit suicide. If Meng Tian had no wisdom, she would never have thought that the emperor's royal decree was false, and Meng Tian's doubts were not Hu Cai, but judgments based on facts. He believes, “His Majesty (Qin Shi Huang) is an outsider, has not established a prince, and his envoy will guard the 300,000 people. The prince is a prison guard. This is also a heavy responsibility in this world.” (“Historical Chronicles: The Biography of Lee Si Lie”) In fact, Meng Tian already knew Qin Shi Huang's mentality that he wanted Fusu to succeed to the throne.
So was Meng Tian's wisdom and farsightedness innate? Obviously not. Like the vast majority of people, Meng Tian's wisdom and ambition were cultivated acquired. Due to her excellent family environment, Meng Tian received a good education since childhood, and Meng Tian studied very well. First, because Meng Tian was once a “book and prison book literature,” and second, Meng Tian had a very high level of conversation with the Huhai envoy before his death, and was able to cite the scriptures many times. This is enough proof that Meng Tian is a very talented person.
Such a wise and loyal minister also had a side of sorrow and regret. He was unable to oppose the tyranny of Qin Shi Huang as much as he did to support the Soviet Union, and failed to appreciate the suffering of the people.
5. SAD ENDING
The end for Meng Tian and the entire Montessori family was quite sad. After Hu Hai took over the throne, Meng Yi was slaughtered, Meng Tian was forced to commit suicide by taking poison, and his family was annihilated, becoming a funeral product and victim for Hu Hai to seize power. Why would someone with great wisdom and a strong army end up so miserable?
First, Meng Tian's sad ending was the result of Zhao Gao's revenge. Zhao Gao, the lieutenant who helped Hu Hai come to power, was once sentenced to death by Meng Yi, and almost got beheaded by Meng Yi. This incident has always made Zhao Gaohuai hate this incident and is looking for opportunities to retaliate. First, one of the reasons why Zhao Gao, Li Si, and Hu Hai seized power was that Zhao Gao “complained about the rule of law instead of himself.” (“Historical Records: The Biography of Meng Tian”) Second, Zhao Gao's lobbying to win over Li Si to help Hu Hai seize power was to compare Li Si with Meng Tian, saying that Li Si was not as good as Meng Tian. Third, after Fusu committed suicide, Hu Hai originally intended to release Meng Tian, who had been imprisoned in Yangzhou, but was strongly opposed by Zhao Gao. Zhao Gao “destroyed the Meng clan day and night, begged for their crimes, and impeached them.” (“Historical Chronicles: The Biography of Meng Tian”)
Second, Meng Tian's death was the result of Hu Hai's consolidation of the throne. Although Meng Tian has been loyal to the Qin dynasty for three generations, he will not rebel against the Qin dynasty. However, second-generation Hu Hai conspired to seize power. He took the throne of Fu Su, who was close to Meng Tian, murdered Fu Su, and imprisoned Meng Tian in Yangzhou to kill him. Hu Hai is very afraid of the living Meng Tian. If Meng Tian is released, and Meng Tian, who is supported by the public, leads his 300,000 army to avenge the Soviet Union, it seriously threatens Hu Hai's rule. From this perspective, Meng Tian's tragedy was inevitable under feudal rule. Because no emperor will keep a general alive who has a heavy army and is in conflict with himself. Meng Tian could only sigh, “Why did I sin against heaven and die for nothing?” A generation of famous military commanders just took medicine and went west.
Meng Tian's tragedy is not unique to Meng Tian; it has been repeated over and over again throughout the long course of history. Because in feudal dynasties, authoritarianism ruled ruthless, cold-blooded animals. In their eyes, they only had power and desire. In order to achieve their own power and desires, they did not hesitate to sacrifice any means, and the life of any loyal general was irrelevant. Meng Tian's tragedy was not Meng Tian's personal tragedy; it was a historical tragedy, a tragedy of the times. After all, it was an era where “if you wanted to die, you had to die.”
