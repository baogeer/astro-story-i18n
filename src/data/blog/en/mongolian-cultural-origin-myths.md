 There are many myths about the origin of culture among Mongolians. For example, in the myth of the origin of fire, it is said that there is no fire in the world; when humans look up at the sky and see countless fires burning in the sky, they want to steal the fire from the sky back. As a result, people sent swallows to steal fires. The swallow flew into the sky and flew in through the skylight of a household. It was about to fly away when it was caught by the housewife. The housewife caught the swallow's tail with scissors. The swallow had trouble breaking away and flew back to the world. Since then, there has been a fire in the world, and the swallow's tail has changed to what it is today. After there was a fire in the world, the lower world also wanted to steal human fire, so they sent moths to steal the fire. However, the fire that the moth stole was a blue flame without heat, so the moth repeatedly stole the fire. It is said that the moths fought the fire to steal the fire. This myth very graphically reflects the characteristics of Mongolian mentality — in the concept of the upper, middle, and lower realms of Mongolian shamanism, the upper world and the lower world live the same as real Mongolians. They live in yurts and use fire scissors. In fact, the Mongols imagined and created the heavenly and underground worlds according to human life.
The horse head is the most characteristic Mongolian instrument. The myth about the origin of the horse head goes like this: Burhan Bagshi (God)'s mother died, and Burhan Bagshi was very sad because of this. To get rid of his sorrow, he wanted to create an instrument, but he always couldn't do it well. So he went to the fortune teller to ask the reason. The fortune teller told Burhan Bagsi: “You should make a box out of wood, cover it with snakeskin, use a horsetail as a string, use curved wood as a bow, and make a piano.” Burhan Bagsi did what the fortune teller said, but the sound of the piano was always bad, so he went to the fortune teller to ask the reason. The fortune teller said, “It was the devil who took your mother's soul; he should have told you.” Burhan Bagsy went to find the devil. The devil told him, “If you rub rosin on your horse's tail, it will make a nice sound.” Burhan Bagsy did what he said and eventually made a horse head. Ibid., p. 167. In Mongolian tradition, the origin of music, songs, and stories is all related to death. For example, in the myth about the origin of the story, it is said that after a blind person called Tajwa died half dead, the soul left the body, traveled to hell, got stories from hell, and brought back to the world.
