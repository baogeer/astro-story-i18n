 Zhang Ji's “Night Stay at Fengqiao” is a household name. Today, all passbooks are written: “The moonrise is dark and the sky is full of frost, and Jiang Feng's fishing is sad. At Hanshan Temple outside Gusu City, the bell rang to the passenger ship in the middle of the night.” This is the same in current elementary school language textbooks and popular books. Readers praised the poem, yet few people thought deeply about the problems it contained. For example: 1. If the “moonset” is untimed, how can you use the surrounding search to accurately position it in time? 2. There are no maple trees on the banks of the Suzhou River. Why did the poet sing “Jiang Feng”? 3. Since it is the night of “moonsetting”, how can we identify riverside tree species? 4. Where is “sorrow”? Why do poets have to sleep with “sorrow”? These many questions directly affect the correct understanding of poetry. In particular, “Moonfall Cries”, if not accurately positioned, can lead to confusion in the narrative logic of the entire poem and contradictions between things.
There are two main opinions about the timing of the “moonsetting cry”. One thought is “half of the night,” which echoes the last sentence, “Midnight Bells,” such as Shi Zhi Cun Yun: “Because of the severe cold, crows can't sleep, so they're still crying. The moonset is already in the middle of the night, and it must always be in late autumn or early winter.” (“100 Tales of Tang Poems”) Liu Xuekaiyun: “The title is 'Night Stay'. In fact, I only write about the scenery and feelings of 'Midnight Night'. The first sentence of the poem describes three closely related scenes at midnight: moonset, black smoke, and the sky full of frost.” (“Selected Tang Poems”) Another way of thinking is that it should refer to the dawn of the day, such as Yuan Yang Shihong's “Tang Yin” with fourteen clouds: “The speaker didn't understand the poet's living language; it was in the middle of the night, so he said many songs. I don't know the first sentence, “The moon falls and the sky is full of frost,” but it's only after dawn; isn't it really midnight?” Huang Sheng of the Qing Dynasty also said, “From being sleepy in the middle of the night until dawn, the bell rang too early, which stirred people's dreams.” However, the first words of the poem will be revealed, and the last one will be written in the middle of the night. Therefore, Huang Sheng also believes that this is a “flashback of the laws and regulations” and “this is already a way to retrace the circumstances of last night” (“Three Commentaries on Tang Poetry”).
In fact, both of these opinions are wrong. If it's the middle of the night, and the second sentence mentions “river maple,” how can you identify riverside tree species when the night is dark? Also, when they return home at dusk or forage for food at dusk, such as Ming Beiqiong's poem “The Wind Forest Sunsets, and the Night Clash” (“Seongnam Suki”), Song Song Yi's poem, “Shu Shu Woo Tear” (“Sending the Quiet Sea to the Sky”). Although Gu Lefu has the song “Dark Night Whisper”, it is an abnormal state of affairs; “Half of the Night” is not really a time for “Utei”. “Old Tang Shu: Music Chronicles” says, “Cries in the Dark Night”, performed by Song Linchuan and Wang Yiqing. In the 17th year of Yuan Jia, Wang Yikang moved to Yuzhang. During Yiqing, he met in Jiangzhou, and cried when they met. He was surprised by the emperor and returned to his home. He was terrified. The concubine heard a cry at night, and Kousaigaku said: 'Tomorrow should be forgiven. ' It was also the year Southern Yanzhou Assassins, and I wrote this song.” It is precisely because they don't cry at night during normal times that night cries are regarded as a sign. However, if you position “Moonfall Cries at Dawn,” there is no problem from a common sense point of view, yet when considering this poem as a whole, there are narrative contradictions: the first sentence is dawn, the second sentence goes to bed, and three or four sentences in the middle of the night. This chaotic state of narrative doesn't make sense no matter what. Although Huang Sheng thinks this is a flashback, it really doesn't fit the hectic state of mind of people who start early at dawn. When the Qing dynasty decided that the first sentence was written in plain language, he also felt that “the law is too sparse,” so he adjusted the order and rewrote the poem to read: “Guan Gu Sou Ships Outside the City, Jiang Feng is sad. The bell rang at Hanshan Temple in the middle of the night, and the moon fell and the sky was full of frost.” (“The Chronicles of a Serious Essay” vol. 9) In this kind of rewriting, the narrative logic is correct, but there is much less poetic flavor.
The author believes that the most reasonable time for “Moonfall” should be dusk. This can be proven not only by the natural phenomenon of “twilight crows in the forest,” but can also be confirmed from the second sentence. The second sentence in this book is “Jiang Feng's fishing fire”. If you restore the situation to the scene, you will discover the problem. Because of the riverside outside of Suzhou City, there are no maple trees at all! Although the vast majority of versions I've seen today, including “ZTE Miki Collection” by Tang Chinese Gao Zhongwu, are all about “Jiang Feng's Fishing Fire,” after all, the opposite of the situation itself. Fortunately, another version was found in the Song people's old books. Gong Ming of Southern Song's “The Chronicles of China and Wu”, Wu Zeng's “The Chronicles of Being Able to Zaizai”, and Hu Zi's “A Series of Fishing Hidden Tales” all wrote “Jiang Village.” On the back of the monument inscribed by Yu Yue at Hanshan Temple, there are eight lines of note: “Tang Zhang's poem “Night Stay at Fengqiao” is very popular, but the only sentence with the words 'Jiang Feng's Fishing Fire' is quite questionable. Song Gong Ming's “Chronicles of China and Wu” is a work of 'Jiangcun Fishing Fire', and Song's old book is also a treasure.” There are also seven amazing clouds: “Fortunately, “The Chronicles of Zhongwu” is there. The word “Thousand Gold” is 'Jiangcun. '” There is an inscription on the side of the monument by Jiangsu Governor Chen Yilong, which affirms Yu Yuzhi's statement: “The Chronicles of China and Wu” contains this poem 'Jiangcun Fishing Fire'. Song's old book is sufficient basis. Qu Yuantai's epic poem proves that this poem is certain now and later.” But why did Yu Yi say “the four words 'Jiang Feng fishing fire' are quite questionable” while the word “village” is “one thousand gold”? Due to the limited number of characters in the inscription, Yu was unable to expand his explanation. If you think about it carefully, you'll find that the word “village” not only dispels the many doubts at the beginning of this article, but also clears up the logical barriers of the entire poem. “Jiangcun” is a riverside village, and a “fishing fire” is a light on a fishing boat. Dusk in Cangshan is when the house lights are on. The twinkling candlelight in “Jiangcun” and the lights on the fishing boat all tell the story of family reunion, and the wandering travelers felt very lonely and desolate at this time. “My stomach breaks most after dusk”. “Jiangcun” and “fishing fire” in the poet's eyes turned into constant new sorrow. The so-called “feeling sad” means sleeping over the new sorrow ignited by “Jiangcun” and “fishing fires.” However, the rules of life at dusk, “the lights are scarce in Jiangcun” and “the fishing fires are at dusk” all position the time for the first sentence “moonsetting and dark.” At dusk, the sun set, and the moon had not yet risen. The noise of crows dazzled the guests. The chill of the autumn night gradually hit, and the lights of the family reunion between Jiangcun and the fishing boat caused the guests endless homesickness and made them sleepless at night. The bell in the middle of the night increased the customer's frustration. The story of the poem is in an orderly manner, and there is no such thing as “the law is too sparse.”
Looking at the first sentence, can “moonfall” be a poetic expression of dusk? The answer is yes. Objectively speaking, “moonset” is not an accurate concept of time. In January, the moonset time changes at any time, and there can also be a “moonset” at dusk. Yuan Sadura's “Watching the Plums at Dusk in the West Tower”: “The Moonlight Has Already Dusk in the West Tower”; Qing Qian Chengzhi's “Crossing the Rock Sha Ji”: “Moonsetting at Dusk and Flying Geese”; Qing Chen Shixiang's “Secret Meeting”: “Shangqian Moonsets at Dusk Courtyard” can all testify. Therefore, “Jiangcun” and “Moonfall Witch” jointly determined that the beginning of this poem means “dusk,” so the problems described above can be solved one by one.
The restored poetry also posed another question for us. First, why not just use “sunset”? Second, why was “Jiangcun” changed to “Jiang Feng,” and the latter almost became the only choice? First, because “Moonfall” or “Jiang Feng” is more poetic. Moonlight's unique gentleness and mystery makes it itself an excellent aesthetic value. Especially in homesick poems, the moon image has a unique function of arousing homesickness. For example, Zhang Yi's “Sending Friends”: “Let's Watch the Moon Tonight, Be a Stranger”; Bai Juyi's “Seeing the Moon Is Feeling”: “Watching the Moon Shall Cry Together; Five Places of Homesickness Are the Same Night”. Therefore, in this poem, “moonset” has a stronger emotional projection than “sunset.”
The difference between “Jiangcun” and “Jiang Feng” is that the former is a poetic scene created by the author, while the latter is a poetic scene created by readers after breaking away from the truth itself. The color and connotation of the word “maple” surpassed the word “village” and won out of historical choices. The so-called reformulation of the poetic landscape usually occurs after classical poetry has left the creative environment. In the process of dissemination in plain text form, the aesthetic practices of future readers gradually replaced the author's own intentions, intentions, and feelings, intervened in the revision of the poem, and re-created it. If the test “Jiangcun” was changed to “Jiang Feng,” this is the case. The word “maple” comes from inheriting traditional images relating to autumn thoughts. Since Qu Yuan's “Zhanzhanjiang River has maple in the water, it hurts the heart of spring for a thousand miles”. Combining “maple” and “river”, the expressive method of grafting sorrow has continued to be inherited and carried forward. For example, “Qingfeng is invincible in Pudong” in Zhang Ruoxu's “Spring River, Moonlight Night”, brings beauty and sorrow to the extreme. Furthermore, maple leaves and river water both have hints about time. The former is a biological characteristic of freely turning green to red, and the latter derives from the deep-seated example of “the dead”; on the other hand, “maple” is also often associated with “frost”, such as Tang Liu Changqing: “Shaking Twilight, Green Maple Frost Leaves Thin” (“Yu Gan Hotel”), Song Zhang Yi: “Frostleaf Dance Danfeng” (“Chaozhongcuo · Fisherman's Father”). Of course, this is also due to the fact that the maple leaves turn red just when the frost falls in autumn. The feeling fluttered over my face. The word “maple” echoes “frost” before and “river” at the back. It touches on the two natural characteristics of the seasons and the ease with which time passes. It gives a strong hint that people are sad and fall when they are sad. As a result, it opens up a common sense of common sense between people's hearts, and sorrow can be superimposed and output.
This brings us back to the original point: the poem describes an accidental docking experience. The poet experienced loneliness in the midst of the hustle and bustle of a riverside village. This explanation rationalises the narrative logic and is also true to the author's emotions. However, not everyone can get the poet's sad experience. For readers, the beauty of watching Qingfeng gradually turn red is easier to capture, and the sorrow of “following four o'clock to sigh” is even easier to touch. If the original poetic landscape comes from the author's personal life experience, then the recreated poetic landscape is the recipient's common life experience. Traditional delivery and reading memory together suppress the image template of “Jiang Feng,” blurring the geographical environment of “Jiangcun,” and ignoring the timing of “moonset,” forming a common feeling of not having to be subject to rationality, which is enough to surpass everything else and become a judgment that affects the fun of appreciation.
