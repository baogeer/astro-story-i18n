 Eid al-Adha is a grand festival shared by ethnic minorities such as Huai, Uighurs, Kazakh, Uzbek, Tajik, Tatar, Kirghiz, Salah, Dongxiang, and Baoan.
“Qurban” is called Al-Qurban, or Al-Azuha in Arabic. “Elders” means holiday. “Gurubhan” and “Azuha” both mean “sacrifice” and “devotion,” so this festival is generally called “Sacrifice Festival” or “Eid al-Adha.”
Eid al-Adha is a religious holiday on December 10 of the Islamic calendar. The calendar used in Islam is divided into the solar calendar and the lunar calendar. The solar calendar is used for farming. It has 365 or 366 days in a year, which is basically the same as the Gregorian calendar. The lunar calendar is used for teaching. There are 354 or 355 days in a year, which is a total difference of about ten days from the reincarnation of the four seasons. The second day of the Prophet Mohammed's entry into Medina (July 16, 622 AD) was New Year's Day, the first year of the Islamic calendar. Due to differences between the dates of Eulban and Muzi and the Gregorian calendar, these holidays are sometimes winter and sometimes summer. The seasons are different, and the colors of the festivals are different, which makes the New Year extremely colorful.
Eid al-Adha originated from an Islamic story: to test the loyalty of the Prophet Ibrahim, God gave him a dream at night, asking Ibrahim to slaughter his own son to sacrifice. Ibrahim did so without hesitation. When he wanted to break his son Ismaili's throat with a knife, God sent a messenger to replace Ismaili with a black-headed sheep. Because of this origin, every Muslim family has to slaughter at least one sheep during the Eid al-Adha. Some also slaughter cows, camels, and horses (Saks and Kirghiz), and those who are too poor have to slaughter a chicken. However, due to various conditions, today's urban Muslims have no choice but to buy slaughtered beef and lamb at the market.
As the Eulban Festival approaches, housewives in every household get busy. They have to make lots of deep-fried dumplings and all kinds of exquisite dim sum to prepare enough food for family, friends, and visitors from afar during the holiday season. The grand festival is also a big display and competition for housewives' craftsmanship and family manners.
During the Eulban Festival, everyone visits each other to celebrate the festival. Every time they arrive at the house, the host will bring a large plate of stewed lamb to the guests, and no matter how full the guests are, they also have to try the owner's lamb. When family and friends meet, people of all ethnic groups in Xinjiang play the piano, sing, and dance. There is a happy scene everywhere.
After the big ceremony, every household should go to the graveyard to pray and remember and bless their dead loved ones.
The early morning prayer of Eulban is the largest in the year. All adult men have to go to the local temple to attend the ceremony. The scene is spectacular. The most famous is the big gathering in front of the Atigarh Mosque in Kashgar. After the ceremony, musicians climbed to the top of the Atigarh mosque, knocked on the nagra (iron shell drum), and blew the Sunai (suona), and the men in the square in front of the temple danced a passionate shaman dance.
Uighur Eulban Festival customs
Qurban is an Arabic transliteration meaning “sacrifice” or “blood sacrifice”.
Before Eulban, people have to make all kinds of preparations, especially to buy livestock in advance as “sacrifices.” On the morning of the festival, people bathe their whole body “thoroughly” and then dress up at the mosque to attend the ceremony. After the liturgy, the first thing people do when they go home is kill the blood sacrifice. As to whether to slaughter cows to kill sheep or chickens to kill geese, this is determined by the financial strength of each company. Normally, men only start praying for the festival after slaughtering the livestock sacrificed by blood and stewing large chunks of bone-in meat in a pot, while women stayed at home to stew meat and arrange holiday food, burn tea, etc., to prepare to welcome the guests. On the first day of the festival, the first is to pray and express condolences to the townspeople who have recently experienced family disasters, such as funerals, etc., the second is to pray for the elders of both husband and wife, and third, to pray for close neighbors and elders. In this kind of ceremonial festival prayer, apart from praying for the elderly, the husband and wife go together, other festival worship activities are often held in groups of three or five, and men and women are held separately. In Uyghur folk customs, it is generally not allowed to mix men and women.
After that, it was a festival of prayer between family and friends of the same generation. In addition to congratulating and greeting each other, everyone also had to share food and drink, and have fun together. Uyghur folk worship is an important part of Uighurs strengthening social ties and adhering to the rules of respectful communication.
Hui Eid al-Adha customs
“Qurban”, Arabic transliteration of “Er? “Gurubon”, “Eard? “Azuha” means “sacrifice” and “devotion,” so it is also known as “Eid al-Adha,” “Sacrifice Festival,” “Chung Hao Festival,” and “German Festival.” The Hui people in the mountainous regions of southern Ningxia are called “Dardes,” and the Hui people in Yunnan and other places are called “Little Eid.” It is one of the three major Islamic festivals, and is generally held 70 days after Eid al-Fitr. This holiday falls within the scope of the Muslim Hajj ceremony. Islam stipulates that every year in early December, Muslims go on a pilgrimage to Mecca. On the last day of the pilgrimage, festivities begin.
Why did the Hui celebrate Eid al-Adha? Legend has it that Ibrahim, one of the ancient prophets of Islam, tested his devotion to Allah by dreaming of Allah killing his son Ismaili at night.
Ibrahim sharpened his knife to a brilliant shine and was very sharp. When Ismaili lay down on his side, he placed the knife on his son's throat. At this point, he was sad and crying, like a stream of tears. The first cut left only a white mark on my son's neck, and the second one scratched the skin a little bit.
Ismaili said, “My father, turn me over and let me lie on my back, so you can make up your mind and obey God's command.”
Ibrahim listened to his son's advice, turned him over, and when he removed his knife and slaughtered hard, God was moved and sent the immortal Gibbeyeil to carry a black-headed sheep as a sacrifice in place of Ismaili. At this point, Ibrahim picked up a knife and slashed the sheep's throat, and the sheep fell down. Since then, Muhammad designated December 10 of the Islamic calendar as Eid al-Adha, which is the origin of the legendary “Qurban.”
The Eulban Festival for the Hui people in our country is a traditional national festival that has evolved over a long period of time based on the historical content of this religion: it has many overlapping contents, which is already very different from a simple religious holiday, and the main content is no longer religious content. Today, the Hui people in our country, whether religious or not, celebrate the Eulban Festival according to the different customs of various regions. The scale is generally large. It is mostly carried out in units of villages, townships, towns, lanes, etc. In some places, it takes three days, and in others, it only takes one day.
Clean indoor and outdoor hygiene before the holiday season. The family courtyard, streets and alleys were all cleaned up, and things were piled up in an orderly manner. Every household has to fry incense, rice balls, flowers, etc. before the holiday season. The kids dressed up in holiday costumes and had fun running around.
At dawn, wash your body, burn incense, and put on clean clothes to attend the ceremony at the mosque.
There is a popular saying among the Hui people: “If you can't go back month by month, you have to go back year by year.” The meaning of this statement is that no matter how busy you are, you should attend this annual ceremony and celebration. Even if you don't understand the customs of the Hui people, then you have to follow the customs.
The ceremonies of Eid al-Fitr are as solemn as Eid. Everyone gathered together, and the imams led all the returning people to the west to bow and bow and pray. If it were held in a big town, it would be full of people, not too much chaos. At the ceremony, everyone should remember what mistakes they have made and what crimes they have committed during the year, and the imam will preach “Waltz,” that is, the teachings and matters that need to be observed by everyone. Finally, everyone greets each other “with their eyes.”
After the ceremony, a grand ceremony will also be held. This is a festival where, in addition to frying incense, rice dumplings, and ceremonies, cows, sheep, and camels are also slaughtered. Generally, economic conditions are good. Each person must slaughter one sheep, and seven people slaughter a cow or a camel together. There are also many precautions when slaughtering. Lambs under two years old and calves and camels under the age of three are not allowed to be slaughtered; animals that are blind, have weak legs, lack ears, or few tails; they must choose slaughter that is strong and fit. Slaughtered meat should be divided into three parts: one for self-food, one for family, friends and neighbors, and one for charity to help the poor.
The practice of Muslims in our country eating meat during Ramadan and slaughtering during the Eid al-Adha was surprising to ordinary Chinese people in the past, and the Tang people Du Huan, who had a correct understanding of Islamic teachings, specifically pointed out these two events in “The Book of Sutras,” saying “fasting for meat and using slaughter as merit.”
After the slaughter ceremony was held, the household was bustling again. While cooking meat, the elderly ordered the children: after eating the meat, the bones should not be thrown to the dog to chew; they should be covered with loess. This is a special point on Eid al-Adha. Once the meat is cooked, cut it into slices and mix it into parts; the lamb must be stewed into a dish. Afterwards, they visited and asked friends, gifted oil incense and food, and visited each other to celebrate the festival. Some also want to invite imams to their homes to read the sutras and eat incense. At the same time, they also need to visit graves and remember their ancestors.
There are many forms of this celebration of the festival, and there are differences and differences from place to place. In addition to attending ceremonies and visiting family and friends, some places also organize various cultural and sports activities. People in the Xinjiang region enjoy organizing all kinds of amusement activities during the Eulban Festival. They are very happy and very lively. In rural Xinjiang, events such as sheep picking and singing duets are also generally held. When singing duets, they experience the scene and improvise. They especially love to sing songs such as “Flowers and Teenagers.” Hui youth from Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai and other places sing “flowers” at festivals.
Islamic associations around the world usually hold receptions during the Eulban Festival, inviting famous people from the Hui community, imams, and others. China's people's government also clearly stipulates that during the Eulban holiday, people will be given a day off, and that urban households will be given extra supplies of lamb, sesame oil, premium flour, etc., to give special care. Party and government leaders at all levels also participated in holiday festivities with the Hui people, shared their friendship, congratulated the Hui people on their happiness and happiness, enhanced national unity, and added new content and meaning to traditional Hui festivals.
