 Li Yuan, a poet from the Tang dynasty, wrote quite a few good poems. The poem “A Long Day Only Eliminates a Game of Chess” spread to the palace, and even the emperor knew it.
Li Yuan became a junior high school student. Prime Minister Ling Hu discovered his talents and invited Tang Xuanzong to send him to write a history of assassins in Hangzhou. Xuan Zong listened and was not happy to say, “Isn't that the phrase 'only one game of chess in a long day' just what Li Yuan said? Can this kind of person who only knows how to play chess day and night manage a good state for us?”
Ling Hu thought to herself: God! They write poems; you can't take them seriously! But I didn't understand the explanation to the emperor. After talking too much, the emperor didn't like to listen. As a result, he changed his strategy and said to the emperor, “The Holy Lord saw it very well. However, Li Yuan is a talented and very clean person. He spent his time playing chess only because he usually had nothing to do. If you send him to govern Hangzhou, wouldn't it be OK if the decree says he doesn't play chess?”
Having said this, the emperor was happy and said, “Then follow your lead and send him to Hangzhou to try it out. It's just that he's not allowed to play chess anymore.”
Li Yuan certainly became a Hangzhou assassin, and managed very well. He is also really clean. For example, no matter how honored the guest or official, when he arrived in Hangzhou, he only gave two ducks as gifts; he never gave gold or silver jewelry or silk garland. Of course, his own life is also very simple.
