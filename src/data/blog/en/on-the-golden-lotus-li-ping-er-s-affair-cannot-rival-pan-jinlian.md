 We say Pan Jinlian's pursuit of individual lust is somewhat accompanied by a sense of subjectivity awakening. This is because she clearly felt her presence, felt that she had “some colors and ingenuity,” clearly pursues what she loves, and strives to escape the fate of a woman “100 Years of Bittersweet by Others” (Part 12). Moreover, in her pursuit of self-worth, she was so bold and proactive. She disregarded social etiquette, didn't believe in any preaching, only believed in herself and tried to control her own destiny. Unlike this, although Li Binger and Pang Chunmei also have their own personalities in the process of seeking the satisfaction of lust, they are also notorious as “prostitutes,” and therefore are notorious as “prostitutes,” but this is mainly due to the impulse of primitive lust and lack a sense of subjectivity, so they are different from Pan Jinlian.
Please take a look at Li Bing.
Previously, as Liang Zhongshu's concubine, she “only lived in the study room outside” because she was afraid of her wife.
She later nominally married Hua Zixu, but “slept in another room with her husband” (17th time). Her uncle Hua Eunuch went to Guangnan and actually took her “to stay for half a year” (10th time). It can be seen that she is actually a plaything for eunuchs.
I met Jiang Zhushan, who was “flirtatious and deceitful,” only to randomly ask him to “have no reason to follow when it comes to family insurance” (Part 17), and had no own opinions. As a result, Jiang Zhushan was easily fooled by Jiang Zhushan, while Jiang Zhushan was an impotent “person who doesn't like to eat Wax Gun, King Eight”, and “often the officers don't say it” (19th).
Through these three personal stories, the novel clearly tells people that Li Binger from “Good Wind Moon” has been in a state of sexual hunger for a long time; when she is seeking a solution to the “unsuccessful intention” of “getting stuck in the middle,” she really had no clear personal opinion (Part 17). Later, the main reason she fell for Ximen Qing was that she learned about his “storm and rain,” and deeply felt: “Whoever seems to be a slave like an enemy means like medicine for a medical slave.” (#19) Only Ximen Qing really satisfied her sexual desires. Therefore, although Li Bing'er also “cheated,” she only stayed at the level of her original instincts, lacked self-awareness and clear pursuit, and was at a certain distance from Pan Jinlian.
Moreover, Li Binger is not as lawless as Pan Jinlian. Her individual consciousness disregards social norms. Even if she personally poisoned her husband, in a blink of an eye, she was rushed to nothing by new pursuit and joy, leaving no shadow on her conscience. But Li Bing'er was different. Her sense of individuality is a sense of social norms, and her subjectivity completely melts into the object. In her mind, she still regards being unfaithful to a husband she doesn't like, or even just a formal one, as a serious sin. Her anger was futile, and although it gave her Ximen Qing, it also made her feel a heavy sense of guilt. Her death was actually crushed by social morality. Of course, when the novel was written, she died of illness. The cause of her illness, according to a doctor's diagnosis, was that “my heart flushes up my blood vessels, and then I'm worried. When the qi fights with the blood, the blood falls like a collapse” (Episode 61). In particular, after the death of Brother Guan, he was extremely sad, and he was bullied from time to time by Pan Jinlian. “This dark atmosphere, combined with worries and worries, gradually became confused, and my dreams were turned upside down, and I had fewer meals every day” (Part 60). However, her condition worsened and her death apparently had a close relationship with her sense of guilt that she was unable to overcome. Back then, when she conspired with Ximen Qing to get rid of her wealth, she decided to “not be alone with men”. She did everything quite well, leaving Hanako to death. At this point, she was engrossed in the excitement of breaking free from a kind of restraint. Furthermore, after all, the death of Hua Zixu was different from the death of Taketaro; she could not bear any legal responsibility. However, time has changed, especially when I arrived at Ximenqing's family. Looking back on the past, I inevitably feel that I owe my past husband, and a sense of guilt rises. Her deep inner distress was portrayed in detail by the author, who is good at describing mentality through dreams. When she was seriously ill, she was in a trance and had three dreams of Hanako coming to death. This was clearly a reflection of her sense of guilt. In particular, in the 59th episode, it was written:
Now that I'm lying in bed, I can't sleep. I had a dream where Hanazixu came from the front door and was wearing a white coat. She was just at her best. Seeing Li Bing Er, she scolded loudly, “Prostitute, how can you resist stealing my property and Ximen Qing? Summon, I'll sue you now too!” Li Bing'er grabbed his sleeve with one hand. Yang Yi said, “Good brother, if you forgive me, then that's it.” Hanako took a moment and was surprised to give up, but it was Nanko's dream. When I woke up, I was holding back the sleeve of Brother Guan's shirt. He whispered a few times and said, “That's weird, that's weird!” As soon as I heard more drums, I was playing three or three points. Li Bing was so sweaty all over her body that her hair stood up.
The next day, Ximen Qing came into the house and told Ximen Qing about his dream. Ximen Qingdao: “I know he died there! This is your old dream. Just keep your mind right and stop worrying about him! ...”
Dreams are a reflection of her subconscious mind. In fact, as soon as she married to Simon's family, she was humiliated, and there were twists and turns in her thoughts. Ximen Qing scolded her: “Prostitute, since you lost your heart, why did you hang yourself at my house! ...” (#19) The words “lose heart” just hit her pain point. Later, Brother Guan became ill and died. She even more suspected that it was retaliation, so she often dreamed that Hanako had come to take her child. Ximen Qing was quite sober about this, telling her that it was an “old dream”. Every time Li Bing-er told him about her dream, she was always advised to “keep her mind right and stop questioning him” (Episode 62), which frees her from feeling guilty about Huazixu. However, heavy moral pressure, along with a sense of karma, always left her breathless. Until she was about to die, she still had a dream, “That guy led two people and messed up with me again and said, 'You asked the mage to send me; I've already told you to be in the dark; I won't let you! ' They hate it, and they'll take me tomorrow too.” (#62) The phrase “dismissal in the dark” also reflects that she is at a loss in her heart and is aware that her sins are serious. She asked Wang Gu Zi to “recite more of the Blood Bon Sutra” after her death to confess my sins. Deeply lamented, “I still don't know how many crimes I've fallen into!” (#62) As a result, it can be said that Li Binger's death ultimately came to an end not because of the law, but because of the heavy pressure of morality. Social morality actually accommodates her personal will, and she still lacks a conscious sense of subjectivity.
As for Pang Chunmei, she is also a woman who lacks a sense of subjectivity. Ximen Qing intended to “adopt” her. Under Pan Jinlian's arrangement, she was “adopted” without saying a word (10th); later, Pan Jinlian also called her to “sleep with your brother-in-law,” and she took off her Xiang dress without saying a word, making Chen Economy “take advantage” (episode 82). After writing Pan Jinlian and Chen Economy in the 85th episode, “Two Good Careers” and being caught by Wu Yueniang, she was bored and unhappy, so she advised Pan Jinlian to say:
Ma'am, your elderly family also have less to worry about. ... Life is alive, day by day.
This is her goal in life. Chong Zhen originally criticized this: “He later died of greed, but it has already come to an end.” Zhang Zhupo criticized it: “It's the result of Chunmei.” This all highlights Chunmei's greedy nature. At the time, she “saw that the two dogs below her rank were in a relationship and said, 'Animals still have this much fun, let alone humans? '” Zhang Zhupo criticized it here: “You can't do it for a dog.” What she is looking for is the satisfaction of this animal instinct. Later, in the guard house, it was because her husband “took care of the military situation day by day, did the affairs of the imperial court, and worked hard” and “didn't get involved for a long time”, causing her to “sleep alone and get horny”, and finally “died of excessive lust” with Zhou Yi and “died on Zhou Yi” (100th time). She was a true “prostitute” because of lust, and in the end, she “died of fornication”. Humans are subjects, and their opposite objects are originally two: one is nature; the other is society. People have an independent sense of subjectivity; they must not only be bound by nature, but also by society. If Li Binger's consciousness is still unable to separate itself from the social norms of the time and dissolve individuals in society, then I'm afraid Pang Chunmei's “prostitution” could not even control nature. She is just a burial item that human natural instincts need. Confucianism's emphasis on “if you want to go, you can't go,” and “if you are happy and organized, it is a peaceful life test,” shows the subjectivity that humans can surpass nature and conquer nature when dealing with lust issues. Unfortunately, in most cases, Pang Chunmei's lust doesn't even have the slightest wave of affection or love; it is simply an instinctive impulse. In the end, this natural instinct eats away at the individual life of a young woman, so they have nothing to do with the awakening of subjectivity. Under the influence of social morality and the laws of nature, their primitive carnal impulses can only push them to the stage of tragedy in their lives. When they quickly died down, I'm afraid they didn't realize what their individual value was.
