 Famous writers from the Song Dynasty often included bureaucrats, scholars, and writers. Among them were Fan Zhongquan, Ouyang Xiu, Song Qi, Yan Shu, Wang Anshi, Su Yi, Fan Chengda, Lu You, etc. If you want to name one of the most representative figures, you would undoubtedly count Ouyang Xiu.
Ouyang Xiu was an outstanding politician, writer, and scholar in the Northern Song Dynasty. Politically speaking, many of his major political activities in the early Northern Song period were closely linked to him; from a literary point of view, he was pioneering in various genres such as poetry; academically speaking, he made outstanding contributions in many fields such as economics, history, metaphysics, and catalogues. As the representative doctor of the Northern Song Dynasty, Ouyang Xiu's life is multi-faceted and diverse. This article mainly introduces Ouyang Xiu from four aspects: political life, academic life, daily life, and literary life.

Ouyang Xiu lived in an eventful autumn in the Northern Song Dynasty. His political life has always been turbulent. From entering the military to his retirement, he experienced many migrations from the central government to the local area.
The characteristics of Ouyang Xiu's academic examination can be summed up in four words, which is “the first in three tests.” First, the rural test was defeated. The first time he took the academic examination was in the first year of Tiansheng (1023) to take the local township exam. One of the test questions is “The False Argument of Zuo's Loss”, which requires candidates to demonstrate the absurdity and untruth of “The Legend of Zuo”. In the 5th year of Tiansheng (1027), Ouyang Xiu took the liturgy exam and was ousted again. However, Ouyang Xiu had a great influence at this time. When he left Beijing and returned to his hometown, many friends saw him off. In the 8th year of Tiansheng (1030), Ouyang Xiu went to Beijing again to take the liturgical department exam. In addition to “Three Trials,” what is commendable is that Ouyang Xiu presided over Jiayou's devotion for the 2nd year (1057), suppressing the dangerous and bizarre literature of “Taixue Ti,” and has since changed the style of writing. Since then, Master Ouyang began his career. High achievements in all areas
Literary, historical, etc., achievements especially
In Chinese academic history, Ouyang Xiu has great achievements in various fields such as literature, economics, history, metaphysics, bibliography, and genealogy.
The Song Dynasty was an era of advanced economics. Ouyang Xiu was in it. His important contributions and influence have always occupied an important position in the history of Chinese economics. His outstanding performances were “Yi” studies and “poetry” studies. Ouyang Xiu's masterpiece of “Yi” studies is “An Easy Boy's Question”. The discussion uses “I Ching” as correct, and doubts “The Discourse.” In the history of “Yi” studies, Ouyang Xiu was the first person in the “Discipline”, and his remarks were quite complete, starting a culture of doubt in the ancient Song Dynasty that began with doubts about the scriptures. Ouyang Xiu's masterpiece of “Poetry” is the fifteenth volume of “The Essence of Poetry,” which does not record the scriptures; they are all essays. This work is completely different from the previous study of ignoring the verses of the “Shijing”. The Kaisong people spoke at the end of “Poetry” with their own intentions. After that, they talked about sunrise, and the old meaning gradually faded; in fact, Ouyang Xiu opened up a new situation in the study of “Shijing”.
Ouyang Xiu's famous works in history are “The New Tang Book” and “The History of the New Five Generations.” “The New Book of Tang” is a work written by Ouyang Xiu in collaboration with Song Qi, Fan Zhen, and Lu Xiaqing. For the first time in terms of style, he wrote “Military History” and “Election Chronicles,” which systematically discussed military systems such as the Tang Dynasty military system and discipline inspection system. This is a major pioneer in China's orthodox genre history books, and was carried over from “Song History” and others. When the book was first published, it was reviewed by Song In-jong.
