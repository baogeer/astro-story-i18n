 Peony messenger
Peony culture is an integral part of ancient Chinese culture. Chinese culture has a long history. Chinese peonies were also introduced to Japan along with Chinese culture as early as the 8th century AD. It was introduced to England in the 18th century and planted in Kew Gardens. Later, more than 30 varieties were introduced from Shanghai. Later, French missionaries brought yellow peonies and purple peonies wild in our country's Yunnan to France one after another. By 1936, Britain had brought back the wild yellow peony from our country's Tibet. The United States also obtained purple-spotted peonies from Zhuoni in southern Gansu in the 19th century. In this way, England, France, the United States, and Japan used these wild species native to China to carry out complex interspecific interspecific crossbreeding, and have successively cultivated peony varieties in their respective countries. Currently, there are more than 400 types in the US, and more than 200 in France and Japan. These varieties or hybrid series all have their roots in China. Some people often say, “In the life of modern moonflowers, half of the blood of Chinese moonflowers is flowing,” and we can confidently say that in the bodies of all peonies in the world today, all blood of Chinese peonies flows.
Peony genealogical documentary
Peony is a famous traditional Chinese flower. There are no fewer than 20 or 30 kinds of monographs and biographies of writers throughout the ages. Mythological stories about peonies are even more well-known. Among them are “Luoyang Peony Chronicles” by Ouyang Xiu in the Northern Song Dynasty, “Luoyang's Book of Flowers and Trees” by Zhou Shihou; “Tianpeng Peony Spectrum” by Lu Yu during the Southern Song Dynasty; “Qunfang Spectrum” by Wang Xiangjin in the Ming Dynasty, “History of Peonies in Huzhou” by Xue Fengxiang; in the Qing Dynasty, there are also “Flower Mirror” by Chen Zi, “Guang Qun Fang Pu” by Wang Hao, etc., all of which are the most glorious works in the history of peonies in China. The cultural wealth of, which has accumulated rich experience in breeding, cultivation, and application; it also records the origin, development, and manpower of peonies in China Beliefs and facts about “triumphant success.” Examples of cultivating new varieties were also quoted by the famous British biologist Darwin in his book “The Origin of Species.”
Peony poetry, calligraphy, and painting
There are many moving things in nature. Among them, “flower” is the main one. What's more, peonies are also “fragrant and beautiful”, so poets, painters, and artists of all ages have loved peonies, and poems, calligraphy, and paintings on the subject of peonies are widely popular. Products named after peonies, trademarks, and peony decorative patterns on various household items are endless. In history, there are the most works of singing peonies, painting peonies, and singing peonies. In “400 Peony Poems from the Past”, edited by Yang Maolan, there are 125 songs from the 5th Tang Dynasty, 167 from the Song Dynasty, 27 from the Jin Yuan Dynasty, 37 from the Ming Dynasty, and 67 from the Qing Dynasty, for a total of 419, with a total of 203 authors. The content reflects the customs and hobbies that peonies aroused in society at the time in every way. Painters with peonies as the subject are recorded by Yang Zihua painting peonies as far back as the Northern and Southern Dynasties (550-577). By the Tang Dynasty, there were many peony painters. According to Dong Shou's “Hirochuan Paintings”, the Tang Dynasty painter “Bian Luan painted peonies, a wonderful business, without losing its vitality.” “Peony Map”, painted by Xu Xi during the 5th Tang Dynasty (923-936), has a beautiful pen, and is very interesting, and is absolutely amazing. In the Ming Dynasty, Xu Wei used the splash ink method to paint peonies. It was an innovation. Yun Shouping in the Qing dynasty painted peonies, which were elegant and self-contained. In modern times, the famous painter Wang Xuetao painted a large number of peony paintings. The images were varied and full of life. The peony paintings drawn by master painter Qi Baishi are simple with a pen. They usually talk about a few strokes, but they are full of life. Folk hobbies of peonies
China is a multi-ethnic country. Although people of all ethnic groups have different customs, their love for peonies is the same. Growing peonies, enjoying peonies, singing peonies, and peony flower parties have become their own customs and folk customs. For example, Tujia in Hubei and Guizhou, the Bai people in Yunnan, and ethnic minorities in Gansu, Qinghai, and Ningxia all grow different numbers of peonies in their courtyards, as few as three or five, and as many as hundreds, to symbolize good fortune and wealth. Other peonies, such as Langshan Mountain in Longxi, Gansu; peony watching in Dali, Yunnan; peony, the worship god Peony in Chaohu Lake in Anhui; “Flowers” singing peonies in Linxia Hui in Gansu; and peony fairs and lanterns held in Luoyang, Heze, Beijing, Hangzhou, Sichuan, and Uranium in Anhui, all have rich folk characteristics. “Flowers” is a popular folk song in Qinghai and Ningxia. In traditional folk lyrics, peonies are the embodiment of beauty and a symbol of love. People call the season when people gather to sing “Flower Party” “Peony Moon” or “Wave Peony Club.” The “Flower” song includes “White Peony Order”, “Green Peony Order”, “Ten Peonies, Nine Blossoms”, etc. Many of the lyrics refer to people as flowers. The language is sincere and vivid and moving. For example, “In April, on April 8, peonies are blooming under the thorns. I want to take care of her without picking them, and I want to get rid of the thorns. I woke up early and was afraid of being killed by frost; I was afraid of sheep scattering at night. Buy a basket basket and watch her blossom or not.” Others, such as the Henan folk song “Flower Basket”, and the contemporary film “Red Peony” interlude “Peony Song” all praise peonies enthusiastically. Various parts of our country also attach great importance to the use of peony patterns and paintings to decorate buildings, temples, places of interest and residences. For example, the “wood carvings” of Dali, Yunnan, the “stone carvings” of Huian, the “brick carvings” of Linxia in Gansu, and the “porcelain carvings” in Jingdezhen, Jiangxi, all exude the fragrance of peonies. They are both practical and appreciated, and have high artistic value.
