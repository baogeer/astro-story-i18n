 Qi Jiguang (1528-1588) was a famous anti-Japanese general in the Ming Dynasty, a national hero, and an outstanding military figure. The character Yuanjing, known as Nantang, and later known as Meng Zhu, is a native of Penglai, Shandong (originally from Dingyuan, Anhui, born in Jining, Shandong). Born in General, he aspired to defend the country and the people since childhood. He once wrote down the famous phrase “It is not my intention to conquer the emperor; I hope the sea is peaceful.” At the age of 17, he took the position of his father and became the commander of the Dengzhou Guard. At the age of 25, he was promoted to the position of command of the Ministry and responsible for defending the entire coast of Shandong Province against Japanese invaders, and achieved impressive results.
In the 34th year of Jiajing (1555), Qi Jiguang was transferred to Shishu, Rendu, Zhejiang, and soon became a general, guarding the three provinces of Ningbo, Shaoxing, and Taizhou. In September of the 35th year of Jiajing (1556), more than 800 Japanese pirates invaded the Longshan site. He led an army to counter the attack. There were few rounds to catch the enemy. The Ming army fought in a cowardly battle and retreated one after another. In a critical moment, Qi Jiguang leaped onto a high rock and fired three arrows in a row to knock down 3 Japanese pirate leaders. The Japanese pirates saw the situation and fled in a hurry.
In the 38th year of Jiajing (1559), Qi Jiguang recruited more than 3,000 brave farmers and brave miners from the mountains of Yiwu, Zhejiang, and formed a new type of army using the four-level formation method of battalion, officer, guard, and team. A team is a basic combat unit. Team members are equipped with different weapons according to their age and physique. When fighting, all team members use their strengths, cooperate in battle, have both offense and defense, and are flexible in advance and retreat. This kind of battle team can combine abilities, and is known as the “Mandarin Duck Battle.” After Qi Jiguang's strict training, this new army soon became a military powerhouse, known as the “Qi Jiajun.”
In the 40th year of Jiajing (1561), Japanese pirates invaded Taizhou on a massive scale. Qi Jiguang led the department in nine battles and nine to achieve the world-famous Taizhou triumph. The Japanese pirates were terrified and gave Qi Jiguang the name “Qi Laohu.” The following summer, he led Qi Jiajun south to Fujian to destroy the three major nests of Japanese pirates in Hengyu, Ushitian, and Lindun. In the 42nd year of Jiajing (1563), he achieved great success in Pinghai with Yu Dayou of Fujian General Army and Liu Xian of Guangdong General Army. The following year, he was promoted to general officer to guard the entire province of Fujian and the two capitals of Jinhua and Wenzhou in Zhejiang. In November of this year, 20,000 Japanese pirates besieged Xianyou. Qi Jiguang “used oligarchy to break the siege in one call; surprisingly, they won the complete victory in three battles.” Since then, Qi Jiajun has invigorated China's maritime frontiers, and Japanese pirates have fled, and the Japanese people who have been harmed for a long time have finally been brought to an end.
In May of the 2nd year of Longqing (1568), Qi Jiguang was appointed governor of the capital, Tongzhi, Prime Minister of the military service in the three towns of Jizhou, Changping, and Baoding, leading troops to guard the northern border. After he arrived in office, he created infantry battalions, cavalry battalions, vehicle battalions, and heavy battalions based on the combat characteristics of Mongolian cavalry, and made each battalion a synthetic army capable of cooperative combat under unified command. At the same time, the original side walls were raised and thickened on the basis of the old Great Wall, heavy walls were built at important locations, and hollow enemy platforms were created along the Great Wall, thus truly building a strong and unbreakable line of defense. He spent 16 years in the northern Imperial Villa, “while preparing for repairs, Jimen is safe”.
In the 11th year of Wanli (1583), due to being ostracized by the DPRK and Chinese rulers, Qi Jiguang was transferred to serve as a town governor in Guangdong, where Japanese suffering had already leveled down. He was depressed and unmotivated. After three years, he returned to his hometown and returned to Penglai, Shandong. He died in his hometown of Penglai in the 16th year of Wanli (1588).
Qi Jiguang spent more than 40 years in the war, “in 360 days a year, mostly on the same day as Hengge”, either wiping out Japanese pirates along the southeast coast and clearing the sea; or training troops in the north to keep Jimen safe. He can be called a generation of famous patriotic generals. He is intelligent and brave, resourceful, and well-trained. Commanding Qi Jiajun “spirited forces and destroyed major invaders over and over,” there was even a rare case where thousands of enemies were annihilated, yet no one “Qi Jiajun” was killed in action. It is known as China's “one of the few generals who have won a victory since ancient times.” Not only did he excel in combat, but he also made many achievements in military theory. He is the author of two military books, “The New Book of Chronicles and the Practice of Military Training,” which were admired by later generations of soldiers, and “those who talk about the military follow suit.”
