 Source: China Newsweek Author: Wu Xianning
Qiu Jin (1875—1907)
Qiu Jin's Last Three Years: From Women's Liberation to National Liberation
Qiu Jin's idea of “equal rights for men and women” comes from the concept of “gifted human rights, freedom and equality.” She believes that women not only have the same equal rights as men, but can also be “pioneers of civilization” and put forward a profound proposition combining women's liberation with national liberation
Reporter of this magazine/By Pang Qinghui/Wang Yifan
In the early morning of July 15, 1907, the sky had not yet brightened; it was a dark hour before dawn. The prison guard's wife opened a single cell in Shanyin County Prison in Zhejiang Province, and a large group of soldiers armed with guns poured in. An emaciated Qiu Jin tidied up her messy hair. The police put heavy iron shackles on her and tied her hands behind her back.
A large number of soldiers carrying torches surrounded Qiu Jin and walked to the entrance of Guxuanting, a kilometer away. The flames illuminated the eerie execution site. Qiu Jin, who was kidnapped by Five Flowers, was executed at the age of 31.
There were only 3 days between his arrest on the afternoon of July 13 and his execution in the early morning of July 15. After Qiu Jin was murdered, public opinion gathered to criticize the Zhejiang government's various barbaric acts in handling the Qiu Jin case. Attacks by public opinion at various levels, such as “a society governed by the rule of law demands the enactment of a constitution” and “criticizing local justice in the spirit of constitutional government,” left the Qing government in a state of panic and almost no power to fight back.
Only 4 years later, artillery fire from the 1911 Revolution rang all over the city of Wuchang.
Join an alliance
Qiu Jin's revolutionary path was different from many male revolutionaries. “My initial inspiration came from dissatisfaction with my marriage.” Wu Xianning, a member of the Standing Committee of the China 1911 Revolution Research Association and author of “The Biography of Qiu Jin,” told China News Weekly.
A trip to Beijing in 1903 was a watershed moment in Qiu Jin's life.
That year, her husband Wang Tingjun used money to donate a small Beijing official who is the head of the household and took Qiu Jin to work in Beijing. Their neighbor is Wu Zhiying, a civilized and progressive woman. Wu Zhiying is the niece of Wu Rulun, a famous “Tongcheng School” scholar in the late Qing Dynasty. Her husband Lian Quan is also an open-minded figure. She participated in the “Book on the Bus” initiated by Kang Youwei. Qiu Jin and Wu Zhiying made a mutual agreement and tied the knot with Jin Lan.
Through Wu Zhiying, Qiu Jin often saw some new books and newspapers published at the time, and came into contact with some new ideas. After the signing of the “Xinchou Treaty”, the land of Shenzhou was full of danger. After reading Chen Tianhua's “Warning Bell” and “Looking Back”, Qiu Jin was deeply moved. She called Chen Tianhua her “enlightenment and enlightenment.” She is poetic and inspirational: “Men are not fit, but their heart is better than that of boys.” After that, Qiu Jin wore men's clothing, saying she would never wear women's clothing from the Qing dynasty again. Men's clothing became Qiu Jin's signature garment, and she still wore a long dark yarn shirt until the time of her birth.
Qiu Jin's thoughts took a leap forward, and she clashed more and more with her husband, who spent the whole day indulging in official affairs and in the flower garden. “A soulmate doesn't return to the common man; he will always hate and swallow his heart for life.” This is Qiu Jin's lament about her marriage.
In February 1904, Qiu Jin met a Japanese woman, Shigeko Hattori, at Wu Zhiying's home. From Shigeko Hattori, Qiu Jin learned about the various advantages of Japanese girls' schools, and she decided to study in Japan.
In May of that year, Qiu Jin paid her own travel expenses to study in Japan, “grieving the failure of Chinese education and the decline in national power.” She said that she wants to learn the skills to save the country and save her compatriots, especially the 200 million female compatriots.
On June 28, Qiu Jin boarded the German passenger liner “Independence” leased by the Japanese from Tanggu and embarked on a trip to Japan.
Due to her husband's objections and travel expenses were scarce, she had to travel in third class. Qiu Jin, dressed as a man, arrived in Japan carrying a dagger and rubbed shoulder to shoulder with the Sanjiao Jiulu.
At this time, Japan was just after the Meiji Restoration. Everything was flourishing, capitalism developed rapidly, and Western ideas about democracy, freedom, and human rights spread widely.
Qiu Jin first enrolled in a Japanese language school to study Japanese, then transferred to the Qingguo Women's Intensive Teacher Training Course at Aoyama Practical Girls' School in Tokyo the second year. At school, Qiu Jin read tenaciously, and her perseverance was amazing. Everyone else had already turned off the lights and went to bed. She still wrote and read until late at night. Every time she wrote, she pounded her chest and cried, and she didn't want to be angry. Many of the poems currently included in “The Collection of Qiu Jin” were written during this period.
Shortly after arriving in Japan, Qiu Jin wore a kimono, and took a solemn photo at a photo studio to show that she had broken up with Manqing. Outside of her studies, Qiu Jin actively participated in various club activities organized by local students and met a wide range of revolutionary activists. She and Chen Xiefen initiated the Mutual Love Association, which put forward the slogan of women's liberation. It is the earliest patriotic group founded by modern Chinese women.
In August 1905, Sun Yat-sen formed an alliance in Japan. Qiu Jin's school happened to be on the same street as the place where the League held a preparatory meeting. You can imagine how easy it was to get in touch with each other. Qiu Jin met Sun Yat-sen at this point.
Soon, after being introduced by Feng Jiao, Qiu Jin completed the formalities of joining the League at Huang Xing's residence. She raised her right hand, stood at the table, and read out her oath of admission: “On the same day, Qiu Jin vowed to exterminate the Tatars, restore China, establish the Republic of China, average land rights, and be committed to being loyal and determined. If so, let everyone punish them!” The oath was taken, and Huang Xingjiao used the handshake code and three secret passwords when the members of the alliance met.
Qiu Jin was very convinced of Sun Yat-sen's revolutionary strategy. Since then, her thoughts have been greatly influenced by the spirit of the League's political program.
On December 8 of this year, Chen Tianhua committed suicide by throwing himself into the sea. Qiu Jin was greatly shocked and decided to return home immediately and participate directly in the anti-Qing struggle.
The “China Women's Daily” was founded
In 1906, Qiu Jin returned to China and rented a house at No. 9l, Houdeli, North Sichuan Road, Shanghai, to plan “China Women's Daily”.
Qiu Jin has a penchant for newspapers. In September 1904, she founded “The Vernacular” in Japan. Under the name “Qiu Jin, the Lady of Jianhu Lake,” she published articles such as “To China's 20,000 Female Compatriots” and “Warn My Compatriots” to promote the anti-Qing Revolution and promote equal rights for men and women.
It costs money to file a report. Qiu Jin is running around, raising money from various sources, and is also advertising in newspapers, calling for everyone to invest in shares. However, there were few respondents; at the end of the day, only a few hundred yuan was raised. Qiu Jin was keenly aware of the lethargy of the Chinese women's community; “it's just a bit insensitive.”
Qiu Jin, who had no choice, finally thought of a “no solution.” That is to go to my in-laws to raise money. Her in-laws are very rich, and her mother's family can no longer support her financially due to the death of her father.
In early winter, Qiu Jin returned to the royal family in Xiangtan, Hunan. The father-in-law knew about the conflict between his son and daughter-in-law, and they had already separated. Seeing that his daughter-in-law suddenly visited him, he thought he had changed his mind, and treated him warmly.
Qiu Jin told her father-in-law that she wanted to run a school, but she lacked funds, and hoped her in-laws would provide financial support. Qiu Jin's father-in-law was happy to give Qiu Jin a sum of money in order to be able to reconnect with his son and daughter-in-law. A few days later, Qiu Jin changed into men's clothing and said goodbye.
After leaving home, Qiu Jin declared that she had broken up from her family relationship to avoid being tied to her family.
On January 14, 1907, Qiu Jin founded “China Women's Daily” in Shanghai, relying on this money, plus 1,500 yuan that others had painstakingly donated.
“China Women's Daily” is a 16-page booklet with a picture of a woman holding up a flag with both hands on the cover, symbolizing women's awakening and progress. For the first time in Chinese history, the newspaper proposed the establishment of a Women's Federation with the purpose of “opening up ventilation, promoting girls' studies, connecting relationships, forming groups, and creating a foundation for the Chinese Women's Association for the first time in Chinese history.”
In order to enable most undereducated and illiterate women at the time to understand, women's newspapers always used vernacular, and used popular forms such as lyrics and songs, which were lively and easy to understand.
The vast majority of the newspaper's manuscripts were written by Qiu Jin. In addition to her “Press Statement,” she has also written political opinions and literary works such as “Telling the Sisters,” “Remembering Women's Power,” “Feeling Angry,” “Feeling Time,” and “Jing Wei Shi,” and compiled the “Nursing Science Course,” and is known as a “Journalist Woman.”
In “China Women's Daily,” Qiu Jin tried to invent the idea of “the nation” and the “mother of the nation.” She believes that “the people” are greater than the imperial power, and that men and women are equal — “reform the authoritarian system of government, become a republic, and 40,000 people have sovereignty over the country's major affairs.” Of these 40,000 people, it goes without saying that 20,000 women are included.
Qiu Jin's idea of “equal rights for men and women” comes from the Western concept of “natural human rights, freedom and equality,” which is also an important part of Sun Yat-sen's ideology of civil rights in the Three Nationalism. Qiu Jin believes that not only do women have the same equal rights as men, but they can also be “pioneers of civilization” and “pioneers of civilization.”
At the time, on the other side of the ocean, American women were still in full swing in the feminist movement to become citizens in the true sense of the word and obtain the most basic political right — the right to vote. It wasn't until August 26, 1920, when the 19th Amendment to the Constitution was passed that American women were given equal voting rights as men.
Qiu Jin unequivocally promoted women's human rights and put forward a profound proposition combining women's liberation with national liberation, which was undoubtedly the highest level in that era. At this point, she has completed the transformation from female emancipation to national liberation, and from family revolution to social revolution.
According to press records at the time, “China Women's Daily” caused quite a stir in the community as soon as it was published. Cultured women scramble to read it, and uncultured women also try to ask others to read it to them. Everyone enthusiastically discussed the article in the newspaper and debated it. The feudal veterans were very outraged when they saw it.
On June 17, Qiu Jin wrote to a friend from Shaoxing, saying that the third issue of the magazine had been prepared and that “it must be printed around this month.”
However, the third issue will never be printed.
“A revolution requires bloodshed to succeed”
On July 10, Qiu Jin received news that the Qing government was about to arrest her.
In April of this year, Qiu Jin held a secret meeting of the leaders of various parties and military academics in Zhejiang at Baiyunan in Hangzhou to officially form the Liberation Army and put Xu Xilin as the leader, with Qiu Jin acting as co-leader.
Xu Xilin joined the Liberation Association in 1904 and founded Chase Academy in Shaoxing, Zhejiang the following year. Xu Xilin was busy with the revolution and neglected the management of the Shaoxing Datong Academy, so Qiu Jin took over as the school's supervisor. The full name of Datong Academy is “Tong Normal School”. It was founded in 1905 by Xu Xilin and Tao Chengzhang. It was a base for running schools to cover revolutionary activities by taking advantage of the government's abolition of science and running academies.
Qiu Jin, who is the principal, personally teaches. She goes to school every day to handle affairs, takes students on horseback to the field to hit targets, practice shooting techniques, and also trains such as gymnastics with various devices, mountain climbing, river cruises, and night marches.
Early in the morning, when an excited trumpet woke the students out of bed, Qiu Jin was already wearing a military uniform, a handgun, and a Japanese sword around her waist, and riding a horse.
While training students, Qiu Jin has been working secretly with Xu Xilin to prepare for armed uprisings in Anqing, Anhui and Shaoxing, Zhejiang. Qiu Jin's plan of action for the uprising was: take Jinhua first, then capture Hangzhou. If Hangzhou cannot be captured, then return to Jinhua, leave Jiangxi and enter Anhui, join forces with Xu Xilin's insurrection forces, and try again for big things. Although the plan failed to be realized, it was highly praised by people at the time: “It was carefully arranged and bravely, and thought that since the revolution, its preparations had not been as perfect as it was.”
On July 6, the Anqing Uprising broke out. Xu Xilin assassinated Anhui governor Enming, but the uprising failed, and Xu Xilin was arrested for justice.
On the 10th, news of Xu Xilin's horrible death came. Qiu Jin cried bitterly and lost his voice, speechless. Some people advised Qiu Jin to leave Shaoxing immediately, while others advised her to go to Shanghai and find a secluded home for her in the French Concession in Shanghai. She refused all of them. She said, “A revolution requires bloodshed to succeed. If Mannu were to bind me to the guillotine, the revolution could be at least 5 years earlier.”
Qiu Jin ordered that the guns and ammunition at Chase Academy be scattered and concealed, and also burned a large number of secret documents in the secret room of Hechang Tang's home, and asked the students to go home on their own.
At 4 p.m. on the 13th, when Qing soldiers surrounded Chase Academy, Qiu Jin was the only one left in the academy.
Qiu Jin was interrogated overnight to ask her to find out the inside story of the revolutionary organization and her party list. With a pen in hand, she pondered for a moment, and first wrote the word “autumn,” which is her last name. Urging her to write again, she continued with 6 more words. This is a famous phrase from 100 years ago: Autumn wind and autumn rain make people sad. This is Qiu Jin's only “written confession.”
In the early morning of July 15, 1907, Qiu Jin was put in a position to “immediately execute the right law” and drink the sword at Xuan Ting.
Qiu Jin's beating caused great shock. Xuantingkou was the place where Jiang Yang's thieves were killed. The most serious execution of women at the time was hanging, not decapitation.
Civilians took turns bombing the Qiu Jin case: “If you want to kill, kill; if you want to arrest, search. You don't have to be certified, you don't have to be willing, and you don't have to follow the law.” They also started by criticizing the Qiu Jin case and instead questioning the determination and future of the Qing government to prepare a constitution, and continued to appeal: “In today's world, not implementing a constitution is not enough to recover.” This is a tremendous amount of pressure that the impoverished Qing government can't bear at nightfall.
Li Zhongyue from Shanin Zhigen, who participated in the search, interrogation, and execution, was removed from office 3 days after Qiu Jin's beheading. During his leisure time in Hangzhou, he often shed tears, staring alone and reciting Qiu Jin's secret relic “Autumn Wind and Autumn Rain Saddens People.” Less than 100 days later, on October 29, Lee Jong-ok committed suicide.
When other officials involved in the Qiu Jin case were transferred to the local office, the local squire declined in writing. When they were traveling, they needed military protection. “However, people along the way burning silks and dung their way all scolded incessantly”, which surprised and frightened them.
What the people want is out of place, and it is doomed to the fall of Manqing.
After the success of the 1911 Revolution, Sun Yat-sen praised Qiu Jin's revolutionary deeds several times, and visited Hangzhou twice to hang Qiu Jin's tomb. In his speech at the welcome party of various circles in Shaoxing, he said, “Shaoxing has three martyrs Xu Xilin, Qiu Jin, and Tao Chengzhang to overthrow the dictatorship and establish a republic. They have contributed greatly to restoring the cause of liberation!” ★

Mian Feminist Song (1907)
My generation loves freedom and encourages freedom to drink a glass of wine. If men and women are gifted with equal rights, how can they be left behind. I wish to stand up and wash away the shame of the past. If they get along, they'll get back to work.
Old habits are the most embarrassing; women are actually like cowboys and horses. Dawn has a fresh start, and independence takes the lead. May slavery be eradicated, intellectually and intellectually learned. Responsibility is on one's shoulders, and the National Women's Achievement Period is unshouldered.
From a “family revolution” to a “political revolution”
Among the patriots and martyrs of Xinhai, Qiu Jin is a very unique one. She was originally a carefree and talented girl born in Shuxiangmendi. She would have been written into literary history by becoming a Li Qingzhao style boudoir poet; however, the misfortune of her marriage and the turbulence of the times gave birth to a heroic and strong side of her personality. Various complicated factors led her to embark on a revolutionary path full of anti-Qing dynasties. Therefore, her contemporaries say that she reached a “political revolution” through a “family revolution.”
The purpose of the League is more focused on “exterminating the Tatars and restoring China”. This is a characteristic of Liberation Conference leaders such as Qiu Jin and Xu Xilin. This characteristic is related to the fiercest and longest struggle against Qing during the Ming and Qing Dynasties in the Jiangsu and Zhejiang regions; it is also related to the spread of the nationalist ideals of Wang Zongxi and Liu Jishan in this region.
