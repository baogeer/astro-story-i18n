 “Cian Yun made a trick, flying stars spread hate, and traveled all the way to Yinhan in secret.” The 16th is the traditional festival “Tanabata.” Folklore experts say that Tanabata has a history of more than 2000 years in our country and is the most romantic traditional Chinese festival.
Wang Laihua, a professor at the Tianjin Academy of Social Sciences, explained that the Tanabata Festival, also known as the “Beggar Festival” and “Girls' Day,” “Double Seven,” “Scent Day,” “Qiao Xing,” “Women's Festival,” “Orchid Night,” “Children's Festival,” and “Needle Wear Festival,” is one of the most romantic traditional Chinese festivals, and it is also a day that girls and women value and love.
Wang Laihua said that among the names of the Tanabata Festival, “Qiqiao Festival” is the most famous. “Begging” is when the cowherd and weaver meet at Magpie Bridge on the 7th day of July of the lunar calendar. Young women beg the clever weaver for a pair of ingenious hands and a skill, and ask her to teach her the popular craftsmanship of spinning, weaving, and sewing.
The custom of begging for Tanabata originated in the Han Dynasty in our country, and was only later combined with stories of cowherds and weavers. On the night of Tanabata, the girls hold stitches and put needles in the moonlight; whoever wears them first can “get the hang of it.” In Ge Hong's “Miscellaneous Notes on Xijing” by Eastern Jin Ge Hong, there is a record that “Han Cai women often wear a seven-hole needle in a open jacket on July 7, and everyone learned it.” This is the earliest record of begging people seen in ancient documents. In Tang poetry and Song lyrics, women are repeatedly described as begging for Tanabata. Wang Jianyou's poem from the Tang Dynasty said, “The stars of Lanshan Mountain are adorned with pearls, and the Tanabata Palace is busy.”
In addition to begging for luck, people also have a custom of praying for the moon on Tanabata. On this romantic festive evening, the girls looked up at the sky and the moon, arranged fresh seasonal fruits, and prayed in the sky. In addition to begging for skilled knitters, they are also begging for a good match in marriage.
In order to protect this most romantic traditional festival in China and let more people, especially young people, know and understand the cultural connotations of this festival, “Tanabata” was included in the first batch of national intangible cultural heritage lists in 2006.
