 August 16 (July 7 of the lunar calendar) is the traditional Chinese festival Tanabata Festival. Folklore experts say Tanabata is the only traditional festival in China where women are the main characters, also known as “Girls' Day,” “Women's Festival,” and “Girls' Day.”
Wang Laihua, a professor at the Tianjin Academy of Social Sciences, explained that the Tanabata Festival, also known as the Qiqiao Festival, is an exclusive festival for women. In ancient times, people paid great attention to the annual custom of begging, and the various contents and forms were also interesting. The method of begging has been constantly being refined throughout the ages, and the customs and customs of different places are also different. The “Bu Qiao” custom is to ask a woman if she will be clumsy or clever in the future. The most widely spread ones are “piercing the moon” and “getting a needle and begging for luck,” also known as “Saikaou.” This is the earliest method of begging. It began in the Han Dynasty, that is, women wear needles in competitions. They tie colored threads and wear seven-hole needles. Whoever wears them faster means the more ingenious they are. Dressing slowly is called “bad luck.” People who “lose luck” should give gifts prepared in advance to those who are clever.
“Acupuncture the Moon” means that on the evening of July 7, every household cleans up the courtyard. Young women and girls must first kneel and pray to Weaver to bless their ingenuity. Afterwards, they took out the multicolored silk thread and seven silver needles they had prepared in advance. Whoever finished wearing the seven needles first indicated that she would become a master craftsman in the future.
As lovers of the “Magpie Bridge Encounter”, the cowherd and the weaver have deeply touched the hearts of generations of people for thousands of years. Therefore, Tanabata Festival is also a festival where tens of millions of daughters and girls place their dreams of love and desire to meet their lovers. Therefore, it is also called “Girls' Day” and “Girls' Day”. It is a traditional Chinese festival for women. On this night, in addition to begging for skilled hands from the weaver, the girls are also begging for a perfect marriage. In the past, marriage was a lifelong event that determined a girl's happiness for the rest of her life, so on the night of Tanabata, women in the world would pray for a happy marriage in front of the stars.
Folklore experts say that whether they are begging for ingenuity or a happy marriage, it shows a woman's sincere yearning for a happy life, adding infinite shade and beauty to this most romantic festival.
