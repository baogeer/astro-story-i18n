Comparing the subtle differences between “Historical Records” and “Han Shu” about the politicization of the gender relationship between the rulers of the Huns and the Central Plains, it is not difficult to discover the mystery:
First, the incident of Empress Dowager Xuan having an affair and a monarch of the Huns, as recorded in “Historical Records” and “Han Shu”, respectively, is also detailed in the Qin State's annexation of Yiqu land through this;

Empress Dowager Xuan was the mother of King Qin Zhaoxiang during the Warring States period (known as King Qin Zhaoxiang for short; later generations use its abbreviation). Her mother's family was a Chu aristocrat. Her last name is (sound “Mi”), and her full name is Ki Bazi. “Miyabako” may also be a nickname or an unofficial title.
The birth year of Mian Bazi is unknown. He died in the 42nd year of King Qin Zhaoxiang (265 BC), and controlled politics for 41 years during his time as a son. In other words, over 70% of King Qin Zhao's 56-year reign was actually ruled by her mother. The reason for this situation was quite coincidental: Qin Zhaowang, named Li Ze (also known as Ji), pledged to Yan Guo when he was a teenager, but his half-brother, Ying Dang, or Qin Wu Wang, died after being in power for only 5 years, and was only 24 years old; as Wu Wang's half-brother, 19-year-old Li was sent back by Yan Guo to take over his older brother's class, but he had no experience in governing the country; he had no experience in governing the country; he had no experience in governing the country; he had no experience in governing the country; he had to rely on his mother to handle it.
Empress Dowager Xuan appointed Wei Ran, her half-brother and half-brother, as general to be responsible for guarding the capital Xianyang. Exactly, the rebellion of Wu Wang's other younger brother, Shu Chang Zhuang (probably the position is the branch chief, and his name is Li Zhuang) was put to rest by Wei Ran. As a result of this incident, a number of senior ministers and high-ranking nobles were killed. As a result, the authority of Empress Dowager Xuan's sister and brother was also established.
At this time, the Qin State was actually ruled by Chu people.
Mihako traded her body for land
Under the iron-fisted rule of Chu Guo's sister and brother, the Qin State became more powerful. For example, Wei Ran defeated Wei three times and had more than 60 cities, which forced Wei Guo to lay down 400 miles of land east of the Yellow River. While presiding over the military, Wei Ran also promoted Bai Qi to reserve heavyweight military talents for the future expansion of the Qin nation. The powerful Qin State can intimidate Central Plains countries, but there is nothing to be done about the Huns in the north. In particular, the Yiqu branch, which is close to the border with the Qin State, poses a great threat to the Qin State.
The leader of the Yiqu clan (known as “King Yiqu Rong” in historical books) also had plans for Empress Dowager Xuan's appearance, and was quick to win. Empress Dowager Xuan was middle-aged and intolerant of loneliness, so she began an extramarital affair with Tiandi and Rong Wang of Yiqu. Yiqu Rong Wang left behind his land and people and went to the Ganquan Palace in Qin to rejoice with Empress Dowager Xuan. The two had two more children. However, in addition to sexual happiness, Empress Dowager Xuan also had national strategic considerations. Therefore, she murdered King Yiqu Rong at the right moment and immediately launched an army within Yiqu. The people of Yiqu learned that their leader had died in Qin and fled all the way north. The large area of land left behind was planned by the Qin State as the three counties of Longxi, Beidi, and Shanggun. On the northern border of the expanded territory, the Qin State built the Great Wall as part of the entire Great Wall after later unification.
The Qin State established three northern counties, putting tremendous military pressure on the Central Plains countries, especially the neighboring countries in the east. Once there is war, the Qin State can not only send troops from the western front, but the three northern counties can also respond, or the northern front sends troops and the west side responds.
According to the heritage of the Qin royal family, Empress Dowager Xuan is the oldest grandmother of Qin Shi Huang, or “old grandmother.” The folk saying “what happened at the time of Qin Shi Huang and his grandmother” means that the matter is far away and untestable, yet the case of Qin Shi Huang's “old grandmother” can still be examined. Her land strategy laid a strong foundation for the Qin nation to unify the world. Arguably, without Empress Dowager Xuan, there would be no Qin Shi Huang!
Later, Jia Yi, a Hanfu writer and politician (also known as a “strategist”) during the Han and Wen dynasty, said in her “Passing the Qin Theory”, that “up to the first emperor, fight for the last strength of the sixth generation, and defend the world”. Among them, “the sixth generation” referred to Xiaogong, King Wu, King Zhaoxiang (that is, King Zhaowen), King Xiaowen, and King Zhuangxiang, respectively. In fact, King Wu Zhaoer can definitely be called a generation, but what you call him is not important; what is important is that Empress Dowager Xuan's 41 years in power played an important role in this “sixth generation” (or fifth generation).
In the 41st year of King Qin Zhao, following the advice of strategist Fan Ju, Li, 59, took over her mother's power, dismissed her uncle Wei Ran's position and switched to Fan Yu. Empress Dowager Xuan was old and died a year after handing over power. It was the 42nd year of King Qin Zhao, or 265 BC.
