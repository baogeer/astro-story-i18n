 Author: Liu Jixing
The most perfect man in Taiping was none other than Shi Dakai. He was not only a handsome man with a very bright image (he was once praised as “the figure of the dragon and the phoenix”), he was also a great revolutionary, politician, military man, and writer of the time. His short life (he was only 32 years old when he died) uncovered many flashpoints, illuminating the otherwise poor historical sky of Taiping.
Shi Dakai is a native of Guixian County, Guangxi (present-day Guigang). Born in a farmhouse, he lost his father and went through hardships, but when he was a teenager, he was diligent in farming and studying. He is very familiar with poetry and books, and is particularly happy with Grandson's art of war. Joined the Worship God Church at an early age. On the eve of the Jintian Uprising, in the fall of 1847, Hong Xiuquan and Feng Yunshan visited Shidakai in Guixian to invite them to plan a major event together. The history of Tianping Heaven refers to “visiting Shi Xiang Gong” as “taking care of everything.” In this way, 16-year-old Shi Dakai embarked on a revolutionary path against Manqin's decadent rule, and became the core leader of the revolutionary organization.
At the time of the Jintian Uprising in 1851, Shi Dakai led more than 4,000 people to the Jintian Regiment Camp. He was crowned the captain of the Left Army of the Taiping Army. He was crowned the captain of the Taiping Army and was crowned King Yi 5,000 years old during the reign of Yongan, meaning “Wings Heavenly Dynasty.” Among the rulers of Taiping, Shi Dakai was the youngest and most military talented. He was a brave and heroic young commander from Yushu. If he were to walk on the streets of today's cities, there would definitely be quite a few girls screaming about how cool and handsome they are. At this point, although he was already famous, judging from his entire glorious life, his military career had only just begun with Jingo Iron Horse's decisive victory over Shachang and built his merits.
The Taiping Army marched from Guangxi to Jinling, passing through Hunan, Hubei, Jiangxi, Anhui, Jiangsu and other provinces. Shidakai was a pioneer in pioneering the way, besieged the city, and was invincible. In particular, after Xi Wang Xiao Chaogui was killed in action in Changsha, Hunan in 1852, the Taiping Army fell into a counterencirclement of the Qing army under the city of Changsha. The situation was extremely critical. Shi Dakai led the army to cross the Xiang River and open up a base in Hexi, mitigating the danger of the Pacific Army's lack of food. They also defeated the invading enemy many times and achieved “Shuiluzhou Dajie”, severely dampening the morale of the Qing military. Afterwards, he took the lead of the entire army. He safely withdrew his troops from Hexi, broke out of the anti-encirclement circle and took Yueyang, occupied Wuhan, and went down to Jinling 20 from Wuchang They advanced 1,200 miles in eight days, invincible, and invincible, leaving the Qing army disheartened and called it “Shi Dang.”
In March 1853, after the Taiping Army built Tianjing (present-day Nanjing), Shi Dakai was responsible for the garrison of Tianjing City, established a strict defense and warning system, and was a very good garrison commander. Liu Jixing found that after Dingdu, hedonism was on the rise, and beautiful women were selected, and houses were destroyed in order to build the royal palace. According to the treasury's wealth, only Shi Dakai took care of himself and never participated. It was very difficult and valuable.
In the fall of 1853, Shi Dakai was ordered to leave the town of Anqing to control the Western Expedition. He broke with Taiping's previous tradition of focusing on seizing the city pool and disrespecting base construction, adopted a steady and steady strategy, gradually expanded the scope of the base, personally commanded the conquest of Luzhou (present-day Hefei), the temporary capital of Anhui Province, and forced Jiang Zhongyuan Bing, the famous city-defending general, to defeat himself.
In Anhui, Shi Dakai showed superhuman political talent. In the past, Taiping Tianguo had no grass-roots government, and local administration was empty. After Shida arrived in Anhui, it soon organized local people to register accounts, elect grass-roots officials, and conduct tests to recruit talents and establish a local administrative system at the provincial, county, and county levels, so that Taiping Tiangguo truly had the size of a country; at the same time, it cleaned up military discipline, restored security, relieved the poor, and paid for the suffering, so that farmers and businesses were able to secure their businesses and enacted tax laws and levies taxes to provide the materials needed for Taiping Heaven's political and military activities. At the beginning of 1854, Shi Dakai left Anhui amidst praise from the people of Anhui and returned to Beijing to debrief. The leadership of Taiping Tianguo gave full recognition to his political practices. Since then, he has abandoned his fantasies of absolute egalitarianism and comprehensively implemented economic policies in line with reality.
In the summer and autumn of 1854, the Taiping Army faced a fierce backlash from the Xiang Army on the battlefield of the Western Expedition. They were steadily defeated and lost thousands of miles. Shi Dakai saw that the biggest difference between the two armies was the sailors, so he ordered people to build ships in the style of the Xiang Army and step up the training of sailors. At a critical moment when the military forces of the Xiang army were directly attacking Jiujiang, Shi Dakai once again became the head of the Western Expedition and personally commanded the former enemy. In early 1855, he defeated the Xiang Army twice in Hukou and Jiujiang. The Xiang Army sailors were unable to form an army. Commander Zeng Guofan threw himself into the water and was rescued by his subordinates, and the Western Front military reached its peak. In the fall of the same year, Shi Dakai moved to Jiangxi again. For four months, Shi Dakai joined seven provinces and 47 counties. Due to his strict military discipline, pragmatic administration, care for the people, and thirst for talent, the people of Jiangxi vied for support, and many intellectuals who were originally unfriendly to Taiping Heaven also supported the Taiping Army. The team soon expanded from more than 10,000 people to more than 100,000 people. The enemy lamented that “the people's mentality has completely changed, the general trend is over.”
In March 1856, Shi Dakai defeated the Xiang Army in Jiangxi. At this point, Nanchang City, where Xiang Army commander Zeng Guofan is located, has been surrounded on all sides by the Taiping Army's bronze and iron walls, and all foreign contacts have been cut off. Unfortunately, Shi Dakai was transferred back to Tianjing to participate in the battle to break the siege. Next, Shi Dakai broke through the Jiangnan battalion again and lifted the three-year siege of Tianjing by the Qing army.
In September of the same year, conflicts within the Taiping Tianguo leadership group intensified, and internal strife broke out in Tianjing. Eastern King Yang Xiuqing and more than 20,000 of his subordinates were murdered. Shi Da heard the news that he was driven back to Tianjing from Hubei. He would be victimized because Wei Changhui had indiscriminately killed innocent people. That is, Lucheng escaped from Tianjing, but his family was killed. After arriving in Anqing, it was difficult to set up troops and return to Beijing. Emperor Hong Xiuquan was forced by circumstances to execute Wei Changhui and ordered Shi Dakai to return to Beijing to assist the government. After Shi Da returned to Tianjing, he was admired by martial arts throughout the DPRK and was revered as the “King of Justice.” Under Shi Dakai's deployment, the Pacific Army held steady and sought an opportunity to counterattack. Emerging figures such as Chen Yucheng, Li Xiucheng, Yang Fuqing, and Shi Zhenji began to take the lead and stand alone, gradually reversing the passive situation caused by internal strife. However, when Emperor Hong Xiu saw that Shi Dakai was deeply popular, he was suspicious, and restrained Shi Da in every way, and even intended to cause harm.
In order to avoid another outbreak of infighting, Shi Dakai left Beijing in May 1857 and moved to Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Hunan, Guangxi, Guizhou, Yunnan, and Sichuan. In the process of establishing the Sichuan base, the Taiping Army reached the Dadu River. In May 1863, the Taiping Army arrived at the Dadu River. There was no Qing army on the other side. Shi Dakai ordered more boats and rafts to cross the river the next day, but heavy rain fell that night, and the river skyrocketed, making it impossible to sail. Three days later, the Qing army rushed to the garrison one after another. The Taiping Army was prevented by an early rise in water in the Dadu River, which had not been seen in 100 years. They were unable to rush the ferry several times, ran out of food and grass, and fell into a desperate situation. In order to establish the miracle of “Capturing Shi Dakai,” Sichuan Governor Luo Bingzhang sent an envoy to persuade them to surrender. Shi Dakai decided to give up his life with all three armies. After negotiations between the two sides, the Taiping Army dismissed 4,000 people on its own. Most of these people were able to escape. The remaining 2,000 men kept their weapons and joined the clean-up camp with Shi Dakai. After Shi Dakai was taken to Chengdu, the Qing army betrayed their trust and betrayed, and all 2,000 generals under Shi Dakai's command were killed in battle.
On June 27, 1863 (Tongzhi 2), Shi Dakai was tried at the Chengdu Public Court. He made a generous statement, leaving the presiding judge humble and speechless. Luo Bingzhang led Qing soldiers to kidnap Shi Dakai, Zeng Shihe, the middle leader, and Huang Zaizhong to the execution site. Shi and Zeng were each bound face to face on two cross wooden camellias. When Ling was executed late, the executioner first stabbed Zeng Shihe. Zeng Shihe was too hurt and screamed. Shi Dakai reprimanded him and said, “Why can't you stand this time?” Only then did Zeng Shihe clench his teeth and stopped shouting. When Shi Dakai was executed, he was stabbed more than 100 times, and he was silent from beginning to end. The officers and soldiers of the Qing army were shocked by Shi Dakai's dignity and strong will. The viewers were all moved and sighed as a “strange man.” Even Liu Rong, the rival envoy of the Sichuan government, had to praise him so much: “Fierce and strong, and his words were not arrogant or inferior; he did not wag his tail and beg for pity. ... At the time of punishment, he had a pleasant appearance; in fact, he was the toughest person in the category of scandals.”
People admired Shi Dakaijun very much. For example, when Li Xiucheng talked about the strengths and weaknesses of each king, “they were all in the middle of the cloud, and the sole surrender of Shi Wang said that his strategy was very profound.” Chen Yucheng believes that “none of the generals of the Taiping Army are generals; only Feng Yunshan and Shi Da can do anything.” Tsang Kwok-fan said, “Che Thief Channel is the most powerful. He incites the crowd, and uses stone as the greatest.” Tso Tsung-tong said that he was “cunning, winning the heart of a group of thieves. His intellect is superior to all thieves, and watching his actions is quite charming. He is anxious to find talent. He is the lord of thieves and I am afraid of it.” Luo Bingzhang said that he “can clean up people's hearts with his cunning, and can also impress the crowd with his ferocity.” The toughest battle of all evils.” A Gong Sheng of the Qing dynasty publicly stated at the Xiang Army military dinner that he had “the appearance of a dragon and a phoenix,” and Zhou Xun, the landowner, called him a “strange man” in “Shu Hai Talks”. Until nearly 40 years after Shi Dakai's death, there was also a description like this in the book “Jiang Biao Zhi” written by another landowner writer: “Until now, Jiangjianhuai still claims... Shi Da Kaiwei's instruments are unattainable.” Along with American missionary McGowen, he praised Shi Dakai in his newsletter: “This young leader, as the current backbone of the Pacific Army, has been described by various reports as heroic and chivalrous - brave, fearless, honest, and irreproachable. It can be said that he is Peyard (a famous French general and national hero) in the Pacific Army. He has a gentle temperament and is loved by all...”
