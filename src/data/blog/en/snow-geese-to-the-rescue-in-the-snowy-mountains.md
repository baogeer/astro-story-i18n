 Ma Zizhong
Half a slope on the western side of Diancangshan, there are quite a few farmhouses scattered in Ye Village. Farmers use the pottery technique passed down from generation to generation. They often go up the mountain to cut and break bamboo and drag them home to make baskets. They are more durable than other agricultural tools woven with bamboo, are more durable, more expensive, and are better sold.
As the saying goes, “seven bamboo and eight trees” refers to the bamboo in July of the lunar calendar and the wood of August; insects are immovable. In Dianchang Mountain in July, there was often a lot of rain and snow, and the depth of the snow was several feet. Going up the mountain, cutting bamboo and breaking the bush. Not to mention the hardships, sometimes even risking your life. Poor people have to gnaw their teeth and climb the mountain to support their families, no matter how hard they are, no matter how dangerous they are. Some people froze their ears, some froze their feet, and others never return... The poor looked at Mount Cangshan with compassion and mercilessness, and I don't know how many bitter tears they shed.
At the end of July of this year, a man who went up the mountain to cut down bamboo was frozen by the wind and snow...
This poor man, whose name is Ah Niu. The sky was dark this morning. He didn't listen to the advice of his parents and went up the mountain with a hard “cow.” I just climbed the bamboo slope. It rained heavily, then it snowed again. The sky was white, and the snow was several inches deep during the break. He is hoping that the weather will clear up after the snow and wind, and he is unwilling to return home empty-handed. Use your hands to heat the fire. If the firewood doesn't burn, use a knife to cut the bamboo. There was a sharp blow of wind and snow. It was so cold that his hands couldn't grasp the handle, and he shook like bran all over his body. He gnawed his teeth and cut down tree after tree, and cut mangosteen after another. However, the wind never stopped, the snow continued, and they were cut down. The wind and snow were suddenly mixed with hail, and the horn knocked down. At the beginning, it was the size of a pea, and later it became the size of a broad bean, causing him pain in his ears, pain in his face, and numbness in his hands. He ran under an old tree and took refuge. A few hours passed, the hail finally stopped, and the wind and snow continued. He's cold all over his body... He knows he can't cut bamboo anymore, and he knows that there is only a dead end if he stays any longer! He shook his head and sighed helplessly, stepped forward, and set out on his way home.
There's a lot of snow left. He walked down the hill step by step. He got up and pulled his legs as if struggling in a muddy pool, and walking slowly like a cripple. It took quite a while. After walking only two or three miles of snowy roads, he was so tired that he breathed like a windbreaker, and his feet stopped listening more and more. He was in a hurry. If he dragged on like this, he was only half way there when it was dark. He wanted to roll up the slope with his head, and was afraid of hitting a tree and getting hurt, and even more afraid of falling into a river ravine... but he had to clench his teeth and struggle to walk down the slope and trek another mile or two. As he walked, he felt panicked. His hair fainted, his eyes blushed, and his feet became numb, so he fell down and climbed on the snow. It crawls and crawls, and the more it climbs, the slower it is like a snail moving slowly. My hands were numb, and my whole body froze. He couldn't climb, he was desperate: “I'm going to die on a snowy mountain!” He regretted it: “Why didn't I listen to my parents' dissuasion!” He gasped loudly. His breath formed ice on his lips, and he burst into tears. Tears froze to ice on my face. He couldn't see the snowy mountains clearly; the snowy mountains were blurry; he couldn't see the forest; the forest turned black. He felt that his body was getting farther away from him...
He finally woke up from his trance. He heard the birds chirping. It's already the second day. He wanted to rip off the hot duvet cover and touched it, but it was warm fuzz. He couldn't help but open his sleeping eyes and take a close look, but he found himself lying under a duvet. He was amazed. When he stuck his head out of his feather, he was even more amazed. They were two large snow-white birds with heads and yellow beaks like a house goose, but their bodies were half larger than the house goose. With their eyes half open and half closed, they spread their legs, spread their wings, and lay down on him. One protects his upper body, the other warms his lower body, warms him like a hatching egg... He is puzzled: “Is this true, what's going on? Are you dreaming?” He pinched his thigh with his hand and knew it was painful. Only then did he remember cutting bamboo yesterday; he froze and fainted; he is still lying on a snowy mountain. It was these big, kind birds who kept him warm, shielded him from the wind and snow, and made it through the long night! It was this pair of big birds rescuing hardships that carried him back from the cold underworld to this cozy world. His heart trembled: “This is a lifesaver! It saved my life!” He was crying, crying very sad, and crying with joy. How should he repay the big birds that brought warmth in the snow... The big birds opened their eyes, closed their wings, and moved and looked at this miserable person who had come to life warmly. He hurriedly rolled over and knelt down on the snow, kept knocking at the big bird, choking and said, “Thank you... big bird... help! Thanks Big Bird... help...” At this point, the sky cleared up and the snow stopped. The big bird spread its wings and flew three circles around him, crackling and cheering, as if urging him to go home soon. It then slowly took off, waved its wings and flew far away, and disappeared into the vast sky. He saw clearly that the big bird had the same paws as a house goose.
Ah Niu set out on his way home empty-handed. Halfway through the way, I met a few friends from the same village, and they were trusted by Ah Niu's parents to go up the mountain to find him. “Where did you go wild with this Gu Zi cow?” A partner said, “Your parents are in a hurry!” A partner reached over and grabbed his hair: “When I found you, my buddy was like your parents crossed paths.” We all clapped and enjoyed it for a while. A Niu recounted the adventure of freezing to dusk on a snowy mountain and being saved by a big bird. The friends listened. Some nodded in amazement, saying that he had met the Sky Bird sent by Guanyin's mother; others shook their heads and disbelieved, saying that he can actually make Liao Zhai...
When Ah Niu returned home day and night, the parents were naturally very happy. After hearing how his son was rescued in Xueshan, the old man was so moved that he was busy knocking his head in front of Tiandi Jun's mentor. Grandpa said, “Your grandfather told you that during the Ming and Hongwu years, such a strange thing happened on a snowy mountain. This big bird that saves people on snowy mountains looks like a domestic goose. Its feathers are white. The ancients called it a snow goose. Since then, I've never heard of snow geese saving people. You were lucky when you were a kid.
The miracle spread from the village to the outside of the village. It came to the ears of a scoundrel. As soon as his eyes swirled, he was overjoyed and patted his thigh with ecstasy: “Laozi has something to eat!” This person's last name is Lai. He is short and thick, with concave eyebrows and a prominent beak, and when he is alive, he looks like a toad. The villagers called him Lai Gong Tao by his last name. He also has ugly morals. He often steals chickens and dogs, is idle, and doesn't do his job properly.
Next, Lai Toad had enough to eat and drink. He took a few wheat noodles, a gourd of soju, and a twine knife and put them in his back basket. He was dressed thick and sturdy, and went up the mountain wearing a brown hat. People who cut bamboo never carry a basket. He went up the mountain with a big stomach on his back, and he had his own plans in mind.
He climbed the Hachiku-ri slope along the mountain road, and heavy snow fell from the sky. The higher he went up, the thicker the snow became. Walking on snowy trails and skating with body weight. He fell a few knocks and almost smashed the wine gourd. He's not annoyed. It's not snowing, and he's not singing today. He had a hard time climbing the bamboo slope. Instead of looking for firewood to burn the fire, he tilted his neck and poured a few sips of soju into his mouth to cool off. Lean under an old tree that can block some of the falling snow, smoke dry smoke, and sneak around in the sky to see if any snow geese have flown by. I saw nothing but a lot of snowflakes. After three meals, he didn't know how many times he looked at the sky, and he didn't see any signs of snow geese. I've been sitting under the tree for a long time; the more I sit, the colder it gets. The snow got heavier, and the cold wind kept blowing furiously. He shivered like water spilling out of a bowl of water. I've filled my stomach with quite a bit of soju, so I still have to keep some to drink on my way home. He felt hungry, the earth was big, and it was a big problem that his stomach wasn't full. He took out a few cold breads and swallowed them. I was full, and I smoked dry cigarettes again. After a long meal, I still haven't seen any snow geese flying in. He slowly bowed down his head and complained that his luck was not good. Stay and wait. If you can't wait for snow geese to come, but you can't wait for hail, then it's bad. Besides, it's not too early. He was desperate; he just went home early.
He was in a hurry to put things in his basket. “Kaka, Gaka...” Birds are chirping from far away in the sky, almost like the cry of a house goose! He looked up; it was a snow goose! It flew in the sky from afar. He had a burst of euphoria in his heart. He hurriedly fell down on a snowy slope, pretending to die of freezing and stasis. Open the toad's eyes and keep an eye on the sky. The snow goose has already flown over him. The snow goose also found a “poor man” lying on the snow. Clack clack scream, circling in a circle in the sky, and he squinted through the eyes of a toad to take a peek. The snow goose circled and lowered one after another, fluttering and fluttering to his side. The two snow geese had already plucked him, and he felt warm all over his body. The idea came true, and he really liked it: “Yes, they are a couple of birds. Strangely, they are obese. Once your body is warm and warm, it's not too late to grab it again! He calculated how to grab one with one hand, catch a pair with both hands, how to use twine to keep them secure, and how to put them in a backpack with a big belly. If you can't catch a living one, even a dead one...
After three bags of smoke, his body was so hot that he couldn't hold it down. He slowly moved those evil thieves and slowly reached for the Snow Goose's foot. He touched the foot pole, took a strong shot, and caught it with force. It shocked the snow goose. He was busy fluttering his feet and soaring into the air. The wings flapped his face in pain. He desperately held the snow goose foot pole and wouldn't let go. Unexpectedly, the wings of the snow goose were so big that they carried him three feet above the ground and flew over the snowy slope for a while. Hoist to the edge of a cliff quickly. He was shocked and had to let go of one with his right hand. He landed on both feet, otherwise he would hit a cliff and hit to death! He said to himself in his heart that he could never let go of the one left. The snow goose was still flapping its wings and flying upward, dragging him on the snow. His face and eyes were constantly being slapped by its wings. The pain was unbearable. “Kill it!” He quickly pulled a sharp knife from his belt and stabbed the struggling snow goose. Unexpectedly, the snow goose that had just escaped flew back, swooped down at him, and pecked him in the face. “Oops!” He screamed, threw away the bird, and covered his face with his hands: “My... eyes... my eyes!” He cried and tumbled on the ground in pain. Blood flowed from the seams of the fingers. He cried, tumbled, rolled down a cliff, fell into a deep stream, and never ate swan meat.
These merciful auspicious birds floated softly into the distance like two holy white clouds in the cold wind and snow.
Heavy snow flew, and snow waves evaporated. Feathers shaking like snow geese covered up dirty blood stains, buried a terrible and sad crime, and made Cangshan a white world.
Since then, I haven't heard of the miracle of snow geese being rescued for a year or 200 years.
