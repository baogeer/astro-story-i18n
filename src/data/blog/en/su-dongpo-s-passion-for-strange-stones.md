 Song dynasty writer Su Dongpo was addicted to stone all his life. He had a special affection for strange stones, and left behind many of Yong Shi's poems.
When Su Dongpo was in Yangzhou, his cousin Cheng Deru knew he had a stone addiction and gave him two pieces of precious stones (a type of limestone produced in Yingde, Guangdong), one green and one white. When Su Dongpo saw these two strange stones, he remembered the dream he had during his recent tenure in Zhizhou, Yingzhou. In his dream, Su Dongpo went to a government office. “Qiu Chi”, the mountains and rivers here are clear and deep, and extraordinary. Friends told him that this is the aftermath of Fukudi's Xiaoyoudongtian. This is what Du Fu said about “Wan Gu Qiu Pi Cave, sneaking through Xiaoyoutian” in “Miscellaneous Poems from Qin Zhou.”
Su Dongpo named these two stones Qiu Chi Shi based on his dream of Yingzhou. He also wrote a poem in “Double Stones,” saying, “Dream Shiliang sees the wrong time; he is foolish because he draws water and buries it. However, when I saw that Yufeng was too white, I chose Emei from Bird Road. The autumn breeze and the smoke and clouds, and the twilight of the sun is full of greenery. Where is a little empty, the old man really wants to live in the pool of enemies.”
His poets saw Shi Qi's poetry and were in harmony with it. Qin Guan wrote a poem like this: “I believe it's Xiao Qiu Pi; the weather is just perfect. There is an empty hole under the rock, and the belly of Pahang is full.”
Back then, Dowei Ma Duwei Wang (shēn) knew that there were strange stones in Su Dongpo and wanted to borrow them to see. Knowing that he was trying to steal, and was afraid to refuse, Su Dongpo wrote the first long poem in advance. In the title of the poem, Su Dongpo called strange stones “Ancient Treasures.” To express his love for Qiu Chi Shi, he also wrote this at the end: “A merry noble boy wandered through Wudang Valley. I must have hated seeing the mountains; why take what you want? If you want to keep Zhao Weak, I'd rather lose to Qin's songs. Be careful not to let the word go; go ahead and act fast.”
Sure enough, after getting Qiu Chi Shi, Wang Yi put forward a claim based on his own. Forced to be helpless, Su Dongpo proposed “Two Ma Tu” by Han Gan, a Tang dynasty painter, in exchange for Wang Yi's collection. Naturally, Wang Yi was unwilling to hand over her family heirloom; as a result, the two sides couldn't get along. The relationship was very strained. Their friend's attitude on this matter was divided into two groups. Qian Xie (xié) initially opposed stone and painting, and instead proposed to own all of Qiu Chi Shi and “Erma Tu”; Jiang Zhiqi initially advocated that Su Dongpo cut love, but when he saw this stone, he actually proposed to burn the gravel. The two can be described as going to extremes; in fact, they advised both Su and Wang not to get tired; it was meant as a joke. Su Dongpo later wrote another poem, showing his attitude. This incident left almost no one in the opposition unaware. In the end, Qiu Chi Shi still returned to his original owner, and Su Dongpo's stone habits and poetry were even more popular.
Later, while in exile, Su Dongpo carried Qiu Chi Shi through Lake in Poyang, Jiangxi. A type of limestone “Hukou Stone” similar to Taihu Stone is produced locally. He saw a strange stone at Li Zhengchen's home. It had nine peaks, all over the place, and was exquisite and transparent. Su Dongpo admired it. He wanted to buy it with Baijin to match Qiu Chi Stone, and they all came up with a name: “Jiuhua in a pot.” However, he didn't have the strength to buy it at the time, so he wrote a poem called “Jiuhua in a Pot” and said that he must buy this stone after returning to the North.
Eight years later, Su Dongpo was pardoned and returned to Hukou. As a result, “Jiuhua in a Pot” was bought by others. Su Dongpo was so disappointed that he never forgot about it. Soon, Su Dongpo died of illness.
In May of the following year, the poet Huang Tingjian passed through Hukou. Li Zhengchen showed a poem by Su Dongpo. Huang Tingjian was very moved. He wrote the poem “Chasing the Dongpo Pot to Jiuhua”, which he missed the deceased: “Some people go to the mountains in the middle of the night and notice the warm, green sky. May I ask how to arrange a house in the middle of scattered clouds? Being able to return to Zhaobi and being able to live in Nanke is no longer a dream. Lai Youshuang couldn't take the bell by storm; it sounded so loud.”
It is worth mentioning that most people who enjoy watching Rain Flowers and Stones revere Su Dongpo as the originator, because he wrote the famous “Confusion to Strange Stones” (and “Promised Stone Later”). Actually, the “strange stone” he enjoyed was not Yuhua Stone. According to various art, literature, and historical books, he hasn't actually seen Yuhua Stone, but he does like to keep some colorful pebbles in a water basin and use them as copywriting arrangements, such as Dengzhou Stone (present-day Changdao, Shandong).
Yuhua Stone (or “Liuhe Stone”, named because it was produced in Liuhe, Jiangsu) was not played until the Southern Song period, and the gameplay was also very simple. “If natives pick patterns or colorful spots, they cleverly grind them into objects” (Du Wei's “Yunlin Shipu”), and there is no practice of nourishing it with fresh water.
Su Dongpo's love for stones is also reflected in his life. His book in Dingzhou called Xuelangzhai was taken from a strange stone: Xuelang Stone. This stone was obtained from Hengshan Mountain in Beiyue during his tenure in Dingzhou. The black and white pattern surges like waves. It is quite similar to a painting by the late Tang painter Sun Wei. Su Dongpo loved it. It was specially placed in a stone basin and engraved on the basin. He also wrote the seven-word long poem “Snow Wave Stone”: “The painter competed to copy the snow waves, and there were no traces of thunder axes in heaven. There is no Shu Shi sitting around the river on all sides, so there is no question about who the Shu Shi is sitting. The old man played in the rain and sat down to watch the pearls jump in the pot. It's a fantasy or not a dream; let's talk about the landscape of my hometown.” Later, Su Dongpo was repeatedly ostracized by demeaning officials and moved south. This stone stayed in Dingzhou (present-day Dingxian County, Hebei). (Lee Hak-Pu)
