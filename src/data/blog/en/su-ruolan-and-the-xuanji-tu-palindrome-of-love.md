 During the Eastern Jin dynasty, the north was occupied by the five ethnic minorities of the Huns, Xianbei, Gong, and Qiang. Before and after, a total of 16 regimes, large and small, were established, known in history as the “Five Hu and Sixteen Kingdoms.”
During the confrontation between Dongjin and the Five Hu Sixteen Kingdoms, cultural relics from the South were at its peak, and there were many talented people, and there were many loyalists and talented people; the northern country was submerged in mutual attacks, and war continued, and there were almost no famous figures. Other than Wang Meng, the former prime minister of Qin, and Wen Tao, who helped to unify the North, thus making her famous out of the annals of history, even Su Hui, a talented and beautiful woman. _
Su Hui, Zi Ruolan, a former native of Qin Shiping, grew up in a wealthy local family. She had delicate skin, bright eyes, and elegant demeanor, just as beautiful as a Jiangnan woman. Regardless of the flames of war raging around the house, she was engrossed in poetry and talent. Her temperament was out of step with the living environment where she was passionate about playing with guns at the time, and she pinned all of her joy, anger, sorrow, and joy into poetry. Her poetry is elegant, emotional, and her expressions are often mysterious, so her talent is hardly taken seriously, and she has never met an acquaintance who can discuss her poetry essays with her. As a result, a mentality of self-blame and self-admiration developed, just like an orchid in an empty valley.
At the age of marriage, she had no choice but to marry Dou Tao, a former general of Qin Annan. Before leaving the cabinet, I was very hopeful that I would meet a well-informed son-in-law. Who knows, General Dou Tao was born a martial artist. He was very good at riding horses, archery, and playing with swords and guns. As for Su Hui, apart from liking her beauty, he couldn't appreciate her poetry at all. She was greatly disappointed, and as a result, she became more and more relaxed. From time to time, she was unable to control her emotions and let off her temper.
At first, Dou Tao was quite obedient to her. He thought he was a beautiful wife. Occasionally, she lost her temper and was forgiving, but later he met the singer Zhao Dang and married a detached house. Not only can she sing and dance well, but she is also beautiful and charming, making Dou Tao very fond of her. Seeing Concubine Zhao's flirtatious demeanor, Su Hui naturally felt at ease. She couldn't help but reprimand her as a real wife. Concubine Zhao didn't dare to confront her, so from time to time she misunderstood Su Hui in Dou Tao's ears; Dou Tao also became more and more aloof to his wife. Later, Dou Tao was ordered to leave the town of Xiangyang and wanted to go out with her wife and concubine, but at the time, Su Hui was mad at the incident with Concubine Zhao and couldn't talk about it. This remark is in the middle of Zhao Ding's anger. She strongly encouraged Dou Tao, saying that if Su Hui doesn't go, she doesn't have to be reluctant. As a result, Dou Tao only brought Zhao Ding to office.
Su Hui stayed alone with her girlfriend in Chang'an. After a long time, she felt lonely and unbearably lonely. When my husband was around, I couldn't feel much benefit from him. Now, as soon as I leave, my feelings are unforgettable. It's really been a day for husband and wife! She couldn't help but feel a bit sorry for her initial demeanor.
In addition to her remorse, she spent the day and night writing troupe poems to relieve her time of loneliness. One day, she was absentmindedly playing with an ingenious little teapot. The words “You can be clean” were engraved around the pot. While playing, she suddenly discovered that no matter which character you read, these five characters can be a very interesting sentence. As a result, inspiration struck, and she thought she could use this ingenious textual phenomenon to compose some peculiar poems.
At this point, she was full of contemplation and deep feelings, and now she has found this ingenious method of expression, so she has gone to bed and thought about it. The idea came up, and it took her months of effort to weave the poem on the brocade. The brocade was eight inches long and divided into 29 lines. Each line was exactly 29 characters, and each word was aligned vertically and horizontally; the text was multicolored, and repeated in chapters, and contained countless poems of various genres. Su Hui named this brocade “Xuanji”. Xuanji originally meant the Beidou in the sky. The reason she named Xuanji meant the characters on this picture. The arrangement was as mysterious and sophisticated as the stars in the sky. Those who know can recognize it, and those who don't know are at a loss. Of course, it also implied her love affair with her husband, which was as deep and unchanging as a star.
After the “Xuanji Map” was ready, Su Hui sent someone to Xiangyang to hand it over to Dou Tao. The people next to me saw this picture and didn't know what it meant. But Dou Tao, who didn't really understand the poem, held “Xuanji Tu,” and was able to fully understand his wife's affectionate feelings. This is probably what is called having a smart heart! When others asked about the reason, Dou Tao meaningfully said, “This is my family's language, not my family's people; I can't understand it.”
Immediately, Dou Tao sent a group of people to Chang'an to pick up Su Hui. Since then, the couple's love has deepened, and Dou Tao has gradually studied poetry with Su Hui. The two often wander in the ocean of poetry together.
Su Hui's “Xuanji Map” shocked that chaotic era. Everyone scrambled to copy it, try to read it in sentences, and analyze the style of poetry, yet few people were able to understand it. “Xuanji Tu” was passed down to future generations, and I don't know how many literati people, Yashi, have broken their minds. Wu Zetian, a woman from the Tang Dynasty, deliberately promoted “Xuanji Tu,” and won more than 200 poems. A high priest in the Song Dynasty founded Zong, broken it down into ten pictures, and obtained 3,752 poems. Kang Wanmin, a scholar from the Ming Dynasty, spent his whole life studying and wrote the book “How to Read Xuanji”, explaining that the handwriting on the original picture was divided into five colors to distinguish between three-, five-, and seven-word poems. Later, the transcribers all used ink books, and were unable to distinguish their bodies, making interpretation difficult. Kang Wanmin has developed a complete set of reading methods, divided into 12 reading methods: positive reading, backwards reading, backward reading, backward reading, horizontal reading, oblique reading, quadrangle reading, intermediate radiation reading, angular reading, opposite reading, and reverse reading. He was able to obtain 4,2006 five-word, six-word, and seven-word poems; each poem was deeply affectionate, revealing true feelings.
