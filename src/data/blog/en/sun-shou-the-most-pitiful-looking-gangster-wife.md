 Author: Hou Hongbin
Sun Shou: The wife of Liang Ji, a powerful minister in the Eastern Han Dynasty. Leung Kee is a prominent and arrogant person. But he was extremely afraid of his wife Sun Shou. Sun Shou is beautiful and jealous, and a private domestic servant. The husband and wife acted recklessly, embezzled the law, and were extravagant. Later, in the midst of a political struggle, he committed suicide due to fear of crime. In “The Book of the Later Han Dynasty: The Biography of Liang Ji,” Sun Shou is “beautiful and good, bewitched, and depraved.”
Liang Ji was a famous minister with relatives outside the Eastern Han Dynasty who ruled the dynasty. The Leung family was extremely prominent in the late Eastern Han period. One of his sisters and two younger sisters were empresses, and six sisters were nobles; among the men, seven were feudal rulers, two were generals, three married princesses, and 57 other senior officials. Liang Ji served as four emperors during his lifetime, and was in power as a general for more than 20 years. Three of the emperors were manipulated by him to rise to power, and another was poisoned by him.
However, it is this kind of person who acts as a blessing, when they are salivated and scolded, held in the ear, and beaten to the point where they kneel down to ask for forgiveness. His opponent is his wife Sun Shou.
Leung Ki-ki is extremely ugly. He has hunched shoulders, slanted eyes and nose, and stutters. He looks very imaginative. Other than the sound of dogs and horses, they have no talents; they don't know all the characters in Dou Da. However, this guy has a very beautiful wife, Sun Shou. She has a beautiful face and a graceful physique; he is also good at making all kinds of charms. This beautiful woman, to Liang Ji, is “my savage wife”; to the outside world, she is “my underworld wife.” They are straightforward and have an outspoken personality. Sun Shou also acts bluntly and out of nature. However, her nature is bad, so being savage is not just as simple as using her subordinates as ashtrays.
Sun Shou is extremely jealous by nature, and is particularly strict about Liang Ji. While Leung Ji was in mourning, she secretly lived in seclusion with friends in the west of the city. Sun Shou invited Liang Ji to go out, led many slaves, snatched the friend's day, shaved off his hair, shaved off his face, and severely tortured him. He also planned to write a book to the emperor to denounce Liang Ji's flower incident. Liang Ji listened. He had no choice but to bow and plead in front of his old mother-in-law before Sun Shou let him go. Liang Ji was confused and bold. He had fornication with friends as usual. He gave birth to a son, Bo Yu, and hid it in a wall where he couldn't see the light. Sun Shou instigated his son Liang Yin to kill You Tong Ji, and her husband didn't even dare to make a sound. Sun Shou isn't a virgin either; she took the opportunity to hook up with a prisoner called the Qin Palace. It's not that Liang Ji doesn't know; he's just mute and doesn't dare to say it — tigers, sticks, and chickens give one thing at a time; it's really uneasy to retaliate.
The couple were corrupt, murdered and set fire, and did nothing wrong. The little emperor couldn't follow suit and scolded Liang Ji: It really was General Ba Hu. Liang Ji poisoned the emperor, and no one was allowed to save him. Sun Shou also sucks up his nose with Liang Ji.
However, Sun Shou has also made an indelible contribution to the traditional beauty and hairdressing industry in China. This humble woman has five “demons,” and her appearance is the most graceful and pitiful in history. “Folk Customs Guide” describes her attire as “sad eyebrows,” which draws her eyebrows thin and twisty, showing a sense of sadness. “Makeup” means putting makeup under your eyes to make you look like you've cried; “falling down on a horse bun” means putting your hair on the side to show laid-back and slack, as if you had just fallen off right away; “bending your back” means walking like the wind and swinging, and your lower back legs are so thin that you're about to break; “laughing with dental caries” means laughing as if you have a toothache. You can only laugh lightly; you can't laugh out loud. With this “poor face,” men have no choice but to be loved by Rei. Since then, Chinese women have generally been overwhelmed. They are shy and unassertive at the corners of their eyes and eyebrows. They are weak and laid-back like Persian cats, making men develop an impulse to protect and rape. They are all a group of creatures that need to use the weak talents of others to counter their own strength. This is how beauty standards are created. Even if your slender hand is like Zhao Feiyan or Sun Shou, you have killed countless people, robbed countless money, and hugged countless men, you must act shy and weak, and drift away as soon as the wind blows.
Even today, Sun Shou's outfit isn't out of date — who dares to say that those glamorous people aren't classy? Those with round, round, and lively eyes had no choice but to be little swallows.
