 The Spring Festival is a traditional festival for our people. The beautiful treasure island of Taiwan is linked by flesh and blood to the mainland of the motherland and is closely related. Taiwan's folk customs for the New Year are basically the same as those in mainland China, but they also have their own unique features.
In Taiwan, the fireplace is the size of the whole family on New Year's Eve. They sit around a round table with hot pot and have dinner together. It's called a “fireplace.” People who participate in the “Fireplace” are required to drop chopsticks for every dish on the table, regardless of size. Women who don't usually drink alcohol should also drink a symbolic sip of alcohol to gain good fortune. Every dish on the table during the “fireplace” is very interesting: fish balls (balls) and meatballs take “three yuan”, which symbolizes reunion. Radish, like in Taiwan and southern Fujian, is called “cabbage head” which means “good color head” and has a good omen. The “chicken” of a whole chicken is synonymous with “home” in Taiwanese, which means “start by eating chicken.” Some deep-fried foods are fried in oil to show a “prosperous family”. Huh, Taiwan criticized it as “fat.” Eat what you want to “get rich and have good fortune.” The vegetables in the “oven” are not chopped with a knife. After washing and cooking the roots, they are not cut off when eaten. Instead, they are slowly eaten in the stomach from beginning to end to wish parents a long life. During the “fireplace,” if someone in the family goes out and it's too late to get back, they should also highlight a seat and leave this person's clothes in an empty space. It shows that the whole family misses him.
Walking through spring “walking in spring” is a Taiwanese folk saying about “celebrating the New Year,” and it is still very popular today. Every time people arrive at the house, they say something auspicious to each other. The host takes out candy in a vermilion wooden box or a Kowloon pan and serves sweet tea, and warmly entertains them, showing the closeness between the hosts and guests. When the guests leave, the host and guest give each other red envelopes to each other's children.
Eating long-standing dishes During the New Year and Spring Festival, Taiwanese compatriots have a habit of eating long-standing dishes. The so-called perennial vegetable is actually a type of mustard with long stems and leaves that have a bitter taste. In Taiwan today, people's various activities are quite superstitious. They think this dish, with long leaves and long silk, symbolizes longevity. Some people also deliberately add some long-time fans when cooking, saying that it is so soft that it lasts a long time if you eat it. Everyone knows that vegetables absorb oil. During the New Year's Spring Festival, big fish, big meat, and greasy ones. Eating vegetables not only relieves oil, but also soothes appetite. Therefore, the Taiwanese compatriots' habit of eating “long-standing dishes” during the New Year and Spring Festival is a very scientific way of eating.
Debt avoidance dramas are in the past. During the Lunar New Year in Taiwan, the public had such a unique and humane program — “Debt Avoidance Drama,” that is, on the night of New Year's 30th, some temples performed. Those who owe debts to others and were unable to pay them off a few years ago secretly run to “watch a movie” in front of the “debt avoidance show” in front of the temple. If people collecting debts or collecting debts actually break into the “debt avoidance drama” stage and ask someone to ask for money, not only will they cause “public outrage,” but they will also be “beaten by a group.” It's not fun to ask for money. This drama was performed from New Year's Eve until New Year's morning. In this way, those “watching movies” who are in debt can also go home safely and unharmed to “enjoy” the New Year. Today, this custom is still maintained in some Taiwanese folk villages.
