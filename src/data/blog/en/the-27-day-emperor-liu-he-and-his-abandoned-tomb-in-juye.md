Hanchangyi Country is located in the lower reaches of the Yellow River basin. The Yellow River often breaks down and sediment accumulates, and the ancient city of Changyi is already buried deep underground. The country of Hanchang is in the southwest of present-day Juye County. After exploration, Guchang Eupseong is in the Changyi area of Daxie Market Town, Juye County. During the Xia Shang period, Guchang-eup was the country of the father country. At the beginning of Western Zhou, King Wu feigned Uncle Mao, the son of Gong Zhou, here and became Mao Guo. During the Warring States period, due to convenient transportation and important geographical location, people were stationed and traded here, and gradually gathered to form a city, Chang-eup. Changyi County was established during the Qin dynasty. In late Qin, Xiang Liang ordered Liu Bang and Peng Yue to attack Changyi County. At the beginning of Western Han, Liu Bangfeng and Peng Yue became King Liang. They were all in Dingtao. Changyi was under its jurisdiction. Changyi was already a famous big city at the time. After Peng Yue was killed, the capital of Liang Guo moved west, and Prince Liu Hui, the younger brother of Lu Hou, Lu Shan, the fourth son of Emperor Han Wen, Liu Yi, and Empress Dou, Liu Wu, second son of Empress Dou, were successively crowned King Liang. After years of operation, Liang Guo became the most powerful feudal state in Western Han. It had more jewels and jade than Beijing, and had more than 40 cities at its largest area. Liu Wu once helped his older brother Emperor Han Jing maintain his power and was deeply trusted. Later, he also wanted to be an emperor, but after it was revealed, he stood on the sidelines. In 144 BC, Liu Wu died, and Liang Guo was split in five. The capital city of Sanyo Province, which was established in it, is set to be Chang-eup.
In 97 BC, Emperor Han Wu named his son Liu Yi King of Changyi and changed Sanyang to Changyi Province. After Liu Yi's death, his son Liu He succeeded King Changyi.
