When she grew up, she married Cen Meng, a civil servant in Tianzhou, Guangxi, and changed her last name to “Watashi.” Cen Meng was defeated by Yao Yi, the emperor of the two Guangdu capitals, and fled to Guishun Prefecture, where he was killed by Zhizhou Cen Zhang (father of Cen Hua). In the 32nd year of Jiajing (1553), Japanese invaders (known as Japan in ancient times) invaded China's coastal regions of Jiangsu and Zhejiang, and they were in a state of emergency for thousands of miles along the coast. The Ming dynasty ordered General Secretary Zhang Jing of the military department to go to Jiangsu and Zhejiang to fight against Japan. Governor Zhang Jingzeng of Liangguang and the military knows that the Guangxi military (Zhuang Turkish officers and soldiers) are brave and good at fighting, so he decided to recruit soldiers from Tianzhou and other places to go out. Because her great-grandson Dalu was too young to take up military service, she requested permission from the superintendent to take the military. Zhang Jingsu knew that Wa was proficient in martial arts, witty, and bold, so he approved her request and awarded him the rank of “female general.”
With the determination to fight with the enemy and protect the nation and people, Mrs. Wah led a total of 6,852 soldiers and 450 war horses (mainly local Debao ponies) from Tien, Nandan, Guishun, Donglan, and Nadi.
