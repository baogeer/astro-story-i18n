 Once upon a time, there was a family in Yangmeiling. The couple were in their early sixties and had only one son, who was only 12 years old. The son is beautiful, strong and sturdy. He is very well-behaved from an early age, and can help his parents live with each other at the age of seven or eight. Dad is happy for him, and his mother is happy for him. She named him Xie Er. Everyone in the village boasted that he was a good and capable guy.
The old couple loved their son and didn't want him to be blind, so they sent him to study at a private school in a small temple in the village.
One day, after early schooling was released in the school, a group of ten or so young people rushed out of the village to have fun.
Xie played around with everyone for a while and saw that the chimney in front of the village was already smoking. Remembering that her mom wanted to cook lunch, she rushed home. He stepped through the door and heard his mom shouting, “Hey, the water tank is empty. Go get me a bucket of water.”
I shouted in joy, put down my bag, picked up my bucket and left. Walking to the edge of the creek, I saw two loaches walking back and forth in the water, grabbing a bead. He thought it was funny, so he chased the loach away and picked up the bead.
Carrying a bucket of water and pinching a bead, Xie happily walked home. When others saw that he found something fun, they jumped and jumped over to take a look. He was only afraid that someone else would take the beads and not return them to him, so he held the beads tightly in his hand and held them up. Wherever they were willing to put it, they hugged and grabbed it as soon as they shouted. Xie couldn't stand up to so many people; she was busy holding the beads in her mouth. The boys then screwed his face, slapped their mouths, and it was still itchy in the corner of his arm. Xie wanted to call her mom to help her. She slapped her mouth. Unexpectedly, she didn't make a sound, but the bead “grunted” and swallowed it in her belly.
It was a pity that Xie swallowed the beads. He came home and his mom hadn't cooked lunch yet. When Xie was idle and fine, she unpacked her school bag, put in a spoon of water, polish the ink, and lay down the floor to draw red characters on the table. After a while, Mom brought out the meal from under the stove and was shocked. She saw her son's face bloated purple, his eyes sticking out like bronze bells. He had two horns on his head, his mouth split to the edge of his ear, and his throat sounded “humming, thumping” like thunder, and his body grew longer and longer. It turns out that what Xie swallowed in her belly was a dragon ball — he became a dragon.
Dragons need water to fly! Xie bowed her head to the inkstone, licked off a puddle of ink she had just polished, and immediately turned into a dark oolong. “Whoa whoa” burst out of the house and flew into the air. The higher the oolong flew, the farther it flew, the thicker and longer its body became thicker and longer. The dragon head drilled into the dark clouds, and dragged the dragon's tail down and dragged it on Yangmei Ridge. For a moment, dark clouds covered the sun, the wind blew loudly, thunder loudly, torrential rain flooded the ground, and the oolong swallowed the fog and flew eastward.
A child is the heart and soul of his parents; how can he bear to fly so far away! Braving the heavy wind and rain, the old couple stumbled out of the house. While chasing them, they shouted:
“Happy, come back!”
Dad shouted one voice, mother called one voice after another; when Oolong heard his parents shouting at him, he stopped and looked back. Dad shouted nine times, and his mother called nine times. Oolong stopped for a total of 18 stops and turned his head 18 times. At this point, he had already reached the sky above the Qiantang River, lowered his body, and swam along the river to the East Ocean. Oolong's tail was dragged over Yangmei Ridge and scraped out of Jiudaogou Ditch. The ditch was filled with rain and murmured into Jiujiao Creek; Oolong returned 18 times along the way, and 18 sandy beaches accumulated in the place where he returned. This is what people often call the “Eighteen Beaches of Jiuxi.”
