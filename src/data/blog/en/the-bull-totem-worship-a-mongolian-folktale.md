 Mangyu totem worship is also a belief of Buryat Mongols. “The History of Buryat in Mongolia”, which Lombutse still wrote, records myths and legends about the ancient history of Buryatia. Legend has it that the “Yidugan” (witch), born in Buryatia called “Ashukan”, was roaming in Lake Baikal and saw a mangrove cow puffing at the mouth of the lake. I thought, “This isn't my average encounter; it's a blessing from heaven”, so I made out with Mangniu. Soon after, Ito Kane gave birth to a second son. His brother's name was Briata, and his brother's name was Hiroritai. While hunting in the mountaintop jungle, Briata met a woman as his wife and gave birth to a second son. His brother's name was Iguro Hitoshi, and his brother's name was Paula Gart. The children and grandchildren who grew up from Igor and Boragat have always had a saying in the jungles of the Han Mountains: “the father of Mangniu (king), the mother of the bush Hatun (posterity)”, and there is also a tradition of offering fresh milk to the head for blessings. Some tribes that still inhabit Lake Baikal still have this custom. The mythology and legend of the cult of Yimangniu (Tianzi Mangniu Nayang) also describes the origin of Mangniu Nayang, Yigerite, and Baolachite. Legend has it that Mangniu was originally a son of heaven, yet while walking around in Mang cow leather, he looked at the princesses of Taiji and Khan and made them pregnant. Later, the two boys born from his eyes made him pregnant became the originators of the Baulagat and the Japanese family.
The above two myths are all myths about women coming into contact with Mangniu to have children and become ancestors of their own clan. They can be seen as works reflecting the form of matriarchal social attitudes. In the previous work, Yidugan was a witch who exercised religious theocracy during the feminist era. She herself had the status of a clan leader. The son of the clan leader and Totemman Cow became the ancestors of the clan, and Welling had a prominent position of her own. It was truly remarkable in terms of the origin of the clan lineage. In the Neolithic period, people from different clans lived along Lake Baikal; they were the ancestors of the later Buryats. Paula Ghat and Ihezhte are probably the leaders of one of these clans. They believe in the Mangniu totem, and they also have their own genealogical history. The latter myth also believes that their ancestor was the son of a famous princess who was conceived by the god Mangniu, the son of heaven. No matter how the status of the first grandmother changed, their ancestors were the avatars of mangniu totems, which is the core of their belief.
Also, looking at eye pregnancy from the totem belief's concept of fertility is also in line with the characteristics of early thinking. Primitive people didn't understand the origin of giving birth to boys; they believed that pregnancy was the result of a totem soul or baby soul entering a woman's body.” Birth is due to a totem entering a woman's body, and death is a person returning to their clan's totem.” This is a very important idea of the Totem Clan. They think that you can get pregnant by looking at something, dreaming about something, or touching a totem, so ancient myths of pregnancy and emotion are constantly emerging. The story of eye pregnancy is a legacy of the ancient concept of totem fertility.
