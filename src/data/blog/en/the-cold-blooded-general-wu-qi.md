 Wu Qi was an outstanding military man, famous politician, reformer in the early Warring States period, and a representative figure of the military family. A person from Wei Guo Zuo (speaking of present-day Caoxian County, Heze City, Shandong Province, and Dingtao District, Heze City, Shandong Province). Throughout his life, he lived in the three kingdoms of Shilu, Wei, and Chu. He was familiar with military, legal, and Confucian ideals, and had great achievements in internal affairs and military affairs. While in Chu, he hosted “Wu Qi Bian Fa”. Later generations referred to him as “Sun Wu,” and “Wu Zi” and “Sun Tzu” were also collectively known as “Sun Wu's Art of War”, and occupied an important position in ancient Chinese military books.
Main achievements: In the 14th year of King Zhou Weilie (412 years ago), Qi attacked Lu Guo. Wu Qi was ordered to lead an army to the front line, first “showing weakness” to the opponent, using it to paralyze the Qi army's generals, be arrogant, and then unexpectedly suddenly launch an onslaught against the Qi army. The Qi army hurriedly responded to the battle and was destroyed as soon as it was hit. More than half of the casualties were lost, and the Lu army won a landslide victory.
In the 17th year of King Zhou Weilie (409 BC), he led Wei's army to conquer Linjin (present-day Dali East, Shaanxi) and Yuanli (present-day south of Chengcheng) in the Hexi region of the Qin Kingdom, and built these two cities. The following year, they attacked Qin again, and continued to attack Zheng (present-day Huaxian) in the hinterland of the Qin State, forcing the Qin State to retreat to Luoshui and build fortifications along the river to defend it. In addition to this, Fan Pang (present-day southeast of Hancheng City, Shaanxi Province), which was attacked and occupied by the Gongzi in 412 years ago, Wei Guo occupied all of the Hexi region originally belonging to the Qin State, and established Xihe County to serve as the governor of Xihe County. During this period, he “fought 76 battles with the princes and won 64 total victories” and “cleared four sides and covered thousands of miles.” In particular, during the Battle of Yin and Jin in the 13th year of King Zhou An (389 BC), Qin Huigong sent 500,000 troops to attack Wei's Yin Jin. Wu Qi personally led 50,000 people with no military merit, plus 500 chariots and 3,000 cavalry to defeat the Qin army. It became a famous battle with few wins in the history of the Chinese war, and also made Wei Guo a powerful princely country in the early days of the Warring States.
After Wu Qi invaded Chu, he helped King Chu Mou carry out drastic reforms, rapidly improving the overall national strength of the Chu country. The strengthened Chu country attacked Baiyue to the south, and expanded the territory of Chu to the Dongting Lake and Cangwu County. In 381 BC, Chu sent troops to aid Zhao Guo and fought against Wei Jun in western China (west of present-day southwest Wuzhi County, Henan Province). The Chu army crossed Liangmen (a gateway located northwest of Daliang), garrisoned troops in the forest (north of Liangmen), drank horses in the Yellow River, and cut ties between Hanoi County of Wei and the capital Anyi (northwest of present-day Xia County in Shanxi Province). Using Chu's offensive, Zhao Guo attacked Jinpu (present-day south of Weixian County, Hebei Province) and conquered Huangcheng (present-day south of Guanxian County, Shandong Province). Both Chu and Zhao defeated Wei Jun.
