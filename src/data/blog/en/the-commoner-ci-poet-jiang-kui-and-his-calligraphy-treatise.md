 The Song Dynasty literary scene was full of stars. Previously, there were Fan Zhongquan, Ouyang Xiu, Wang Anshi, Su Yi, Huang Tingjian, Sima Guang, and Zeng Yi; later there were Lu You, Xin Qiji, Li Qingzhao, Yang Wanli, and Fan Chengda. Compared to many superstars of the same era, Jiang Kui (known as Taohito Baishi) is not outstanding. However, with his talents, studies, and knowledge, he founded the Song phrase “Empty”, which influenced the literary world for hundreds of years; furthermore, his talents spanned the three realms of literature, music, and calligraphy, and were successful, which is really rare. Behind the achievements is hardship. Jiang Mei's twists and turns, from being a lyricist to everyone in the art world, from her path to art and her personal style, are worthy of being enjoyed by future generations.
Life's ups and downs
During Song Huizong's Shaoxing years, Jiang Mi was born into a dilapidated family in Poyang, Jiangxi. According to Chen Si's “The Chronology of Shiraishi Taohito,” he was born in 1158 (1154 or 1155). Its most developed ancestors can be traced back to the 12th ancestor during Tang Desong's time. Officials who advised doctors and worked with Ping Zhang Men were the same as prime ministers. However, after entering the Song Dynasty, Jiang Yi's father joined the ranks and once “knew Hanyang with Xinyu County”, that is, as the deputy mayor of Hanyang County. However, after 3 years in office, he died in office. The family lost their backers, and the family became even more stranded.
Jiang Yi, who lost her family as a teenager, lives with her sister's family in Shanyang, Hanchuan (west of Wuhan), and is wholeheartedly preparing for the exam. The dream of ancient book readers was to “go to Tiansherang in the morning and go to Tianzidang in the past” to change one's destiny through academic studies. Jiang Mei traveled with her father since she was a child and received a good education. He is familiar with poetry, is proficient in sound, and has a knack for literature, but he is particularly slow to “start a business.” Under the Song dynasty system, taxis were tested every three years, and they were examined in their home country. Jiang Yi went back to her place of origin exam for the first time at age 17. Since then, every 3 years, until the age of 26, he has taken the exam 4 times in a row, but every time she is famous for Sun Mountain.
Adolescence suffered major setbacks in life, leaving the teenager's heart completely lost. As a marginal person in society, whether watching the hustle and bustle of the world or the clouds and smoke, I can't help but be overshadowed by a layer of tragedy. When Jiang Yi condensed the tears flowing from her broken children's heart into the pearls of words, a unique style of “empty words” in the Song dynasty lyric industry quietly sprung up.
Fate closed one door for him and quietly opened another door for him.

Fan Chengda was born into the family of three Chinese governors. He was the chief minister of government affairs (Deputy Prime Minister). Talent, learning, knowledge
Summarizing the reasons for Jiang Yi's career success in the first half of her life, apart from the times, chance, and opportunity, there are three words left: talent, learning, and knowledge. These three formed the foundation of his strength as a grassroots writer who became famous all over the world and eventually became a master of literature.
“Talent” in a broad sense refers to unique imagination and creativity as far as the field of art is concerned. It is based on learning and knowledge. Without learning (extensive knowledge intake), difficult knowledge (excellent insight and opinions), it is impossible to support so-called talent — comprehensive comprehensive creative ability. Here, learning and knowledge are progressive; they nurture, relate, and support each other.
“Talent” in the narrow sense refers to the high sensitivity of artistic thinking and the ability to put it into practice and create works of art. As the French sculptor Rodin said, “So-called masters are people like this. They use their own eyes to see what others have seen, and they can find beauty in what others are used to.” (“Rodin's Art Theory”) The first reason the artist became an artist is that he has different eyes from ordinary people. He can see the truth of the world and see hidden life, power, and beauty in places that ordinary people can't see. For example, when Zheng Banqiao painted bamboo, he had the “bamboo in his eyes”. Through his superb skills, he drew the “bamboo in the chest”, which encouraged “vigor in the heart,” and then created “bamboo in the hand” to complete artistic creation. Talented, knowledgeable, and skilled here, they can transform stones, soil, plants, fish and bugs, and even spiders, mice, and cow dung into visual aesthetic images and works of art. This is an artist.
Speaking of “talent” — the formation of Jiang Mi's unique temperament as a poet and artist, apart from the unknown factors of harmony between heaven and earth and natural nature, the origin of a broken family is an important factor in giving him a kind, delicate, and persistent personality as an artist. The huge contrast between the glory of his ancestors and the bleak reality made his heart sting all the time, yet he was unwilling to sink down. This is a deep source of psychological motivation for him to look at the world from a unique perspective, take a different approach in the Song dynasty rhetoric scene where the extravagant and graceful factions stand side by side, and pioneer the “empty language school.”
Second, “learn.” “The shade of the gate extends from generation to generation, and the jade hall blossoms” is the dream of all Shihua families. Therefore, urging the children to follow the dial, work diligently to learn, and maintain the family style is the perseverance of the Huanshi family. Judging from Jiang Yi's poetry, regardless of “Shijing,” Chu Suo, Han Fu, or Jin and Tang style, he must have been infiltrated from an early age. This can be said to be a family legacy.
Let's talk about “knowledge.” What kind of ideas does a poet have, are inherently acquired, and the causes are complicated. Jiang Mi's fortune was that the creation of heaven and earth gave him a mind that dared to think independently. This enabled him to have sufficient ideological power to break out of his own artistic path in a pattern where the Southern Song literary world confronted each other.
Poetry is Jiang Mei's most confident art. In his poetry, we can see his artistic proposition: not to follow suit, not to imitate. In the preface to “Shiraishi Taohito's Poetry Collection,” he proposed that the poems are sounds of nature, there is no fixed format, and that “there is no body in the poem; “The 300 chapters” all speak for themselves. In “Shiraishi Taohito's Poems,” he said, “The words of a family have their own flavor... Although the imitator's language is similar, it doesn't have any rhyme.”

After five generations of turmoil in the late Tang dynasty, two trends of thought were always popular in the Song dynasty. One advocated retro, and the other advocated opening up something new. Retro artists seek the continuation of the moral system, emphasize the transmission of context, and history must not be broken; the pioneers seek to expand the moral system, citing Bai Juyi's purpose of “writing articles from time to time, songs and poems to be written together”, believing that one generation should have one generation of literature, try to avoid conformism, and not fall out of place.
In the group seeking to open up new things, there are also those who advocate borrowing lineage and turning the old into the new. As advocated by Wong Ting Kin, the so-called “turning a stone into gold” law - “a phrase made of no easy intention, the so-called bone replacement method; described by peeking into its meaning, the so-called method of stealing a fetus” (Huihong's “Tales of Lengzai Night”), is a new saying, or a phrase that is old and new. His philosophy is that literature should be “written for good,” “poetry is based on curiosity, and is interesting,” “My writing is like a source of dendrobium, and it can come out of any place”; “and it twists and turns, takes shape with things, and is unknown. Those who know often do what they want to do, often stop at the end of what is necessary”. What is particularly noteworthy is that Su Shi emphasized that “my calligraphy is impossible” and “although I am not good at writing, Xiao Shu is nothing like me. It is often said that if you can get it through, you can't learn”. “Continued Book Score”: The Final Settlement to a Sad Life
Calligraphy is an art that “people get old”: it requires refinement in life, and in turn, “refines” life.
Jiang Mui had a very early relationship with calligraphy, but she started her career very late.
It is a rule that has not changed for thousands of years to learn a book, go deep and get it right, then hit it out with the greatest courage. Jiang Kui, who was born in Shuxiangmen, knows all of this, of course. However, the poet's youthful restlessness kept him out of the wall of calligraphy art for a long time. According to his own account, before the age of 40, he had dreams many times, hoping that calligraphy would be rewarded, but every time he posted, it made him feel like a wall. In her twenties, in Changsha, Xiao Dezao gifted “Dingwu Lanting”, a rare book on the theme “Dingwu Lanting” from his old collection. But everyone's Tezawa didn't invigorate the young poet's heart — in the face of Yoshitaka Furumoto, who was so stubborn and unmoved.

The refinement of the years changed his body, which had been uncertain over the years: in the past, the life of an abstract black and white world seemed to have suddenly opened up to calligraphy. At this point, a life that has gone through ups and downs and had glory has come to an end.
The inspiration for “Continuing the Book Book” should come from three important works in the history of calligraphy: the first is “Book Score” by Tang Sun Guo Ting, the second is “Shugan” by Tang Zhang Huaiyi, and the third is “Continued to Break the Book” by Zhu Changwen of the Northern Song Dynasty. There was Zhu Changwen's “Continued Book Break” before Jiang Kui's “Continued Book Score” later. There is some kind of ideological connection here.
However, “Shugan Shu Shu” is a follow-up to the history of calligraphy in the same form as “Shugan”. It is linked in time, and the two are a perfect combination. At the time, it could form a complete history of calligraphy. “Continued Book Score” is not modeled after “Book Score,” so I'll talk about its related theories below. To be precise, it is a new chapter under the name of “continuing the book.” This is a self-structured monograph on calligraphy theory. This covers not only calligraphy, history, and origins, but also calligraphy, technology, theory, aesthetics, and even lifestyle applications (such as book cards); from the most basic problem — “copying books,” to the integration of “temperament,” “Continued Book Score” is covered one by one. Therefore, it can be said that Sun Guoting and Jiang Kui stood at different historical points and each completed the work entrusted to them by history and the times: “Book Book Book Book” is the peak of theoretical exploration and pioneering an era, while “Continuing the Book Book Book” is the peak of dissemination and explanation of the times. One is exploring and pioneering, and full of new theories; the other is explaining and interpreting later, popularizing them one by one. However, Jiang Yi's calligraphy was well-cultivated, and her explanations were also creative, so she was sought after by many later calligraphers. Zhao Mengyi and Yu He from the Yuan Dynasty, and Shen Can, Feng Fang, and Zhu Yunming from the Ming Dynasty all transcribed the full text or excerpts of “The Book Continuation”; Zhao Mengyi wrote “The Book Continuation” for friends, and copies were distributed overseas.
What is particularly important to note here is that “Continued Book Score” is a manuscript that Jiang Yi painstakingly devised during her lifetime. He died suddenly when the manuscript was not finished. “Continued Book Score,” which is currently being used at home and abroad, is not Jiang Yi's original book, but a “collated edition” made by writers and scholars from the Ming and Qing dynasties after several adjustments and finally formed during the Kangxi years.
If you think of “Continued Book Score” as a work that Jiang Mui has already completed, then there are many questions that cannot be explained.

If it were an official draft, it would obviously be too unserious and unreasonable. Also, there are 20 chapters in the book. The logic of the narrative has not been completely straightened out; it is not fully consistent with the book theory. The above problems have already been noticed and pointed out in the “Summary of the Complete Works of Siku” compiled by Qing people. If it were a finished draft, these issues would be unimaginable. It can only be understood that Jiang Yi's “Continued Book Score” manuscript as seen by Xie Tse is an unfinished manuscript. Jiang Mi probably died of a sudden emergency during a fire, moving, and struggling to write “Continued Book Score.”
Therefore, it can be said that “Continued Book Score” is the last sustenance of Jiang Mui's life and the final settlement of a sad life.
It can be described as a trilogy of Jiang Yi's life when young people learn poetry; having fun in middle age and hitting walls everywhere; studying books in old age and becoming famous again. How much of this is a mixture of sorrow and joy is inexhaustible.
