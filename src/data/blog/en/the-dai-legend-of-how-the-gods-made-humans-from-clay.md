 Many ethnic groups have myths and legends about gods using earth to create humans. For example, the Han people say that the woman made people, and the Dai people are no exception; there is also a legend that God made people.
According to the legend of the Dai people, when heaven and earth were first formed, there was only bare land and vast ocean water, and there were no humans, animals, or plants. The god higher in the sky then sent two gods to earth with fairy gourds. They both broke apart the fairy gourd and scattered the gourd seeds on the sky and the earth. The sun, moon, and stars appeared in the sky, and the earth had forests and various other plants.
However, there were still no people on Earth, so the two gods made adults out of clay in their own image. One made a man, and the other made a woman. God gave life, soul, and vitality to men and women, baptized by sunshine, wind and rain, and made them husband and wife.
After the clay people became living people, at first they couldn't talk and had no thoughts, so God taught them to talk and think, and named them “people.” Since then, there have been people on Earth who have gradually learned to work, live, and recuperate under God's guidance.
Then, using mud, God created tens of thousands of species of birds and animals, such as elephants, horses, cows, sheep, pigs, dogs, chickens, ducks, tigers, leopards, wolves, mackerels, and birds, and gave them different names, taught them different sounds, and released them into the ocean and forest. Since then, there have been aquatic animals such as fish, shrimp, crab, and shellfish in the ocean, and various animals and insects on land.
