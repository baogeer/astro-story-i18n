 Legend has it that Qu Yuan has a cousin named Qu Yigu. She is a poor fisherman who grew up drinking the Three Gorges. She is handsome, smart, and has a strong personality. She once sailed a fishing boat to welcome the wind and waves in Xiling Gorge; she accompanied Qu Yuan up mountains and mountains, listened to folk songs, and picked Chu style, and helped Qu Yuan write the poem “Orange Song” in her hometown orange grove. Qu Yuan returned to his hometown from Yingdu to recruit troops to fight the invading Qin army. Qu Yigu immediately led the fishermen and boaters on the Xiajiang River, first raised a flag against Qin Baochu, and called for war against Qu Yuan.
One day, Qu Yigu had a dream. She saw a beautiful sparrow flying around her head while shouting, “My brother -- go back -- yo, my brother -- go back -- yo!” Later, the little finch flew away, and Qu Yigu pulled out her leg to chase her, and chased him to the mouth of Xiling Gorge, and disappeared. She heard someone say these two words: “The river reversed 3,000 miles, and Qu Yuan died in the fish's stomach.” At this point, Qu Yigu saw a big fish popping up in the river, carrying Qu Yuan's corpse on its back.
The next day, Qu Yigu told the sisters in the village about this dream. The sisters ran to the mouth of Xiling Gorge with Qu Yigu. While washing clothes, they shouted, “My brother -- go back -- yo, my brother -- go back -- yo!” In the blink of an eye, it was dark, like a flood was about to come. A wave of waves from the riverside slapped it and flooded the stone pier where the sister guys were tacking clothes. They looked at the surface of the river in horror, only to see something that looked like a banana leaf swirling through the waves. Looking closely, ho, it turned out to be the fin of a big fish. Qu Yigu then said to the big fish with “ah-ho,” “Fish, fish, if you carry my brother back, come to the shore!” As soon as the conversation fell, the big fish actually swam to the shore. Once on the stone beam, it suddenly jumped wildly. The two lanterns shed tears with their big eyes, then gently flapped their gills, showing off their white, bright bellies.
My aunt stared at the big fish's belly. She conveniently removed the flat hairpin from her head and cut open the fish's stomach. A stream of blood came out of the fish's stomach, gradually revealing a red coffin. The sister guys jumped up and uncovered the coffin. At first glance, what was lying inside was Dr. Qu Yuan's body.
The sister slit the fish's stomach, let the fish live, then carried the red coffin back to her hometown for burial. She cried and shouted all the way: “My brother -- go back -- yo, my brother -- go back -- yo!”
Grandma Qu was full of grief and anger; the more she cried, the more sad she was. Suddenly, she hit the tombstone and turned into a bird with a red mouth and golden green feathers. It flew through the golden sun and into the jungle. She is still shouting, “My brother is back, my brother is back...”
Since then, every year during the Dragon Boat Festival, Qu Yuantuo in Guizhou has held a dragon boat race. The witty Gong and the brave rider (paddler) all shouted the trumpet in the sound of drums: “My brother... go back! My brother--go back,”
That sacred fish, firstly because of a stomach injury, and secondarily because she cared about Qu Yuan's hometown. Since then, it has never been able to go up to the beach or go down to the blue beach. It has stayed in Xiling Gorge forever, and has to go on a trip to Qu Yuan Tuoli every year.
