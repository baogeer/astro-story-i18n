 Legend has it that the Emperor Qianlong in the Qing Dynasty loved to enjoy the mountains and water, and was also very good at poetry, and wrote joint inscriptions wherever he went. Once, when he went to Jiangnan, he dressed himself as an ordinary person and went to the public for a private visit.
He went to a place and saw that a farmhouse was hosting a happy event. The Qianlong Emperor made an ingenious move and asked the attendant to take out three copper coins and write another one to send together as a congratulatory gift. The synonym is:
Three copper gift gifts. If you don't think they are scarce, don't accept them; if you receive them, you love wealth
At this point, the owner of the farmhouse can be in trouble. It's not good to accept it, and neither should complain about this uninvited guest. Coincidentally, when the farmhouse owner's younger son saw it next to him, he said to his father that he should write a response. The father thought to himself, what do 10-year-olds know, and should they be right for each other? While I was hesitating, my son had already written down the following link:
Two cottages welcome guests. Don't come if you're afraid of being poor; the visitors are delicious
The response was very measured and generous, showing that the farmers treated people sincerely. As a result, the Qianlong Emperor instead made it difficult; he couldn't stay, and he couldn't leave. Fortunately, he wasn't wearing a robe, and he wasn't afraid of losing his identity, so he went in under the pretext that he could write about contacts.
