“The Empress Dowager and I” is a manuscript written by Baron Emmond Barquess before his death in 1944. It is written by a handsome 29-year-old young man from an exotic country who falls in love with a 67-year-old emperor of power. After reading this book, Yan Lianke, a famous writer from mainland China, even called it “Strange book! Strange book!” However, there are also those who accuse it of “deliberately and methodically falsifying evidence and stealing the world's name,” which completely harms customs.
According to Wang Xiaoge, the translator of this book, Emmond Bacchus was born in 1873 in Richmond, Yorkshire, England. His ancestors were once prominent Quirks. As the eldest son, Barquess inherited the title of baron and attended Oxford University. Bakshi came to Beijing in 1898. He is a linguistic genius. In addition to his native English, he is fluent in various languages such as Japanese, Latin, Russian, Chinese, Manchu, and Mongolian, and soon became an interpreter for “The Times” and the British Foreign Office. In 1903, the Qing government promoted him as Professor of Law and Literature at Beijing Normal University (later Peking University). A year later, he became a British Foreign Office commissioner.
Pak Si's friends described him as having an eccentric personality and charming demeanor. Everyone who has seen him says he is chatty and fun. But he's also a hermit. He has lived in Beijing for 45 years, away from the protection of the embassy area. He dressed and lived in a completely Chinese style, resisted dealing with foreigners, and tried his best to avoid contact with Westerners.
In January 1944, 71-year-old Ba Kesi died of illness in Beijing. The year before her death, with funding from her friend Dr. Hopeley from Switzerland, Bach Kesi wrote “The Past Is Gone” in the form of a memoir about her youth in Europe and “The Empress Dowager and I”, which she experienced living in China in the late Qing Dynasty. However, due to war and other factors, and extremely controversial content, the manuscript was delayed and difficult to publish. Hopley transferred multiple copies of the manuscript to the British Museum in London, the National Library in Paris, and the Harvard University Library in Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA. He died in 1973, and his friend left the manuscript to the Bordelian Library at Oxford University, which he loved during his lifetime.
In 2007, in the “Science and Education Watch” program on Beijing TV, the Deputy Director of the Beijing Palace Museum Library revealed to Si for the first time that Cixi had had an international relationship that had lasted 6 years.
It was this report from Beijing TV that focused everyone's attention on the person Emmond Bakshi.

Conversation with translator Wang Xiaoge —
The literary value of “The Empress Dowager and I” is greater than the value of historical materials
Guangzhou Daily: How difficult was the process of translating this book?
Wang Xiaoge: The author is a rare linguistic genius. The entire manuscript is in English mixed with French, Latin, Russian, Greek, Japanese, and even authentic Chinese. What's even more amazing is that he doesn't just know a little bit of fur. He freely quotes the classics from several languages, and freely transforms with history, allusions, strange news, and anecdotes from various countries. You need to know that this book was written in 1943. The author was already 71 years old at the time. He wrote it from full memory without any reference materials, let alone an internet or search engine, which is amazing. Of course, this also makes translation extremely difficult. With the help of an American editor, I first compiled all languages into English (except Chinese in the original book) and then translated it into Chinese by me. In order to correspond to the quaint nature of the original text, I used half-written, half-white language.
Guangzhou Daily: As a translator, what do you think of the original writer's life in China?
Wang Xiaoge: The glorious period of Barkessi's career was in 1910. In 1910, Barkessi and “Times” reporter Brand co-wrote the book “China Under the Empress Dowager,” which became popular all over the world immediately after publication. For the first time, the book uses a unique perspective to show readers images of Empress Dowager Cixi, the last powerful ruler in Chinese imperial history, and the shaky Qing dynasty under her rule. The following year, the Qing dynasty came to an end, and the author became famous in Japan. Bakusi and Brand also co-authored “Memoirs of the Beijing Court”, which is highly praised in academia.
Bakshi passed away in January 1944. He was supposed to return to silence with people's respect. I didn't expect the English historian Hugh Trevor Roper to revisit the old story. In 1976, Trevor Roper published “A Hidden Life: The Mystery of Sir Emmond Barkes” (later reprinted and changed to the more well-known name “The Beijing Hermit”). The book accuses Bak Kesi of deliberately and methodically falsifying evidence and stealing fame. His work is an obscene imagination of an autistic homosexual, and it is his “last vent to suppress his distorted sexual desires.” With the publication of “The Hermit in Beijing,” Emmond Barkos and everything he did about Chinese scholarship were thrown into the pile of old paper. He became a wild story in history, an erotic joke. Not to mention that a few years later, Trevor Roper's academic reputation and authority were questionable because he wrote “The Hitler Diary.” The mere opinion of one person erased all the value of this book, which was inevitably due to choking.
Guangzhou Daily: How to understand Cixi's love history with the author of this book?
Wang Xiaoge: The accusations about this book mainly focus on two points. One is “untrue,” and the other is “pornographic.” In terms of these two aspects, I think I can understand it this way:
As for the accuracy of the historical materials involved in the book, it is difficult to fully summarize in one word true or false. There may be a history of his relationship with Cixi, but it is unknown whether it was so frequent and intense; the major historical facts he described, such as the deaths of Cixi and Guangxu, may be heartwarming, but his descriptions of royal nobility and life in the market are detailed.
As a translator, I would like to emphasize that the literary value of this book far exceeds the value of its historical materials. The language of this book is fluent and elegant. In between writing, the author often changes his pen and pen, or is lyrical or commentative. The style of writing is varied and rich in content. So if we don't look at it as a historical document but as a novel, then it's definitely an extraordinary book.
Regarding “pornography,” the “Editor's Foreword” mentions that the era described in the author's book was a time when masculine style flourished. Although it cannot be said that it was a work adapted to the scene, at least homosexual works already had a foothold. Homosexual novels such as “Flower Spectrum” and “The Treasure Book of Flowers” were already popular in the same era. Therefore, we can think of this book as another masterpiece on this subject.
Does Cixi, who is in her sixties, have 20-year-old skin?
After the book was published abroad and in Hong Kong and Taiwan, it immediately caused heated controversy. The focus of the controversy was the authenticity of the book. As a history of private writing, many of the records in the book are very different from those recorded in official history, and readers are full of doubts. In particular, the records about Emperor Guangxu being strangled by people sent by Empress Dowager Cixi and the death of Empress Dowager Cixi being shot dead by Yuan Shikai are completely different from history books. How true are they?
Also, the book also describes the skin of Cixi, who is in her sixties, and girls like those in her 20 years old! Is this real?
Yu Shanpu, an expert on Qing history and former deputy director of the Qing Dongling Cultural Relics Management Office, once said that the historical Cixi, widowed at the age of 27, paid great attention to physical maintenance, especially cosmetic procedures. Around 1902, Cixi was surrounded by two sisters, De Ling and Yong Ling, who had just returned from France. They acted as Cixi's former female officials. While serving Empress Dowager Cixi, they saw with their own eyes the body of Empress Dowager Cixi. Her body was plump and skin was tender.
In “The Legend of Yu Xiang,” De Ling praised Cixi, who was in her sixties. She turned out to be a 20-year-old girl. She was simply a beautiful woman. In the two sections “Empress Dowager Dressing Table” and “The Jade Emperor's Dressing Table”, De Ling described Cixi's skin care and nourishment methods such as dyeing her hair, eating pearl powder, applying egg white, and eating human milk. Yu Shanpu wrote about Te Ling's section on Cixi drinking human milk, depicting the magical effect of human milk on the face. He felt that it was a bit false. He thought that De Ling was simply “gaining popularity.” However, when Yu Shanpu checked the “Qing Palace Archive” at Beijing's First Historical Archive, he unwittingly found a list of women in the palace who supplied Cixi with milk.
De Ling also wrote that Cixi also let a young woman who had just given birth to live in a deep palace with her child. Sometimes the cries of the child flew out of the palace wall. At the time, the scandal that Cixi had an illegitimate child broke out. Also, from the 1904 large dyed photographs of Cixi in the Beijing Palace Museum library, it can be seen that Cixi is old and still has beautiful eyes and a beautiful appearance.
