 Recently, archaeologists confirmed that the Jiangcun tomb on Bailuyuan in the eastern suburbs of Xi'an is the tomb of Emperor Han and Wen. So far, the locations of the 11 imperial tombs in Western Han have all been confirmed. Emperor Han Wen and his son Emperor Han Jing jointly pioneered the “Wenjing Governance”, which was China's first reign after entering the Dai Yi Dynasty.
Shigenong Soghan
In “Han Shu,” Bangu once commented on “the governance of the Chinese landscape”: “Han Xing, get rid of annoyances, and rest with the people. As for filial piety, in addition to being generous and thrifty, filial piety followed the business. For five to sixty years, Li Min was very kind when it came to changing customs and changing customs. Zhou Yuncheng and Kang, Chinese language, and scenery are beautiful.” According to Bangu, the “rule of the Chinese landscape” is comparable to the time when King Cheng and King Kang of Western Zhou reigned. At that time, the world did not need to be punished for more than 40 years. The people respected the law, lived and worked peacefully, and the style was very beautiful.
Changes in social customs did not come out of thin air, but were based on a certain economic foundation. The ancients knew this very well. There was a saying, “We knew the truth and manners; we knew honor and disgrace with enough clothing and food.” From 209 BC, Chen Sheng and Wu Guang rose to prominence in Daze Township, Liu Bang and Xiang Yu emerged in the peasant uprising in the late Qin, to the defeat of Xiang Yu in 202 BC. The four-year “Chu Han dispute” came to an end with Liu Bang's victory. Eight years of war and turmoil left the local atmosphere in Shenzhou seriously damaged, the economy was ruined, and people's livelihood was difficult.
After Liu Bang, the ancestor of Han Gao, took control of the world, the Tianzi Travel Agency was unable to gather four horses of the same color in horse-drawn carriages, and ministers sometimes didn't even have horse-drawn carts; they could only use ox carts. From this, it can be seen that the economy in the early Han Dynasty declined. In order to restore the economy and society, the Han Dynasty adopted the administrative philosophy of “doing nothing to govern” and placed “rest with the people” as a top priority. Healing by doing nothing is not about doing nothing. Instead, don't act arrogantly. You should decide what to do and what not to do based on your current situation, and what to do first and then do later. “Huainan Zi”, a book written by Han Chu, criticizes the concept of “those who do nothing, are silent, indifferent, cannot be promoted,” and endorses that due to sentiment guidance, like water flowing to a low place, people build canals to guide it into the fields. Just like crops are planted in spring and fall, people can harvest according to the rules of crop growth.
Ancient China was based on agriculture. The most important aspect of “resting with the people” in the early Han Dynasty was to encourage people to actively engage in agricultural production, which was not contrary to agricultural time. The Han Chutian tax rate is very low. The “15 taxes and 1” set by Han Gaozu was already lower than the “tithing tax” in the Warring States period. Emperor Han Wen was reduced to “30 taxes 1”. In 167 BC, he even ordered field tax exemptions. Since then, until Emperor Han Jing took office in 157 BC, he was exempt from field tax for more than 10 years. This is very rare in ancient Chinese history. After Emperor Han Jing ascended the throne, the “30 taxes and 1” tax system was restored.
In addition to field taxes, ancient farmers were also burdened with labor. Generally, during peasants' leisure time, Emperor Han Wen changed “once a year” to “once every three years.” Whether it is reducing farm taxes or reducing labor, the purpose is to allow people to invest more energy in agricultural production and live a good life at home, so that society can stabilize and save energy for larger undertakings.
On the one hand, Emperor Han Wen conveyed to the community the governing policy of putting agriculture first through pro-farming ceremonies, etc., and on the other hand, advocated frugality and opposed luxury in everyday life. Some people once offered Emperor Han Wen a millimeter horse, but Emperor Han Wen didn't accept it. He said, “When Tianzi travels, there is a Luanqi opening the road, and an attendant guard later. Under normal circumstances, I walk 50 miles every day. If I lead an army, I only walk 30 miles every day. If I travel with an army, I only walk 30 miles every day. Where can I go first?” Emperor Han and Wen also ordered the country not to donate rare treasures.
“Historical Chronicles” says that during the 23 years that Emperor Han and Wen reigned, “the palace gardens kept dogs and horses and had nothing to gain. There were inconveniences, and they were easy to relax to benefit the people.” Emperor Han Wen planned to build a terrace. The terrace was a building higher than a flat ground where they could climb far away. At that time, there was quite a culture of building a high platform among the members of the clan. Hou Yichun of Western Han built five high platforms to visit. It cost quite a bit of money to build a high platform. Naturally, Emperor Han Wen knew, but when he heard that it would cost 100 yuan to build a terrace, he immediately cancelled the construction plan. He lamented: “I inherited the palace left behind by the emperor. How about building a high platform?”
leniency in prison
In the administrative measures of Emperor Han Wen and Emperor Han Jing, mitigating punishment was also an important element. Punishment is necessary as a means of governing society. The early Han dynasty had many roots in the Qin administration, and many severe punishments were applied.
Emperor Han Wen abolished the law of succession when he first took office. In his view, the law was fair and the sentence was fair and appropriate. The people naturally rejects good and evil, and the social culture also rejects good and evil, and punishes criminals as appropriate, but it is unfair to have innocent loved ones on the sidelines, which is also contrary to the fundamental purpose of the law to stop atrocities and guide the people towards good.
Emperor Han Wen also abolished cruel corporal punishment. The historically famous “Ti Ying Saves Her Father” mentioned this. Ti Ying is the daughter of Qi Guo's Taicang Ling Chunyi. When she committed a crime, Ti Ying couldn't bear her father's punishment. She went to Chang'an to express her feelings to the imperial court, saying that once a person is mutilated, their body can no longer be recovered. She would not join the government office as a slave in exchange for her father to be exempted from physical punishment, preserve her physical integrity, and leave the possibility for a fresh start and return to normal life in the future. Emperor Han Wen was moved by his filial piety after learning about Ti Ying's affections. You need to know that Emperor Han Wen himself was also a filial son. When her birth mother, Empress Bo, became ill, she personally fed him with soup. What is even more invaluable is that Emperor Han Wen was able to demand from himself based on his affections, “Today's law has three physical punishments, and more than rapes. What is the cause? Isn't it because I have poor morals and don't know how to teach?” Emperor Han Wen ordered the abolition of corporal punishment and replaced it with flogging.
However, flogging during the time of Emperor Han and Wen was still a very heavy sentence, and there were irregularities during execution. During the time of Emperor Han Jing, the imperial court stipulated what kind of punishment to be used for flogging, flogging should only be applied to the prisoner's hips and not to other parts, and the number of flogs was reduced twice in a row. The first time, “500 flogging is 300, 300 is 200,” and the second time “flogging 300 is 200.” Emperor Han Jing once passed away. Prison is a national priority. Relevant officials must be careful. If local cases are not approved, they should hand them over to the imperial court to handle, “if they want to make those in prison feel leniency first.”
After the “reign of Wenjing”, the Han dynasty showed this kind of scene. On the economic side, “the money of the Beijing teacher was huge, and it was impossible to fix it; in Taicang, Chen Xiang was overflowing and accumulated.” The Han Dynasty copper coins were tied together with a rope in the middle, and the rope that strung the money was rotten, and it was impossible to figure out how much money was actually stored in the warehouse; in terms of social culture, “everyone loved themselves and broke the law again, and was disgraced before being humiliated.” They put benevolence and righteousness first, and hate doing things that make them ashamed of others.
The era of Emperor Han Wen and Emperor Han Jing was an era of energy conservation. Facing attacks by the northern Huns, the Han dynasty was basically on the defensive. Facing South Vietnam in the south, the Han dynasty maintained a good relationship with it. Emperor Han Jing settled the “Seven Kings Rebellion,” but did not completely resolve the issue of emperors and kings with the same surname. All of this has yet to be resolved by Emperor Han Wu. Emperor Han Wu relied on the strong economic and social foundations laid down by the “rule of the Chinese landscape” and was able to open up a big deal — Nanping South Vietnam, attacking the Huns from the North, enshouting the Order, and respecting Confucianism, a promising era has arrived.
Discovering Baring
Emperor Han Wen, known for his frugality, explained his fate during his lifetime. According to Sima Qian's “History Chronicles of Xiaoben”, Emperor Han Wen objected to his heavy burial at the time, leading to family bankruptcy and excessive mourning, which caused physical damage. He ordered a reduction in the time required for his mourning. In the past, according to the ceremonial order, the death requirement for nine months was reduced to 15 days, and the death requirement for five months was reduced to 14 days, etc. Regarding the construction of mausoleums, “all mausoleums are made of earthenware; they must not be decorated with gold, silver, copper, and tin; if they don't treat graves, they want to save money; don't annoy people.”
Baring is also written as Baling. It is named after being near BaShui. BaShui is a river located on the east side of Chang'an in “Eight Shui Circles Chang'an”. Baring often appears in poems. For example, Li Bai has a phrase “Remembering Qin 'e”: “The sound of a sigh sings, the Qin Empress will break the Moon of Qin Lou. When Qin Lou Yue grew willow, Baling got hurt goodbye. Enjoy the Yuanshang Autumn Festival, and the Xianyang Ancient Trail was completely noisy. There was no sound and dust, the westerly wind was shining, and the Han Family Mausoleum.” The area where Baring is located is on the main transportation route in and out of Chang'an. I don't know how many differences this place has seen.
The ancients knew the area where the tomb of Emperor Han Wen was located, but the exact location was a mystery. There is no specific location in the “Historical Records” and “Han Shu” books. Instead, a scholar from the Yuan dynasty named Luo Tianxiang clearly wrote in “Changan Zhi” that Baring is under the mouth of Phoenix in the northern part of Bailuyuan. Scholars from the Ming and Qing Dynasties accepted this view. Bi Yuan, a university student who wrote works such as “The General Guide to Capital Renewal and Governance” in the Qing Dynasty, also erected a famous monument under Fenghuang's mouth when he was an official in Shaanxi, telling the world that it was the tomb of Emperor Han and Wen.
Is that really true? In 2011, archaeologists carried out archaeological exploration work on Fenghuangzui. Because of clear records from the ancients, archaeologists also initially thought this was Baring, but from the bottom of the mountain to the mountainside to the top of the mountain, they found no traces of manual excavation, proving that there were no tombs under Phoenix's mouth. True faith is better than no book. Archaeology has pointed out a mistake that has been around for over 700 years, yet the exact location of Baring has not been found. At this point, the Jiangcun Tomb, two kilometers away from Fenghuangzui, came into the eyes of archaeologists.
In 2002, six pieces of Western Han pottery appeared at an auction in the US. These six pieces of pottery came from the wrong place; they were stolen by tomb thieves and then resold abroad. Twenty minutes before the auction began, we successfully removed them from the auction list. After a year, six terracotta figures returned home. These six terracotta figures are originally thought to have come from Empress Dou's tomb at Bailuyuan. Empress Dou is the wife of Emperor Han Wen. The mausoleum of Empress Dou is a typical Western Han Emperor's mausoleum. The surface is very similar to an ancient meter “bucket”, so it is known as a “covered clay seal”. Later, archaeologists confirmed that the terracotta figures actually came from a tomb next to Empress Dou Mausoleum, because it was in Jiangcun.
After exploring the Gangchon Tomb, archaeologists were surprised to find all kinds of evidence showing that it was a high-grade tomb of the Western Han emperor. After years of hard work by archaeologists, it is now possible to confirm that the Jiangcun Tomb was the tomb of Emperor Han and Wen. People of different statuses also enjoyed different burial methods after death. The tomb of the Gangchon Tomb has a burial path on the east, west, north, and south sides. Overlooking its shape from the sky, it is like Asia in traditional Chinese characters. This is an important piece of evidence. Another important piece of evidence is that archaeologists have discovered a rammed earth fence about 1,200 meters long from east to west and about 860 meters wide from north to south. The Western Han people agree that they should be “married and married” and should also be buried together after death, using the “same tomb” burial system, that is, the couple each have their own tomb, but in a cemetery, they can only be buried with Empress Dou.
There are no tall earthworks on the surface of the Gangchon Cemetery. This does not seem to match the size of the tomb of the Western Han Emperor, but it confirms the “Historical Record” that Emperor Wen of Han requested “no treatment for the tomb,” and the artifacts unearthed in the outer storage pit of Baring are also quite simple, confirming the frugality of Emperor Han and Wen. It should be pointed out that the archaeologists did not excavate the tomb chamber; instead, they excavated 8 of the 115 outer hidden pits around the tomb and discovered a large number of terracotta figures, gold and silver objects, and famous organ marks. The outer hidden pit looks like an underground government office.
On the banks of Weishui in the northern suburbs of Xi'an, there is the Yangling Mausoleum of Emperor Han Jing. The Hanyang Mausoleum Museum is now a national-level museum, and visitors can see what the Western Han Emperor Mausoleum is like here. Archaeologists have unearthed 10 outer hidden pits in Yangling. The museum builders covered the outer hidden pits with thick glass. Visitors can walk on them, and if they look down, they can see the dense arrangement of terracotta figures in the outer hidden pits 2,000 years ago.
These terracotta figures are now naked, but at the time of burial, their bodies were wrapped in silk and other fabrics. As time passed, their coat gradually eroded, yet the terracotta figures still retained their old demeanor. They either pouched their lips, or bowed their eyebrows, or smiled...
Bringing back smiles from 2,000 years ago and bringing people closer to history today is probably the charm of archaeology.
