 Song Mausoleums are located in Shaoxing, Zhejiang, and Gongyi, Henan. The tomb area in Gongyi, Henan, is the “Eight Tombs” of the Northern Song Song Dynasty and the tombs of many emperors, relatives of the emperor, and Xun Chen. The tomb area in Shaoxing, Zhejiang is the tomb of six emperors and several empresses of the Southern Song Dynasty, known as the “Six Tombs of Southern Song.”
Kaifeng, Henan, the capital of the Northern Song dynasty, was located in Gongxian County, far from Bianliang in Beijing. The main reason for this is that the landscape is beautiful, the soil is excellent, and the water level is low, making it suitable for digging tombs and rich burials. Ling District has Mount Song in the south and the Yellow River in the north. It can be called “resting on the Yellow River and stepping on Mount Song”. It is regarded by Feng Shui as an auspicious place to “come from high mountains and water.” Zhao Yanwei once described it like this in “Yunlu Manshi”:
The seven (8) tombs are all north of Songshan Mountain and south of Luoshui. Although Gangfu is not very tall, they are in both situations. From the western slope of Yongan County, you can see the three tombs of (Yong) An (Yong) Chang, and (Yong) Hee in Pyeongcheon. Berlin is weave, Mount Wan'an, and there are few places. The Sanling Mountains are close to Berlin. The ground is as flat as the palm of your hand. The ground is 13 hectares, 12 miles long.
The Yong'an Mausoleum, Yongchang Mausoleum, and Yongxi Mausoleum mentioned above have good feng shui, but the Yongding Mausoleum, the tomb of Song Zhenzong and Zhao Heng, had poor feng shui. As for the reason for the fall of the Northern Song Dynasty, there is an argument that this was due to poor Feng Shui in Song Zhenzong's tomb. According to the Song Dynasty's “Chronicles of Chunzhu”, Xu Renwang, a native of Baiyun Mountain in Shinzhou (present-day northwest of Shangrao County, Jiangxi Province), made a public statement and discussed with Prime Minister Ding Jin Gong (the tomb of Song Zhenzong). In the first year of Gan Xing (AD 1022), Ding Jingong, who was in charge of the mountain mausoleum (formerly the tomb of the emperor), did not listen to Feng Shui master Xu Renwang's words; he stubbornly determined that the tomb of Zhenzong was located behind Mount Niutou. Xu Renwang advocated using the land in front of Mount Niutou, while Ding Jingong insisted on using the land behind Mount Niutou and was not allowed to replace it.
Xu Renwang once requested that he be placed under house arrest at Dali Temple (Dali Temple is the place of administration of justice, and the lord of Dali Temple is the official of Zheng Sanpin). After three years, he can verify who is right and who is wrong, but he still cannot change Ding Jingong's erroneous approach. Xu Renwang once described the damage “behind the mountains” in his statement at the time: “There was a long flow of water, and the disaster occurred during the Bingu year; Ding Feng directly hit the disaster. When the end of the year ended in the end of the year, a fire broke out in the state, and the county was stolen.” Not surprisingly, the Golden Army invaded the capital city of Kaifeng in the first year of Bingwu Jingkang (that is, 1126 AD). In the year of Ding Wei (that is, the first year of Jianyan, 1127 AD), Song Huizong and Song Qinzong were all taken prisoner by Jin soldiers, and the Northern Song Dynasty fell. Due to war and turmoil, fires continued in the counties, and about half of the land became an area where bandits were rampant. According to historical materials, Ding Mu Nian “burned down like a disaster. The situation was still endless; half of the area was stolen.” Xu Renwang's prophecy certainly came true.
