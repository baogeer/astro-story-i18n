 A long, long time ago, Tenjin took away the fire of the Hani people. The story unfolds from here, leading to the story of Minza, a young Hani man who went to Tenjin to get back Tinder.
On the way, Minza met a dead old tree stump and a frozen goddess of streams. He saved them with water and dirt brought from his hometown and set back on the path he was looking for. A small antelope sent him half of the journey. Minza gradually entered a fantastic situation, such as an invisible staircase, a dark cave, and the decisive beauty and suffering of the American fantasy drama “Frozen”. He stepped on the Golden Ladder and passed through the stone rhino that can fight, and finally saw Tenjin.
Minza shouted, “Tenjin! That's our fire source! Please give it back to me!”
Minza didn't think Tenjin was supreme; what he wanted was the fire that originally belonged to them. He is neither arrogant nor inferior.
However, Tenjin said:
People think they are superior to everything, but they don't know that I am the only one who is superior to everything! Minza, I'll take away your fire and make you cry, shout, and face death. Only then will you know how animals that have never used fire live!
Tenjin's words stand at the highest point of morality and condemn humanity. Humans have original sin, and human arrogance has led to the destruction of nature.
At the end of the story, we'll find out that “minza” means “fire” in Hani.
This is both an adventure story, a growth story, and a story about how humans and nature develop in harmony. It is also a traditional story of the Hani nation.
