 Li Guang (? —Former 119), a famous Western Han general, a native of Chengji, Longxi. His ancestor, Li Xin, was a famous general in Qin. He led the Qin army to chase Yan Taizidan all the way to eastern Liaoning. In 166 BC, the Huns invaded the border in a big way, and Li Guang joined the army to fight against the Huns. He fought bravely and killed many enemies, which was greatly appreciated by Emperor Han Wen. Nine years later, Emperor Han Jing ascended to the throne, and Li Guang was promoted to a cavalry general next to Emperor Jing. During the rebellion of King Wu and King Chu, Li Guang and followed Taiwei Zhou Yafu in the rebellion with Captain Zhou Yafu to conquer the rebellion, seize the rebel flag under Changyi City, and achieved remarkable combat success. After the rebellion of the kings subsided, Li Guang was transferred to the northwest border of Shanggu, Shanggun, Longxi, Yanmen, Dai County, and Yunzhong to fight against the invasion of the Huns.
Once, the Huns attacked Shangjun, and Emperor Jing sent a personal companion to Li Guangjun. This relative accompanied dozens of rider guards on a trip, and met three Huns knights along the way. As a result, all of the guards were shot dead, and their own companions also escaped with an arrow. When Li Guang heard the news, he immediately led 100 cavalry in pursuit, personally shot and killed two of them, and captured one. Having just tied up the captives, thousands of cavalry from the Huns arrived and saw Li Guang and others. They thought they were soldiers luring the enemy by the Han army and hurriedly occupied a plateau. Li Guang's hundred cavalry hurriedly got on the horse and wanted to flee. Li Guangchang said, “We are tens of miles away from the army; we must die if we escape! If we don't escape, the Huns think it's a trick to lure the enemy, and they will definitely not dare to attack us.” As a result, he led the soldiers to welcome them to the Huns cavalry. Two miles away from the front of the Huns, he ordered the soldiers to break down. The Huns couldn't figure out their intentions. They certainly didn't dare to attack. They only sent a general to test. Li Guang grabbed the front of the battle, shot him down, and then calmly returned to the team. By the middle of the night, the Huns thought that the Han army must have ambushed the night attack, so they drew troops.
In 140 BC, Emperor Han Wu ascended the throne and transferred Li Guang as Weiyang's Lieutenant. Four years later, Li Guang led his army out of Yanmen Pass and was surrounded by a multiplied army of Huns. The Huns glorified Li Guangwei and made sure their subordinates captured him. Li Guang was eventually wounded and taken prisoner due to being outnumbered. While being escorted, he flew over to seize enemy horses, shot and killed countless chasers, and finally returned to Han Camp. Since then, Li Guang has won the title of “General Han Zhifei” in the Huns army. After returning to the DPRK, Li Guang was removed from military service by the Han Empire and demoted to a common man.
A few years later, the Huns killed Taishou of western Liaoning and defeated General Han Anguk. Emperor Wu re-appointed Li Guang as Taishou of the North Right. The Huns heard that “General Fei” was guarding Youbeiping, and they didn't dare to commit the crime for several years.
Once, Li Guang went out hunting and saw a big rock in the grass. He thought it was a tiger and shot it with an arrow. When he took a close look, Fang knew he had hit a big rock, but the arrow hit it deep into the stone. He shot back at the stone with his bow, but he was never able to shoot an arrow into the stone again.
In 120 BC, Li Guang led 4,000 cavalry out of Ubeiping. Cooperate with Zhang Qian in his expedition to Hungary. The soldiers advanced hundreds of miles and were suddenly surrounded by 40,000 cavalry led by King Zuo Xianwang of the Huns. More than half of the Han soldiers were killed and wounded, and they almost ran out of arrows. Li Guang kept his soldiers out of control; he himself shot and killed several generals from the Huns in a row with his big yellow bow. The Huns were greatly frightened and were held back by Li Guang's bravery and did not dare to act recklessly. Until the next day, when the main forces of the Han army arrived, Li Guangjun was able to break out of the heavy siege.
In 119 BC, General Wei Qing led an army to attack the Huns, and Li Guang assumed the post of former general at the age of 60. After leaving the military, Wei Qing learned about Dan Yu's whereabouts from his captors. He wanted to get rid of Li Guang and achieve great success, so Li Guang's forward army merged into the right wing and left the East Wing. He brought his own Chinese army to chase the order. Li Guang's efforts were to no avail, so he led the army out of East China by joining forces with General Zhao Shiqi. I finally lost my way because the road was difficult to walk and there was no sound guide. At this point, Wei Qing fought and only escaped. Wei Qing had to return in vain, only to join the right-wing forces on his way back to the army. Wei Qing sent a close friend to express his condolences to Li Guang and asked him about how the right-wing forces lost their way. He said Wei Qing wanted to report to Tianzi, putting the blame for the loss on General Right Zhao Shiqi. Li Guang was honest; naturally, he did not agree. Wei Qing was overjoyed, and he also sent people to push Li Guang's staff to go to the Chinese army for questioning. Li Guang said, “They are not guilty. I am responsible for getting lost; I will go to trial myself.” Take full responsibility on yourself. After the visitors left, Li Guangwang, a general who lived and died together for many years, exclaimed: “I have been in the military since I was young and fought with the Huns for more than 70 years. I can't imagine being pressured by the General in this way. I am already old, so I can endure this kind of humiliation again!” Say, pull out the equipped sword and beat yourself off. A generation of famous generals so wronged and died tragically.
Tang dynasty poet Wang Changling Shiyun: “Qin Shi Ming Moon Han Shi Guan, and the people of the Long March have not returned. But the Dragon City will fly there, not called Humadu Yin Mountain.” The border war in the early Han Dynasty was a special war, and its natural and cultural characteristics determined the extraordinary hardship and cruelty of this war. Long-distance attacks away from the rear, sudden and stormy encounters, and solo battles with a large number of people have become frequent methods of combat. Li Guang is undoubtedly an outstanding general adapted to these combat characteristics. Extraordinary bravery, determination and adaptability, honesty and honesty, and a different way of governing the military, made him a generation famous general favored by his subordinates and disheartened by his enemies.
