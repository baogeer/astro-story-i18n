 In the late years of Dading, Kim Sejong had a man named Tieli in the middle office of Zhengcun in the north of Yangqu County in Taiyuan Province (present-day Beijiuyangqu, Taiyuan City, Shanxi), who specialized in hunting foxes.
One day, under the ancient tomb north of Zhangwanggou, he tied a pigeon as bait and hid himself in a tree waiting for his prey.
Two more days later, a group of foxes came and said in human language, “Tie Li Tie Li, are you using pigeons to make money for me? Your father and son are like a bunch of donkeys. They don't want to be farmers; they only learn how to kill. Our six parents, inside and outside, are all harmed by you as a thief. Today's number of days has arrived. Let's get down the trees; otherwise, there's nothing to say about cutting down the trees.” Then, there was a sound of a tug-of-war, and the fox was heard shouting, “Cook oil in a pan; cook this thief.” A fire also ensued.
Tieli was so afraid that she didn't know what to do. Seeing that the only thing in his back was a big axe, I thought if the trees fell, I would cut them down randomly.
As soon as it dawned, the fox was gone. Iron Li saw no saw marks on the tree, and there were several beef ribs on the side.
Tie-li knew it was the fox's change, and that night he went to the tomb again.
Before the second round, the fox arrived and cursed Tieli's whole family.
The fire pot was set on fire, set on fire, and thrown under a tree. The pot exploded, making a loud noise.
A group of foxes roamed around, caught by a net, and killed by Iron Li with an axe.
This story was seen in Jin Renyuan's question “Continuing Yi's Perseverance”. It may seem absurd, but the recorded phenomenon of the use of gunpowder and explosives in the Jin Dynasty is very precious.
