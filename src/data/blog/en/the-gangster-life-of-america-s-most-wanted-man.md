Irish teenager who wants to be the “boss”
Balger, 81, was born in an Irish-populated area in South Boston, USA. Since he was a child, he loved to fight in the streets and go racing in the alley. He craves adventure and dreams of leading the whole neighborhood to be their “boss.” Because Barger had gray hair, the locals nicknamed him “Bald Eagle,” but no one dared to call him that in person; otherwise, the response would have been a big punch.
However, there are also people in the area who miss Balger and think he is a Robin Hood-style thief. Barger is the most generous person in the area. Not only did he pay out of his own pocket to decorate stores for poor hawkers, but he also distributed turkeys to the townspeople on Thanksgiving Day. Brown, who has been a store owner in South Boston for most of his life, always remembers this. This incident has also always been imprinted in John Connolly's mind. He grew up as an FBI agent, yet he always regarded Balger as a spiritual idol.
American Mackenzie once wrote a book “Street Fighter,” which describes South Boston under Balger's control, saying, “The residents regard him as a leading man with endless power; unfortunately, he took the wrong direction.” As an adult, Barger treated “crime” as his job: robbing banks with a gun, robbing truck drivers, and threatening and defrauding companies, and doing almost nothing evil. In order to evade the hunt, Barger dyed his hair black and even did not hesitate to do plastic surgery. Despite this, he was arrested by police late one night in 1956.
While in prison, Barger's continued provocation and outrage angered the prison guards. Coupled with his attempt to escape, he was transferred to “Alcatraz Island” — a prison located in San Francisco Bay with an area of only 0.0763 square kilometers and deep cliffs on all sides, used by the US government to hold serious criminals. Barger was given the harshest form of “solitary confinement” here — only wearing underwear and staying in an unheated iron cage. But he invented a “technique”. He survived the cold winter by squatting in an iron cage, using only his elbows, knees, and fingertips to support his body, and as little as possible by being next to a cold iron cage. In 1963, the “Alcatraz” prison was closed by the US government, Balger was transferred to Leavenworth Prison in Kansas, and he was paroled in 1965.
Prototype of “Infernal Affairs”
After being released from prison, Balger returned to his old hometown of South Boston. In the early 1970s, he was introduced to join the notorious gang “Fuyushan Gang”. This gang does everything from selling drugs to reselling arms, and is also linked to many North American and European criminal organizations, including the Irish Republican Army. Because of his violent temperament and ruthlessness, Barger quickly gained a foothold in the “Winter Mountain Gang.”
At this point, he received an “olive branch” from the FBI. It was John Connolly, a young boy he met in an ice cream shop back then, and is now John Connolly, an FBI agent. Connolly persuaded Balger to become an FBI informant. Balger is responsible for reporting to the FBI, providing information on the movements of criminal gangs in the Boston area, and also using this to rule out dissidents and expand his territory; Connolly also provided news to Balger to change his plan of action in due course to avoid colliding with the police.
In the early 1980s, with this kind of “tacit cooperation,” the FBI successfully arrested Howie Winter, the former leader of the “Winter Mountain Gang.” Balger then became the boss and took control of the entire Boston underworld. Balger's legendary past was also brought to the screen by famous Hollywood director Scorsese. The police and bandit film “Infernal Affairs”, based on Balger, won four major awards at the 79th Academy Awards in 2007: Best Film, Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay, and Best Editing.
