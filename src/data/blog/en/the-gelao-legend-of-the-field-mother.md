 Yilao people live in mountains and forests. After many years, the number of people continued to increase, and there were fewer and fewer wild animals in the mountains and forests. Sometimes, their prey couldn't even fill their stomachs, and people became anxious and worked hard to find new food. At this point, the sky was dark and heavy rain soon began, and the river at the foot of the mountain where they lived began to rise. It continued to rain for three days and three nights, and the river reached the mountainside. At this point, the ancestors saw a dog struggling on the surface of the water trying to climb ashore, so the ancestors rescued the dog. After the dog went ashore, it ran to the leader of the ancestors and shook off the millet, millet, and baogu that had stuck to its back that had not been wetted with water. The leader picked up a bag of rice and put it in his mouth, and felt that it was fragrant and sweet, so he understood that this was a new source of food for the tribe. He carefully collected the remaining seeds and led the tribe to cultivate the land.
They specialize in finding flat, fertile land to reclaim, and carefully plant the seeds. Of course, their labor paid off. Over time, the flat land was soon reclaimed by the Yilao people. Thanks to a new source of food, the Yilao people quickly flourished. Just as the tribe was happy with the prosperity of the tribe, suddenly, one year, the seeds that had been planted grew yellow and small, and did not produce much food. For a while, the old, the weak, and the sick all starved to death. The strong and strong ones had a hard time digging up wild grass.
In order to be able to continue to survive, the leader brought his people to seek mountain gods, tree gods, and earth gods, hoping for a good harvest year in the coming year. However, the next year, the crops planted were still unharvested. Hunger made the tribe fewer and fewer, and the leader had no choice. He sat in a large field and cried loudly. At this point, an old man with white beards and white eyebrows floated in and asked the leader why he was crying. The leader said, “The crops that have been planted have been confiscated. Seeing that the tribes starved to death, can we stop crying?” The old man said, “This is your fault. Is there any reason why a son doesn't take care of his mother?” The leader said, “We don't have any. Our tribe has always worshipped our elders. There will never be anyone who is unfaithful, offends gods, and is retaliated by this.” The old man said, “The field is also your mother,” and when he finished speaking, he drifted away. The leader understood what the old man meant, led the people, opened a canal to divert water, and turned over the soil to fertilize. As a result, the crops had a good harvest again. It turns out that the old man was an incarnation of the god Earth.
Later, the Yilao people not only revered the fields like their old mother, but every year during the planting season, they also used the food produced in the fields to make pastries, spread incense, burn incense, and bow their heads in worship. The name is: Sacrifice the mother of the field.
