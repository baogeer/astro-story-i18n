 The Yuan dynasty has always maintained a special relationship with Goryeo. Since the Yuan Dynasty's ancestor Kublai, almost all of the kings of Goryeo have been the son-in-law of the Yuan dynasty emperor (although their queen was not necessarily the biological daughter of the Yuan dynasty), and beautiful Goryeo women also became a special tribute. After the fall of the Yuan dynasty, the Ming dynasty continued to receive this kind of tribute, and the later Qing dynasty was no exception. Rewarding a Korean woman as a concubine to the minister also became a blessing for the emperor. However, this relationship was not formed overnight, but gradually formed after a protracted war.
The country of Korea at the time was located on the Korean peninsula, not far from the northern part of the country where the Mongols began, so the Mongols soon became associated with Goryeo. In 1216, after a Khitan army originally attached to the Mongols failed in rebellion, wandered into Goryeo, occupied Dongseong in Goryeo, and used this as a stronghold to burn and loot everywhere. In 1218, Mongolian General Ha Zhen's army chased the Khitan rebels into Goryeo. King Goryeo sent General Zhao Chong to lead the army to assist and provide food and food to the Mongolian army. The following year, Jiangdong City surrendered, and Ha Zhen and Zhao Chong made an oath. Mongolia and Korea are fraternal countries. However, the Mongols are obviously not kind to this fraternal country. Since then, they have been sent to Korea to ask for all kinds of items (everything from fur, satin to pen, ink, paper, ink), making Goryeo people think “Mongolia is the most ferocious of Yidi.” Mongolia's massive extortion year after year has left Goryeo breathless, and has gradually heated up feelings of hostility towards Mongolia. In 1225, a Mongolian envoy was killed on his way back to Goryeo. Although the murderer was never found out, the Mongols still put this account on Goryeo's head. Killing the messenger is viewed by Mongolians as an unforgivable crime, and they have gone to war with other countries many times over this. However, due to the Mongolian army's advance to the West, Genghis Khan later died while conquering Western Xia, so Mongolia did not immediately retaliate against Korea. It wasn't until 1231 that the Mongolian army attacked Goryeo for the first time, and the Mongolian army, which fought against Lien Jie, went straight to the royal city of Goryeo. Goryeo Gojong had no choice but to send an ambassador to the peace. The Mongolian army extorted huge sums of money and then retired. However, each capital, province, and county still in Goryeo left 72 Daru Huachi (town guards) to control Goryeo indirectly.
However, the Goryeo people did not surrender because of this. The anti-Mongolian wave of Goryeo people never stopped, and Mongolian Daru Huaqing was killed one after another in many places. In order to avoid retaliation from Mongolia, Choi Yi, the head of Korea, decided to move the capital to Ganghwa Island. The enraged Mongolian army invaded Goryeo once again. Although the Mongolian army was invincible on land, they were helpless and did not practice water warfare, while the Koryo dynasty was strong and clear and vowed not to leave the island. Until 1257, Mongolia had sent troops to conquer Goryeo 7 times, but all returned to no avail. Of course, they robbed quite a few gold and silver treasures, beautiful women, and craftsmen. Although the Republic of Korea refused to surrender, the Mongolian army's repeated raids caused a profound disaster for the Korean people. Many places have attached their cities to Mongolia for fear of being slaughtered by the Mongolian army. Many ministers from Korea have also raised questions about the policy of confronting Mongolia to the end. At the same time, Mongolia is also planning to establish another proton from Goryeo as the new monarch, and once this becomes a fait accompli, the Goryeo Imperial Court faces the risk of being marginalized. In 1258, the ministers of Korea staged a coup d'état, killing the Choi clan, who advocated abandoning the island, and landed in Mongolia. In 1259, Mongolia and Goryeo reached a settlement agreement. The Mongolian army withdrew from Goryeo, and the Goryeo royal family also promised to move to land, and the state of war between the two countries finally came to an end.
In 1259, Emperor Goryeo entered North Korea at the request of Mongolia and went to the Mongolian military camp in Dahan Mongo, which was supervising the battle on the front line in Sichuan. When he arrived at Liupanshan, he received news that Mongo died in Diaoyu City in July (the official history says he was infected, and some say he was wounded and killed by Song Jun during the siege), so he diverted south and happened to meet Kublai, who had returned from the front line. At this point, Kublyeh was planning to go back and compete with Brother Ali for the position of Khan. Emperor Gaozi is visiting the shrine at this time, which is certainly a good sign. He was very happy and said to his subordinates, “Goryeo is thousands of miles away. Even if they are as wise as Tang Taizong, they cannot be surrendered by his own conquest. Now, the Emperor Gaozi has taken the initiative to attach himself to me. Isn't this God's intention to make me sweat?” As a result, Kublai asked Wang to travel north with himself to reach Kaiping together. Soon, Goryeo Gojong died, and Goryeo Seizi returned to China to take the throne. This is Goryeo Wonjong. However, after Yuanzong succeeded to the throne in 1260, the country was unstable, and the powerful men Lim Yan and Kim Joon always controlled the administration of the DPRK. However, as Mongolia invaded Goryeo many times, anti-Yuan sentiment within Goryeo continued to rise. The Mongolian king was already very dissatisfied. In addition, when Kublai invaded the South and Southern Song, Goryeo was also forced to build battleships and provide food for the Mongolian army. The Chinese people's dissatisfaction with their parent Yuanzong reached a peak. Under these circumstances, in 1269, Lin Yan, the Korean power minister, took the opportunity to abolish Yuanzong and establish Prince Anqing as king. Upon learning of the news, Kublai immediately sent an envoy to question him. Under strong military pressure from Mongolia, Lin Yan was forced to reinstate Yuanzong. In order to consolidate the status of the Goryeo royal family, Goryeo Wonjong decided to marry the Yuan royal family and use Mongolian power to preserve the royal family, while also preventing Mongolia from annexing Goryeo and maintaining a semi-independent status. In 1271, Goryeo Wonjong sent another mission to Yuan to propose marriage for King Chen, the son of Goryeo, and also sent King Chen of Shizi as a hostage. Kublai agreed to the marriage request of Goryeo Yuan Zong and decided that her 13-year-old daughter, Khudulu Jiali, lost (later Princess Qi), married Wang Yi, and had already joined the Yuan dynasty. The princess was young, and the wedding didn't take place until 3 years later... One month after their marriage, Yuan Zong died, and Wang Chen returned to the country and took the throne for the sake of King Zhongyeol of Goryeo.
The marriage with the Yuan dynasty gave the Goryeo royal family an unexpected benefit. The Yuan dynasty envoys and the Daru people, who were originally rude to the king of Goryeo, are now actually starting to talk about manners and have special respect for the loyal king, and this is something that Wonjong never dared to think about when he was still alive. Obviously, all of this is related to King Zhong Lie becoming Emperor Kublai's mentor. Having tasted the sweetness, the Goryeo royal family made marrying a princess from the Yuan dynasty their top priority to preserve their throne and their lives. After King Chungnyeol of Goryeo, with the exception of King Chungmu and King Chungjeong, who were too young to marry wives, all other kings married princesses from the Yuan dynasty. Among them, King Chungsu married 3 princesses from the Yuan dynasty before and after due to being widowed. However, apparently the Yuan dynasty emperors after Kublai didn't take Goryeo very seriously; none of these “princesses” were the emperor's biological daughters. Despite this, the Yuan dynasty princesses enjoyed great privileges in Goryeo. Once they were married to the King of Goryeo, regardless of whether the king of Goryeo had a concubine or not, the Yuan dynasty princess was immediately canonized as Queen Jeonggung, and their son automatically received the status of heir, and priority was given to the heir. At the same time, the princesses of the Yuan dynasty relied on the support of the Yuan Ting to seize power in the Inner Palace, and even interfered in state affairs several times to decide on the appointment and dismissal of personnel. Beginning with King Chungnyeol, the kings of Goryeo generally suffered from fear of inner feelings (this is in stark contrast to today's Korean men). According to historical records, even though Princess Qi was only 16 years old when she married, and Zhong Lie Wang was 39, Princess Qi did not change the fierce temper of Mongolian women. She often hit Zhong Lie Wang when she reached out, scolded her mouth, and even added a stick. However, Zhong Lie Wang didn't dare to offend this princess at all; she only hid and secretly shed tears. Of course, this kind of patience was also rewarded with many benefits. When King Zhong Lie and Princess Qi met their ancestors for the first time, they requested the abolition of the special post of Goryeo Daru Huachi created by the Yuan dynasty, and Kublye gladly agreed. Later, Goryeo also took back two territories occupied by the Yuan dynasty. To pay homage to the Yuan dynasty, the Goryeo royal family, which originally had the same style as the Han people in the Central Plains, began to follow the example of Mongolia in terms of clothing and hairstyle. Not only did King Chungnyeol usually wear Mongolian clothing, but his hair was also braided. The people of Goryeo shook their heads and sighed when they saw it, even wept bitterly. In 1278, King Chungnyeol even stipulated that all costumes in Goryeo were made according to Mongolian style, so Kublyeol was very surprised after hearing the news; it seemed like he had never done it before.
Of course, the Yuan dynasty didn't just condone Goryeo; being a slave always had to pay a price. In addition to offering some food, beef, horse, and ginseng, the only specialty that Mongolians can see seems to be a woman. At the time, senior officials and nobles all compared how many Korean female slaves they had. There were few female slaves and the “quality” was poor, and they were often made fun of. As a result, the Yuan dynasty continued to solicit women from Goryeo. In 1287, the original ancestors ordered that Goryeo virgins must first register with the government, otherwise they cannot get married. In 1307, the royal court of Goryeo ordered that women between the ages of 13 and 16 from all over the country were not allowed to get married without permission, giving priority to selection by the Yuan dynasty. From 1275 to 1347, Goryeo sent 16 envoys to Yuanxian as virgins. As a result of this, when Goryeo gave birth to a daughter, she often kept it secret; even her neighbors wouldn't let them know, in case she was sent to the Yuan dynasty by the government. However, even Korea's extreme cooperation could not meet the Yuan dynasty's demand for women, because in addition to the Yuan dynasty's emperor and royal ministers requiring Goryeo women as concubines or handmaids for fornication, the Yuan dynasty also needed a large number of women to be married to military personnel. As a result, Goryeo Province set up a special prison for marriage and a separate prison for widows and virgins. In addition to virgins, they also searched everywhere for married women and orphans, wives of traitors, daughters of monks, wives and daughters of criminals, etc. to meet the needs of the Yuan dynasty (it is estimated that Goryeo people are also capable of childbearing). However, the daughter of a high-ranking official from the royal family of Goryeo was even more proud to be dedicated to the Emperor of the Yuan Dynasty or Minister of Crown Prince. As a result, the marriage relationship between the two families became even closer, so even though the emperors of the Yuan dynasty changed quite a bit and several of the emperors' families were hostile to each other, their relationship with Goryeo has always been very close. The status of Goryeo Gong women also reached its peak during the time of Emperor Yuan Shun. Through one marriage and one gift, the Goryeo nation relied on women to survive. At last, it was not destroyed by the Mongolian army. It should still be a cost-effective transaction for the Goryeo royal family.
When Emperor Yuan Shun first took office, a Korean eunuch and Huizhengyuan envoy, presented a number of gifts to the emperor, including a Goryeo Gong woman, Qi Shi. When the Chinchas agreed that Empress Lost was abolished due to the rebellion of the brothers, Emperor Yuan Shun proposed that the Liqi clan be the empress. By March of the 3rd year of Yuan (1337 AD), Emperor Yuanshun appointed his second empress according to Boyan's will. Her name is Boyan Hudu. This queen once gave birth to a son, but unfortunately she died when she was only two years old. However, Qi Shi gave birth to a son named Aiyu Shidara. As a result, Kishi was crowned the second queen, which laid a good foundation for her to take over as empress in the future. Since the empress didn't like to be a steward, all power in the palace fell into the hands of the Ki clan. The Ki clan did quite a few bad things because they cooperated with their childhood neighbor, a Korean lieutenant named Park Bu-hwa, and formed a party for personal gain. Seeing that Aiyu Shiridara became the prince, it is likely that he will ascend to the throne in the future. The Ki family in Goryeo also gradually changed from a poor family to a bully side, seriously threatening the rule of the Goryeo royal family. At this point, it was already the end of the Yuan dynasty. The land of the Central Plains was bursting with smoke, and anti-Yuan rebels were rising one after another. In 1356, King Gongmin of Goryeo took the opportunity to kill the Ki family in the name of planning rebellion. Qi Shi was determined to take revenge. She said that they mobilized Emperor Yuanshun and dispatched 10,000 troops to prepare to abolish King Gongyu of Goryeo, but as a result, they were defeated. In 1365, Empress Boyan Hudu died at the age of 42. Emperor Yuanshun Celiqi was the empress. In 1369, Ming Taizo attacked Kaiping, and Emperor Shun fled to Helin, and later fled to Yingchang. However, the fall of the Yuan dynasty eventually brought disaster to the Goryeo royal family. It's just that this root was planted a long time ago. In May 1351 AD (11 years from Emperor Yuanshun to Zheng), Liu Futong raised the first charity flag in Yingzhou. Jianghuai responded one after another. The rebels wrapped their heads in red scarves, so they are called the “Red Scarves Army.” In order to eliminate the Red Scarves Army, the Republic of Korea also sent troops to assist in the suppression, but the campaign was unfavorable. Several generals were killed one after another, but the Korean army did not leave, but instead carried out extensive burning and looting in places such as Jianghuai. This also aroused the anger of the Red Scarves generals against the Koreans. In June 1357, Liu Futong commanded the 3rd Route Army to the North, and the army pointed directly at Dadu. The Three Red Scarves went to war for thousands of miles and was very powerful. The Central Route Army, led by Mr. Guan and Butou Pan, set out from Caozhou, Shandong and encircled Dadu in a roundabout manner. In order to cut off Korea's military aid to Yuan, consolidate the province of Liaoyang, and besiege the capital, the Red Scarves decided to go east to Korea. In 1359, General Mao Jujing and Shuai of the Red Scarf Army crossed the Yalu River for 40,000 and began the first crusade.
