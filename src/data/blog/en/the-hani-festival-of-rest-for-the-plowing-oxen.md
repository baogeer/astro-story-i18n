 After planting is over, the Hani people in the Luchun County area will have a “Miao Aina” festival. The Chinese word for “Aina” means “a day of rest after planting autumn”, usually in early May. On the day of the holiday, the whole village worked together to kill a pig and a sheep, and each family shared their share. After the “Miao Aina” festival, you can brag about the horn. It is said that bragging a horn is an order to take a break from farming cows. Here's another folk story: Once upon a time, during the “Miao Aina” festival, a shepherd wanted to yell the family's two farming cows up the mountain, but labor-intensive cows never wanted to go up the mountain; they always ran to the field. The shepherd couldn't help it, so he blew up his horn and said to the farm cow, “Lovely old cow, you worked hard a few days ago, but now the seedlings have been planted. Please rest.” Farming cows understood, and went up the mountain with joy. According to the rules of the local people, before breakfast on the day of the holiday, pour a bowl of tea and a bowl of wine on the cut grass, then wrap a bowl of meat and a bowl of rice with herbs to feed the farm cows. Also, the rakes and hoes used during the planting of seedlings must be washed and placed in a certain place. On the night of the holiday season, a lot of fireworks were lit up, and people from all over the village danced and sang until late at night.
