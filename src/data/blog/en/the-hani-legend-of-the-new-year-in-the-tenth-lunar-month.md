 Legend has it that a long time ago, during October of the summer calendar, when the mountains were full of cherry blossoms, a big monster appeared in the mountains. It ruined crops, devoured humans and animals, and caused the Hani people to be at peace.
One day, it knocked down the fence of a household and broke into the house to steal something to eat. Suddenly, it saw a cherry blossom on the wall spitting fire. The red light was burning, so frightened that it escaped from the hospital.
It also broke into another household and swooped at the cow stable to catch cows and sheep to eat. The owner got smart in a hurry and grabbed a copper pot and threw it away. The sound of “ding-ding-dang” scared the devil away. At night, it touched another household in the dark of the night. When the owner heard the sound of “whoa whoa” footsteps, he lit a torch and took a look. The bright flashlight scared the devil away again.
The devil was frightened three times a day and was afraid to stay any longer. Since then, Mount Hani has disappeared.
The day the demons were scared away is said to be Dragon Day. Since then, the Hani people have designated the first Dragon Day in October of the summer calendar as the first day of the year. As a New Year's day, October has continued to be passed down.
