 Every nation has been able to pass down to the present, and they are good at creating their own unique culture so that people can remember it and continue to this day. The main character “Dongba” we want to introduce today is a musical instrument created by the Jingpo people with their own characteristics.
Dongba
Dongba, a double boed instrument of the Jingpo tribe. “Dongba” is a phonetic translation of the Jingpo branch of the Jingpo tribe. It means a pipe flute with a horn in Chinese. The Zaiwa branch is called Biché or Bick. It is popular in Luxi, Yingjiang, Longchuan, Ruili and other places in Dehong Dai and Jingpo Autonomous Prefecture in Yunnan Province.
It has a long history. There is a story that goes around in Jingpo Shanzhai: A long time ago, there was a Jingpo farmer with no children or women. They took a little boy from someone else to adopt. But this farmhouse is very strict about raising children. Every time the rice was about to be harvested, he always rushed to the field early to bombard birds and birds.
The child stayed alone in the field and shed tears of sorrow. At one point, he unwittingly put a stalk of rice in his mouth, and as soon as he blew it, it actually made a sound. He thought it was funny, and he randomly inserted rice stalks into a bamboo tube, and it actually made a better sound. This is how the original Dongba appeared. It has been passed down from generation to generation until today.
The shape is similar to a suona, and consists of a tube body, a spring whistle, a core, and a horn (Figure). The size varies; the most common one is about 52 cm in total length. The tube body is made of soft-textured wood or copper. Most people use locally grown millet or salt fruit wood. The top is thin and thick, and has a conical tubular shape. The tube is about 30 cm long, the outer diameter of the tube head is 2.5 cm, and the outer diameter of the tube tail is about 4 cm. There are five or six round sound holes on the tube body, all with a back hole. Currently, it is mostly a six-hole bar.
The spring whistle is made of wheat straw or rice straw. It is flat and round on the top. It looks like a fan and is about 2 cm long. The core is made of wood and has a hollow cone. A spring whistle is placed at the upper end, and the lower end is inserted into the upper end of the tube head. A hollow feather tube is also used as the core. The speaker is a resonator of a dongba. It is made of hollow horn or wood. It is generally 10 cm to 15 cm long. The wooden one has a slightly thick cone shape and is attached to the lower end of the tube to amplify the volume. A dongba with a horn is also known as an erong dongba in the Jingpo tribe. “rong” is a cow, and “erah” is a horn, meaning a cow horn dongba.
The oldest Dongba from the Jingpo tribe has a total length of 62 cm to 72 cm. The tube is made of wood, thin and thick at the top, and has a tubular cone. It consists of three sections, and there is no horn at the lower end. Each section is connected by a copper socket and can be removed and stored when not in use. Both ends of the tube head and tail are decorated with copper hoops. The copper socket and copper hoop are engraved with patterns or exquisite carvings.
In the Jingpo people, every time the grains are ripe, young people also play dongba in the fields. The length and length of the song are free, mostly improvised. People enjoy listening to it. It is both entertainment and food protection for birds. At the funeral ceremony of Jingpo people, Dongba played a low and sad tune, accompanied by big and small ding-dong rhythms, to express condolences for the deceased elderly. In addition, the Jingpo people also play Dongba during the New Year, wedding night, eating new rice, or grazing grazing. Scenery is popular. Dongba is played from before the fall harvest until after the Spring Festival. It is no longer played during the busy spring farming season, and it is also taboo to play at home.
