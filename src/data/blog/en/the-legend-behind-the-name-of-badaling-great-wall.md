 There are also different folk legends about the origin of the name “Badaling.” Since ancient times, Guan Gou has been known as one of the “Nine Seas in the World.” Gu Yanwu visited the Great Wall twice in the Qing Dynasty and wrote a poem praising it: “Xiongtuoshuodi was old, and Qincheng has continued to grow since ancient times.” In the Han Dynasty, Juyong was the first to be built in relation to the current Badaling. Later, Wei Tuoba unified northern China, and Juyong Pass was abandoned and not used. Li Daoyuan's “Water Sutra: Wet Waste Water” clearly states that the old Juyong Guan was located in Badaling. Badaling already has the name Badaling in the poetry works of the Jin Yuan period. For example, Jin Liuying's two poems are based on the Badaling proposition, “Arriving at Badaling in the Night to Descend Dannai” and “Getting Out of Badaling”. As can be seen, Badaling has attracted people's attention since ancient times.
Regarding the origin of the name Badaling, there are many folklore legends. Although some legends cannot be regarded as having historical material value, it can be seen that people have always attached great importance to Badaling.
One of the legends is that Yuanren Zong, the father of Libali, was born in Yanqing. He saw that the Guanshan Mountains were steep and verdant, so he gave this place the name “Badaling”, and later generations falsely referred to it as “Badaling.”
Another legend is that at the end of the Ming Dynasty, Li Zicheng led the peasant rebellion to this point. From Guancheng they attacked and did not break through; the army was blocked and did not advance for many days. Li Zicheng was distraught. At this point, he told Malay News that this place was called “Badaoling.” As soon as Li Zicheng heard it: Yay! Yibiling has not yet been breached; there are still seven mountains ahead, so they changed their way to attack from Shixia Pass. “Badaoling” is the homonym of “Badaling”;
The third legend is “Chang'an Guest Tales”, written by Jiang Yikui in the Ming Dynasty: “From Yongguan, north to Yanqing Prefecture, west to Xuanzhen, the road was divided, hence the name Badaling.” The name “Badaling” comes from the meaning of being well-connected in all directions.
The fourth legend is that “Badaling” was transformed from “Taling.” Tatars refer to ethnic minorities such as the Khitan, Nemo, and Mongols. The meaning of “putting the Tar Ridge” means guarding a mountain that has been guarded to prevent Tatars from entering customs.
