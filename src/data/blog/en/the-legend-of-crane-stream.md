 Hexi in Jingning County. Legend has it that in the early years it was a large Hunze lake surrounded by mountains. Seen from a distance, it looked like duckweed floating on the water. At the time, people called it Lize; why was it later called Hexi? Legend has it that it is related to the Western Han Confucian student Fuchu Bo.
During the Western Han period, Fuchu took refuge from the south of Chang'an due to war. After living in Qinghui for a few years, he brought two white cranes up the stream against the stream to find a place to hide.
When I arrived in Lize one day, I saw that the mountains and water were beautiful, and it was sparsely populated, so I stayed in seclusion.
Since he was too old at the time to cultivate, he built a stone platform on a large rock by the lake. He went fishing by the lake every day, and bathed with two white cranes every day.
After many years, the water in Rize slowly dried up, and more and more people settled in Rize, and Ukikubu took Shuangkaku to seek seclusion in another place with few people!
Later, people called the place where Ukikiuboku lived in seclusion. The place where he fished was called Ukioka Fishing Platform, the creek in the middle was called Muheke Creek, and the village beside the creek was called Hakkei.
