 West Lake in Hangzhou, Zhejiang, is surrounded by mountains on three sides and is as green as jade. On the southwest side of West Lake, there is Longjing Village. Surrounding the village, there are beautiful mountains and clouds. It is a famous tea production area in Longjing.
Legend has it that one year the Emperor Qianlong of the Qing dynasty went down to Jiangnan to rest at Xiahugong Temple near Longjing Village. The monk in the temple brought famous local tea. Qianlong is famous in the tea ceremony. When I saw that tea, I couldn't help but be amazed. I saw it in a porcelain bowl as white as jade. The slices of tender tea looked like a bird's tongue. The color was dark green, and the blue liquid revealed a hint of fragrance. After taking a bite, he only felt that his cheeks were fragrant, and it was unspeakably useful. So Qianlong summoned the monk and asked, “What is the name of this tea? Where is it made?” The monk answered, “Tell the emperor, this is Longjing tea produced by Xiaomiao.” Qianlong flourished on a whim. Walking out of the temple gate, I saw that the front of the Hu Gong Temple was turquoise and green. Eighteen tea trees had their first shoots. The green desire was low. The surrounding mountains undulated, like a lion. At this point, Qianlong Dragon was overjoyed. The tea name Longjing, and the mountain name Shifeng all seem to be a sign of his outstanding achievements; furthermore, 18 is another lucky number. However, the tea was really pleasing to the eye, sweet and refreshing, so Qianlong sealed the 18 tea trees in front of the Hu Gong Temple as “royal tea” on the spot.
Since then, Longjing tea has become famous.
