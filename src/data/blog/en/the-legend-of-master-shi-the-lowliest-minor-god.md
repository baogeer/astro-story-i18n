 The story of the “terminal” Ojinishi is passed down from generation to generation in Sankai Town in Mingguang City. Folklore doesn't report a single era; it just wants to save trouble; it just says “a long time ago.” Exactly how long this “long time” was, no one could explain it clearly. Nor was it clear, so no one investigated it. This saved me the trouble of writing, so I drew the gourd and followed the cliché at the beginning of the chapter.
A long time ago, there was a writer in the Three Realms. They said it was a literary person because he loved dancing, writing, and ink on weekdays. Everyone called him Grandpa Shi, and few people called him by his name. According to his own account, he took the talent exam when he was 13 years old. At the time, the exam room cheated. Since he had no money to send it, his answers were rubbed into a bunch of waste paper and thrown in the corner. The second and third time was the famous Mount Sun. Before the fourth exam, someone criticized him and talked about it. Since then, Grandpa Shi has abandoned the exam. In order to earn a living, I often help my townspeople write and draw. Because they are good at handwriting, they later learned how to do stonemasons. After completing their studies, who had to set up stone monuments for their ancestors, one person covered all the procedures, from inscriptions to writing and stone carving. With good writing, good handwriting, and craftsmanship, people around ten miles and eight miles love to hand over any work they have to him. In my spare time, I also love talking about ancient times and the present in small teahouses, talking about Wenyin poetry, and making many good friends. I don't know what year and month it started. After drinking a few drinks, Grandpa Shi gradually became addicted to alcohol. He often started talking things that people couldn't understand after drinking. For example, once he had a few more drinks at his friend's house, first scolding the emperor for being unruly, and then criticizing Emperor Yu for being unfair. Why was a stove that swallowed dried firewood sealed as a Vesta? Why can a land that has been sleeping underground all day be sealed off by a landlord? I'm also a god. I'm the little god at the bottom. You'll have to call me Grandpa Shi in the future. Also since then, the “terminal” Ojinishi Grandpa has been called out and shouted, and no one even called him by name.
Mr. Shi has many short stories. People can tell a story or two after dinner or during a dinner party with friends. I've also compiled a few paragraphs here for everyone to wipe their ears and refresh themselves.
Every year at the end of the year, people in the Huai River area love to make a “sacrificial stove” to pay tribute to the old man. In some places, on the 23rd, some on the 24th, and others on the 25th. People in the Three Realms region choose the day of the 24th. Local people have a motto: “If you have money to sacrifice a stove, you don't have money to be impatient”. Wealthy people are also really particular about “sacrificing the stove”; they have all kinds of whole chicken, whole fish, pastries, and fruit plates. There is also a pair of words on the door of the stove. Most of them are praying for peace and good fortune, “the heavens say good things, keep the earth safe”, and some also add “the heyday of peace.” But Grandpa Shi doesn't believe this the most. He always said, “Didn't I have any talent in my thirteen years just because I had no money? Why didn't you lend me some money for this stove? He painted Grandpa Zao on the door with his crooked mouth and slanted eyes, a small pointed mouth, and a pair of donkeys with ears facing the sky. On the bottom of the portrait, “Heaven Will Proclaim You, Let Us Go Down to Earth” is attached, and the words “Why Me” is written horizontally. Every year when Grandpa Shi meets the “Stove Sacrifice” day, he puts this pair of pictures on the stove door. This stove really hasn't treated Grandpa Shi.
Ordinary people respect Grandpa Shi; however, the rich people in the town are not very happy with Grandpa Shi. There is a merchant in the town called Shi Quan. I heard that it is still Grandpa Shi's home. They have made quite a bit of money by trading. They have opened a teahouse in the city and asked the county grandpa to name the restaurant “Yipinquan.” The business is very flourishing. To celebrate the first anniversary of the opening of the museum, Treasurer Shi invited all the celebrities in the county. In order to show off, he also invited a celebrity from his hometown, Grandpa Shi. The crowd gathered to introduce “Yipinquan” as the title of the county grandpa. Everyone praised it. There were well-written words, and there was a wonderful one boasting the name of the store. The teacher, who specializes in shooting horny butts, twists his goatee and shakes his head while shaking his head and brain, saying, “Pinquan is a source of good people, but also tasting the heart of tea.” I understand that it is also the source of Yipin senior officials.” Most of the people present applauded and agreed; only Grandpa Shi shook his head and sighed. Grandpa Shi's behavior was seen in the eyes of his master. This master has always been proud of himself; now there are people who use this kind of gaze to criticize himself, and they are not convinced. So I walked over and asked, “Does this old man disagree with my analysis just now?” Grandpa Shi nodded, “My teacher just now spoke out. Mountain people dared to criticize their opinions. Look at me, this 'tasting spring' is also a trifecta of white water.” When I finished talking, I left without looking back. The people who were there at the beginning didn't respond; they stopped for a moment to hear thunderous applause from behind.
Stone Treasurer's Yipin Spring has since been called “Sanpin Baishui.” Who wants to spend money to drink it? The business quickly fell apart.
