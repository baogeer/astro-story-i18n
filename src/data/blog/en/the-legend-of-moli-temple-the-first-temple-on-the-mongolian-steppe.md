 The first temple in the Mongolian steppes
Moli Temple is located in the Gacha of Moli Temple Sumu Moli Temple in Tongliao City. It was built during the Qing Shunzhi years (1644—1662), and was continuously expanded from generation to generation through Kangxi, Yongzheng, and Qianlong, and the scale grew larger and larger. The east, east, and south are each 8 miles long. The circle around the temple is tens of miles long. There are 8 main streets and 6 alleys within the temple. The first 4 streets are 5 large halls, 4 small temples, 2 living Buddha mansions, temple warehouses, and storerooms, while the back 4 streets are residential courtyards for monks. There is a bell tower and drum tower on the east and west sides of the mountain gate. There are large bells and drums hanging inside the building, as well as huge statues of the Four Heavenly Kings. Outside the mountain gate, there are shade shelters, a pair of stone lions, and a pair of stone flag poles. There is also a venue for dancing the Chama dance.
This place is rich in aquatic plants, fat fish and sheep, beautiful scenery, birds and flowers. The Molimiao Shahu tourist area is famous not only at home and abroad for being the largest desert reservoir in Asia and the longest artificial sand dam in the world, but it is also desirable for its magical and legendary Moli Temple and the “Pearl of the Sand Sea” with its pleasant scenery.
Magical legends
Moli Temple was built in the 19th year of Jiaqing in the Qing dynasty. Legend has it that the reason the temple was built was that King Dahan went to Beijing to see his nephew, Emperor Shunji, and being drunk in the palace caused his nephew's displeasure. Prince Dahan was furious and went back to the prairie, and Wang Gong, who joined several alliances, wanted to send troops into Beijing, thanks to a fat, thick-lipped, yet clever lama from Wutai Mountain who mediated. My nephew and uncle, who are still at a standstill, have individual steps to step down, and are as at peace as ever. As a result, King Dahan wanted to build a “Moli Temple complex” with 5,600 rooms for the Lama, a main temple, 12 sub-temples, and 18 houses for the Lama, in the style of a Tibetan temple with the characteristics of a Central Plains temple, to fulfill his wish of “planning to build a large temple as a reward.”
Back then, it was hard to imagine how difficult it was to build a temple on a prairie without a single tree or stone. Prince Choriktu used 1,000 migrant workers and 1,000 Lele trucks to slowly walk with the load of pouring ice in the cold winter, and it took a full ten years to complete the construction materials. Back then, the depths of the blizzard revealed the sad sound of the migrant workers' trumpets, and a string of horse-lanterns hanging from stone carts rushing in the direction indicated by a bonfire that did not go out all night long. Countless people turned to dead bones, and countless people followed suit with obsession. Finally, the Moli Temple, known as the “First Temple in the Mongolian Prairie”, was built. Finally, it created a splendor in the history of Horqin architecture.
Legend has it that this Moli Temple was originally called “Jining Temple,” and because it is located in a bay where the west comes south and suddenly goes east, it has such a Mongolian name. “Moline” means Wanzi. Moli Temple is actually “a temple on a meadow at the riverbank.” That's such a nice name! The location of the site was clarified, and it was also revealed that the place chosen by King Dahan back then was indeed a place where the water flows slowly. The famous name has not been lost throughout the ages, even though the Moli Temple has completely disappeared. But this name remained in the hearts of generations of old Tongliao people.
Legend has it that back then, Moli Temple held a temple fair four times a year. From merchants in Mongolia and Russia to incense drinkers in Shanhaiguan, tens of thousands of people gathered in front of the temple, ghost hopping events, hawkers, horse market trading, the joy of visitors, etc. How could it be just a lively one? With its strong religious colors and its indescribable magic, Moli Temple formed a happy vortex in the Mongolian grassland. It rotated year after year, and became a happy vortex in the Mongolian grassland. A scene. Legends tell future generations one by one. There used to be a temple known as the “First Temple in the Mongolian Steppe.”
