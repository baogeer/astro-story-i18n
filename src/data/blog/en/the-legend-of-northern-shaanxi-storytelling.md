 Folklore has been told in northern Shaanxi since the reign of the Three Emperors.
Legend has it that a long time ago, an old man gave birth to three sons. When he grew up, he became slaves to his family and chased the spirits. The eldest son was called Rhubarb and had one of his hands chopped off by the slave owner. The second son, Erhuang, left his owner blind, and the three brothers went into exile to Qinghua County in northern Shaanxi to beg for a living.
One day, Rhubarb picked up two rotten wooden boards, knocked on the wooden board and begged along the door. After saying some auspicious words, the owner gave them some leftover soup. Once they ate sheep. They picked up sheep intestines, dried them and stretched them on the wooden board, and a sound popped up. Later, the three brothers made their own pipa, and since then they cooperated with each other while playing, playing, and singing.
Erhuang thought to himself who would knock on the punch after his older brother died. With his ingenuity, he simply tied two wooden boards to his legs and hit himself, hit himself, and sing for himself. Later, the boss and brother went to the Linxian area in Shanxi, but Sanhuang stayed in northern Shaanxi. After marriage, they gave birth to five sons and taught them the art of playing pipa.
After starting a family, the boss took 36 apprentices, passed down the three strings to 18 apprentices, settled down in the Yulin area, and taught the techniques of lotus fall, pipa, and three strings to future generations.
This folklore is enough to witness the long history of storytelling in northern Shaanxi. North Shaanxi storytelling is a folk rap art in northern Shaanxi, which is loved by the people of northern Shaanxi (especially farmers). In northern Shaanxi, storytelling is best performed in a field or on a patio. The audience can be a few hundred or dozens of people. A calligrapher is generally a person, holding a three-string or pipa, tying his feet to a wooden board, and hitting the board with his hands, singing while singing. Some short stories can also be sung to the end without saying a word: or to the end, without singing a single sentence.
The country attaches great importance to the protection of intangible cultural heritage. On May 20, 2006, the music was approved by the State Council and included in the first batch of national intangible cultural heritage lists.
