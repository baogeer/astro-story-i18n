 Dingcheng brick refers to a brick placed on the back eaves of the West Wengcheng Gate Tower in Jiayuguan.
Legend has it that during the Ming and Zhengde years, there was a customs craftsman named Yi Kaizan, who was proficient in the multiplication table. All buildings, as long as they were calculated by him, were very accurate and economical. Supervisor Guan, who supervised customs repairs, was not convinced. He asked him to calculate the number of bricks used in Jiayuguan. After detailed calculations, Yi Kaizhan said, “It takes 99,999 bricks.” Supervisor Guan Yi said, “If you have one more piece or less, you must cut off your head and punish the craftsmen for three years of labor.” After completion, there was only one brick left, which was placed on the back eaves of the Xiwengcheng Gate Tower. The Supervisor Guan was overjoyed when he discovered it. He wanted to use this to deduct the wages of the craftsmen. He said, “That brick was placed by an immortal. It was a Dingcheng brick. If it were moved, the city building would collapse.” As soon as the supervisors heard it, they were afraid to pursue it again. Since then, this brick has been left in place, and no one dares to move it. Today, this brick still remains on top of the Jiayuguan city tower.
