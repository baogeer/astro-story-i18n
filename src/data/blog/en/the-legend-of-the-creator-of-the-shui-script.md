 According to one legend, the Aquarian script was founded by an ancestor named Lu Duo Gong (transliteration “Gong Lukuo” or “Gong Lu Duo”). It took him 6 years to create Aqua Text. At first, most aquarium characters were “packed in piles, one room full”. Later, Prince Lu Duo shocked the emperor by using aquarium characters to calculate the date and method of meeting the Mother of God for a child. The emperor believes that the Aqua script is so powerful that he is afraid that once people have mastered the Aqua script, it will be difficult for people to deal with it. As a result, Master Paitian used a small gourd filled with gunpowder to trick the children. As a result, the fire inside the little gourd burned down the house containing the water book, leaving only a few hundred aquarium characters pressed under the inkstone. Lu Duogong was afraid of being calculated by the emperor again, so after that, he kept all the words in his stomach based on his memory, and no one could steal it. As a result, there are only a few hundred characters left in the Aqua text, all written by word of mouth.
Legend 2. The Aquarian script was learned by six elderly people including Lu Duogong from Immortals. When they first started, these six old people drew images on the sand according to the various animals, birds, and various tools in the aquarium. The fairy looked at the points and acquiesced, and later drew “” (that is, aquarium text) based on this pattern. These six old people memorized their hard backs based on various patterns, and finally created “” and engraved them on bamboo and cloth and brought them back. On the way home, five elderly people unfortunately died of illness. The rest of Lu Duo went to great lengths to bring “” home, but “” was stolen by a son called “Ah Ren” (someone you don't know in water language). In the end, there was only one copy left. Lu Duo wrote down some words from memory, but the word count has been drastically reduced. At the same time, in order to avoid another murder by “Ah Ren Dang,” Lu Duogong deliberately wrote with his left hand, changed his handwriting, and also rewrote, reversed, or added or subtracted some characters, thus forming a special aquarium character that has been passed down to this day.
