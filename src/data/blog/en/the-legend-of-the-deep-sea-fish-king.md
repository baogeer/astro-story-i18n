 In the late Ming and early Qing Dynasties, there was a fisherman named Li Bukong along the coast of Jiangsu and Zhejiang.
On this day, Li Bukong lost his way on the surface of the sea. Suddenly, a white flower appeared on the dark surface of the water; it was actually a big white fish about long! Li Bukong instinctively picked up his fishing fork and inserted it into the white fish. As soon as the white fish flutted its tail, it swam fast forward, and its high spine drew a white mark of water on the surface of the water.
The reason why Li Bukong can't call him empty is because he never went back empty-handed. He forgot his immediate situation and chased the big white fish by boat. Two hours passed, and Li Bukong was exhausted. The big white fish suddenly stopped and looked back and looked at him with his head held high. Li Bukong saw the right time. The fishing fork was hard inserted into the mouth of the big white fish. The tip of the fork came out from the back of the white fish's spine. The white fish struggled for a while and then stopped moving. The surface of the water was bright red.
Li Bukong took a close look. It turned out to be a familiar body of water in front of him. After a further swim, he arrived home. He was shocked, and instantly understood that this white fish was leading him home. Unfortunately, he misunderstood its good intentions!
Li Bou was very sorry, but it was too late to say anything, so he hooked the big white fish with an iron hook and brought it back to shore.

Ah Xiu and Fish Maw were both frightened by his act. Li Bukong then ordered Ah Xiu: “If something really happens to me, you can make up for your life with fish maw gaps. On the evening of the next day, fellow fishermen brought back Li Bukong's bones. They said that Li's empty fishing boat had been turned over by a group of fish. By the time they picked him up, the fish had eaten up all the flesh on his body, leaving only one corpse. Ah Xiu instantly passed out. After Ah Xiu woke up, she made arrangements for her husband's future affairs and spent a difficult time weaving fishing nets for others according to her husband's instructions.
Ah Xiu goes to the cellar every day to worship fish skin; she is in awe of this thing. Suddenly one day, Ah Xiu discovered that tears were flowing from her eyes on the fish's skull, and the round teardrops fell to the ground. Why do dead fish cry? Ah Xiu was shocked. When she changed her mind, she probably had moisture in this place, so she used a washbasin to catch the drops of water.
More than half a year has passed, and half a basin of water has accumulated in the sink. On this day, Ah Xiu was about to throw out the water in the basin. At this point, a blind old beggar was begging for food at her doorstep. Ah Xiu felt compassionate, put down the water basin and went to feed the old beggar. When she came out with a bowl of rice, she saw that the old beggar was washing his face with half a pot of fish tears. Ah Xiu wanted to stop it but was too late. She handed the meal to the old beggar and let him sit in the yard and eat it. Ah Xiu was about to turn around and enter the house. Suddenly, the old beggar “popped” down on his knees: “My old man went blind and went blind. I didn't expect God to take pity on me and be able to see the sun!” Ah Xiu was shocked, then came to her understanding: washing the eyes with fish tears can actually revive a blind man! Sending away the old beggar, the news that Ah Xiu's family has sacred water that can restore the blind was immediately spread. There was an endless flow of people coming to the door to seek divine water, and the lives of Ah Xiu's mother and son gradually improved.
On this day, an old man with a clean coat came to Ah Xiu's house and called him a “benefactor” when entering the door. As soon as Ah Xiu saw it, it turned out to be an old beggar; he was still followed by two followers.
