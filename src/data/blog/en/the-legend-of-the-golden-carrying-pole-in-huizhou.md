 Legend has it that a long, long time ago, 100 Sanskrit characters suddenly appeared on the rock wall here. Whoever can finish reading it in one go will get a “golden pan.”
For a while, literary talent from the four villages came in, yet no one understood all of these 100 Sanskrit characters.
Over time, people only wanted to be excited and sigh.
One day, all three came by a young, clear-eyed student who claimed to be able to finish reading it all at once.
The weather was clear and sunny, and people from the four villages gathered on the banks of the Zhang River, waiting for a miracle to happen.
Around noon, the scholar came to the bottom of the rock wall, carefully reviewed it several times, cleared his voice, glanced at the villagers around him, and read it calmly and aloud.
The crowd saw it and continued to cheer.
When the scholar read the 98th character, the stone wall made a loud “open” sound, breaking a gap. Then, a dazzling “Golden Bian Dam” came out.
The crowd immediately cheered.
Just when “Kim Bian Kum” was within reach, I was born out of a sense of wealth and forgot to read the last two words, so I was in a hurry to reach out to seize the “Kim Byeong-dab.”
Speaking of being late, it was fast. I only heard a “pop” sound, and the “gold pin” retracted back, and the scholar only pulled down a flat load (a bamboo stick embedded in the head of the flat carrier to use when resting the shoulder).
Since then, how did Ren Shusheng read it all at once, and “Kim Bian Tam” never showed his face.
The students had no choice but to return home disappointed, and the crowd had no choice but to disperse.
The purpose of this folk tale from the Huizhou region is to persuade all living beings not to see profit, forget righteousness, and seek something outside of them.
