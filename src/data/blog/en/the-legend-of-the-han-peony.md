 In Beihao Village, Tongbai County, Hebei Province, there was an ancient temple in the Han Dynasty called Mituo Temple. The monks in the temple specialize in peonies, and it is also known as “Botan-ji Temple.” There is a peony inside the temple. The flower is as big as a lotus. Every year in mid-spring, there is an endless flow of flower admirers. There are many songs on the temple walls. They all say it is a Chinese peony. On the side of the peony, there are a few peonies. Every year the peony sheds, and the peony blooms again. It is fragrant and colorful, which is comparable to that of peonies. There is also a legendary story about Chinese peonies.
Legend has it that a long time ago, an old man from Beihao Village woke up early in the morning and walked to the village west field. Passing through the entrance of Mida-ji Temple, the fragrance overflowed, which was refreshing to the heart. He looked up and saw a bright light in front of him. He saw two boys and girls, covered in red and green, like clouds. They floated down the middle of the sky and quietly fell into the temple. The old man was so surprised that he almost screamed. He flew home and told his family. After a while, the incident spread to ten, ten to hundred, and shocked the whole village. Adults and children flocked to the temple in droves to find out. However, when I arrived at the temple, there was no trace. Suddenly, a girl shouted, “Look there!” She pointed to two ground-breaking flowers in an open area of the temple. The water was fresh, tender, and very cute. People are arguing. This is why I believe the old man's statement is true. People all say that this flower plant must have been transformed by those two boys and girls. They both came from the flower gods to protect us in Beihao. We have to take good care of them, and maybe they can get away with it.
Since then, villagers in Hao Village have carefully managed and cared for these two flower plants. Strangely enough, these two flower plants quickly grew several feet tall when they saw the wind. Between the turquoise branches, the flower bones are dense. Soon, the flowers bloomed, and the whole village was filled with fragrance, causing colorful butterflies to fly and bees to roar. The scene was charming. The flower's fame is like it has grown wings and spread in all directions. People in a radius of about ten miles have come to enjoy this wonderful world, and the people in Beihao Village are very proud.
How many years have passed. One day, a general dressed in a war robe, dusty servant, and sweaty, whipped up his horse and headed straight to Beihao. It was a time of war and chaos, and none of the villagers dared to do much; they all quickly closed their doors and closed their homes in horror. The general got off the horse, sweated and knocked on the door, begging for a bowl of water to drink, and find a place to hide, even if there was an old temple in a broken house. A villager said across the door that this village has no wells and two temples, so run away quickly. The general was helpless and sighed: “There is a scene in Beihao Village: there is no temple, no well; the earth lives in a hole in the wall.” As soon as the speech fell, smoke was rolling in from outside the village, flags were covering the sun, and war horses were howling. I saw the chase approaching. The general was aware of the danger, so he whipped up his horse and flew west. Seeing a walled monastery at the head of the village, he turned over and jumped down, then passed out under the flowers. Strangely enough, this flower “stretches out” its leaves and covers the general tightly. As a result, the general escaped this disaster.
After an hour, the general was awakened by the scent of flowers. When he opened his eyes, he found himself hiding among the flowers. He felt refreshed and refreshed. He recalled that people often say that peonies are divine flowers, that they are the king of flowers. If the country will prosper, there must be good fortune. The general got up, wrote a poem on the broken wall inside the temple, and whipped off.
The villagers watched the chase go far away. They walked out of their homes in three, three, two places and followed the horseshoe print to Mida Temple, only to see a poem on the broken wall:
Xiao Wang took refuge in a deserted village, and none of the temples were bleak.
There are only a few peonies, and loyalty does not change to King Chung. (Liu Xiu title)
After reading it, they were all shocked and dumbfounded. It turns out that this strange flower is known as the national color Tianxiang peony; the other plant is the peony in the flower; the general was Liu Xiu, the later emperor of Han Guang. Since then, Han Peony's fame has spread.
(Narrated by Wang Shiduo and Wang Shixuan, collected and collated by Liu Jinglin. (Quoted from “National Flower Festival”)
