 The Hani people who live along the Red River worship cuckoo and call the cuckoo “heboama” (mother of the cuckoo). Every year in March of Yangchun, when camellia is in full bloom, every time they hear a cuckoo's cry for the first time, everyone will cheer for spring by saying “I heard it”. It is said that this response will enable the hardworking and kind Hani farmers to be rich in grains, prosper, and live peacefully all year round.
According to legend, cuckoos were sent by Tenjin Abomomi to fly out of a stone cave in the distant sky to bring the news of spring to the world. When it flew over a sea called “Alley Alley Falling Apart”, it couldn't move; it seemed like it was about to fall into the sea. Suddenly, a dragon tail was raised from the sea, then the dragon tail turned into a large tree with many leaves, and the cuckoo rested its feet on it. At last, cuckoos have gone through a lot of hard work and conveyed the message of spring to the world.
According to the rules handed down by our ancestors, after most people heard the cuckoo's cry, they met on a sheep day to prepare delicious dishes, soak glutinous rice with the juice of a big tree, steam out the fragrant, golden glutinous rice, cook red duck eggs, and devout to cuckoo. On this day, young people and girls from the village village were full of spring breeze, dressed in festive attire, and gathered on a modest lawn to celebrate the annual “Rima Lord” festival (spring event) to choose people and talk about love. After the holiday, each family chose a good day. When the birds had not left their nests and the four mountains were quiet, the parents quietly planted three bunches of seedlings in their fields, which meant “opening the door for seedlings.” It is said that if you can't hear the sound of birds when “opening the seedlings gate,” this year's crops will be free from disasters and damage, and good harvests can be obtained.
