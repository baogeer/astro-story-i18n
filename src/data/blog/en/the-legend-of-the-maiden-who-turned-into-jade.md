 In Xinjiang Uyghur folklore, jade is the personification of a beautiful and kind girl.
Legend has it that in ancient times, on the banks of the Jade River in Tian Country, an old stonemason with excellent skills lived and brought an apprentice.
On her 60th birthday, I found a large piece of lanolin jade in the Jade River and carefully crafted it into a very beautiful jade beauty.
The old mason couldn't help but say, “It would be great if I had a daughter like this!”
Sure enough, this jade beauty became a lively and lovely girl. Her father was an old stonemason, and she was named Tashguli (Jade Flower).
The old stonemason died soon, and Tashguli fell in love with the little stonemason.
However, a local bully took advantage of the little stonemason going out and stole Tashguli in a vain attempt to force them to get married.
Tashguli didn't follow; the bully slashed her with a knife.
There was a dazzling spark in her body, igniting the bully's house, but she turned into a white smoke and flew towards her hometown of Kunlun Mountain.
After learning about it, the little stonemason went after him and scattered small stones along the way, which became a mineral seed for future generations to find jade.
Uighur people have always admired jade and love jade. The proverb says, “I'd rather be white jade from the mountains than the carpet on the Bayi (landlord) hall.”
