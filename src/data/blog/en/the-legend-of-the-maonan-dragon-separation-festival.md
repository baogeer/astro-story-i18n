 The Maonan people are also one of the lesser populated mountainous peoples in China. The Maonans call themselves “An Nan,” which means “the people of this place.” The title indicates that they are the indigenous people of Lingxi. Although the Maonans have a small population, they are famous for their long history and unique culture.
The biggest Maonan folk festival is the Dragon Festival every year after the summer solstice. The “Dragon Divide Festival”, also known as the “May Temple Festival”, is a festival unique to the Maonan people. It is held two days before the “Divide Dragon” in the lunar calendar. It mainly sacrifices gods and ancestors. Men and women from all over the village, as well as married women, and family and friends from afar all over come to participate. It is solemn and enthusiastic. When Dragon Festival is over, every household has to steam five-color glutinous rice and steamed meat, and some even roast pork. Fold back the willow branch and place it in the middle, knead the five-colored glutinous rice into small balls, and stick it tightly to the willow branch to indicate that the fruit is full and hope for plenty of grains.
There are two main legends about the origin of the Dragon Festival:
It is popular in most parts of the country, especially in Jiangnan. The date is mostly May 20 in the summer calendar. According to folklore, it started raining in May because Xiaolong wanted to leave Laolong to issue orders to cultivate clouds and spread rain, and shed tears because he couldn't bear separation, so this day was mostly rainy. If it rains below, I think the wind and rain will be smooth this year, and there will be a good harvest in the fall.
In addition to this legend, North China also has a dragon festival. According to folklore, during the year, the five dragon kings who make rain go together. During the period from the fall harvest to spring planting in the second year, the dragon kings all sneaked underground to hibernate. Before spring farming the next year, the dragon kings, who had slept for the winter, woke up and had to follow the wishes of the Jade Emperor or the Lao Dragon King, and all the red, yellow, green, white, and black owners went all the way to their own district to set the clouds and rain. It is customary for people to collectively refer to the days when the five dragons are separated as the “Dragon Festival.”
People in North China explained that on this day, the five dragons that originally lived together will have to be separated, and each owner will have clear and rainy days. If it doesn't rain on this day, people will consider whether they need to sing to pray for rain. China is rich in land, with different climates, different geology, and diverse cultures. Therefore, relatively fixed times to worship dragons and pray for rain are not exactly the same everywhere. For example, the Lingchuan area in Shanxi has been sacrificing Dragon King in early June 1 and June 13; while the Bailong Temple Festival in Beilongshan, Yangcheng, Shanxi, is scheduled for April 3 and June 6 of the lunar calendar, respectively. In the folk world, people call this kind of fixed day praying for rain a “day of death.” Most of these “dead days” are scheduled during periods such as Dragon Lord God's birthday. In comparison, this type of relatively fixed rain prayer ceremony is mostly arranged earlier. It is a means of preventing droughts and an early investment in Dragon King's feelings.
