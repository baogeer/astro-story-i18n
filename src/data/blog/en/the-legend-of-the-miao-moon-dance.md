 Legend has it that once upon a time, there was a Miao family in Gulu (place name, present-day Xintang Township), Pingtang County, Guizhou Province. Both parents died long ago, leaving only two brothers dependent on each other. My older brother is Baka, 19 years old; my younger brother is Ba Mu, 16 years old. Both brothers are smart, hardworking, and loved by everyone. Every day, my older brother goes up the mountain to get firewood. In addition to burning, the rest is picked up and sold for money to buy oil and salt; my younger brother goes down to the river to catch fish and shrimp; in addition to eating, he also sells the rest of the dried kangkan to get back the supplies he needs. The brothers had a pretty good time.
My older brother Bakka, who is smart and hardworking, finds some bamboo and reeds and sits on a large blue slate and plays with pipes every time. He cut the end of a bamboo tube into an oblique opening with a knife, sandwiched an aloe leaf into the oblique mouth, and blew air into the tube with his mouth, making a very beautiful beep. Every time he went up the mountain to get firewood, he had to make a reed tube to play with, so he left quite a few reed tubes of different sizes and lengths on the bluestone slab. Later, he picked them up one by one and played with them, making different sounds. As a result, he wanted to blow these reed tubes of varying length and size together so that all kinds of sounds could be made at the same time. He thinks about it every day. When he was cutting firewood, he cut down a paulownia tree and discovered that the center of the paulownia tree was empty. Finally, he came up with a solution: drill a few holes at the end of the paulownia wood to go through the center, insert reed tubes of different sizes and lengths into the hole, then blow it at one end with his mouth. As a result, several sounds were certainly made at the same time. When Baka returned home, it was carefully developed and made into a “Jin Lu Sheng”, which can play all kinds of beautiful and beautiful tunes. Every time Baka blows the sound of Jin Lusheng, men, women, and children from villages near and far come to listen; even the birds in the sky have to fly down to listen.
There is a giant tiger cat (the local common name for tiger) in Oyama Cave not far from Goura. It is very vicious and greedy. It wants to take possession of Baka and makes fun of it every day. As a result, the tiger cat became a fat woman and arrived at Baka's place. Seeing that the door was locked, she jumped over the wall and jumped into the house. Seeing that there were quite a few dried fish on the mat, she quickly devoured it. At this point, Baka and Bama opened the door and saw them. Baka stepped forward and asked, “Why are you messing around in my house and stealing dried fish?” The tiger cat cheekily said, “Not only do I eat fish, I want to start a family with you!” As a result, Tiger Cat took Baccarat out of the house and walked straight up the mountain. My brother Ba Mu panicked and rushed to catch up with my older brother. How can they both beat Tiger Cat's energy? As a result, Baka got dragged away! Suddenly, my brother Bama saw a fat woman showing a tail under her dress. Only then did she know it was a tiger cat. He quickly went to various villages to invite people, and brought his bow, arrow, and pike to the mountains to search. Since October, I've found Fuyutsuki, look for it. I don't know how many mountains I've gone through, and how many dense forests I've gone through, and I still haven't been able to find my favorite Baka. Until the first day of the winter month, they found a dried orchid on the top of the mountain in Xiayang Farm, and a dried orchid shouted at everyone in the tree: “Grin, stuttle” (Miao transliteration, that is, in a thatched pont). They followed this search. Sure enough, they found a big stone cave behind a giant palm tree. They saw that tiger cat was guarding Baka in the cave. As a result, everyone shot the tiger cat to death with arrows and rescued Baka. When Baka saw his younger brother and loved ones in various villages, he was happy to pick up Jin Lusheng and play together. They all danced and played together around Baka to celebrate their victory and reunion.
Since then, on the first day of the winter month of the lunar calendar, the Hmong people in the Gulu area have to concentrate on singing and dancing parties at Xiayangchang. It has gradually become an important way for young local Miao men and women to socialize and find friends. People call it “jumping the moon,” also known as “looking for an older brother.”
