 An interesting legend recorded in the Tibetan history book “The Chronicles of the Tibetan Kings”:
Guanshiyin Buddha on Mount Naputuo taught a divine macaque a commandment and ordered it to practice on a snowy plateau from the South China Sea. This macaque came to a cave in the Yalong River Valley to dive into the heart of compassion and Bodhi. Just when the monkeys were seriously practicing, a female devil came to the mountains, did her best to be lustful, and bluntly proposed: “Let's combine the two of us!” At first, the macaque answered, “I am an apprentice of Guanyin Bodhisattva and have been ordered to practice here. If I join with you, wouldn't it break my precepts!” The witch then said again, “If you don't join me, then I'll have to do my own thing. I was destined to become a demon; because of my relationship with you, today I am looking for you as a loved one. If we don't get married, I will definitely become the wife of a devil in the future. I will kill tens of millions of lives and give birth to countless grandchildren of demons. At that time, the snowy plateau was all a devil's world, and many creatures had to be maimed. So I hope you agree to my request.” The macaque heard these words because the Buddha came into the world and thought to himself, “If I marry her, I will have to break the rules; if I don't join her, I will also cause great sin.” Thinking about this, the monkey went to Putuo Mountain to find the Guanshiyin Buddha and ask for instructions on how to do it. Then Guanshiyin thought about it and said, “This is a heavenly meaning; it is an auspicious sign. It is a great good thing that you can combine with her to breed humans in this snowy region. As a Bodhisattva, you should see good and act courageously; go and get married with a witch. In this way, the macaque formed a partner with the witch. Later, the couple gave birth to six little monkeys. These six little monkeys had different temperaments and tastes. The macaque in the form of the Bodhisattva sent these six little monkeys to the grove, so that they each sought food for life.
Three years later, the monkey father visited his children and discovered that they had already given birth to 500 children. At this time, there were fewer and fewer fruits in the woods, and they were about to dry up. When the little monkeys saw the old monkeys coming, they shouted, “What shall we eat in the future?” He spread his hands one by one, and looked very miserable. Seeing this scene, the macaque said to himself: I followed the will of Guanshiyin Bodhisattva when I gave birth to so many descendants. Today's incident has broken my mind. I might as well ask Guanshiyin to think about this. He immediately came to Putuo Mountain to ask for guidance from the Holy Ghost. The Buddha said, “Your descendants, I have enough to raise them.” As a result, the macaques followed the orders of Mount Sumi, took natural grain seeds and sprinkled them on the earth. The earth grew with all kinds of grain basins without cultivation, and only then went back to the cave. Because they received enough food, the monkeys slowly shortened their tails, started talking, and gradually became humans. These are our ancestors in snowy regions.
Note: The story of a macaque becoming a human has been widely spread among Tibetans, recorded in ancient scriptures, and also included murals in the Potala Palace and Roblinka. The cave where the macaques lived is said to be on Mount Kampot near Zedang, and “Zedang” is also named “a place for monkeys to play.”
From discoveries made in the past 20 years at the ruins of Linzhi, Motuo, Dingri, and Qamdo, we can see that these discoveries are in line with the legends. It tells us that human activity began in Gaoyuan in Tibet a long time ago. Later, Qiang people living in Gansu and Qinghai migrated to the southwest and developed into the “Bo” - Tibetans, following the example of the original indigenous people in Tibet.
