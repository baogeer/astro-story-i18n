 Legend has it that a long time ago, there was a goddess named “Shalang” who lived in the mountains. Every year on February 1 and March 4 of the lunar calendar, she came out to teach the Qiangmin dance. Her beautiful and exciting dance steps and hip movements are peculiar and beautiful. Every year in these two days, Qiangzhai selects a beautiful Qiang woman to study dance in Kamiyama. The girl who has returned from her studies will give singing and dancing to every woman in the village. People call this kind of song and dance Shalang, which later became a general term for Qiang song and dance. Judging from the movements and characteristics of “sirang”, it was initially a pure female dance, which highlighted women's shoulder and crotch movements. “Shoulder-shedding” has the basic posture of women in everyday work, while “turning” reflects the beautiful posture of Qiang girls. It can be seen from this that Qiang women play an important role in production and life.
In order to thank this beautiful goddess Sharang, every year from February 1 to May 15 of the lunar calendar, the Qiang Shalang Festival is held in Beichuan. Shibi of the Qiang people will also choose the time to take the Qiang people to a sacred place to slay sheep. Since the spread of sarang, it has slowly formed a unique style of dance for the Qiang people. On the same day, after the grand Qiangzhai welcome ceremony, the Qiang girls in the shaizi were dressed in national costumes and had bright smiles on their faces. The Qiang guys sounded the original sacrificial drum. Shi Bi, armed with a sheep whip (called a magic whip in the Qiang people), led the villagers in the village to perform sacrifices. After the grand opening ceremony of drinking, the young people in the stockade dragged fat sheep into the village to slaughter them. Shi Bi enshrined the sheep's head on the White Stone Pagoda, praying for good weather and rain in the coming year. Next, the young Qiang girls held hands and sang mountain songs around the bonfire and danced sirloin. At night, drinking sheep soup and eating lamb, the sons and daughters of the Qiang ethnic group sang and danced hand in hand around the roaring bonfire. The girls, young people, and even the guests were thrown into the air by happy crowds, and Qiangzhai became an ocean of joy.
