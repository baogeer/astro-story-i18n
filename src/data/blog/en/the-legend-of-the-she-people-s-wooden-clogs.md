 In the past, the Jin family had a pair of in-laws who lived in Takayama Daling.
The two in-laws are affectionate and live a happy life in harmony.
However, this dense forest is a place full of people and bears.
One day, the old woman went to the mountain to cut firewood. It was being cut down. When she saw a human bear on a hill in the distance, she turned around and fled.
When humans and bears saw people running away, they chased them from behind.
She escaped fast, and the humans chased faster. Run away, chase her. It won't take long for her to be chased by bears. Fortunately, at this point, her husband arrived and saved her life.
Another day, my husband came back from work in the mountains, and he was the only one walking alone on the mountain road.
People and bears recognize people's feet and can recognize the direction they are going. After a while, Ren Xiong arrived from behind. Fortunately, her wife came to help her, and only then did Ren Xiong return to the mountain.
After a long time, my two in-laws had dinner and went to bed.
In the middle of the night, footprints of people shining brightly came to his door.
People and bears knocked on the door, frightening his two in-laws to escape through the back door.
They knew that people and bears had discovered this place and couldn't stay any longer, so they moved to another place.
Somewhere else, it's also a mountainous area, and there are bears. What should I do?
The two in-laws discussed how to deal with humans and bears. Thinking back and forth, I finally came up with a solution: humans and bears can recognize people's footprints before they can follow people. They made two rectangular wooden shoes. The front and back are the same size. When they wear them on their feet, you can't see that they are human footprints.
People who walk in these shoes don't recognize people and can't keep up with them.
These wooden shoes are called “wooden clogs.”
Later, in order to escape the danger of people and bears, the children and grandchildren of the She family wore this kind of wooden shoes, whether they went up the mountain to get firewood, planted in the field, went to the vegetable garden, walked with relatives, married a daughter-in-law, or carried burdens.
Today, even though no one in Shemin wears wooden clogs anymore, this story is still circulating.
