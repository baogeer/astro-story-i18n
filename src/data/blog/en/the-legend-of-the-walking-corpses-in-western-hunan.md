When visitors die from other places, their ancestors must be buried if they wish; filial children and grandchildren must have to return to their homes in death, and family and friends are also obligated to fund this matter.
However, in the upper reaches of the Yuanjiang River in western Xiangxi, the area is poor. Most poor people go to the eastern Sichuan or Qiandong regions to make a living. There are many mountains and mountains. The plague in the mountains is very severe. Malaria is often prevalent, and the living environment is extremely poor. Other than the local Miao people, few outsiders go there.
None of the Han people who died in those places were rich. Traditionally, the Han people had a deep idea of transporting dead bodies back to their hometowns for burial. However, even if they were rich, it was difficult to carry them in a car or stretcher, so some people invented this strange economic method to transport the dead back home.
Legend has it that thousands of years ago, Apu (Hmong: father-in-law) Lu You, an ancestor of the Miao people, led an army to fight hostile forces along the Yellow River until the dead spread all over the field and blood became a river.
After the battle was over, they had to retreat to the rear. After the soldiers carried away all the wounded, Apuchiyou said to Sergeant Apu, who was around him, “We can't leave behind our brothers who died here. How about you use some magic to get these good brothers back to their hometowns?” Sergeant Apu said, “OK. You and I will change your outfit; you lead the way with 'runes' in front, and I will supervise in the back.”
As a result, Sergeant Apu dressed up in the image of Apuchiyou, stood in the middle of the dead bodies of the brothers who died in battle. After meditating on spells and praying to the gods, he shouted at the corpses: “Damned brothers, this is the place where they died. It is really sad to die in vain. The parents in the hometown are hoping for Lu, and the lovely wife and young child are hoping to return home.
“There is no need to be lost in your soul. Hurry up like Ritsuryo, let's go!” All of a sudden, the corpses lying on the ground all of a sudden stood up and followed the rules of the “Festival of Runes” by Aphuyu Gaoqing and headed south. The enemy's pursuit has arrived, and Apu's military team's manual method brought in the “Five Greater Fog,” trapping the enemy in an ecstasy.
Since it was the “master” (execution and operation intention) spell of General Apu's army that got everyone out of danger, everyone has since called him “Boss”; also because the last enemy used by Apu Lao Si was actually “fog art,” and the “fog” strokes were too difficult to write, so it was replaced by the word “witch.”
In fact, this witchcraft is also a hieroglyph: the upper horizontal represents the sky or fog, and the one in the middle represents the “rune”; the vertical one on each side of the vertical represents Appiyu, and the one on the left represents Apu Lao, which means that two people must join forces to perform witchcraft.
The truth of this legend can no longer be tested, but there is no doubt that witchcraft is popular in the Hmong region. Research shows that the Miao people were the first people in our country to invent weapons, criminal law, and witchcraft. In some areas populated by the Hmong people, they still have strong traditions of witch culture. To a certain extent, the extermination of corpses is an expression of the witch culture in western Xiangxi.
