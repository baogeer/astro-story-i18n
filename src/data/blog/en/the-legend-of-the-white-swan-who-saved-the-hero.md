 Legend has it that there is a young tribe leader named Kalchahader. He fought bravely and well. He lost a war and was seriously injured. He walked alone on the Gobi. Hunger and thirst put his life at risk.
At this point, a white female swan flew down from the air, drool a few drops of saliva on his raised mouth, and led him all the way to the riverside.
When Kalchahader recovered from her injuries, this swan also became a beautiful young girl.
The couple, who are in love, have a boy named Kazakh (meaning white swan) after becoming husband and wife.
Later, Kazakh gave birth to three sons. Their descendants were descendants of the three major Kazakh tribes, Dai, Middle, and Little Yuz. Later, they used “Kazakh” as their national name.
