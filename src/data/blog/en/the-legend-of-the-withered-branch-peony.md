At the end of the Northern Song Dynasty, Jin soldiers invaded the Central Plains. An anti-Jin general, surnamed Bian, led his department through Luoyang. The season was right around the middle of the winter season. It was full of desolate scenes of grass turning yellow and thousands of trees withering.
The general was anxious to hasten the horse's journey. The horse's whip was broken, so he left behind a section of dead branch on the side of the road and headed east.
After a few changes of war, one day he led his team to Biancang Town in Yancheng, Jiangsu Province. There was already a shortage of people and horses, and there was an urgent need for recuperation. The general got off the horse, looked around, and inserted dead branches of Quan Zuo's horse-whip into the ground so that the Ministry would set up camp here...
In the spring of the following year, the dead branch inserted into the ground actually took out shoots and displayed new leaves. As the valley rain passed, bright and beautiful flowers magically bloomed again. The local people heard the incident and came from hundreds of miles from Fangyuan to watch the flowers and burn incense. According to the drug farmer, it is known that it is a peony. People rushed to say, “Big Song is hopeful, Da Song is hopeful.” These peonies are also called “dead branch peonies.” Later, local people donated money to build Taiwan and built a “dead branch peony garden,” where these peonies were enshrined.
(Quoted from “Peony Grand View” by Liu Xiang and Xu Xiaofan)
