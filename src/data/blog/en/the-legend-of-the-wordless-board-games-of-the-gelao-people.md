 In the people of Wuchuan in Guizhou, especially in places populated by the Yilao ethnic group, there are still some ancient unwritten chess games that are still being played today. No matter if anyone wants to play chess, adults and children immediately sit on the ground, find a stick, draw east and west on the ground, and some strange board appears, then find a few stones of different sizes or colors everywhere, nagging spells or shouting singles and doubles in their mouths and hands on the back of the hand, and the battle starts. Onlookers and supporters also gathered to hear the shouting, and the scene seemed very lively.
These ancient unwritten chess pieces, such as “monk chess, dog egg chess, pork trotter chess, hexagon chess, etc.,” all have different ways of playing, and stories are passed down.
Monk chess. According to legend, there was a monk who hid in the mountains all day to read the sutras and went down the mountain to become friends with nothing to eat. One day, the monk went down the mountain and came back empty-handed. He slipped back to the mountain temple, sat on the ground, and wrote about the day with a wooden stick. He drew his temple in the shape of a “◇,” he wrote the word “ten” in the middle, and the word “field” under the temple. He also thought of many fields, so he divided the character “field” into four “fields” and connected them to the temple. The monk grunted because he was hungry and had no intention of writing it again. He just typed an “x” on every word “field”, and his mouth murmured: it looks like he's hungry today. He dropped a wooden stick and touched a few stones in his hands everywhere. The monk was sitting cross-legged, and there was a small stone in his hand in a daze. In the picture of a few stones falling from his hands to the ground, the monk's eyes lit up, and he felt refreshed. If he wants to find a way to gain wisdom, he must not want it in vain; let the farmer be willing to give him food. As a result, he compared himself to a stone and placed it at the crossroads in the temple, and placed a farmer with food around a large “field”. He saw all the strokes as a path, which could be easily connected. The monk imagined that if he walked by himself, the farmer would take a step, and if they couldn't meet on the road, they would become friends with others. He came up with a solution, which is that he only needs to meet two farmers on a straight path and stand in the middle, and the farmer has to give everything. This is called “picking,” the farmer doesn't want to give, and he has to think about changing the route. The key depends on how the monk himself goes before he can “pick” all of the farmer's children. This is the only way to give fast food.
The monk decided to go down the mountain and try this method. So he came to a yard and found a few farmers who didn't give it to him. He drew a picture of his own invention on the ground, then found a few more stones, and replaced it with a small wooden stick to explain to everyone how they went. Whoever lost would be given food. As soon as everyone saw it, this method worked and was fun, so they started playing together. Later, people called this kind of play “monk chess,” and others called it “five-horse chess.”
The one that is more difficult to play than monk chess is “Shang Tianqi,” which in some places is called “369.” This kind of chess is impossible without a few fights. Legend has it that there are three Heroic Emperors, each rebelling in the East, West, and South, fighting for supremacy, and continuously attacking each other and expanding their regions. I don't know what year and month, the three king soldiers faced off against each other in a dam in three ways. The terrain is surrounded by mountains on three sides. There is only a large field on the north side. Whoever wants to expand northward must pass through the fields. In order to reduce casualties, the Three Kings discussed using the chess method. Whoever wins and who goes first, everyone thought this method would work, so they ordered the person to first draw the word “king” on their side, then write the word “ten”, connect the three kings as a path forward, and write the word “field”, connect the empty end of the “ten” character, and then use the word “vertical” to connect the word “field” to the left. At the top is the word “heaven,” which is the “heavenly chessboard.”
The board is drawn. Each of the three kings uses stones of different colors or sizes to represent themselves, with one son underlined by the word “king.” Each person is required to hold three or more stones in their hands and play chess using the method of estimating how many stones are thrown each time, but they must hide the stones in their hands, clench their fists, and shout out their guesses to confirm that who guesses the sum of the stones and who walks first to win. If the two say the same number, it's not a repeat game, and you must not be lazy. If anyone gets to a fork in the road first, and the next time a party wins, they will also have to walk at the intersection that was taken first. If they hold it first, they must concede defeat and return to the original “Wang” underscore. What is particularly interesting is that when the stone is in the middle of the bottom edge of the word “field,” it must first go from right to left to bottom, then go straight up from the middle, making a big detour without touching the opponent. Often at this point, the emotions of winning and losing are emotional, and those who are impatient will jump like thunder, or even cheat. The three Heroic Pirates competed for the world. It's just a legend. In the end, they relied on war to resolve victory or loss, but “Shang Chess” remained.
Chess with pork trotters. I heard that there are two brothers. My parents died early, and they were so tasty and lazy to cook, so they begged to eat and live their lives. One day, they got a pork trotter, and the two brothers got into a fight. On weekdays, the older brother asked the younger brother to eat as much as possible. But today my brother couldn't let it go. He wanted to have a fat pork fork; he hasn't fainted in oil for a long time. But we can't argue. We have to think of a way. My brother drew a “pork trotter fork” on the ground. First, he drew an “” frame, then hit an “X” on the inside, and found four stones, two for each, in different colors, and placed them in the four corners of “Oka.” I said to my younger brother, “Let's walk around. If anyone can't move around the opponent's kid, even if they win, it's just a pork trotter. As a result, the older brother lost, but the younger brother still gave his brother a fork to eat. From then on, the two brothers used this method to distribute food as long as they wanted something tasty.
Dog egg chess is a type of chess that scolds. Boys between the ages of 89 and 145 love to play this kind of chess. Whoever loses is a dog egg egg. The board is also very simple. Two people each have three children, but both have different colors or shapes, so you can find them anywhere. Place the pieces on the left and right sides of each side. The bottom three circles indicate “dog egg”. Whoever gets to the other party's kid in the three rounds wins, and the loser is scolded as a “dog egg” by the winner. Onlookers also take advantage of this opportunity to touch the losing family or take off their pants and touch the chicken and chicken for fun. Those who were not convinced came back and went, and didn't stop until everyone present was a “dog egg.” In this kind of situation, the cows are often beaten by their parents when they get home. Because they forgot to take care of the cow by playing chess, the cow ate the crop, and someone else went to the house to find a loss; they should have been beaten. However, the next day they went up the mountain to herd cows, and the cows played “dog egg chess” again.
