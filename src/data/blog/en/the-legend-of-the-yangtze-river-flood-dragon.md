 Legend: “A snake is a hundred year old python, a thousand year old dragon, and ten thousand year old dragon. I only saw snake skin, no corpses.” This means that if snakes can grow for 100 years, they can form giant pythons; after a thousand years, they become dragons in legends. If snakes can keep growing, they can turn into Jiaolong, so you can't see corpses. This is also the reason you only see snakes peeling skin, and you can't see snake corpses.
So is that really true? Snakes are nothing but legends. First, snakes generally only live about 20 years, and only very few species can live to around 30 years old; it is impossible to grow to 100 years old; secondly, there are many types of snakes, and there are more than 200 species in our country. The size a snake can grow does not depend on age, but on its breed. Some snakes won't be too big even if they grow for a few decades, mainly limited by variety.
In the end, dragons are legendary, and there is no evidence that dragons actually exist. The transformation of snakes into Jiaolong or dragons is just people's imagination. Seeing snakeskin is easier to explain, because snakes molt mainly for growth, and their bodies become larger every time they shed their skin. Another reason is to get rid of parasites, because snakes are easily parasitized by parasites, and they have no hands or feet, so they can get rid of surface parasites during molting.
So why can't we see dead snakes? It is mainly related to the natural ecological cycle. If snakes die after aging, they will be swallowed by other animals. This is a purification process in nature, and not every grain of “food” is wasted. Also, there are very few snakes that actually die naturally, because when they are about to get old, their physical strength declines, and they are easily preyed upon by other predators, so corpses cannot be found.
Even if snakes die naturally, they are difficult to detect, because snakes are nocturnal and hide in stone crevices, tree holes, or nests during the day. When they are close to death, their ability to go out to prey or do activities is greatly reduced. Most deaths are in stone crevices or nests, so they are not detected.
