 Every year on June 24 of the lunar calendar, it is the traditional festival of the Yi people - the Torch Festival.
When night falls, from Stone Forest to Dieshui, from Mount Gui to Changhu, countless torches illuminate the night sky and brighten people's smiles. Passionate singing is intertwined with the majestic sound of the big three strings, and the cottage is immersed in the joy of the festival...
There is such a magical and moving legend about the “Torch Festival”.
In ancient times, on a high mountain, there was a castle. There was a warrior in the castle. He had mouse eyes, broom eyebrows, and a mackerel beak. With his pointed chin, his thin face was covered with dark pimples. People called him the nickname “Black Devil.” This dark demon did nothing wrong. His subordinates supported a large group of families and thugs, and ruthlessly ruled and oppressed the Yi people. He made a clever name for himself. He had to pay tax when he had a child; if he went up the mountain to hunt, he had to collect fish donations... All kinds of exorbitant taxes made the people really out of breath. He held many uprisings to resist the cruel rule of the Black Devil, but the strong castle of Toast was difficult to take down, and many people were caught and killed alive.
An intelligent and capable shepherd, whose name is Zaka, came up with a way to outsmart Toast Castle. He secretly linked poor people in 99 villages and decided to keep sheep from all households in stables from June 17, only feed them some water every day, not feed them fodder, and starve for seven days and seven nights. The rebels rushed to build shuttle signs at night, sharpened bamboo sticks, sharpened machetes and axes, and tied torches to every goat horn. Everyone agreed to revolt on the night of June 24. By this night, when the moon had not yet appeared, and the breeze in the green forest blew softly, I only heard the sound of a long cry of horns, using the first “torchlight mother”. At this point, various rebels immediately opened the sheep stable door, ignited tens of millions of torches tied to the horns, and drove the flock to attack the castle of the Black Devil of Toast.
The uncountable flock was already hungry enough, so they scrambled up the mountain to eat the leaves, green grass, and grass, using the light of the fire. Zaka led the people in the uprising and fought bravely. I was almost at the castle, and the drumming and shouting were earth-shattering. Black Magus hurriedly climbed to the castle and saw that the entire mountain had become a sea of fire. The people surrounding the castle from all sides had already begun attacking the city gate. While Black Demon ordered his family and thugs to guard the city gate, he quietly penetrated the cave himself and prepared to escape. At this point, various uprising forces had already broken through the castle, and bees poured in. I've searched everywhere, but I haven't seen the Black God of Toast. Later, Zaka arrested the housekeeper for questioning. Fearing death, the housekeeper knelt down and begged for forgiveness, and immediately led Zaka to the cave where the toast was hiding.
Zaka then told the big butler to go down to the cave and ask Black Demon to surrender. This big butler, who usually pretends to be a tiger, was so frightened that he was afraid to go down to the cave. All of a sudden, I fell to the ground and couldn't climb up. The crowd was looking out. Suddenly, a dagger flew out of the cave, and a cold light flashed through, shooting the dagger down to the ground. It turns out that Zaka had been on guard for a long time. He anticipated that Dark Magus would not surrender easily and would struggle to death, so as soon as the dagger from the local cave flew out, Zaka had quick eyes and waved a machete, and shot the dagger down!
When Zaka and the crowd saw that Black Demon couldn't die, they decided to burn him to death with a torch. As a result, under an order, tens of millions of torches immediately piled up the area around the cave into a hill. I saw that the raging fire was burning even more vigorously. In a moment, the ground was also burnt red. The evil God of Toast was buried in the torches. To commemorate the victory in this struggle against tyrannical rule, June 24 is scheduled to be the “Torch Festival”.
