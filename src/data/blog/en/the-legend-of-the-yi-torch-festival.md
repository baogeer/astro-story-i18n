 Once upon a time, there was a Hercules in the sky called Sereabi, and there was a Hercules named Atilabah on the ground. Both had the power to climb mountains, and no one could knock them down. When Sreabi heard that Atilapa was a powerful man on earth, he came down from heaven and tested with him. Attilapa happened to go out that day. As he left, he said to his mother, “Hercules from heaven came to me and asked the old man to entertain him with a piece of iron. They said it was something I ate, so I invited him to try it. Let him wait a little while, and I'll be right back to wrestle with him.”
Having said that, Atilapa left. After a while, Sreabi came. Atilapa's mother actually carried a piece of iron for Sreabi. Sreabi took a bite and couldn't move at all. He thought: If Atilapa eats iron nuggets, he must have more strength than me. Thinking about it, he hurriedly went back.
After Attilapa's return, his mother recounted the story. When Atilaba heard that Sreabi couldn't bite a block of iron, she knew that he definitely didn't have much strength, so he ran after Sreabi. Just as soon as they were about to go to heaven, they were overtaken by Atilapa.
“Don't go, since you asked me to wrestle, let's try it out!”
When Sreabi listened to him, he opened his hands and hugged him violently, and the two began wrestling. Their strength was really amazing. The earth was shaken violently by them, and the mountains and trees all shook. Sreabi hugged Attiraba and fell. Attila stumbled and was crushed to the ground. But when his back wasn't yet on the ground, Atilabah stepped forward and rolled over Sreabi's head and pressed against him. Sreabi's back fell to the ground, but he didn't accept the defeat and asked for another fall. this time. Attilapa stood in a horse-riding position. Sreabi hit him hard. His feet seemed to have taken root and didn't move at all. Attilapa did not pay attention, grabbed one of the other's arms and knocked it down with force. Sreabi was thrown more than two feet away and was unable to move while lying on the ground. Atilapa took a close look. Sheabi had vomited blood and was dead.
When Atilapa returned home, when the Buddha learned that Sreabi had been knocked to death, he was very angry, but there was no way to deal with Atilaba, so he sent a large number of bugs to eat the crops on the ground. On the night of June 24 of the lunar calendar, Attilapa cut down many pine trees, led the people to burn bugs, burned all the bugs sent by the Buddha, and protected the crops. Since then, the Yi people have designated June 24 of the lunar calendar as the Fire Festival.
