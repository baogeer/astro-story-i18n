 Legend has it that when Emperor Qianlong went down to Jiangnan, he visited the foot of Shifeng Mountain in Longjing, Hangzhou, to see rural women picking tea as a sign of insight into the people. On this day, Emperor Qianlong saw a few rural women picking tea in front of more than ten leafy tea ponds. They were very happy and learned to pick it. I just picked one, and suddenly the eunuch reported, “The Empress Dowager is sick. Please return to Beijing quickly.” Emperor Qianlong heard that the Empress Dowager was sick, so he put a handful of tea leaves in the bag and drove them back to Beijing day and night. In fact, Empress Dowager Dowager only ate too much seafood. Her liver fever rose for a while, her eyes were red and swollen, and her stomach was not seriously ill. Seeing the emperor arrive at this point, I just felt a scent come through, and I asked him what good things he had brought. The emperor also thought it was strange. Where did the fragrance come from? As soon as he touched it, ah, it turned out to be a handful of tea leaves from Shifeng Mountain in Hangzhou. After a few days, it had dried. The rich aroma just came out. The Empress Dowager wanted to taste the tea. The Queen made the tea and delivered it to the Empress Dowager. The Empress Dowager took a sip, and her eyes felt much more comfortable. After drinking the tea, the redness and swelling went away, and her stomach didn't swell. The Empress Dowager said happily, “The tea leaves in Longjing, Hangzhou are truly a panacea.” Emperor Qianlong was so happy to see the Empress Dowager and immediately passed down the order to seal the 18 tea trees in front of the Hugong Temple in Shifeng Mountain in Longjing, Hangzhou as royal tea, and pick new tea every year to pay tribute to the Empress Dowager. So far, these 18 imperial tea trees are still preserved in front of the Hu Gong Temple in Longjing Village, Hangzhou. Many visitors to Hangzhou have also made special trips to visit and take pictures.
“The beauty in tea is Longjing,” and Longjing tea is especially beautiful in West Lake Longjing. The legend of West Lake Longjing is also beautiful. During the Qing dynasty, Emperor Qianlong toured the South six times and visited the Longjing Tea District four times to see tea farmers picking and making tea. Legend has it that this long-lived tea lover once picked tea by himself. He brought back to Beijing some of the tea buds he had picked at the old Longjing Temple of the Hugong Temple in his book and presented them to the Empress Dowager. The Empress Dowager liked the tea buds that had been flattened in the bookcase, and she decided to want this kind of gong tea. Since then, Longjing tea has maintained its unique bookmark shape. Today, when traveling to Hangzhou, you can also see 18 old tea trees growing outside the Mount Gate of Hugongmiao that were picked by the Qianlong Emperor back then and named “Imperial Tea.”
