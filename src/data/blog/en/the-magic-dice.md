description: "A poor old man was given dice by a fairy. By throwing them three times, he was able to fulfill three wishes in exchange for a new house, barley field, and dairy cows. The story warns people that if they are insatiable, they will end up with nothing."

 There are two families living in the deep mountain gorge. The family is quite rich, full of cattle and sheep, and groups of slaves; the family is so poor that even mice don't want to stay at his house.
Poor people have only a lonely old couple; they all rely on the old man collecting firewood in the mountains in exchange for pickles. Once, after three days of heavy snow, the old man ran through several mountains and didn't even cut down a single firewood. He felt so sad that he started crying against a cliff, and still muttered in his mouth: “Oh my God! Now my old lady and I are starving to death!”
Who knows, just at this point, the cliff behind you opened like a door, and a beautiful fairy came out and said, “Old dad, don't worry, I'll give you a pair of dice. If you throw them three times, you can fulfill your three wishes. Remember, if you make four wishes, my dice won't work!”
The old man took the dice, stumbled back to his little stone house, and told the old lady about this happy event. The old lady was overjoyed and prayed, “Fairy! Our house is about to be crushed by snow, so please give us a house sheltered from the wind and rain!” The old man also said, “Please give us a piece of barley, even a small piece.” The old lady also said, “It would be great to have two cows, so we can drink ghee tea.” While they were talking, their roof was raised and turned into a strong and beautiful little house. There was a field of barley in front of the door, and the golden barley was ready to be harvested; they had two fat cows tied to the barley field, so they called “”, “”, “” and “” so happy!
The changes in the lives of poor neighbors shocked rich owners. The rich owner came over and smiled and asked, “Good neighbors, where did all of your new houses, cows, and barley fields come from?” The old couple wouldn't lie; they told the original story. The rich man hurriedly changed into torn clothes, carried a wood chopper, and sat where the old man used to sit, crying bitterly: “Oh my god, my old lady and I are starving to death now!” Sure enough, the fairy pushed open the stone gate and also gave him a pair of dice.
The rich owner was overjoyed and returned home with the dice. Before he had time to pray, his fat wife took the dice and shouted, “I want gold, gold!” The rich man grabbed the dice and shouted, “I want to be king, king!” The fat wife took the dice again, held up her hands, and said, “I want to live forever, always be as young as 18!” The two of them competed against me and got into a big fight in the house. One was careless, the dice fell to the ground, and the two lost their voices and shouted, “Fuck it!” Sure enough, the dice turned into a blue smoke and disappeared without a trace. You two look at me, I see you. I don't know who to blame, because the fairy said a long time ago that when you make a fourth wish, the first three wishes will come to nothing.

Note: This story is very common in Tibet, and people everywhere will tell it. Some people say the dice are hermits, some say they are gods lions, and others say they are snow pigs that are immortals. But the ending is essentially the same.
