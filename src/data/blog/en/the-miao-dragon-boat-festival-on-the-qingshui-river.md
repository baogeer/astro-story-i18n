 In May of the lunar calendar, the Han people have the custom of dragon boat racing during the Dragon Boat Festival. The Miao people living in Kaili, Guizhou also have the custom of racing dragon boats at the Dragon Boat Festival in May of the lunar calendar. The Han Dragon Boat Festival is on May 5 of the lunar calendar, while the Miao Dragon Boat Festival consists of four days from May 24 to 27 in the lunar calendar. The Hao word for Dragon Boat Festival is “how to paddle a dragon boat”, also known as “lou urn” (meaning eating dragon meat).
The Dragon Boat Festival is usually held on the Qingshui River. The Qingshui River passes through Kaili, also known as the Longtou River. Every year in May of the lunar calendar, the Miao people who live here celebrate the Dragon Boat Festival.
There are two folklore legends circulating in southeast Qiandongnan regarding the origin of the Miaojialong Boat Festival.
There is a legend that Dragon King, who has rained for generations, once made a mistake in the rain, and the time when it rained was against God's will. Tiangong was furious, causing Lei Gong to split Dragon King into several sections and throw it into the middle of the river. Since then, there has been a major drought, and the Miao people are paddling along the Qingshui River in dragon boats, symbolizing that the Dragon King, who was cut to death by Lei Gong, has been revived, and that rain falls on the earth as usual. It's an ancient tradition of racing dragon boats for rain.
Another legend says that a father and son are fishing in the river. The son was taken by the Dragon King and used as a pillow. The father angrily set fire to the Dragon Palace and cut the dragon into several sections to float in the middle of the river. Everyone ate the dragon meat separately. However, after eating meat, it was completely dark for nine days and nine nights, regardless of day or night. One day, a woman brought her child to the riverside to wash clothes. The child slapped the river water with a stick and mallet, and kept on hearing the “thump” sound imitating drumming. Soon the sky lit up. Afterwards, the Miaomins near this river all went to the Dragon Boat Festival and raced dragon boats with ideas on drums.
The Hmong dragon boat is made of fir wood. The mother boat has three parts. The center of the mother ship has six cabins at the front and back. The middle four cabins carry pigs, sheep, geese, and food from racing sailors given by family and friends during the Dragon Boat Festival. The subboats on both sides are paddling places for sailors. Miao dragon boats paddle while standing, unlike Han seated paddling.
The dragon head is beautifully carved. On the dragon horn, which looks like an ox horn, auspicious words such as “good wind and rain” and “good fortune for the people” are usually written. Normally, dragon boats are placed inside the “dragon boat boron,” which is used exclusively for hulls. Some “dragon boat sheds” are also filled with couplets, such as “Dragon boats are a thousand years old, loyalty and filial piety have lived for a thousand years, the legend lasts a thousand years, and pedigree stretches through the ages, and a hundred years of prosperity.” The widespread criticism was mostly “people reuniting” and “Marlboro coming to Korea.” Before the Dragon Boat Festival, after the villagers held a grand ceremony to sacrifice the dragon boat, they lifted the dragon boat and put it in the Shimizu River for practice and competition.
There were as many as 340 Miao sailors in the dragon boat race. The leading figure was the “dragon boat head”. This refers to the drummer on board. All sailors on the boat followed the drummer's finger and paddled to the rhythm of the drums. Another little boy dressed up as a woman and knocked on a gong wearing silver jewelry from the Miao family. A strong man with good energy stands at the helm and is supported by a strong man who knows the nature of water. There is also a special person responsible for firing artillery on the boat at the start of the race, when leaving the camp, and when returning to the village. There are also “cultural people” who can count books, who write down gifts from three relatives and six relatives on the boat. During the festival, the meat and wine that sailors enjoy at three meals a day are all gifted by villagers and family and friends from villages along the river. After the Dragon Boat Festival, you also need to enjoy a goose or duck. The selection of sailors is very strict; if a woman has children in the family, no one can be a sailor.
At the end of the Dragon Boat Festival, each village must pick the fattest pig in the village. After slaughter, the whole village is invited to have dinner once to reward the whole village for supporting the Dragon Boat Festival.
May 24 to 27 of the lunar calendar is the official day of the Miaojialong Boat Festival, and as early as May 18, the dragon boat had to be cleaned up. By the 22nd or 23rd day, the mother ship will be integrated into the ship and the faucet will be installed. A grand opening ceremony was held on the 24th. A square table was placed on the riverside or beside a well or ditch where the dragon god is located. Tie the legs of the table with branches removed from the leaves and bark, tie an umbrella, and tie red cloth, paper money, and a handful of green grass. Put a liter of rice on the table and remove a hairy white rooster to ward off evil. On the first day of the Dragon Boat Festival, you had to ask wizards to read the sutras, but now most of the magic rituals here are simplified. However, there are constant rules of witchcraft. For example, dragon boats must not stop or rest halfway while paddling, and must paddle all the way to the end. After the dragon boat turned its head, the person at the helm gave each sailor a bunch of green grass to drive away evil, and they all threw themselves into the river.
During the Dragon Boat Festival, both sides of the Shimizu River are full of people. In addition to boat racing, there are also folk activities such as drumming and singing. This period is also a festival for young men and women to sing and talk about love.
