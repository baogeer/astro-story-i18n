 A three- and four-year-old boy ran into the wilderness. The hunters chased away the wolf and brought back the boy. They didn't know why he was born, so they named him “Sharu.” With his ability to speak, Shalu was able to understand all kinds of animal languages; and he was drafted into the military and fought with Genghis Khan. Once camping, when Sharu heard the howling of the wolf, he told the boss that there was a flood and that they had to set up camp again. Sure enough, the night was full of wind and rain, and the original camp was flooded. Since then, whenever people camp at night, the leader asks Sharu, he knows what is good and bad. Judging from the above historical records and folklore legends about werewolves, it is obvious that Mongolians have the idea that wolf totems are worshiped.
Deer, docile and good at driving, are beautiful and have divine power. The ancients naturally adored them. There is no shortage of records of this in history books. Mongolian shamans believe that deer can show their spirits and exorcise evil spirits. The hats worn by shamans (Bo) from regions such as the Cuban tiger, Chahar, and Horqin in Inner Mongolia are all decorated with tin antlers. The bronze mirrors and drums used also depict the image of a deer, indicating that the ancestors of Mongolia, especially forest hunters, used deer as totem gods. Therefore, the “Blue Wolf and White Deer”, a totem myth about a pair of Mongolian ancestors that once had great cohesion, is recorded in “The Secret History of Mongolia” and is very precious. Judging from the development of the totem system, the oldest totem is the “original totem”. There is only one for each clan, and it is an actual object that exists in their surroundings, such as totem entities such as wolves and deer.” In a situation where both types of production (material production and human reproduction) continue to develop, clans are bound to clashes, alliances, relationships, marriages, and mixed marriages. ... The blood family was greatly impacted and eventually disintegrated. Expatriate marriage led to changes in totem worship, and 'quasi-pristine totems' came into being.” Quasi-primitive totems are actually a transition between a clan from a single totem to a confrontation between the two peaks of a matrilineal totem and a patrilineal totem. At first, the latter did not occupy an important position. By the time people paid more attention to paternal totems, they entered the late stage of primitive society, that is, the period of patriarchal clan communes.” The “Blue Wolf and White Deer” confronted each other and appeared in the form of a husband and wife. It can be seen as a form of totem that existed in Mongolian society during the patriarchal clan commune period.
