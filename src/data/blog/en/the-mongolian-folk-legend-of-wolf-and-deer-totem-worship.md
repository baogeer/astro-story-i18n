 The first sentence of “The Secret History of Mongolia” reads: “Genghis Hekhan's ancestor was born by fate, Kortechina, and his wife Huemalanle crossed the sea and lived in front of Mount Buhan at the source of the Foran River. They gave birth to their son Batachikan.” ① We think this is a long-standing and recorded totem myth. Although it is difficult for this sentence to become a vivid mythical story, in 1382 (the 15th year of Ming Hongwu), Huoyuan Jie and Ma Shahei spelled it in Chinese characters according to the original Mongolian sound, they spelled “blue wolf” for the word “blue wolf” and the word “exempt Emmalanle” with a special note on “bleak white deer”. This move was probably not random. Because “The Secret History of Mongolia” contains many names with the connotations of various wild beasts, they are all replaced by “name” or “person name” in the side notes, and the animals actually referred to by these two terms are clearly and unmistakably translated, indicating that they are annotations based on a deep understanding of the meaning of this sentence, rather than referring to people's names in general. Therefore, this phrase, which seems to be extremely brief, is profoundly imprinted that Mongolian ancestors worshiped wolves and deer totems during the totem culture period. From the analysis of this sentence, there is a view of destiny, and it is probably a stereotyped myth and legend from the patriarchal era. Although the details of how the wolf became their first grandfather and why the deer became their first grandmother have long been forgotten, images of the wolf and deer clans marrying and migrating from far away places are still circulating. The migration mentioned here is probably not an ordinary migration, but rather a historical trace of the two clans with a marriage relationship that developed over a long period of time and gradually expanded outward.
Although the ancient and vivid mythology of wolves and deer totems in Mongolia is hard to find, it can still be deduced from records in the history of northern peoples and Mongolian historical books, and from Mongolian shamans' expressions of humble ideas, that Mongolian ancestors did worship wolf totems and deer totems. The phenomenon of wolf totem worship was almost shared by our ancestors living in the northern prairies. Not only did the Huns and Turks worship wolf totems, but the Huiyans also had vivid stories of the wolf god. It shows that in the wild boreal grasslands, wolves are very fearsome beasts for primitive people. They often gather in groups. Whether they prey on or fight against invading enemies, they fight together, and are ferocious and spiritual. As a result, people worshiped them out of fear and treated them as their own relatives and the like. This is the birth of wolf totem worship. According to the cultural development theory of “parallel theory” linked to Taylor's theory, when any two different tribes live in roughly similar environments, their culture must show the same adaptability. Well, it's not surprising that Mongolian ancestors, who were also in the northern prairies or woodlands, worshiped wolf totems.
This is evidenced by historical books and oral works.
