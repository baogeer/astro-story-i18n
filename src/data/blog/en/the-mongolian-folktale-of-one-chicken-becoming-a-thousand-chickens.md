 Tales from the Virat dialect. It is spread in Bortala Mongol Autonomous Prefecture in Xinjiang. It tells the story of a poor person who wants to be a lama and raises a thousand chickens to the sun in vain. Once, when the old living Buddha went to a poor person's home, the family's two sons slaughtered the only white chicken to entertain the living Buddha. After eating chicken, the living Buddha blew up the leftover chicken bones and turned it into 1,000 chickens. Just when the two sons were herding a flock of chickens, their father came back. As soon as the father learned about the situation, he rushed to the living Buddha and asked to become his apprentice Lama. The living Buddha wanted him to go back and kill his two sons. After his father “copied” the living Buddha asked him to sit in a cave and told him not to look into the cave. But the father couldn't help but look out of the cave with curiosity and saw his two sons and daughter-in-law watching their chickens. When the living Buddha saw that he didn't have the sincerity to be a lama, he let him go home. When he got home, he lived a peaceful life with his son and daughter-in-law, depending on the thousand chickens.
Oral description from Northeast China, recorded in 1990 by Otuka. 32 pages, 4 pages, 2000 words. Edited by the Xinjiang Civil Research Association. Included in the first issue of the “Khan Tengri” book series published by the Xinjiang People's Publishing House in 1990.
