 The myth of the swan totem is spread among Buryat Mongols; “Holytomert and Holidamergen” (hereinafter referred to as “Holytomert”) is one such work. The work is marked as two people, but it actually only tells the story of Holly Tomert alone.
Legend has it that Holly Tomert is a single young man who hasn't started a family yet. One day, while roaming on the shores of Lake Baikal, he saw nine swans flying from the northeast and falling off the lakeshore and turning into nine fairies jumping into the lake to bathe. He stole a swan's feather coat and hid. After bathing, the eight swans flew away in their feather coats, leaving one as his wife. When the eleventh son was born, the wife wanted to return to her hometown and asked her husband to return her clothes, but her husband did not allow it.
One day, his wife was doing needlework, and Holly Thomas was cooking with a “gripper” (two felt sheets to keep hands from getting hot). The wife said, “Please give me the goose coat. I'll take a look at it. I want to go in and out of Baomen. You can easily grab me, so let me try it out!” Holly Tomert thought, “What would he do when he wears it?” So I took the white goose coat out of the box and gave it to my wife. The wife immediately became a swan when she put on her goose coat and spread her wings in the room. Suddenly, the horn spread its wings and flew out of the skylight.
“Ho ho, you can't go, don't go!” The husband shouted in surprise and reached over to grab the swan's calf in a hurry, but in the end, the swan flew to the sky. Holly Tomert said, “Go as you go, but you have to name your eleven sons before you go!” As a result, the wife named her eleven sons Xubud (xubud), GalJud (XowaCai), Qalbin (Qalbin), Batunai (Batunai), Huodai (xodai), xusid (xusid), Chagan (cAYAN), saraid (saraid), Budunggud (Budunggud), and Harghana (xAryAna), and became an eleventh father, and said, “May you for generations to come. Enjoy your blessings and have a happy and prosperous life!” After speaking, it flew in the northeast direction. This “man and goose match” story is widely spread mainly in Buryatia and the Balkha region.
There is also a variation story on the same theme. The swans became women and young people married and bred into Buryat tribes such as the Hori and Balkos, so swans were recognized as the original grandmothers of these tribes. This not only reflects the ancient Mongolian worship of the presiding god “Tengri” (heaven), but also leaves traces of the matriarchal society's worship of women. They believe that the ancestors of this family are all gifts from heaven and are related by blood to God, yet this concept is reflected through perception and association with reality. What's the reality here? It was just a swan flying around in front of me.
According to geographical conditions, Lake Baikal has mountains and water, making it ideal for all kinds of birds to live and reproduce. There are also many swans, so the lakeshore cliffs have left rich rock paintings of swans. The Horitomites are a tribe on the east shore of Lake Baikal. They are located at the mouth of the Barkhuzhan River. They are the hometown of swans, so they have respect for swans.
Why do Buryat and Mongolia worship swans, and the Balhu people living in the Hulunbuir region of Inner Mongolia also have this custom? This is because the Baltic Tigers claim that their ancestors are from the upper reaches of the Foran River and the area around Lake Baikal. The tribe name of the Balkur Tiger is commensurate with the place name “Balghuzhen”. It can be seen that the ancestors of the Baltic Tigers basically lived in the same region as Huli Balti, so they formed a common concept of cult of swan totems from ancient times.
There is also a saying among Mongolians in Xinjiang that white swans are ancestors of Mongolians. On the Mongolian plateau, many ethnic groups or tribes used white swans as a symbol of good fortune, and even sacrificed the sacred bird “Weng Yi”. Buryat Mongolian shamans began singing the ode “Swan Ancestors, Birch Tree Stands” during religious ceremonies.
When swans return to the north and soar in the sky in spring, Baltic tigers and Buryats should offer white fresh milk to express their blessings. Like a song by a shaman, “Welcome the Spring Water Bird House” sings: “Swans fly, ice and snow melt, flower mules and horses grow, and welcome the blessings. Huri! Huri! Huri! ... Their worship of swans and the strict taboo customs of banning fishing, killing, and fasting are the result of the tradition of swans as ancestral totem gods from ancient times.
