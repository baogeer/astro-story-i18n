 The clear turquoise pond in front of you is called Qingtang. Legend has it that Qingtang originally was an oil cylinder filled with cooking oil. A mountain rat sneaked in from the back mountain in Qingtang, stuck its sharp mouth into the cylinder, and desperately tried to steal cooking oil. It just so happened that an immortal passed by this place and swept the dust. Since then, mountain mice have turned into mountains, and oil tanks have turned into green ponds, leaving behind this beautiful scene of mice stealing oil. Mice are at the top of the Chinese zodiac. Opinions think this place has excellent Feng Shui.
There's also a story going around here. Once upon a time, a Feng Shui master from Jiangxi came here and determined that if a mouse built an ancestral tomb on its back, it would take 18 soldiers. As a result, he transferred the patent to a financial owner. When the two sides reached a deal, Mr. Feng Shui said that because he ejaculated on the open air plane too early, he would become blind, and that the financial owners would take good care of him until the end of his old age.
In less than a few years, the children and grandchildren of the wealthy family came out with 18 marshals, and they all went back to their hometowns. The 18 red carps in the pond have already passed the 18 tests, so the relentless rich owner kicked this blind Feng Shui man out of his house to beg. This was learned by Mr. Feng Shui's apprentice. He vowed to avenge his master and made the master's family build an eight-pillar pavilion in front of the tomb to break this good Feng Shui. When the last tile of the pavilion was covered, heavy rain suddenly poured down. The water in the green pond washed out of the embankment, and 18 bright red carps leaped out of the bottom of the pond and took advantage of the water to escape into the good stream. When the last one was about to leap out of the pond, the apprentice suddenly felt compassionate, waved a long cigarette, and returned the fish to the pond. Soon, the owner's 18 warriors went rampant and bullied the public. Except for one lame soldier, they were all investigated and prosecuted. The owner was also in the midst of anger and remorse, and he cried out for his life.
Turns out, this green pond doesn't rise and doesn't dry up at the end of the year, so that 18 carps can live steadily, all thanks to this mouse. Every time the pond is full, it sucks water into its stomach; every time the pond is shallow, it spits out the water in its stomach. But when this eight-pillar pavilion was built, it looked like a mouse clip, just to hold mice stealing oil. As soon as the mouse was caught to death, the water in its stomach could no longer be hidden, and it all came out. The pond was full, and the carp couldn't stay any longer. This story conveys a very simple truth, which is, “If people want to know good fortune, they must never cross a river to demolish a bridge.”
