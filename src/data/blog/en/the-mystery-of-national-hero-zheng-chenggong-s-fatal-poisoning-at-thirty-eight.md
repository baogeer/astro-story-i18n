Zheng Chenggong is a native of Nan'an, Fujian. Emperor Ming Long Wu once gave the surname Zhu, and his name was successful. Therefore, later generations also often referred to him as “Grandpa with the surname of the country.” Zheng Chenggong's father, Zheng Zhilong, was both a merchant and thief in his early years, and finally went to the Fujian General Army. When Zheng Zhilong lived in Hirado, Japan in his early years, he married a local woman, Mr. Tagawa, and gave birth to Zheng Chenggong. Zheng Chenggong returned to China from Japan when he was 7 years old and began receiving his Confucian education. In the first year of Shunzhi (AD 1644), Emperor Yongli of Nanming proclaimed Zheng Zhilong as Nananbo, the main town of Fujian, and was responsible for anti-Qing military affairs throughout Fujian Province. The following year, brothers Zheng Zhilong and Zheng Hongkui became emperor of the Tang dynasty in Fuzhou. Under the name Longwu, Zheng Zhilong was canonized as the Marquess of Nan'an and was responsible for all military affairs in Nanming. When the Qing army marched into Fujian, Zheng Zhilong surrendered, and the Longwu regime came to an end. Zheng Chenggong learned that his father wanted to be cleared, and he painstakingly dissuaded him. Seeing that his father was obsessed, Zheng Chenggong went alone to South Australia in anger and recruited thousands of people to resolutely resist the Qing dynasty. On several occasions during the Qing dynasty, they were abducted and surrendered by Zheng Chenggong.
After Zheng Chenggong became stronger, he joined forces with anti-Qing general Zhang Huangyan and led the army to attack Nanjing by land and water, and fought all the way to the city of Nanjing. However, the Qing army's plan to fake surrender was defeated and returned to Xiamen. After Zheng Chenggong returned to Xiamen, he began planning to take over Taiwan, using this as a base for the rebellion of the Qing dynasty and the Fuming. At this point, He Tingbin, who worked as an interpreter in the Dutch army, rushed to Xiamen to seek Zheng Chenggong and persuade Zheng to take back Taiwan successfully. He Tingbin also gave Zheng Chenggong a map of Taiwan showing the military forces of the Dutch invading army. In March 1661, Zheng Chenggong sent his son Zheng Jing to lead part of the army to stay behind in Xiamen. He personally led 25,000 generals, took hundreds of warships, and set out from Kinmen in a big way. After the army crossed the Taiwan Strait, they rested in Penghu and prepared to take Taiwan directly.
In order to stop Zheng Chenggong's army from attacking Taiwan, the Dutch invading army concentrated in Taiwan and built two castles and sank the ship at the port to prevent Zheng Chenggong's fleet from disembarking. Under the leadership of He Yanbin, Zheng Chenggong took advantage of the rising tide in the seawater to sail into Luermen and land on Taiwan Island. After a fierce battle, the Dutch invading army suffered a fierce defeat, and the turtles huddled in two cities and were afraid to fight. On the one hand, they sent people to the island of Java to rescue soldiers, and on the other hand, sent messengers to Zheng Jun's camp to seek peace in an attempt to get Zheng Chenggong's army out of Taiwan in exchange for 100,000 silver. Zheng Chenggong flatly rejected the demands of the Dutch invaders and forced the Dutch who were entrenched in Red to surrender by cutting off the water source of the Red Sea. Zheng Chenggong decided to use a long-term siege against the invading army entrenched in Taiwan City to force them to surrender. After eight months of siege, Zheng Chenggong ordered a strong attack on Taiwan City. The Dutch invaders had no choice but to surrender. In early 1662 AD, Zheng successfully drove the Dutch invaders out of Taiwan.
Shortly after Zheng Chenggong recovered from Taiwan, he suddenly became seriously ill and died at the age of 38. Regarding Zheng Chenggong's death, there is a saying: While regaining Taiwan, Zheng Chenggong also received bad news, saying that his father was denounced by his family member Daggi. Yi Dagi claimed that there were occasional correspondence between Zheng Zhilong and Zheng Chenggong, and that the plot was unscrupulous. The Qing dynasty was outraged and executed Zheng Zhilong's entire family. After hearing the news, Zheng Chenggong felt a pat his heart and cried, “If you listen to my advice, how could it be fatal?”
Soon, Zheng Chenggong also learned that the rebel general Huang Wu had dug up Zheng's ancestral tomb. Zheng Chenggong even beat the crime in the chest and cried all day long. He clenched his teeth and vowed, “What does it have to do with the dead when people live to form resentment? If one day I lead the army back and I don't break your corpse to pieces, I'll be fine with nothing on earth.” Zheng Chenggong's wish came true 14 years later. When Zheng Jing captured Zhangzhou, he also dug up Huang Wu's grave and whipped up his father.
In April 1662 AD, Lin Ying, the director of the Southern Ming military department, was cut off as a monk. He fled from Yunnan to Taiwan to see Zheng Chenggong and cried to Zheng Chenggong: “The emperor (Emperor Yongli) listened to the words of his traitor Ma Jixiang and rebelled against Li Guotai and took refuge in Myanmar. Now that Wu Sangui has attacked Myanmar, the king of Myanmar has dedicated the emperor to Wu Sangui. I heard that Wu Sangui has already been murdered.” Zheng Chenggong even cried when he heard it.
Who knows, wave after wave, wave after wave. Zheng Chenggong's subordinate Tang Xianyue denounced Zheng Chenggong's son Zheng Jing for adultery with his nanny. Zheng Chenggong immediately blocked his chest and immediately sent someone to Xiamen to kill Zheng Jing and her baby and the nanny Chen, but the crowd left behind in Xiamen did not carry out the order. Zheng Chenggong climbed high every day to see if any boats were coming in the direction of Penghu. As a result, he suffered from wind chill. On the 8th day, he suddenly furiously shouted, “How did I see the pioneers underground?” He just grabbed his face with both hands and died. Therefore, according to the “Taiwan Chronicles”, Zheng Chenggong died of a cold and wind chill.
Based on Zheng Chenggong's abnormal performance before his death and the background of the internal struggle within the Zheng Group at the time, some people think Zheng Chenggong was poisoned and killed. The main basis for this statement is that Zheng Chenggong's situation before his death was very similar to the symptoms of a toxic attack after poisoning. Zheng Chenggong's death was recorded separately in Li Guangdi's “Rongcun Quotations Sequel”, Xia Lin's “Minhai Chronicles”, and Lin Shi's “Tales at the Door” of the same time. For example, in “Rongcun Quotations Sequel”, “Dr. Ma Xinjian thought he had a heat stroke and took a cooling agent, so he died late.” “Talks at the Door” says, “(Success) suddenly went wild and died after being bitten off his finger”; “The Minhai Chronicles” said that Zheng Chenggong threw medicine on the ground before his death, and then “stepped down and shouted.” Zheng Chenggong probably discovered that someone had murdered him, but it was too late.
Previously, the Qing government did have the idea of murdering Zheng Chenggong. “Taiwan Foreign Journal” describes that at the time, the Qing government sent a senior military officer and mixed in with Zheng Jun with a branch of peacock guts and used heavy money to buy a chef who cooked exclusively for Zheng Chenggong, so that he poisoned Zheng Chenggong and his general during meetings with his subordinates. Although this chef was greedy for money, he was afraid that the matter would come to light, and was afraid to take action after repeated trade-offs, so he left the matter to his younger brother. When his younger brother actually got poisoned, “every time he wanted to take medicine, he had chills all over his body.” In addition to being terrified, he told their father about this. Her father was “shocked” and scolded the two of them, saying, “Murdering the owner is disloyal; promising others and not doing it is dishonest. I'd rather be unfaithful than disloyal. How can we exterminate the nine ethnic groups? Hurry up and surrender yourself, and maybe you'll be acquitted.” They were then taken to Zheng Chenggong's residence to surrender voluntarily. Zheng Chenggong not only did not punish them, but he also gave them great praise and confidently said, “I was born, how can I be poisoned by mortal people?” Since then, Zheng has successfully strengthened security measures. In this way, even if someone “wants to poison themselves, why can't they get close (meaning Zheng Chenggong)”. But that doesn't rule out the possibility that Zheng Chenggong was poisoned.
The mysterious death of Zheng Chenggong's general Ma Xin also seemed to prove that Zheng Chenggong might have been poisoned. Ma Xin was a surrender general and later became Zheng Chenggong's close friend. On the day Zheng Chenggong died, he was prescribed a prescription by his recommended physician. Zheng Chenggong died at night, and he himself suddenly died without illness. According to Li Guangdi, Ma Xin died the day after Zheng Chenggong's death. As recorded in Jiang Risheng's “The Foreign Chronicles of Taiwan,” his death was only 5 days before Zheng Chenggong's death. Therefore, Ma Xin may have directly participated in the murder of Zheng Chenggong, but was later murdered and destroyed.
If Zheng Chenggong was poisoned, then who is the perpetrator? Of course, the Qing government has serious suspicions. At the same time, there are also people who think they are Zheng Chenggong's siblings, such as Zheng Tai, Zheng Mingjun, Zheng Xi, etc., especially Zheng Tai. Zheng Chenggong, who is violent, is used harshly. Many of Zheng's subordinates, including his elder relatives, have been executed by capital punishment, and the generals are anxious. Many of them defected under the temptation of senior Qing court officials. Relations within the Zheng clan are extremely tense. Zheng Tae had a conflict with Zheng Chenggong as early as when Zheng Chenggong led an army to attack Taiwan. At the time, Zheng Tai was a food transport officer. When Zheng Chenggong's army had supply difficulties, Zheng Chenggong was extremely dissatisfied with Zheng Tai's negligence. In front of his seat, he wrote down the five major words: “Convict a lost household first!” Meaning, if something goes wrong, first punish Jung Tae. After Zheng Chenggong's death, Jung Tae and others falsified Zheng Chenggong's beast## #郑经，并抬出有野心但无才干的郑袭来承兄续统。 In the end, their plot was thwarted by Zheng Jing, and Zheng Tae was imprisoned and died. Zheng Mingjun and his family surrendered to the Qing dynasty. According to this analysis, it is possible that Zheng Tai and others were planning to murder Zheng Chenggong.
After Zheng Chenggong's death, Jung Kyung was first busy dealing with Jung Tae's rebellion, then sought the huge amount of money Jung Tae had in Japan. He himself was almost killed by Zheng Chenggong due to his adultery, so the cause of Zheng Chenggong's death was not thoroughly investigated at the time. It seems that the cause of death of a generation of national heroes needs to be discovered in more historical materials to confirm.
