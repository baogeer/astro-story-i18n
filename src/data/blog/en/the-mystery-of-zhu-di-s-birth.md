 In the TV series “Mountains, Rivers, and Moonlight,” there is a puzzling plot: Why did Zhu Yuanzhang not choose Zhu Di, who has many achievements and outstanding abilities, as his successor after Prince Zhu Biao died of illness?
According to common sense, when the second son, King Qin, and the third son, King Jin, have both died, the fourth son Zhu Di should also take over. Moreover, Zhu Yuanzhang is already very fond of Zhu Di. General Lan Yu once tentatively asked Zhu Biao, “Who is the emperor's favorite among the princes?” Zhu Biao answered, “Sizidi.”
Then why did Zhu Di still lose the election? One important reason is that Zhu Di was not an heir, but rather born out. In a feudal society with a feudal system, this is a major issue. It directly relates to whether a person is eligible to inherit the throne.
Who is Zhu Di's birth mother? Judging from the various historical materials available, there are roughly five types of claims about Zhu Di's birth mother: 1. Empress Ma gave birth to five sons, Zhu Di as the fourth child; 2. Prince Zhu Biao, the second son, King Qin, and the third son, King Jin, were not born to Empress Ma; 3. Empress Ma gave birth to Prince Ma, King Qin, King Jin, and King Zhou. 4. Zhu Di was born to the concubine of Emperor Yuan Shun; 5. Zhu Di was born to the concubine of Emperor Yuan Shun. Among them, the fourth and fifth statements are the most widely circulated.
According to the Mongolian history book “The Origin of Mongolia”, vol. 8, Zhu Di's birth mother was the Mongolian Hongjila clan. She was originally the concubine of Emperor Yuanshun, and her father was Taishi Honggiratoktor; she was pregnant when Hongji entered the Ming Palace, and Zhu Di, who gave birth to her, was the widow of Emperor Yuanshun. This statement is clearly untenable. How could Zhu Yuanzhang accept a pregnant woman as a concubine? Furthermore, Zhu Di was born in the 20th year of Yuan Zhi Zheng (1360), and in August of Yuan Zhi Zheng 28 (1368), Xu Da led an army to conquer Dadu, and Emperor Yuan Shun was forced to flee north in a hurry. Even if Emperor Yuan Shun's concubine was included in Zhu Yuanzhang's harem, this only happened after conquering most of the capital.
As for the opinion that Zhu Di was born as a concubine, many people don't really understand. Why are there Korean people in Zhu Yuanzhang's harem? If you read history carefully, you'll find that at the end of the Yuan dynasty, there were many women from Goryeo in the court. For example, Emperor Yuan Shun's empress, Qi Shi, was a Goryeo native.
According to research by Ming historian Wu Han, Zhu Di's mother should have been a Consort. After the “Battle of the Rebellion” ended and took the throne, the first thing Zhu Di did was to build a large-scale temple to repay her parents' nurturing blessings. The main hall of Daihoon-ji Temple is enshrined not by Empress Ma, but Empress Dowager. Historical materials record that the main entrance to the main hall is closed all year round, and no one else is allowed to enter.
There is also evidence to support the status of Empress Dao: there is a palace dedicated to her ancestors in the Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum. According to the official document “Nanjing Taichang Temple Chronicles”, which is in charge of the royal temple ceremony, the ranking order of the gods is “Xiaoling Shaoling, Emperor Gao and Empress Gao of Taizu, facing south. Left: Consort Li, Crown Prince Sheng Yiwen, King Qin Wei, King Jin Gong... Consort Tang became Emperor Zuwen and Dexilie.” As the mausoleum of Taizo Zhu Yuanzhang, Taizo Temple doesn't dare to make a mistake when it comes to receiving the rank of god, but the world has always had many opinions about the authenticity of “The Chronicles of Taichang Temple in Nanjing.”
Furthermore, according to the “Record of the Lee Dynasty,” Lee Fang-won, the Prince of North Korea (who later became the King of Korea), passed through Beijing during his memorial service and was intended to meet Zhu Di. He was told that it was on the day of her mother's death, so it was inconvenient to see visitors. Li Fangyuan discovered that day was not Empress Ma's death day, which confirmed from the side that Zhu Di was not born to Empress Ma.
Probably because her birth mother is Korean, Joo Di has special feelings for North Korea. When the imperial court held the grand ceremony, messengers from various countries lined up. Zhu Di actually let the North Korean heiji who had come to Asaga sit next to him. The courtesy was so high that the messengers from all countries were amazed.
In fact, there is another phenomenon that is puzzling — Zhu Di has repeatedly emphasized that she was born of Empress Ma. In the opinion of future generations, this seemed unnecessary, and she even wanted to make up for it. When he launched the “Battle of the Restoration”, he told the generals: “My father, Emperor Gao, the heir of Empress Gao, is the heir of the compassionate Empress Gao, and the country is close.” He also emphasized in the edict issued after leading the army into Nanjing: “I am the legitimate son of Emperor Gao. My father has a Ming Xing motto: 'There are no rulers in the DPRK; there is evil within; Wang Dexing asks his master for it. '” As soon as Yongle's first year official history book “The Difficult Chronicles of Fengtian Jing” was opened: “Today's emperor is also the fourth son of Emperor Taizu Gao, and his mother is empress Cigao.” It seems that as soon as they claim to be their heir, they have plenty of reasons to set up an army to seize the throne. Speaking of which, these are only limited presumptions and investigations by people today. After all, the truth of history has gone along with the vast dust and smoke, and there is no trace of it.
