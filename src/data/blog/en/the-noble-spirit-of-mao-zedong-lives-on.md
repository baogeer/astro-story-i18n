 The cultural relics warehouse at the Shaoshan Comrade Mao Zedong Memorial Hall holds two lists of Mao Zedong's gifts, which record how Mao Zedong handed in gifts from 1958 to 1959. Among them are radios, video clips, dragon whiskers, ginseng, antler, wine glasses, etc. Most of these gifts were given by Chinese and foreign friends. According to central government regulations, Mao Zedong ordered the staff to make books one by one and hand them over to the national treasury.
Strict rules have been Mao Zedong's lifelong principle of life. He believes that if you ask others to do it, you must do it yourself first; you must never have the slightest idea of being specific because of your special status and status. In response to gifts from Chinese and foreign friends traveling in the country, Mao Zedong never allowed staff to stay. In 1964, a Malaysian guest sent Mao Zedong 32 kg of bird's nest. He ordered the staff to send it to the Great Hall of the People for a state banquet, saving the country's expenses. The General Assembly Hall is also required to issue a receipt for self-inspection. The staff had no choice but to send it to the General Assembly Hall right away and hand it over with a receipt like this. Another time, a Swiss friend gave Mao Zedong two gold watches. The staff saw that the Omega watch the chairman usually wears was too old, so they advised him to leave one. Mao Zedong believes that leaving this watch is breaking the rules established by himself and the central government; that is, making it special. He said with great emphasis to this comrade: This is for the President of the country, not for an individual. If you're the chairman, he'll give it to you too. I represent the people. This kind of gift cannot be accepted; it must be handed over to the national treasury.
China is known as an “ancient civilized country, a country of manners”. Since ancient times, there has been a style of gift-giving. When it comes to festivals, some people even take the opportunity to give gifts. Mao Zedong is very dissatisfied with this culture. He believes that if this trend continues, it will corrupt the party style and lose people's hearts. As early as the Second Plenary Session of the 7th CPC Central Committee, Mao Zedong proposed to the whole Party to establish the famous “few no” within the party: no living, no gifts, less toasting, and less clapping. It also called for “entering Beijing to catch the test”, using Li Zicheng's greed for comfort and extravagance after entering Beijing, and finally losing his country and mountains, to warn the whole party to continue to maintain a style of hard work, continue to maintain a style of arrogance, and be wary of the bourgeois “sugar-coated cannonball” attacks. On the 30th anniversary of the founding of the Party in 1951, some departments forgot the rules of the Second Plenary Session of the 7th Central Committee, forgot the lessons of Li Zicheng's fiasco, and wrote congratulatory letters, pennants, and gifts to Chairman Mao and the Party Central Committee. Mao Zedong angrily indicated in a document: This was not only a waste, but also a political mistake! In October of the same year, people in Qichun, Hubei sent him 50 kilograms of “water grape rice” that used to be used as “rice.” Mao Zedong asked the General Office of the Central Committee to harshly criticize in his reply, “Once the money is sent, don't give away any material to the central government or leaders in the future. This is not permitted by our Party discipline. He also specifically commissioned Premier Zhou to criticize provincial leaders at a people's congress in response to the local practice of sending souvenirs to central government leaders to try them out early. The Prime Minister said, “I would like to convey Chairman Mao's message to everyone that the local practice of donating souvenirs to the central government is bad. This is the fruit produced by the hard work of the working people. We eat it for nothing. If any of you send something in the future, we will definitely return it in its original form, and we must criticize it. In Mao Zedong's view, serving the people wholeheartedly is the purpose of the Chinese Communist Party. Using their special status and status to take pride in their merits, seek enjoyment, and engage in specialization in private fraud is prohibited and runs counter to the interests of the people. This is Mao Zedong's guidelines, Mao Zedong's style, a real capitalized Chinese Communist Party's code and style!
See spirit in the details. A thin page gift list reflects the clean lives of a generation of great people and the lives of honest service! Chairman Mao left us almost 30 years ago, but his greatness and nobility, and his majestic integrity will always stay on the world!
