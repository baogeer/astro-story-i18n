 The child's daughter-in-law lived a life full of disasters and hardships, and she was an unlucky person. However, there was also a child daughter-in-law in the Han dynasty. She was the empress superior of Emperor Liu Fuling of Han and Zhao. She did not become a child daughter-in-law due to her mother's poor family; quite the opposite; on the contrary, she had a foundation of great wealth. Her father, Shangguanan, and Princess Gai, the sister of Emperor Zhaodi, concocted this family affair for their own benefit. Although the nature is very different from folk child marriage, it is also unfortunate for those involved in the marriage. Although she is a noble empress, the misfortunes she has experienced in her life are similar to those of the people.
Shangguan's grandfather was General Zuo's Shangguanping. His father was Shangguan An, and his maternal grandfather was Grand Sima General Huo Guang. When Emperor Han Wu died, his eight-year-old son, Emperor Zhaodi, succeeded to the throne and assisted the government by Huo Guang, Shangguanping, and General Jin Ri. Huo Guang and Shangguanjie are children and daughters. He also has this kind of relationship with Jin Ri. He is actually in charge of the administration. Emperor Zhao was so young that he was cared for by his older sister, Princess Eyi Gai, who lived in the palace. Shangguan was only a few years old at this time. Her father, Shangguan An, wanted to send her to the palace as an empress to improve her status, but Huo Guang thought she was too young and did not agree. Princess Gai has a foreigner from Gai Fu Ding. She wants to designate Gai Fu as a leader according to Princess Shang's system. Shangguanan said that through her father-in-law and father, helping Princess Gai and Ding Wairen, help Princess Gai. Such an exchange of interests made Princess Gai agree to let Shangguanan's daughter enter the palace and become an empress. This was in the fourth year of the first century AD (83 BC). Emperor Zhao was 11 years old, and the Shangguan clan were not able to get married, so Sang Guan had children in the palace and waited until they were older to actually get married. Because she was made an empress, the imperial court pardoned the world. She visited the Gojo Temple and gave money to her family, nobles, and senior officials. Shangguanan was crowned General Sang Lehou and initially satisfied his desire for power.
In the first year of Yuanfeng (80 BC), the father and son of Shangguanli and Shangguanan rebelled and tried to stand on their own feet. They were killed in failure. The Shangguan clan was only 9 years old. She did not participate in the intervention at a young age, was not tied to the government, and continued to be the empress. Her grandfather, Huo Guang, came to power and protected her as well. When Shangguanan sought rebellion, some of his party members said that the rebellion might affect the empress's safety, but Shangguanan said they couldn't care less; opposition to the Han family was the main thing. After the accident, Shangguan still stood up for his grandfather and father, and used personal slaves to guard the tomb. Emperor Zhao was only considered an official adult when she was crowned at the age of 17. At this point, the superior official was 12 years old. It is estimated that she began managing the affairs of the palace. Huo Guang hoped that Emperor Zhao and Shangguan would have a son and create obstacles so that Emperor Zhao would not get close to other palace women, so that they could only live as husband and wife with the empress. However, they didn't give birth.
In the first year of Yuan Ping (74 BC), the 21-year-old Emperor Zhao died, and Shangguan, 15, became a widow. Huo Guang and others welcomed King Liu He of Changyi, the grandson of Emperor Han Wu, as emperor, and Liu He honored his aunt Shangguan as Empress Dowager. Liu He was indecent and reckless. Huo Guang and others discussed abolishing him, inspired the Shangguan clan, and went to the Chengming Hall of Weiyang Palace, wearing short jackets adorned with jewels, serving hundreds of people with weapons, summoning Liu He, and listening to Shang Shu instruct him to read out Liu He's charges on behalf of the group. The Shangguan clan accused Liu He of “being a pervert for a courtier”, then deposed him (“The Legend of Huo Guang”). Under the arrangement of Huo Guang and others, the Shangguan family agreed to appoint Liu Xun, the great-grandson of Emperor Wu, as emperor, that is, Emperor Xuan of Han, and Emperor Xuan's uncle, Shangguan, as Empress Empress Dowager Emperor. Emperor Xuan was personally in charge of government affairs; the Shangguan clan didn't have any political affairs to do, but something unexpected happened in the palace. Huo Guang's wife, Dong Lu Xian, loves her youngest daughter Cheng Jun and wants to include her as the queen in the palace. Emperor Xuan married the Xu clan before entering the palace and was born to Emperor Yuan. Emperor Xuan ascended the throne and became empress soon after. Huo Guang's wife failed to achieve her goal. It just so happened that she became pregnant and became ill, so she took the opportunity to buy a female doctor to kill the queen, and sent Cheng Jun to Gongye to become an empress. Empress Huo was the aunt of Shangguanshi in her mother's family, but when she arrived at the Liu family, she became a niece and a daughter-in-law. Due to their dual relationship, Shangguan also showed due courtesy to Empress Huo. Shangguan lived until the 2nd year of Jianzhao (37 BC) and died at the age of 52. She spent 47 years in the palace, but was a widow for 32 years.
When she was 15, Shangguan became a woman. Although she is revered as Empress Dowager and Empress Dowager, how much fun does she have in her life! As a woman, 47 years in the palace were tantamount to living in prison; only the material life was better than being a prisoner. Her marriage with Emperor Zhao was also an arranged marriage. It can be seen from this that even at the highest level of marriage in society, arranged marriage was a disaster for the parties involved.
