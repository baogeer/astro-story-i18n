After Empress Dowager Cixi and Emperor Guangxu returned to Luang from Xi'an, Sai Jinhua thought that everyone would probably pay tribute to her; however, Wang Gong and the ministers all only scrambled to show their merits, and even forgot about her existence. Even if one or two people remember, how could they share Yihe's great credit to a Qinglou woman?
Sai Jinhua knew that she could no longer be allowed to “settle down,” so she changed her name to Fu Yulian and restarted her work in an alley in Shaanxi to earn a living as a prostitute. There are also ministers in power, such as Hong Jun's friend Sun Jiayi, and Hong Jun's son and family Lu Runxiang, who cannot allow her to be ugly and disgraceful in Beijing. In 1903 AD, in order to cover up the ugly for the old champion Hong Jun, they used a girl named Feng Ling under Sai Jinhua's “Jinhua Class” to commit suicide by taking opium, disbanded the “Jinhua Class,” kicked Sai Jinhua out of Beijing, and ordered her to return to her native Suzhou.

 Sai Jinhua, an aging person, has been thrown away as something dirty and useless. When she left Beijing, she thought of those who asked for her in the past; now no one sees her, and no one wants her; her tears flickered in her eye circles; then, two bean-sized teardrops burst out and slowly rolled down her cheeks; the tears continued to pour out and flow faster.
 
 Sai Jinhua sat in the car leaving Beijing. Amidst the monotonous squeak of the wheels, her eyes caught straight. She hoped no one else would look at her. Thinking of the disdainful look people had when they looked at her, she straightened her back and sat a little more straight. Amidst the sound of celebration in the imperial palace of the Qing dynasty, Sai Jinhua left in a horse-drawn carriage, and finally cried at the back of the person.
 
 Back in Suzhou, Sai Jinhua recalled the bustle of the old days with a traumatized heart. It seemed like a spring dream. She used to be a woman who didn't want to be lonely. In her early thirties, she still had a great style. How could she stay in Suzhou forever? As a result, I came back to Shanghai to seek development, but it was no longer comparable to the scenery of the past. The suspension of the “Beijing Shi Sai House” brand has already depreciated its value; it was downgraded from the “Book House” on the same day to the “Chang 3” level. After 2 years of inactivity, she finally got married for a second time.
 
 The name of the marriage partner this time is Cao Ruizhong. She is a general inspector of the Shanghai-Nanjing Railway. They are loyal and practical. This time, Sai Jinhua made up her mind to move from splendor to calm and be a stable and stable housewife. She gave her good friend Sun Zuozhou, who had been living with her for more than ten years. Grandpa Sun's farewell gift is proof. This gift is an exquisite ivory bone folding fan with a picture of a weeping willow and a poem on it. The poem is written like this:

 However, the second marriage did not bring peace and happiness to Sai Jinhua. In the meantime, her loved ones died several times, leaving her in a state of grief. The 1911 1911 Revolution was successful, and the Manqing dynasty was overthrown. Her husband, Cao Ruizhong, also passed away due to acute enteritis. She's living a wandering life again.
 
 At this point, Sai Jinhua was almost 40 years old. It was like a glass of old wine. Although it has gone through many vicissitudes, it exudes an intoxicating aroma. A benefactor I knew earlier, Wei Sling, who was a senator in the Republic of China government at the time, lent a helping hand to her at the right time and took her to Beijing to live together on Cherry Street outside the front gate. By the summer of the 6th year of the Republic of China (1917), Sai Jinhua changed to the name of Zhao Lingfei's girlfriend. As Wei Siling returned to Shanghai to have a grand new-style wedding, it was considered the beginning of a quiet and happy couple for a few years.
 
 In the 11th year of the Republic of China (1922), Sai Jinhua lost her mother in spring and her husband in summer.
 
 After Wei Siling's death, the Wei family thought she was a blessing and often scoffed at her mercilessly. Unable to bear it, Sai Jinhua had no choice but to move out of the Wei family and lived behind closed doors in a bungalow in Ju Renli. One master and one servant depended on pawns and debts. At this point, Sai Jinhua was already an old lady in her 50s. She was sick, and her sides were white. Not many people knew that she was a famous flower of the generation that once bleached half the sky.
 
 In the winter of the 25th year of the Republic of China (1936), Sai Jinhua finally ran out of gas and died at the age of 65. Thanks to some famous people from her hometown who initiated fundraising, the matter was finally settled for her. She was buried near Jonan Taorantei, next to Kozuka and Parmozuka.
