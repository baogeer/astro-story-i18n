 In the early Qing dynasty, there was a Han princess named Kong Sizhen.
Kong Sizhen's father, Kong Youde, was originally a general in charge of Liaoyang in the Ming dynasty. He was crowned King of Dingnan after the Qing dynasty, defended Guangxi, and garrisoned his army in Guilin. In the 9th year of Shunzhi, Zhang Xianzhong revolted and defeated Kong Jiajun. Kong Youde loved his concubine and set himself on fire behind closed doors. Their entire family was killed by the Western Army; only their daughter Kong Si-jeong was rescued by her subordinate Guoan. The family and Empress Xiaozhuang brought him into the palace to raise him in Shunzhi for 11 years. Kong Youde's coffin was transported from Guangxi to Tokyo (Liaoyang) via Beijing. At Kong Sizhen's request, Emperor Shunzhi agreed to bury him in Beijing, and treated Kong Sizhen as a hegge. Later, he married Sun Long's son, Yanling, the son of Sun Long.
In the 12th year of Kangxi, there was a rebellion in San Francisco. Following advice from Kong Sizhen, Sun Yanling said to surrender. Wu Sangui assassinated Sun Yanling after learning the news. Kong Sizheng fell into Wu Sangui's hands, and was not able to return to Beijing until after defeating San Francisco. When Kong Sizheng's family moved to Guangxi, the Qing government took advantage of the momentum to downgrade her status from Heshuo Gege to county chief and clearly stipulated “not to be an example”, so Kong Sizheng became the only Han princess in the more than 200 years of the Qing dynasty.
