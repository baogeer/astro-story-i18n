 The Hani people have this custom. Every elderly man or woman with children and grandchildren dies, they have to hold a grand “don't rub” funeral. When the old man of the family died, he set off three sounds of earthen artillery and reported the message “don't rub” to the three villages.
After bathing and changing clothes for the deceased, every day at dusk, a group of girls from the same family or close family crouched around the deceased and held “Michawei” with their hands holding their chin.
“Michawi” is a traditional elegy sung by Hani women when guarding the spirits of the dead. The cry is mixed with singing, sad and sad words, and the story is beautiful and interesting.
After singing a few words, it was accompanied by an uh-huh, like an old man coughing for a long time. It is said that this humming can make all the heart-wrenching lyrics enter the heart of the dead; otherwise, crying would be futile.
A few days before the funeral, men, women, and children who lost their families all took off their shoes and hats. They did not brush their hair, did not get their hair cut, were not properly dressed, and the husband and wife had separate rooms to express their deep condolences to the deceased.
On the day of the funeral, family and friends from outside the village held chickens and sheep, and invited many men and women to mourn. The dead family had to slaughter at least five or six scalpers. Apart from a small amount of beef distributed to family and friends in the village, all the rest were cooked and eaten together in the village square.
At the time of the funeral, the deceased's uncle asked the deceased to dig a large hole in the back wall of the house. Immediately, the men, women, children, and close relatives of the bereaved families carried the coffin containing the corpses and sang a sad elegy, hugged it back and forth, went through the hole in the wall, and lifted the coffin out and placed it under the roof of the back of the house.
At this point, the clever Bema stayed close to Funeral Spirit, singing an ancient rule song while chopping underground with a bamboo tube containing grains, shells, and silverware. The bamboo tube made a loud sound, which happened to be the accompaniment of the Bema song.
At the same time, men, women, and children from this village and people coming from outside the village are required to wear gorgeous costumes. Some were riding tall, well-dressed horses, and some were paired with men and women, shouting and dancing while rocking fans, and circled around the house of the bereaved family.
The sound of earthen artillery continued, a loud trumpet blaring, and drumming. Some were crying, some laughing, and it was earth-shaking, showing farewell to the elderly until the dead were carried out of the village gate.
At this point, don't rub every pot or bowl in the house shakes and makes a loud noise. In Hani, it is called Olenso, which means that the dragon from the river and the sea is visiting the elderly. There is an ancient legend among the Hani people that comes from being unruly.
Legend has it that in ancient times, when heaven and earth were just founded, there was only one woman named Pengran Ga Ama. She spent her days collecting wild fruits from the mountains and forests for her family.
One day, she was tired of picking wild fruits. She sat under the shade of a tree to rest, cool off, and take a nap in comfort. The hazy feeling was as if a gentle breeze was blowing, and she couldn't help but feel a chill.
Strangely enough, this miraculous breeze actually made this woman's stomach, feet, thighs, hands, toes, and fingers all pregnant. After nine months, this woman gave birth to 77 species of birds, animals, and humans.
Unexpectedly, the children went down to the ground for the rest of their lives and ran away from their grandma one by one.
The eldest son is a tiger, shouting, and ran into the forest; the second son was a wind, and he didn't know where he was going; the third was a human and had gone into the distance; the fourth son was a dragon. He swam deep into the big river and river after seeing his grandmother.
Leaving behind a poor woman, living a difficult life alone.
Year after year, day by day, Grandma is getting older. There is no one to take care of her. Her body is covered with vine bark as clothes to protect against the cold; she cannot climb trees to pick fruit; wild yams picked from Qingbian are used as food.
At noon one summer, there wasn't a hint of clouds in the sky. The hot sun was shining on the earth, and the old grandmother was struggling to plow wild yam by a beautiful mountain. I was plowing. Suddenly a tree rang loudly, and a tiger with a flower neck jumped on the ground.
Grandma was taken aback. At first sight, it was a tiger. She was sad and said, “Tiger, I'm your mom, do you even want to eat it with your grandmother?”
“Grandma, I only eat meat and drink blood. Don't go to the Shanqing side; just walk away.” The tiger said that.
Grandma was very sad. She wanted someone to take care of her, but she didn't know where she was and couldn't find it. She had to come to a suspended side to call out the wind and scream and say, “Grandma, I'm invisible, I have no heart, heart, liver, or heart, and I can't take care of you. Please go somewhere else and ask for help.”
The old grandmother left the hanging rock sad and went to the seaside to call for help from Dragon Er. Hearing her grandmother's bleak cry for help, the dragon hurriedly swam to the beach and said to Grandma, “Grandma, you are so poor, but I'm a dragon in the ocean, and I can't take care of you!”
“I have given birth to so many sons; they are all unscrupulous creatures; I have only one way to die.” The old grandmother said with tears in her eyes.
After listening to Grandma's sad story, Long Er felt very sympathetic and comforted, saying, “Grandma, don't be sad; as long as you get used to living, I'll take you to the Dragon Palace to raise you.” After stopping, Dragon said, “There are lots of loaches and oysters in the Dragon Palace. They are my guards, so you don't have to be afraid.
Dragon helped her old grandmother to Dragon Palace. As soon as I entered the door, Grandma saw some large and small hemp snakes of different shapes, grinning teeth, and screaming and dancing. Grandma was afraid and didn't want to stay in Dragon Palace.
As soon as it dawned the next day, Grandma quietly said to Long Er, “Dragon, Grandma is not used to living in this place. Those hemp snakes with their mouths open all the time are so scary. I want to go back.” Grandma was frightened and clearly saw loach and oysters as scary snakes.
Since Grandma wants to go back, Dragon Er won't be easy to keep. When she left, Long Er gave Grandma a pack of gifts and a pair of drums and said, “Grandma, this pack is a pack of grain seeds. If you take it back and scatter it on the hillside, your life will be better. Remember, don't just open it on the road.”
Long Er went on to say, “Grandma, when you're about to end your life, let people play these drums. As soon as the drum sounds, I'll see you there”.
Grandma said goodbye to the dragon and came back. Halfway through, I was so hungry that I couldn't help but crouched down by a clear stream of water to see if there were any foods in the present to fill my hunger.
Open it up and take a look, ho! There were so many things to eat. She picked up just a few to eat. She wasn't careful when eating. A few varieties leaked out of her hand and fell into a trickling gutter, and one was taken away by the water. Since then, the custom of the Hani people not having lunch by the gutter has been passed down, allowing Gu Soul to live forever.
Grandma went back to the cave where she used to live and scattered the remaining grain seeds on the hillside. By the end of August, when Manmu Xiaoxiao's late autumn, bright yellow valleys had grown on the hillside. Heavy grains swayed with the wind, shone golden under the bright sun, and nodded and smiled at Grandma.
Grandma had a good life. It spread all over the place. It spread to the ears of the sons who had been exiled far away. The sons came back one after another and were surrounded by Grandma. Only the dragon never came back. Grandma taught the children how to cultivate in spring, harvest in summer, harvest in autumn, and harvest in winter, and live a good and rational life.
Having a good life, the sons came back to their side. Grandma smiled and had fun all day long, dancing with joy, and couldn't help but play the drums as a local drummer.
Unexpectedly, the sound of drums spread to the Dragon Palace in the big river. Dragon Er thought her grandmother had died. After a cloud of fog, she hurriedly ran to the world to visit Grandma. Open the door and take a look. Grandma is alive in the spring breeze, surrounded by countless children.
Long Er was very unhappy and said, “Grandma, why are you messing around with drums! I didn't say don't play the drums until you're about to lose your breath.”
“Dragon! The whole family was reunited, had food and clothing, and Grandma was so happy that she knocked on her.”
“Alright, alright, now I'll set a rule. In the future, whenever you're happy, hit the drums, and don't knock indiscriminately.” Dragon said.
Since then, the Hani people on Ailao Mountain have been blessing each other by blessing each other by playing simple and majestic drums as soon as the first day of the year and when the festival is happy.
Dragon said to the older brothers, “Brother, listen carefully. When Grandma dies, don't just beat the drums. I couldn't get up just by hitting the drums; I had to take a bamboo tube with grains, shells, and silverware inside and knock it to the ground. As soon as the stuff inside the bamboo tube makes a loud and clear sound, indicating that Grandma has died, I'll come and see.”
After speaking, Dragon went back to Dragon Palace. Since then, when the Hani people give burials to elderly men and women with children and grandchildren, they have to hold a solemn funeral.
Additional notes:
“Don't rub” is an ancient custom of the Hani people. It is widely spread in places such as the Honghe River, Luchun River, Yuanjiang River, and Mojiang River. “No Rubbing” ceremonies are grand and expensive, so usually only wealthy people can afford to hold them.
“Don't rub” funerals held all over the world are similar. “Don't rub” funerals are held in Honghe River, Yuanjiang and other places. At least nine cows must be killed, including one white buffalo to be called “No Rubbing.” Killing less than nine cows can only be called “compared to rubbed”, that is, a moderate funeral.
At most, ten cows were killed at the “Don't Rub” funeral. During the “Don't Rub” ceremony, the owner's family had to entertain everyone who came to watch with rice (no food), so people of all ethnic groups in nearby villages went to watch.
For example, when there are more than 1,000 people hanging and watching, another cow must be killed. The Hani language is “to rest with the same cow,” and “the same” is “a thousand,” meaning to kill a thousand cows.
