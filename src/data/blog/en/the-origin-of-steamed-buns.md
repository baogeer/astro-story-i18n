 In everyday life, the “steamed bun” that people eat is a type of food made by fermenting and steaming flour. Originally, this type of food had a round shape, but later developed. In the northern part of our country, those without fillings were called steamed buns, while those with fillings were called buns. And our country's food industry honors Zhu Geliang, the famous minister of the Three Kingdoms, as the grandmaster of the steamed bun and bun business. What happened? We have to start with a very interesting folklore story circulating in our country.
Zhu Geliang's “Master Schedule” has the phrase “Five friends cross the border; it's not bad to go deep.” It describes how Zhu Geliang led an army into the southwest to conquer Meng Fou, the lord of Nanman Cave, while assisting Liu Bei in fighting the world back then.
In other words, when Zhu Geliang led his army across Lushui, due to the sparse crowds in this area and the hot summer in May of the lunar calendar, Lushui was different from water in other places. “The miasma was too strong,” and the water was toxic. Soldiers ate Lushui and many people were sick and died. Someone under Zhu Geliang put forward a superstitious idea: to kill some “Nanman” prisoners and use their heads to sacrifice the river god of Lushui in order to ensure the safety of the Shu Army when crossing the river. Zhu Geliang couldn't bear to kill him again. He thought of a remedy. He asked the chef to use white noodles mixed with water to make the head look like a human head, and wrap beef and lamb inside to replace a real head, and named him “Darehead.” On the night of the same day, Kong Ming put on his Bagua robe, placed several beacons on the shores of Lushui, and provided Aries, Black Sheep, and 49 steamed buns for the record. At three o'clock in the middle of the night, they ordered people to throw sacrifices into the river.
At dawn the next day, I saw that the clouds cleared and the fog dispersed, the river level cleared, and the Shu Jun flowing safely through Lushui.
Later, in order to boost morale, he also ordered the soldiers to kill pigs and slaughter cows, mix the pork and beef, chop it into meat paste, and put it in noodles to make a “wild head,” which was steamed and then eaten by the soldiers. As a result, the soldiers soon got rid of their illnesses.
As a result, the rumor that a “wild head” in the shape of a human head could avoid the plague first spread among people around Lushui, and it soon spread to the north of our country. However, it's so scary to call it a “wild head,” so people replaced the word “banshou” with the word “banshou” and wrote “steamed bun.”
