 The traditional Buyei festival “June 6th” has a long history. There are different legends about its origin from place to place. One of them is that in the ancient flood era, “Pangu”, the ancestor of the Buyei people, accumulated experience in cultivating rice during labor and had good harvests year after year. Later, he married the daughter of Dragon King and had a son, named Xinheng. Once the son offended his mother, the dragon girl went back to the Dragon Palace and never came back. “Pangu” had no choice but to get married again. Pangu died on June 6 of the year. Since then, Xinheng has been abused by his stepmother and almost murdered. Unable to bear it, he went to heaven to accuse his stepmother and vowed to destroy the rice seedlings she had cultivated. After her stepmother understood, she finally made peace with Xinheng. Every year on June 6, the day Pangu died, slaughtering pigs and slaughtering ducks, making bread, and sacrificing Pangu. As a result, the Buyei people held an event to celebrate Pangu for their ancestors every year on June 6 to show that their children and grandchildren would continue to harvest grains year after year.
