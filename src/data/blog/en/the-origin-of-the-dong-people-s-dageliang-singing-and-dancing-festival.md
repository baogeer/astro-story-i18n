 The Dawuliang Song Festival, also known as the Song Festival, is held every year on the 18th day before the beginning of the lunar summer. It is not only a forbidden day in Dongxiang, but also a traditional festival in Dongxiang. It was listed as a provincial intangible cultural heritage in 2006.
The Dawuliang Song Festival is also currently the largest folk music feast in the Dong region in China and the world, with the most complete programs and the largest number of participants. It is known as “the precious oral intangible cultural heritage of mankind.”
This event is based on the ancient legends of the Dong people. Legend has it that more than 300 years ago, there was a good-looking girl from the Dong family named Xiao Nu. She loved to work for a long time at her house. The two secretly fell in love.
However, the Ya general, who is stationed in Xiongguan in the three provinces, is salivating her and wants to marry her. In order to be able to look private, Xiao Nu Sulong met and eloped on the day of Dawu. Unfortunately, under the pursuit of General Ya, both of them unfortunately fell to death on Mount Dawuliang.
A strong couple and a poignant and moving love story have created a traditional song festival involving the children of the Xiang Gui Qiandong family — the Dawuliang Song Club.
To commemorate them, every year on the day of “Dawu” 18 days before the beginning of the lunar summer, the men and women's youth associations on the border of the three provinces (districts) of Xiang Gui and Gui dress up in full costume and gather at Dawuliang Mountain to dance, share their feelings with songs, and meet friends through songs.
Sing the “Japanese Song” during the day to let each other get to know each other at first. If the girl looks at each other, she will throw up the opening “Peach Blossom Song”, and the young man will pick up the song if he is interested in the girl.
Throughout the day, Dawuliang Mountain converged into a sea of people, songs, and love! Therefore, the day of the Dawuliang Song Festival is also called “Valentine's Day” for people in Dongxiang.
The village at the bottom of the mountain is just as lively. Young guys and girls all have to wear holiday costumes, pick meals from their homes and set them up for a party. They sing praises, toasts, tea songs, play the piano, play roe, dance, and seek friends.
The old people chatted at Gulou and recalled the beautiful past when they were young. In the evening, young men and women will be invited to various nearby villages to “be for” (meaning: to walk to the village as guests), or men to women's homes or women to “be guests for Ningmei” (that is, enter the household).
