 Autumn Festival is a traditional festival for the Hani people: it is held every year on Pig Day or Dog Day (Chinese Zodiac Day) in May of the lunar calendar, also known as the “Year of May.”
There is a beautiful story about the origin of the Autumn Festival in the Hani people.
A long time ago, two brothers and sisters lived in a Hani cottage. The older brother's name was Helang, and the younger sister's name was Aang. Alan Wu is an excellent artist. Aang is smart and beautiful. They both love to help the townspeople do their work.
At that time, the sun and moon appeared erratic in the sky; sometimes they came out for a few days, and sometimes they didn't come out for a few days, making crops unable to grow normally.
Alan and Aang's brother and sister discussed going to the sky separately to persuade the sun and moon to appear regularly.
On this day, the brother and sister cut down chestnut wood and created Moqiu. They rode Moqiu, and Mo Qiu spun rapidly, sent Alang and Aang to the sky and found the sun and moon.
After exhausting their words, the brother and sister finally convinced the Sun and the Moon to agree that the Sun will rise during the day and the Moon will rise at night.
Since then, the sun and moon have appeared regularly, and crops have grown very well and harvested year after year.
However, the two brothers and sisters never came back.
To commemorate them, every year on Pig Day or Dog Day in May of the lunar calendar, Hani Cottage carries out activities to grind autumn. At the same time, families also kill chickens and ducks, rake spring glutinous rice, and worship the inventors of Tiandi and Mo Qiu — Alan and Aang. Since then, it has become a festival.
