 Maonan people in the Kap Maonan Township area in Pingtang County, Guizhou, have to celebrate the Torch Festival every year on the night of July 30th. There is a legend about the origin of the Fire Festival — legend has it that a long time ago, the ancestors of the Maonan people lived and worked in a place with beautiful mountains and green water in Kappu. One day, a Maonan man who had gone out to do business hurriedly ran back to Zhai Lao and said, “On the 30th night of this year, a team of aliens will invade our territory. They are tall and well-armed,...” Zhailao immediately summoned everyone to discuss countermeasures. Everyone agreed that hard work would cost money; only by using questionable military strategies can the attacks of foreigners be repelled. As a result, Zhai Lao organized men, women, and children from all over the village to put many lights at the foot of the village, on the side of the road, and on the hillside, then used Hou Teng to connect them and hang many firecrackers. When it was almost dark, people from all over the village ambushed, blocked the news, stocked with horns, gunpowder, and gongs and drums, and waited for the invaders. When aliens took advantage of the night to sneak into the foot of the village, there was a sudden sound of artillery. The Maonan people who had been ambushed for a long time lit up the lights and moved Hou Teng. Immediately, the sound of gongs, drums, horns, firecrackers, and roars echoed. When foreigners saw shaking torches on the hillside and on the side of the road, they couldn't figure out how many Maonans actually had been ambushed. They were all shocked and shocked. They were afraid to rush into the stockade, and fled one after another. When some aliens fled, they were caught by the Maonans and dragged into the stockade because they were unfamiliar with the terrain. The villagers personally untied them, entertained them with the best wine and dishes, shocked them, and patiently advised them, which made the prisoners very moved. Since then, aliens have never invaded Maonan territory.
In order to commemorate the achievements of their ancestors, people from all over the village gathered on New Year's Eve to hold lighted torches, beat gongs and drums, brag horns, and set off firecrackers to wish each other a happy new year, and “scolded” and “captured” each other. It is said that the harsher the “curse”, the more auspicious the coming year. The two sides secretly sent people to mix in with each other's crowds. Unexpectedly, they “captured” each other members and returned home to punish them with alcohol and intoxicate them. This custom has continued until now, and the Maonans call it the “Fire Festival.”
