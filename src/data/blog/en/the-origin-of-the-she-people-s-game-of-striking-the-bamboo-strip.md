 She people living in Fujian, Zhejiang, Guangdong and other places like to play “size” during the “February 2” concert and during their free time. “Size” is played like this: Many people form a large circle in an empty field. One person stands in the circle, holding a small wooden stick called a “ruler” about a foot long, and a small bamboo stick called an “inch” long chopsticks. The insiders hit the bamboo strip with a wooden stick, causing the bamboo stick to spin and fly forward, and the people outside the circle ran to pick it up. If a bamboo stick falls to the ground, pick it up and throw it into the circle. People in the circle can catch it with their hands, or hit the bamboo back with a wooden stick. If outsiders can catch the “inch,” even if they can win. Winners replace their insiders. How did this kind of fun, intense, and unique sporting activity come about? Here's another story.
Legend has it that 700 years ago, the Shes hero Lan Fenggao led the She Army to resist attacks by government forces in Tingzhou, Fujian. Since most of the Shes Army were hunters, they carried bows, crossbows, and bamboo arrows, and had hundreds of shots to kill the government army. Later, a large number of reinforcements came to kill them. Blue Wind oversaw the crowd, then ordered the She Army to retreat across the river and left alone.
The two sides fought each other for a while. When the officers saw that he was brave and didn't dare to approach, they concentrated on the archers to shoot at the Blue Wind. Blue Wind fought back and hit, breaking the bow string due to excessive force. In an emergency, he waved the bow and string in his hand to block random arrows fired by the military while stepping back in a boat. The officers and military are afraid that he is brave, and they don't know the truth about the other side; they are afraid to cross the river. Lan Fenggao finally held on to Tingzhou City.
Later, in order to commemorate Lan Feng's resourcefulness and bravery, the children and grandchildren of the Shes family practiced their ability to shoot random arrows. Every holiday or agricultural leisure time, they gathered around an empty field to play “size.”
