 Legend has it that a long time ago, King Yang Gu led the tribe to migrate to the foot of Fanjing Mountain. They saw beautiful mountains and clear water. The trees were lush, and flat dams were all over the place. The ground could grow Yangchun, go up the mountain to catch animals, and go down the river to catch fish and shrimp, so he settled here with his people. In July of one year, a powerful team of foreigners marched into the Fanjing Mountains, set up a camp, and sent messengers to send Yang Gru a letter of war. They agreed to fight on the banks of the Zhouluo River on the first day after the year. When Yang Gu received the war letter, it was already July 27th. As a result, he couldn't eat well, couldn't sleep well, and walked back and forth in the stockade. The people of the tribe are happy to prepare New Year's goods, make rice cakes, and kill pigs and sheep; no one knows that a major disaster is imminent. As Yang Gru walked and thought about a plan, he hit his head against a pine tree with a “thump” sound. At this point, a group of puppies met and laughed out loud. Yang Guru was attracted to the puppies in front of him because they were playing a war game. After watching for half a while, Yang Gu's eyes suddenly brightened up, her sadness disappeared. She brushed her beard with joy and smiled and said, “Saved, saved, we are saved...”
On this night, Yang Gu summoned the heads of the 18-dong and forty-eight villages in Jiuxi to go to his place of residence to discuss the resignation of the enemy. When everyone arrived, Yang Gu took a sigh of leaf smoke and stood up excitedly and said, “We have always lived here. It's been quiet and comfortable. Right now, the foreign enemy army is under pressure and wants to seize our home. For the sake of our children and grandchildren, I announced the Lunar New Year one day early. The day the enemy celebrated the Lunar New Year, when they were defeated!”
On the 29th of July, the leaders conveyed Yang Gu's orders, so all the Tujia were busy celebrating the New Year early. Too late to fry the pork finely, they cut the pork into chunks, put it in the pot, mix it with pork offal, cabbage, radish, and vermicelli, and put the chunks of meat wrapped in rice noodles and steamed with the rice (now called “noodle meat”). At this point, they grabbed the beaten glutinous rice noodles into a pile of rolled soybean noodles and ate it. They scooped up and drank even the bad rice wine. Once everyone has had enough to eat and drink, they hang the leftover meals and rice wine around their waist in lunch boxes and gourds. God swept away the dark, and King Yang Gu set out with his family from the Territory in full gear. Along the way, they crossed the jungle of mountains, crossed dangerous waterfalls, and visited enemy camps 60 miles away. When the light was slightly revealed that day, the Tujia soldiers had already arrived near the enemy battalion. Yang Gu ordered everyone to dig up the pit and lie ambush in the deep mountains and old woods.
Furthermore, on the 30th day of the year, the enemy battalion officers and soldiers from afar all wanted to go home for the New Year. They had no intention of fighting at all, but I saw that they were drunk in the barracks. At this point, horns and war drums suddenly sounded from all sides, and Turkish soldiers waved spears and rushed to the enemy battalion... Soon, more than half of the enemy soldiers were killed and wounded, and the rest fled with their leader in despair. The Tujia soldiers took advantage of the victory in the pursuit. It wasn't until the next day, when they drove the enemy troops out of the big and small rivers, that the class returned to North Korea. Since then, foreigners are no longer afraid to go to Tujia Shanzhai in Fanjing Mountain to harass them.
Yang Gu triumphed with his troops. The happy news spread all over the 18-hole and 48-village of Jiuxi. The whole family was very happy. People set off a pile of roaring bonfires on the dam, put on mellow rice wine, sounded cowhide drums, knocked on bronze gongs, and played trumpets and trombones. For a while, the women were wearing long hair, while the men were bare-chested, covered in earthenware quilts, with golden chicken tails on their heads, and legs tied with straw. Everyone danced and sang back to shoulder around the bonfire. The bold young man also jumped and jumped over the fire.
Since then, to commemorate this day, Tujia generations have followed the custom and eating habits of celebrating the New Year one or two days before the end of the year. Every year after the New Year, families eat mixed dishes, make rice cakes, steam noodles, drink mash wine, light bonfires, and sing and dance; or families sit around the fire pond and sing until dawn. Legend has it that they can't sleep. They still have to be ready to face the battle at any time to prevent the enemy from making a comeback. Later generations call it a “night vigil.”
