When his mother delivered the meal, he forgot to release the bullwhip to pick it up. Her mother mistakenly thought she was going to be beaten again, and died in fear of being thrown into a pond. The son repented and used a wooden statue of his mother in sugar pear for worship. This custom of sacrificing ancestors is the origin of the “clay magnet” of the Yi people. The story exhorts people to act filial piety as soon as possible, and not to wait for their loved ones to die out."

 There used to be two mothers. The son didn't care about his mother, and even beat his mother often. The son plowed the ground every day. The mother was very concerned about her son. She thought he had a hard time plowing the ground and delivered food to him every day, but sometimes the son hit his mother when it was delivered late. One day, my son went to plow the ground and saw a nest of sparrows in the roadside tree. The old finches flew around looking for food and fed them one bite at a time. It immediately reminded him that his mother had also painstakingly and lovingly raised them like old sparrows. He felt a deep sense of self-blame and guilt, and thought he would never abuse his mother in the future. It's already time for lunch, and I haven't seen my mother deliver the meal. I think it's probably a long way. He forgot to put down the whip he was hitting the cow and went along the way to pick him up. After walking, she saw his mother coming, but when she saw him walking with a cow whip, she thought he was here to hit her when it was late, so she hurriedly left for lunch and ran away. While chasing him, he shouted, “I'm here to pick you up; I'm not going to hit you, don't run. However, her mother didn't believe it. She ran all the way to the edge of a pond and had no way to escape. She thought it would be better to die if beaten, so she jumped into the pond and drowned. The son stayed by the pond and cursed for two days and two nights, and a sugar pear tree grew in the pond. He really regretted treating his mother the way he used to. To express his condolences, he used sugar pear wood to carve a statue of his mother and made offerings and salute incense every day. This custom continues to this day.
This is the origin of the “mud magnet” of the Yi people.
