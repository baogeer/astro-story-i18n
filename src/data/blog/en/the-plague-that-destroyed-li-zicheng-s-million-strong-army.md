 The real reason for Li Zicheng's final defeat has always been a mystery. Over the past 100 years, the explanations given by experts have also been benevolent and wise. Some say they are unpopular, some say they are defeated by pride and complacency, others say they are defeated by corruption and depravity, others say they have lost to red and evil, etc., but in any case, none of them are convincing. Because none of these answers can explain a single question: At the time of the occupation of Beijing, Li Zicheng's army was huge and at over 1 million. Why did the army seem to have suddenly lost its combat effectiveness after 40 days of entering Beijing - it was destroyed as soon as the Qing army hit it, the army was defeated like a mountain, and it fell flat ever since?
Is this army undisciplined? Not at all. Li Zicheng governed the army well, and the rebels were very organized. Is it because the material strength of this huge army can't keep up? Li Zicheng's army obtained 70 million dollars in Beijing by fighting corruption and chasing stolen goods, which is equivalent to ten years of the Ming dynasty's tax revenue. He can at least win the hearts of the people by implementing “invading the king without food” for at least a few years. He has enough financial resources to support his huge army.
This is strange. When the military was lacking, Li Zicheng was able to organize an army of one million with repeated defeats and battles. Why did he lose his fighting power after being poor? Why didn't they organize a decent battle after withdrawing from Beijing? You need to know that indomitable character is Li Zicheng's remarkable characteristic. In several previous battles, his army was almost completely destroyed. At one time, there were only 18 people left, and at one time there were only more than 50 people left. However, Li Zicheng relied on perseverance and made a comeback time and time again. In the end, he invaded Beijing with millions of soldiers and destroyed Daming. So, after entering Beijing, what caused Li Zicheng to lose his energy? What exactly is this mystery? It has been stirring up blogger Liu Jixing's heart for a long time. After reading Cao Shuji's scientific arguments in the “Collection of Essays on 50 Years of Historical Research,” I was all of a sudden and cheerful: it turns out that Li Zicheng did not lose to Manqing, but to the plague that raged rampant at the time!
There was a plague epidemic at the end of the Ming dynasty. Around the time Li Zicheng entered Beijing, the plague epidemic spread in the surrounding area of Beijing. There are some documents that document this. Plague is a severe infectious disease, the causative agent of which is Yersinia pestis. It mainly spreads between humans - fleas - mice. It was characterized by swollen lymph nodes, and the deceased had dark skin. Research shows that every time the plague spreads to a new area, it has a high mortality rate and is highly contagious because the locals are not resistant to mice and fleas. The death rate is above 50%. According to some documents, the death rate of the first plague epidemic in Europe in the 7th century exceeded 70%. As can be seen, the plague is a terrible disease that is extremely easy to spread, has a high mortality rate, and is difficult to control.
Li Zichengjun arrived in Beijing in March of the lunar calendar (April and May in the Gregorian calendar). Relevant documents record that the plague appeared in the Beijing area in the fall of Chong Zhen. It was only due to low winter temperatures and the spread of the plague slowed down. Spring blossoms bloomed in March, pink apricots, white, fleas, and mice became active, and large-scale plague broke out naturally. Due to the high death rate of plague infections, it is not surprising that Chong Zhen's Imperial Forest Army lost combat effectiveness in a short period of time. As a result, Li Zicheng occupied Beijing without facing a major war. After Li Zicheng's army entered Beijing, he was infected with the plague, and his natural combat effectiveness was greatly reduced. Of course, he was unable to defeat the Qing soldiers — even though their numbers were far lower than his own. Also, losing the army became the source of plague infection as a result - the literature records that “thieves are everywhere a major plague.” As a result, although Li Zicheng was rich in the world at this time, he was able to recruit soldiers and buy horses on a large scale, but once the new recruits were infected with the plague, they lost their combat effectiveness. No matter how many troops there were, they were useless, and they were never able to resist the Tiger and Wolf Division of Manqing. The plague spread to the military camp and was unable to get rid of it for a long time. Li Zicheng's spirit was devastated, so he lost his country and fell flat. “There was nothing I could do.”
Since soldiers lived in groups, the plague epidemic hit ancient soldiers devastated. Plague broke out in Europe in the 14th century, killing 25 million people, many of them soldiers. Li Zicheng's army of one million people was almost helpless in the face of the raging plague; they had no choice but to sit and wait to die.
So why didn't the Qing soldiers suffer from the plague? Europeans discovered from murals and text left behind when studying the plague in 14th century Europe that only cavalry survived the plague pandemic. As a result, it was concluded that the plague is spread through fleas. Fleas hate horse smell, so cavalry are not infected by the plague! As we all know, the Eight Flags Iron Horsemen and Qing soldiers are mainly composed of cavalry, so they are not susceptible to the plague. Although there was only a hundred thousand troops, the fighting power of the Qing army, which was not affected by the plague, was naturally superior to Li Zicheng's army, which was deeply affected by the plague (although its number was far greater than that of the Qing army).
The plague was a new type of severe infectious disease that the Chinese did not understand at the time. No one understood it, and it was impossible to treat it. Under these natural conditions, the fall of the Ming dynasty and the failure of Li Zicheng were inevitable. In the military game between Han and Man, the plague helped Manqing a lot. Li Zicheng's army of one million was destroyed by a small plague in this way. It is estimated that the majestic leader of the uprising also left a sigh of “Heaven dies, me too” when he died.
