 The most famous Qiang dance is the Shalang dance. “Sharang” means “sing and shake”. It is a typical Qiang Guozhuang dance.
Once upon a time, in the early morning of May 5 of the lunar calendar, the sky was covered with morning glow, and Qiang women and girls dressed in gorgeous costumes stepped on the dew and pulled mugwort in the face of the ethereal fog in the mountains. Some also washed sacred springs to eliminate all diseases. At that time, a married woman had to wear her wedding dress and bring dried food, bacon, and wine prepared for her by the man in her family to participate in the traditional festival of early May 5. While men, women, and children were singing and dancing, Goddess Sharon stepped on her feet and danced around the beautiful Haizi. Her loud singing attracted countless Qiang people, and her beautiful dance enchanted the Qiang people. Everyone couldn't help but learn with her. After dusk, the Qiang women and girls returned to the cottage one after another. The men in the household had already prepared wine, cooked bacon, and potato cakes for them. After these girls and women had enough to eat and drink, they started dancing together... For three days in a row, Goddess Sharon stepped on the clouds and went door to door to teach everyone to sing and dance. She stayed passionate and patient until she taught every Qiang woman to church. Since then, every year at the beginning of May, the Qiang people have gathered around Haizi and waited for “Goddess Sharon” to teach them.
Year after year, the Qiang people gathered by the sea like a mirror, on a lawn like a yin, in front of a tower with white stones. They danced and sang, taught and learned from each other, and performed as an ethnic minority that can sing and dance well.
