Appreciation of works
The author describes two countries in this story: one is the country of Raksha, and the other is the country of dragons. The story also depicts a character, Ma Ji, who crossed the ocean and traveled to two countries before finally returning to his native land.
Raksha Kingdom is an old transliteration of (Russian) Russia during the Ming and Qing Dynasties, but this is a fictional country. In this country, America and ugliness are turned upside down; the uglier they are, the more they hold senior official positions and are in charge of the administration of the DPRK; the more beautiful they are, the more they are viewed as monsters. “The focus is not on the article, but on the shape.” Therefore, the more you are not afraid to demonize your face, the more you can be reused, and the more you can gain fame from senior officials. There is no room for beauty in this country. The walls are black, the dancers look like Yasha, and the music is “resplendent.” Ma Ji painted his face with coal, then people thought he was beautiful; when he sings popular music, “Wang Dayue.” This is the author's revelation and irony of the social reality of the time, showing the anger and dissatisfaction in the author's heart.
In the land of dragons, everything is the opposite. Everything here is beautiful. The environment here is beautiful, bright and clear; the people here are beautiful, the palace is beautiful, the flowers, the trees, the birds are beautiful, and more importantly, the politics here are clear. The wise men are reused here; the values are literary talent, Jun Renchen loyalty, and the husband's wife's sincerity. This is undoubtedly the author's ideal sustenance. The author was depressed and unambitious throughout his life, and failed in his attempts, so he lamented everything in “Tales of Stranger History,” saying that this kind of ideal can only be “heard in the city of mirages.”
Ma Ji, the character throughout the story, was also very successful in portraying. When portraying this character, the author not only made full use of the storyline and the character's story environment, but also used discussion.
When introducing the characters at the beginning of the story, three aspects were highlighted: one was “Mei Fengzi,” the other was “happy to sing and dance,” and the other was literate. These three points all laid the groundwork for the development of later episodes. At this time, the author used Father Ma's words to insert a comment: “A few volumes of books, no cooking in the cold, no clothing in the cold.” This enabled Ma Ji to develop in the direction of seeking wealth rather than fame. When he arrived in Raksha Kingdom, he was confronted with an environment where “the focus was not on the article, but on the shape.” At first, he was surprised and surprised, but gradually the act of “using a coal-coated surface to fly” appeared on him. This is no accident; he is already being influenced by ugliness; this is a kind of denial of himself. Therefore, when Zhilang advised him to demonize his face and achieve the goal of being reused and “good fortune,” even though he verbally disagreed, he actually did it this way. Well, the comment quoted earlier is actually the ideological foundation of Ma Ji's actions at this time. As a result, he went on to “have the effect of being messy and making a popular voice”, and he also used ugliness as beautiful. His “Mei Feng Zi,” which was introduced earlier, was denied, but his “Happy Song and Dance” came in handy here. Fortunately, he was criticized and isolated, so he didn't slide deeper. Fortunately, he ran into the chance to go to Dragon Palace, which enabled him to deny himself once again. At Dragon Palace, his father said, “Don't cook if you're hungry, don't wear clothes when you're cold,” get attention, making his name “noisy all over the world” and making his spirit excited. In this environment where everything was beautiful, Ma Ji's mind also seemed to have been purified. Using the voice of Dragon Girl, the author made another comment, expressing his admiration for life and explaining the meaning of true love from a higher perspective. This discussion prompted the character Ma Ji to develop in a fuller direction. Ma Ji cherishes his love with Dragon Girl very much. He takes good care of his children and never forgets and is deeply attached to Dragon Girl.
However, the author did not say what the future holds for Ma Ji. Ma Ji has returned to reality. What is reality? An outsider said, “When you meet someone, the world feels like a ghost. The habit of being addicted to scabs is the same all over the world”. Although the mirage is visible, the sea market is hard to find, so all of these authors are left to the readers to think for themselves.
Author profile
Pu Songling (1640-1715), an outstanding writer from the Qing Dynasty, the character Liuxian, the word Sword Sword, also known as Liu Quanju, is a native of Zichuan, Shandong (present-day Zibo City, Shandong).
He came from a family of fallen landowners. His father, Pu Huan, was originally a book reader. He abandoned Confucian business because he had no ambition in his studies, and has accumulated a considerable amount of money. By the time Pu Songling became an adult, his family situation had already declined, and his life was very poor. Pu Songling has been passionate about fame throughout his life, and is fascinated by science, but apart from taking the test when he was nineteen, where he was ranked first in the three counties, provinces, and provinces in a row to make up for doctoral students, he has suffered many setbacks since then, and has always been depressed and unmotivated. While teaching, he took the exam for 40 years. He did not pay tribute until the age of 71. He made up for one year, and died four years later.
The ups and downs of his life gave Pu Songling a certain understanding of the darkness of politics at the time and the shortcomings of his academic career. Living in poverty gave him a certain understanding and experience of the lives and thoughts of the vast majority of working people. As a result, he has written quite a few books based on his own personal experience. Today, in addition to “The Strange Tales of Liaozhai,” there are also “Collection of Liaozhai Essays” and “Collection of Poems.”
