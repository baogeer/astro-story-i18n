Li Cunbao's “Oriental Gods” (published in “October”, No. 4, 2002) is called documentary literature by the author himself (see “Selected Novels”, No. 6, 2002, the author's creative discussion attached to this work). After reading this “documentary literature,” I feel that it doesn't live up to its name. If I were to make a joke, I'd rather call it “Ji Huzheng Theory”: most of the events recorded in this work are not historical facts, but fictional; the form seems to focus on historical investigation; in fact, it is mainly based on novels, folklore, and stories; the work is not very literary; it is mostly based on “Ji Xu.”
one
To talk about Guan Yu and the Three Kingdoms, Chen Shou's “Three Kingdoms” should be the most important historical material. However, Li Cunbao's “God of the East” (hereinafter referred to as Li Wen) says that Chen Shou is a “staunch supporter of the Cao faction,” because his father was punished by Zhu Ge Liang and incorporated personal grudges when writing “The Three Kingdoms.” Specifically, “The Biography of Guan Yu,” he “cherishes ink like gold.”
Chen Shouxiu's “The Chronicles of the Three Kingdoms” does use Cao Wei as the orthodoxy, but because he supports Cao Wei, but because he is a son of Western Jin; Western Jin inherited Cao Wei, so he wrote history books, and he must use Cao Wei as the orthodoxy. If I had to say who he supported, I could only say that his support for Sima's Xi Jin had nothing to do with Cao Wei.
Judging from relevant historical facts, Chen Shou had to formally adopt Cao Wei as orthodoxy; as an old minister of Shu Han, he favors Shu Han ideologically and emotionally. In this sense, it can be said that he is a “staunch supporter of the Liu faction.” He named his book “The Chronicles of the Three Kingdoms”, which meant that Wei, Shu, and Wu were equal. In terms of the trade-off of materials, he did not record any statements about Cao Pi calling the emperor more than 100 ministers to persuade and praise his deeds. He wrote a special book on Liu Bei calling the king of Han China and the ministers during his time as emperor, a special book about his true tendencies. 〔1〕
The claim that Chen Shouxiu mixed personal grudges during his history because his father was punished by Zhu Ge Liang is also unfounded. Since Northern Wei, many scholars have defended Chen Shou. The main historical fact is that Chen Shou composed 24 “Zhuge Liang Collection” during the Western Jin Dynasty, played in the imperial court, and made a sheet, in which he gave a fair evaluation of Zhu Geliang. The Qing dynasty scholar Wang Mingsheng synthesized the statements of Zhu Yizun and Hang Shijun and added, pointing out, “(Chen) wrote the “(Zhuge) Highlights Collection” sheet after entering Jin, and even recommended, that this biography is accompanied by a catalogue and book. There is no example for the founder. Zun (Zhuge) is brilliant. If the reviews repeatedly claim that it is a punishment, then the father must not be suspected of being guilty.” Two other scholars from the Qing dynasty, Qian Daxin and Zhao Yi, also pointed out that Chen Shou placed the greatest emphasis on Shu Han figures and spared no effort in praising them. The so-called derogatory statement against Zhu Geliang due to personal misgivings is an ignorant theory. 〔2〕
Seen from this perspective, Chen Shou, as a historian, has good professional ethics; his “Three Kingdoms”, as recognized by successive historians, is the most valuable historical book of the Three Kingdoms, which can be called a “good history.” If we want to explore a real Guan Yu, we must first look for historical facts from “The Chronicles of the Three Kingdoms.” Li Wen, on the other hand, said that due to political attitudes and personal grudges, Chen Shou “cherished his ink like gold; his words were blurry and unclear” when writing “The Biography of Guan Yu.” This assessment is also unfounded. Li Wenna compared the length of “Emperor Wu (Cao Cao)” and “The Biography of Guan Yu,” one “spills over 10,000 words” and one less than a thousand words to prove his argument, but this comparison is meaningless.
Cao Cao is an orthodox founding emperor (so his legend is not called “The Legend of Emperor Wu” but “Emperor Wu Ji”, to show the difference between him and his vassals). According to the practice of historians, his biography is supposed to be the longest. However, although Liu Bei and Sun Quan were not orthodox, they were also emperors; Chen Shou also gave them a lot of space. As for the legends of courtiers from the Three Kingdoms, they are much smaller than those of the emperors, and most of them are written by several people. “The Biography of Guan Yu” immediately follows “The Biography of Zhuge Liang” in “Shu Shu”, and was first shared by the five Guan Zhang, Ma, Huang, and Zhao. It should be said that it is appropriate and is in line with Guan Yu's status in Shu. If you have to compare the word count, you should just compare “The Biography of Guan Yu” with Cao Wei's biography of a similar general. Here, you can't see Chen Shou's tendency to be weak or strong at all.
Li Wen said that Guan Yu “died in exile and left Zhuozhou,” and Chen Shou only wrote these five characters, and “kept secret” about the reason. Li Wen confirmed that the reason was “righteous murder.” He described one of the five versions of folklore, then treated this as an unmistakable fact: “It was Guan Yu who wielded a sword and killed the people that laid the first foundation for Wu Sheng Guan Yu's personality building. However, in “The Biography of Guan Yu,” this piece, which should have been indispensable, was' embezzled 'by Mr. Chen Shoulao.”
I don't know where this talk of corruption comes from? If Li Wen can find out the fact that Guan Yu committed murder in other historical books, it can also be said that Chen Shou was embezzled; however, it couldn't find even one; however, it only listed folklore; however, folklore cannot be regarded as a definitive historical fact; how can it be said that Chen Shou was corrupt, and how could it be said that he deliberately “kept a secret”?
In my opinion, Chen Shou really doesn't know why Guan Yu “died in exile and left Zhuozhou”; if he didn't know, he just didn't know; he couldn't make things up. As quoted by Li Wen, “The Biography of Guan Yu” describes in detail Guan Yu's image of a tough guy who “scrapes bones and treats drugs.” Little things like this are still being reported in detail. If Guan Yu does have the fact that he “murdered people in a righteous manner,” why was Chen Shou embezzled, and why did he need to “keep it secret”?
Seen from this perspective, the first cornerstone that Li Wenjia gave to Wu Sheng Guan Yu personality building has no historical basis; in fact, it doesn't exist.
Li Wen said Cao Cao was “greedy.” As can be seen from the above facts, Guan Yu is probably no less than Cao Cao in this regard; he is only less powerful than his opponent; he is simply helpless against his opponent. Seen from this perspective, after Guan Yu surrendered to Cao, if Cao Cao actually gave him ten beautiful women, I can't guarantee that Guan Yu would definitely not laugh at and enjoy it. “Romance of the Three Kingdoms” greatly glorified Guan Yu in this regard; it is not in line with the historical reality of Guan Yu.
two
Regarding the “Taoyuan Three Ties,” Li Wen acknowledged on the one hand that it was not in official history, and on the other hand, he also said that after research, this story is credible, that is, it is in line with historical facts. He gave two reasons, both of which, in my opinion, are untenable.
First, in “The Biography of Guan Yu,” Guan Yu said to Zhang Liao, “I know very well that Cao Gong treated me well, but I was blessed by General Liu and vowed to die together; I cannot betray him.” Based on this statement, Li Wen concluded, “This shows that Liu, Guan, and Zhang have held a marriage ceremony according to folk customs, and made vows such as 'I don't seek the same life, I hope to die together' in the face of the sky.”
If we think about this statement a little more carefully, we can see that it doesn't indicate anything Li Wen has determined: first, there's nothing about Zhang Fei here, because nothing is mentioned about him; second, it just shows that Guan Yu has been blessed by Liu Beihoun. He made a vow to repay by dying together, and it has nothing to do with the marriage.
Second, Li Wen believes that the evidence that can better confirm the settlement statement is Liu Beifa Wu. Li Wen said that Liu Bei, who always followed Zhuge Liang's words, ignored Zhu Geliang's dissuasion and persisted in attacking Wu. This was because the “Taoyuan Settlement” wanted revenge for Guan Yu. Zhuge Liang advised Liu Bei to abandon the fight against Wu. There is no record in “The Legend of the Pioneers” or “The Biography of Zhuge Liang.” I wonder what Li Wen's statement is based on? I'm afraid it's based on “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” but “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is not a history book, but a novel; it's not enough to be relied upon.
Li Wen also said that Liu Beifen already became the emperor and took revenge for a member of the general. If he had not formed a brotherly friendship with Guan Yu, it would be difficult for anyone to understand that Yu Guo and his family were in love with him and understand himself. This reason actually presupposes that Liu Bei personally sought revenge against Guan Yu. What is the basis for this assumption? Liu Beifan Wu mainly lost a strategic location in Jingzhou; he wanted to take it back. Whether Guan Yu is killed or not, I'm afraid this battle will have to be fought. Here is another evidence to refute Li Wen: According to “The Herald”, after Liu Bei was defeated and defended Baidicheng, Sun Quan took the initiative to seek peace, and Liu Bei agreed. If Liu Bei were to take revenge on Guan Yu and would rather abandon Jiang Shan and lose sight of his net worth and life, as Li Wen said, he would not agree to a settlement; he would inevitably fight with the other party to break the net. Liu Bei didn't do this. It can be seen that in the end, he is still a politician. The purpose of his personal campaign was for actual political gain, not to use his own intentions.
Li Wen said, “After hearing about the death of his brother, Zhang Da and Fan Jiang were killed by his subordinates. He was furious and had his liver broken, abusing alcohol day and night, and angrily lashed out at his men.” These plots are entirely based on “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” and are not from historical books. “The Biography of Zhang Fei” has only two words about this matter: “First, the leader defeated Wu, and Feidang led 10,000 soldiers, from Langzhong to Jiangzhou. Linfa, his subordinates killed Zhang Da and Fan Qiang, took the lead and went with Sun Quan.” Zhang Fei was murdered by a subordinate. This is the only fact; the rest was made up or imagined by the novelist, so it is impossible to take revenge for the “second brother.”
In short, there is no historical fact that the Taoyuan Three Knot, called “the most classic interpretation of human friendship” by Li Wen, actually exists.
Li Wen also spent a lot of time defending Guan Yu's surrender to Cao Cao. The original question of surrender is unjustifiable: when continuing to fight or resist makes no sense in winning or losing the war, surrender is not a bad thing, nor should it be morally condemned. Commanders, in particular, can avoid many needless sacrifices by making this decision. However, Li Wen doesn't seem to see the problem this way. He cites Chinese prisoners of war during the period of resistance against the US and aid Korea as an example. He believes that even if society can tolerate them to a certain extent, once they are taken prisoner rather than die in desperate combat, they will have a stain on their personality at the point of life and death, and they will never be able to return to their former innocence.
As a result, Li Wen tried his best to defend Guan Yu's surrender to Cao, saying that with his strong personality, he should have killed Sungren and sacrificed his life for justice. It was only because he wanted to protect Liu Bei's two wives that he surrendered to Cao instead of fighting. There is no historical basis for this statement. “The Legend of Guan Yu” records this incident with only six words, “Cao Gong captured the feather and returned,” and “The Legend of the Pioneer” only had two sentences: “In five years, Cao Gongdong conquered the pioneers, and the pioneers lost. Cao Gong took all of them, took the lord's wife first, and captured Guan Yu.” It didn't say at all that Guan Yu surrendered to protect Mrs. Liu Bei.
In the case of Guan Yu escaping from Cao Ying, “The Legend of the Herald” has only six words: “Guan Yu died and returned to her ancestors.” “The Biography of Guan Yu” has only one sentence: “Yu did his best, gave up his prayers, and went to Yuan Jun first.” There is no mention of him protecting Liu Bei's two wives from escaping at all (which is actually impossible). Since this is the case, the claim that the original surrender was to protect the two wives is unfounded.
Li Wen said, “Historical materials show that the conditions for Guan Yu surrender to Cao were also quite strict. Among them, the most critical one is: once you know Liu Bei's whereabouts, abandon Liu Bencao. “But he didn't list these historical materials. I'm afraid these historical materials don't exist. The historical records I have seen have only one meaning: Guan Yu was captured alive, “Cao Cao captured Guan Yu and returned.” How can Guan Yu talk about what conditions here? The only historical material mentioned by Li Wen is “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” while “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is a novel, not historical material.
Regarding Guan Yu's situation in Cao Ying, Li Wen acknowledged on the one hand that there is no historical basis for Cao Cao's various rewards described in “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms.” On the other hand, he also said that Cao Cao gave Guan Yu a red rabbit, horse, and beautiful woman, “probably not distorted.” Next, I took this as an existing fact and said, “Of the ten beautiful women given by the Cao clan, he has never been involved” to show that Guan Yu is not good-natured; he is a super “genius” whose “hero can pass the beauty level.”
Is Guan Yu really not good-natured? “The Chronicles of Huayang” volume 6, “Master Liu Xian,” says, “When Qin Yilu asked for help (Lu) in Zhang Yang. (Guan) Yu Qigong (referring to Cao Cao), wife without children, went down to the city, and is the wife of Chu Na-ilu.” “Notes on the Three Kingdoms” quoted “The Chronicles of Shu” as saying, “Cao Gong, Liu Beiwei, and Lu Bu are down. Guan Yu ordered Qin Yilu to seek help, beg to marry his wife, and confess. It was broken, and it started again and again. They doubt that it is different, and they greet them first; because they keep it for themselves, Yu is not at ease. This is no different from what “Wei Shi Chunqiu” said.” At least three historical books (“The Chronicles of Huayang,” “The Chronicles of Shu,” and “Wei Shi Chunqiu”) all talk about this situation, and they say the same thing. It seems that Guan Yu did ask for someone else's wife before the war.
These three sections talk about the time when Liu Bei belonged to Cao Cao and joined forces with Cao Cao to capture Lu Bu. Qin Yilu is a general under Lu Bu. Guan Yu hoped to get Qin Yilu's wife after defeating Xiapi. Cao Cao agreed at the beginning, but later regretted it. As can be seen from the above historical materials, Guan Yu met Qin Yilu's wife before making this request to Cao Cao, or at least knew about the other party's situation. The most basic situation is that Qin's wife is a stunning woman. Guan Yu made this request on the grounds that his wife did not have a son; this is clearly not sincere. How can he determine that Qin's wife will definitely have a son? Can't other women have sons? It's just a cover. He admired each other's beauty.
Since he made this request over and over again, it attracted Cao Cao's attention. It was speculated that this woman was “colorful” (very beautiful), so after the city was destroyed, someone first sent someone to take a look. The result was that she was left for her own use. This also shows that this woman is really outstanding; otherwise, Cao Cao wouldn't have been able to win love for her.

After doing all the “historical research” described above, Li Wen came to the conclusion: “Throughout Guan Yu's life, he has crossed most of the warning barriers of life, such as 'group and self, country and family, reason and desire, poverty and attainment, strength and life, life' and death'”, and is a “person of the greatest strength”, “the most loyal to the most righteous, the most benevolent and brave”. Isn't this too amazing? According to the exact historical facts we know, it is really hard for Guan Yu to bear such extravagant rhetoric.
As a politician and party coach, Guan Yu has quite a few flaws in character and quality. Judging from “The Biography of Guan Yu,” there are at least three of these: First, it is good to compete with my colleagues who comes first; there is a suspicion that they are fighting in the middle. Ma Chao surrendered to Shu Han, and Guan Yu wrote to Zhu Geliang in Jingzhou to compete with Ma Chao for the highest rank; fortunately, Zhu Geliang cleverly wrote back to him, strongly praising him as an “outstanding group,” fully satisfying his vanity, and was only able to resolve the conflict and avoid an internal fight.
Second, it is impossible to properly handle relationships with allies and force friends into enemies. Guan Yu fought against Cao Jun in Jingzhou, and Cao Cao sent someone to join forces with Sun Quan to attack Guan Yu. Sun Quan made no decision and first sent his son to propose marriage to Guan Yu's daughter in order to test Guan Yu's attitude. Guan Yu “insults the agent and does not allow marriage.” Sun Quan was furious, so he decided to seize Jingzhou and turn his face off with Liu Bei. It can be said that Shu and Wu had bad relations, and Jingzhou was taken. Guan Yu's attitude was an important and even decisive factor as the coach of the side.
Again, not being able to properly handle relationships with subordinates has led to internal changes. This is the key to losing Jingzhou. General Wu Guodong Lu Xun summed up Guan Yu's personality as “restraining his courage and devotion to others” (“Three Kingdoms: Wu Shu: The Biography of Lu Xun”), that is, he uses courage to be arrogant and bully others. Mi Fang and Fu Shiren, who are stationed in strategic locations, are usually looked down upon by Guan Yu. When Guan Yu went on the expedition, the two were threatened by Guan Yu to “still be punished (punish again when they return)”. They were often fearful and uneasy, and finally accepted to be abducted by Sun Quan. Sun Quan took Guan Yu's base without spending a single soldier.
From a personal point of view, Guan Yu can be said to be a tragic figure, and this kind of tragedy was also caused by some flaws in his personality. No matter how you look at it, Guan Yu can't talk about perfection, perfection, or “to” anything. The only person who can stand up to these extravagant remarks is Guan Yu after being deified.
The Guan Yu myth has been passed down for a long time and is becoming more and more prominent. The recognition, appreciation, and even deliberate creation of successive rulers is a major factor. Li Wen also acknowledged that Guan Yu's process from man to god was largely caused by the rulers, and the purpose was to consolidate his own regime. Let's take Li Wen's example: the first person who agreed that Guan Yu used Garan to walk up to the temple was Emperor Sui Yang Guang, who “murdered his father and brother, raped his mother and sister, invaded and restrained, and was extremely extravagant.” The one who named Guan Yu four times in a row was named “Zhong Hui Gong”, “Chong Ning Zhenjun”, “King Wu An”, and “King Wu An of Yiyong”. Song Huizong Zhao Ji, who claimed to have come to the world as “Emperor Taixiao, the original son of God,” was immoral and incompetent. He “spared no expense in people's wealth, boosted civil engineering, and forced the government to fight back.” In the end, he was taken by Jin Guo and died in another hometown. In order to maintain Guan Yu's sacred image, the first person to ban Guan opera was Zhu Yuanzhang, the ruthless Founding Emperor in the Ming Dynasty. However, his descendant, Zhu Yijun, of the Ming God sect, “indulged in silence and ignored the administration of the dynasty”, leading to “Lishu's pastoral care,” “to kill people's wrath,” and “Emperor Xietian,” “Xietian Gongguang, Emperor Zhongyi,” and “Emperor Zhongyi of the Three Realms.” In the Qing dynasty, Guan opera was banned to preserve the image of Guan Sheng, and he was also the most violent emperor Yongzheng. However, it was Emperor Guangxu who was helpless in the midst of external worries and internal suffering and whose dynasty was on the verge of extinction, who gave Guan Yu the title of “rise to the peak” and “show courage, protect the country, protect the people, and sincerely appease Yizan Xuande, Emperor Guansheng.”
What is very meaningful is that these emperors are either tyrants, incompetent rulers, or princes on the verge of losing their throne. I think this is probably no accident. Tyrants demand absolute loyalty and ignorant loyalty from their courtiers. They use Guan Yu as the personification of loyalty to demand absolute obedience from their courtiers. Shu Jun is clueless and incompetent in the administration of the DPRK, so he hopes that this loyal god will manage the country's major events for them. And for the king who is about to die and lose his throne, a patron saint like Guan Yu is the last straw to save their lives.
Emperors who have worshiped Guan Yu throughout the ages mainly took the word loyalty from him, and finally implemented it to loyalty. Because he spoke righteousness to a friend, according to the story of Taoyuan's Three Ties, this friend was also the master. Loyal in a relationship with a monarch, plus a layer of emotional affection for his brother and elder brother, I think it is impossible for a king to demand more from his subordinates than this.
How do you explain that the rebels who came out of nowhere also used Guan Yu as their protector? If the ruler mainly took Guan Yu's loyalty, and Yoshiya was also converted to loyalty, then the ruler who overcame the rebellion mainly took his righteousness, and his loyalty was also reduced to the righteousness of rebelling against his buddies. In fact, if you take a closer look, there is no contradiction between these two phenomena: if the rebellion succeeds and the elder brother takes the throne, that kindness of the buddy can also be transformed into a courtier's fierce loyalty to the Lord.
Second, Guan Yu was glorified and deified, and the novel “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” played a big role in it. What Luo Guanzhong promoted and emphasized in his novel is still the word “loyalty,” but through artistic images, they are more acceptable to ordinary people than the emperor's endorsement and praise. In particular, “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” is easy to mislead people: its depiction is real.
