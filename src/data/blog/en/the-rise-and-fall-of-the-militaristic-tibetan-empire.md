 After Lang Ngil Lung proposed unifying the middle and lower reaches of the Brahmaputra River, it established a ruling center in the Lhasa River basin to manage the newly conquered region, so Tibetan historical sources say that his son, Songzan Ganbu, was born in the upper reaches of the Lhasa River, Aaron Zazhugong Palace (Basang Wangdui, “My Question on the 'Chronology of Songzan Ganbu'”, “Tibet Research”, No. 1, 1985). According to Song Zanganbu succeeded to the throne at the age of 13, Lang Ngil Lun Zan remained in power for at least ten years after unifying the small states. During this period, his main activities were apparently to suppress the remaining rebellious forces in various regions, continue to wage unification wars against small states such as the Soviet Union and Xiangxiong. At the same time, he also coordinated relationships between the old and new aristocrats under his belt, resolved the conflicts between them due to the distribution of power, strengthened the position of the Zampu royal family, and consolidated the rule of the newly established dynasty. The “Dunhuang Ben and Tuban Historical Documents” records that during this period he sent troops to suppress the rebellion in the Dabou region. In the end, Lange Lunzan was poisoned and killed by the rebels. It all shows that a fierce struggle was still going on to consolidate the new administration at the time.
The date of Song Zanganbu's birth has yet to be determined. Most scholars believe that he was born in 617 AD (probably a little earlier) and succeeded Zampu in 629. “Dunhuang Ben and Tuban Historical Documents” says that at the beginning of his succession, “the common people who belonged to his father and king had grievances, and the people who belonged to his mother publicly rebelled”. Under the circumstances of internal instability, newly attached small states such as Xiangxiong, Su Yu, Dab, and Gongbu also publicly rebelled. Although Song Zan Kanbu has been a self-government since he was 13 years old, he immediately took firm measures to suppress all kinds of rebels, “and absolutely exterminate all those who took the lead in narcotics, etc., and put an end to their succession. Afterwards, the defected people returned to the administration.” Songzan Ganbu took a series of far-reaching and major measures to consolidate the newly established Tuban dynasty, which played a huge role in the history of Tibet, so he was recognized by his descendants as the founder of the Tuban dynasty.
Song Zan Kanbu's performance can be summarized into three main aspects. First, it created the Tibetan language and enacted laws that have been passed down to this day. According to the degree of social development of the Tibetan ancestors, there was already a need to use scripts during the period when there were many small states; it is likely that some regions used certain types of scripts that are not very complete. Shortly after taking over the throne, Song Zanganbu sent Tun Mi Sambuza and other noble children to India to study philology. After returning, he created Tibetan literature using pinyin, and immediately used it in the administration of the Tuban dynasty. Songzan Ganbu promoted the Tibetan language with great enthusiasm, bringing the ancient civilization of Tibet into a brand-new stage. Songzanganbu enacted a series of laws to guarantee the dominance of the royal family and aristocrats, regulate the codes of conduct of all social classes, and suppress the resistance of the people. Under Zampu, there was one Daehan (which was later increased to many) and one deputy leader to assist Zanpu in charge of military affairs. He also had a member of internal affairs and a deputy minister of internal affairs to manage internal affairs, and a Minister of Justice to oversee the inspection and administration of justice. He also stipulated that officials at various levels were divided into 12 classes, using the rank and powers of officials at various levels, and strictly stipulated the status of individual social classes. People with different statuses were harmed. The penalties for offenders ranged from 11,000 to 102. The following offenders were also accompanied by execution, loss of family property, etc., and the following offenses were also accompanied by execution, loss of family property, etc., and punish them with fines ranging from 100 to 8 times. As class division was guaranteed by law, the remaining social role of clan and tribal kinship in Tuban society was suppressed and weakened the authority of the royal family was established in society. Second, an administrative system combining military administration and the military and the civilian population has been established. Songzanganbu divides the middle and lower reaches of the Brahmaputra River into four parts (ru, also translated as wing), namely Wu Ru (dbu-ru, central wing) centered around Lhasa, Ye Ru (gya-ru, left wing) centered on East China, Ye Ru (gyas-ru, right wing) centered on Nanmu Forest, and Ru-lag (branch wing) centered on Lazi (ru-lag, branch wing). According to the records of “The Five Heritages”, each is divided into the upper and lower parts, each with a general and a deputy general, and each has four thousand households, each with four thousand houses, each with another small thousand households. “The Wedding Feast of the Wise” says that each one is also a Forbidden Thousand Houses directly subordinate to Zampu, for a total of four has 40 thousand households. After conquering Xiangxiong and the Soviet Union, a system such as the Thousand Houses was implemented to Xiangxiong and the Soviet Union. There were ten thousand homes in Xiangxiong, and Eleven Thousand Houses were set up in the Soviet Union. In total, there were 61 thousand households in the whole of Tuban. A thousand households have a thousand heads of households, appointed by Zanpu. Ministers with special merit can be crowned hereditary heads of thousands of households. The division of Siru and Qianhu in Tuban was divided by region rather than by clan, so it was the administrative structure of a dynasty that transcended the kinship of the clan and tribe. Each of the thousand households is also divided into several tribes. Tribes set up official positions such as tribal chiefs, pioneer officers, farmland officers, finance and taxation officers, and clerical officers to manage the tribe's production, military affairs, taxation, etc. Judging from documents unearthed in Dunhuang and Tibetan wood abstracts from the Tuban dynasty discovered in Xinjiang, it is already common for tribes to have multiple surnames, and even some tribes were mixed with people from foreign nationalities. This shows that tribes during the Tuban Dynasty were also transformed into first-level administrative institutions. The third is to continue the cause of unifying the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. After conquering Xiangxiong and Subi, the emerging Tuban dynasty continued to expand northeast. At the time, around Qinghai Lake was the Tuguhun Kingdom. The Tuguhun royal family was a member of the Xianbei clan that migrated from the northeast to the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. Most of their subjects were the local Xiqiang tribe. Tuban unified the Party and Xishan Eight Kingdoms and other Western Qiang tribes, and at the same time opened up access to the economically and culturally developed Yellow River basin in an attempt to annex Tuguhun. Songzan Ganbu personally attacked Tuguhun and occupied most parts of Tuguhun. Tuguhun sought help from the Tang dynasty. There was a military conflict with the recently established Tang dynasty. When Song Zan Ganbu took troops to besiege Songzhou, and Tang Fan had both wins and losses, they instead sought relatives from the Tang dynasty to establish a friendly relationship with the Tang dynasty.
As a British leader of the Tuban dynasty, Songzan Ganbu was a brilliant man. While unifying most regions of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, he made efforts to expand economic and cultural exchanges with neighboring countries. He first sent Minister Gardong Zanyu Song (“The Book of Tang” is recorded as Lu Dongzan) to Nepal to marry the daughter of the Nepalese king's king, Vigdi (recorded in Tibetan historical records as Princess Chizun) as his wife. In 640, he also sent Gar Dong Zanyu Song and others to Chang'an (present-day Xi'an) to seek family from the Tang dynasty. After many efforts, Tang Taizong agreed to marry Princess Wencheng, a matriarch of the family, to marry Song Zanganbu, and sent Li Daozong of the Ministry of Rite to guard the festival. Princess Wencheng entered Tibet in 641. “The Book of Tang” records that Songzan Ganbu personally welcomed Princess Wencheng in Heyuan, showing the importance the Tuban dynasty attached to Princess Wencheng's entry into Tibet. Princess Chizun and Princess Wencheng's entry into Tibet enhanced political, economic, and cultural exchanges between Tuban and the South Asia and Central Plains regions. Some production techniques and techniques were introduced to Tufan, and books on medicine and accounts were introduced, which promoted the development of Tuban's economy and culture. Princess Chizun and Princess Wencheng are both followers of Buddhism. Legend has it that Princess Wencheng also brought Buddha statues and Buddhist monks. The two princesses built the Jokhang Temple and the Xiaozhang Temple in Lhasa to enshrine the statue of Shakyamuni brought by Princess Wencheng from Chang'an, making it the earliest Buddhist temple built in Tibet. Songzanganbu also built a large-scale palace in Red Mountain (Potala Mountain) in Lhasa, showing the advanced construction techniques developed during the Tuban dynasty. Princess Wencheng lived in Tuban for almost 40 years and died in 680. There have been many stories praising her in the Tibetan people for over a thousand years.
During the reign of Songzan Kanbu, a peaceful and friendly relationship was established between the two sides, and the number of envoy exchanges between the two sides increased year by year. After conquering Korea in 645, Tang Taizong returned to Chang'an. Songzanganbu sent Gar Dong Zanyu Song to Chang'an to celebrate his triumph, and showed a seven-foot tall goose made of gold as a gift. In 648, Wang Xuanze, an envoy of the Tang dynasty, was ordered to go to Tianzhu (India). The Tang dynasty envoy was robbed. Wang Xuanze fled to Tufan. Songzan Ganbu sent troops to help, and was drafted to Nepal. Wang Xuanze led the war to Tianzhu, which calmed the war and enabled the Tang dynasty delegation to return smoothly and take prisoners in Chang'an. Later, in the 3rd year of Tang Gaozong's Xianqing trip to Tianzhu (658), Wang Xuanze still preserves a cliff carving entitled “The Tang Tianzhu Envoy” (“Jilong County Cultural Heritage History”, edited by Solang Wangdui, Tibet People's Press, 1993), which is a historical witness to Tang Fan's friendly cooperation. Tang Taizong died in the summer of 649. Tang Gaozong took the throne, sent an envoy to die, and declared Song Zanganbu the king of Xihai County, the chief lieutenant of Lim Ma. Songzan Ganbu sent a special envoy to Chang'an to hang the festival, and wrote a letter to express his congratulations and support for Tang Gaozong's inauguration. Tang Gaozong also promoted Song Zanganbu as “King [Zongbei],” and also engraved the stone statue of Song Zanganpu before standing in the Zhaoling Mausoleum of Tang Taizong to show the importance he attached to Zanpu in Tuban.
After the death of Songzan Ganbu in 650, since his son Gong Song Gongzan died first, his grandson Meng Lun Mangzan succeeded to the throne of Zampu, and Minister Gal Dong Zanyu Song assisted in the administration of the dynasty. The Gar Dong Zanyu Song inherited the policy of Song Zan Kanbu, improved laws, checked household registration, determined tax burdens, and stabilized the interior of Tuban. He also used Tuguhun's internal feud with the royal family to attack Tuguhun many times, and even clashed with the Tang dynasty, which supported Tuguhun. In 667, Gar Dong Zanyu Song died in the Tuguhun region, and several of his sons oversaw the administration of North Korea inside and outside of Tuban. In 670, in order to support Tuguhun, the Tang dynasty dispatched 100,000 troops to escort King Tuguhun back to Qinghai, and the Gar Qin Mausoleum mobilized 200,000 Tufan troops to face the battle. In the Battle of Dafeichuan on the southern side of Qinghai, the Tang army was defeated. Since then, Tuban has firmly controlled the Qinghai Lake region and further competed with the Tang dynasty for the four towns of Longyou, Hexi Corridor, and Anxi in an attempt to gain control of the Silk Road and obtain greater economic benefits. He died in 676, and his son Song Mangbojie was established as Zampu, and the Garqin Mausoleum continued to govern. Due to the Gar family's long-term dictatorship, conflicts with other aristocrats, and also threatened the consolidation of the Zampu dynasty. After Dusong Mangbo grew up, they cracked down on the Gar family from 695 to 698. Qinling died in the Dzongkha region of Qinghai. His younger brother Zanpo and his son Mangbu led the army to surrender to the Tang dynasty, and Zanba was crowned King of Guide County.
After Dusong Mangbojie removed the Gar family, the power of the Tuban dynasty was strengthened and consolidated, but soon after, Dusong Mangbojie died during an expedition to Yunnan Nanzhao in 704. His son, Zuzan Chide, succeeded him. He was only one year old, and his grandmother ruled without the Lu clan. During this period, the Tang dynasty was in the old age of the female emperor Wu Zetian. Both Tang Fan were trapped by years of war, hoping to restore peace and friendship. When they were both married to Song and Mangbo, the Chimalai sought a family for him in the Tang dynasty, and later they also sought a family in the Tang dynasty for the ancestors of Chide. Wu Zetian died in 705, and Tang Zhongzong succeeded him. In 707, Tang Zhongzong, with his nephew Li Shouli's daughter, became his adopted daughter, became Princess Jincheng, and married Chizu Dezan. In 710, an envoy of the Tang and Zhong sect escorted Princess Jincheng into Tibet along with the welcome messengers from Tuban. The Tang Zhong family led a hundred officials on a grand parade to Shiping County in Shaanxi. Tang dynasty monarchs also shared verses and poems with the family messengers from Tuban praising the peace between Tuban. The Tang dynasty also gave Princess Jincheng the land of Jiuqu in the Yellow River as Tang Muyi at the request of Tuban. Princess Jincheng also brought a large number of craftsmen, acrobats, Buddhist monks, etc., who played an important role in the development of Tuban economy and culture. After Princess Jincheng entered Tibet, the two sides maintained a peaceful and friendly situation for decades. In 731, Chizu Dezan and Princess Jincheng sent an envoy to the Tang dynasty to request “Mao Shi,” “Li Ji,” “Zuo Chuan,” etc., and requested a border between each other. In his statement, Chi Zu Dezan said, “My nephew is the cousin of the previous emperor, and also the princess of Jincheng. So they are all in the same family. People all over the world are at ease.” In 733, Tang Fan and the two sides built a monument and demarcated the border in Chiling (Riyue Mountain, Qinghai). The two sides and officials participated in the exchange market, and announced to all regions that the two sides made peace and prohibited each other's looting.
Soon, since the ruling classes on both sides wanted to expand their power, and the border generals also wanted to gain fame from the war, the war between Tang Fan broke out again. The northern side of Tuban joined forces with Tuqishi, and the southeast and Nanzhao of Yunnan. Luo Feng of the Nanzhao Wangge came to rely on Tufan in 751. Luo Feng of the feudal court of Tufan was called “Zanpu Zhong” (meaning Zampudi). The joint army posed a serious threat to the Tang dynasty. The scale of the Tangfan War expanded, and the two sides were unable to keep up with the war in the border area, and a group of generals who supported their own forces appeared. In about 754, Maedong Zeb and Lang Mai Se, who were in charge of the rebellion, led an uprising and murdered Zuzan of Zampchid. It wasn't until 755 that the rebellion was settled by the Tuban dynasty. The rebellion led by the execution of a traitor robbed his family and banished his relatives. He was succeeded by Akamatsu Tokuzan, who was only 13 years old. Just this year, the balance of power between Tang and Fan changed drastically. Due to the “Anshi Rebellion” in the Tang dynasty, Tang Xuanzong fled from Chang'an to Sichuan. The Tang dynasty drew a large number of troops against Tuban to calm the unrest, leaving the defenses in the west empty. Tuban occupied large area of the Tang dynasty in the four towns of Longyou, Hexi, and Anxi, and in October 763, took advantage of the Tang dynasty's discord and concentrated about 200,000 troops to advance eastward and go straight to Chang'an. The Tuban army occupied Chang'an for only 15 days, but they still established King Li Chenghong, the nephew of Princess Jincheng (actually the same age as Princess Jincheng), as the emperor, and installed 100 officers. It was only because they were not accustomed to the intense autumn heat in Chang'an, the disease spread in the Tuban army. Coupled with the approach of King Qin of the Tang dynasty, the Tuban army automatically withdrew from Chang'an. During the reign of Akamatsu Dezan, the territory of the Tuban dynasty expanded greatly. The eastern side and Tang dynasty were generally bordered by Longshan Mountain. They also entered Baoji several times, threatening Chang'an; in the north, Helan Mountain in Ningxia was connected to Huijiao. Nanzhao was a vassal country on the south side, and all four towns of Anxi on the west side competed against big food in Central Asia. According to Tibetan historical records, at this time, Tuban also reached the northern bank of the Heng River south of the Himalayas. It is not an exaggeration to say that the Tufan dynasty was tens of thousands of miles at the time.
The military expansion of the Tuban dynasty certainly brought great suffering and damage to people of all ethnic groups, but on the other hand, the war also greatly promoted economic and cultural exchanges between ethnic groups. Items such as satin and tea from Han are gradually becoming necessities of life for Tibetans from all walks of life, while livestock products and medicinal herbs from Tuban are also flowing in large numbers into the Han region. Tuban clothing, crafts, polo sports, etc. also had an important influence on the Tang dynasty.
Buddhism occupied an important position in the late history of Tuban. Buddha statues began to be introduced during the Songzanganbu period, and since then, there has been a sharp struggle over whether to allow believing in Buddhism. After Akamatsu Dezan succeeded to the throne, he removed the ministers who opposed Buddhism, invited Jigu (Shivatso) and Master Lotus from India, and developed Buddhism on a large scale. Lotus was born from the Buddhist theory of the gods of the world, incorporating mountain gods, lake gods, dragon gods, etc. originally believed in Tibet into the Mahayana Buddhist spiritual system, and created a complete set of sacrificial rituals to facilitate the promotion of Buddhism in the Tuban society. Sanye Temple was built in 767-779, and the “Seven Testers” became the first formal Buddhist monastery in Tibet. At the same time, it established the foundation for the development of Buddhism in Tuban through the children of Tibetan aristocrats who became monks. Since then, Buddhism has developed rapidly in Tuban, and Akamatsu Dezan and his sons Muni Zanpu and Chide Songzan have spared no effort to support Buddhism. The expenses of Buddhist monasteries and monks are funded by the royal family using taxes from the subjects, while monasteries and monks are also strictly controlled by the royal family. In order to administer Buddhism, religious judges have been specially appointed. Later, Chide Song Zan appointed his childhood Buddhist teacher, Niang Dingaizeng, as Bo Shanbu, and confiscated his land and subordinates, and placed him in charge of military and government affairs. He was called the “monk minister.” Following the succession of the throne in 815, the son of Chidoksongzan, Chizu Dezan (Rebaghan), he further promoted the policy of respecting Buddhism, built monasteries, increased the number of monks, and translated Buddhist classics on a large scale. During the reign of their father and son, Buddhism in Tuban developed rapidly. There are many exquisite volumes of Tibetan Buddhist sutras written in Dunhuang, second only to those written in Chinese, which is the cultural heritage of Tuban Buddhism. It is stipulated that seven families support a monk, making Buddhist monks a privileged class in society, and at the same time, laws and regulations are used to harshly suppress those who show slight dissatisfaction with Buddhist monks. As a result, conflicts between monks and customs and ruling groups have intensified. Finally, in 841, Chizu Dezan was assassinated by an anti-Buddhist minister, and his brother Rondama was elevated to the throne. In 843, Rhondama ordered the prohibition of Buddhism, killed the monk leader, forced the monks to return to secular life, closed down monasteries, and destroyed Buddha statues and sutras. Historically, it is known as “Rhondama destroyed the Buddha.” However, the strong ban on Buddhism did not ease social conflicts; on the contrary, it provoked resistance from the monks. In 846, Rondama was shot dead by the monk Lalombeji Yundan.
After Rondama's death, his two sons, Yun Dan and Weisong, each supported by a faction of ministers. A civil war broke out between the two sides to compete for the throne, and the generals with military power immediately broke out in a scuffle, which lasted more than 20 years. The chaos of the ruling group also caused an uprising of the common people in 869 (or the Great Revolt of Slaves and Civilians). In 877, the rebels excavated the tomb of King Zanpu, exterminated the royal family and nobility, and the Tuban dynasty completely collapsed.
