One of the Ming songs is “Selling Dogs and Hanging Sheep”. The author is Xue Lundao. It's about hanging sheep's heads and selling dog meat, which is very interesting.

There was nothing more peculiar than the phenomenon of the Ming dynasty royal family deceiving marriage, which actually tricked the emperor.
Ming dynasty princesses only married ordinary people
The royal family in the Ming Dynasty had rules. Most princesses' marriages chose handsome and kind men, and children of Minister Wenwu were not allowed to marry princesses. Why is this? Turns out, the emperors of the Ming dynasty were very shy about foreign government officials, and feared that ministers and military generals would use the marriage of children to interfere in North Korea's administration, and there was even a crisis of seizing power. In order to completely cut off the risk of foreign relatives interfering with the government, the Ming royal family issued a death order prohibiting marriage between the royal family and military generals. Therefore, in the Ariake generation, the royal princess's mother-in-law's family was often humble. They didn't have much political status, so they didn't exert influence through marriage. From Emperor Hong Wu to Emperor Chongzhen, although the danger of lieutenants acting in government was not eliminated, it was still very intense in certain eras, yet it was effectively avoided by relatives.
The vast majority of the Ming dynasty's grandparents were folk men and did not have a high position in national politics. Nowadays, we often say that a girl married a man whose conditions were not as good as our own, called a “lower marriage.” However, throughout Chinese history, the most qualified people to be called “undermarried” were the princesses of the imperial family in the Ming Dynasty. Since he is in the emperor's house, my husband is almost all ordinary. Princesses in the royal family of the Ming Dynasty were hierarchical. The emperor's aunt was called “Grand Princess,” the emperor's sister was called “Princess,” the emperor's daughter was called “Princess,” and the Prince's daughter was called “County Lord”; the prince's granddaughter was called “County Lord.” These grand princesses, princesses, county chiefs, county chiefs, and county chiefs are all royal golden branches and pearls in their hands. Naturally, the royal family attached great importance to their marriage. All of them were gorgeous marriages!
As a result, folk men competed to be grandmas, and it became a unique landscape in the Ming Dynasty. Due to the separation of the government and the public, the royal family is superior. How to understand Master Ma and how to choose Mr. Ma has become a major problem. At that time, there were no scientific methods of identification; they could only rely on word of mouth and recommendations from others. The difference between good and bad, and good, of Grandpa Ma is just a matter of rhetoric. There was nothing closer to the emperor than a lieutenant, so most of the people who matched the princess were eunuchs. When he meets a lieutenant with good morals, he will naturally do his best to choose a happy horse for the princess; if he meets a villain who only wants profit, he will naturally use power for personal gain, which leaves an outlet for people who cheat on marriage. As a result, there are many folk men who hang up sheep, sell dog meat, defraud the royal family and seek wealth by bribing lieutenants and close ministers. This kind of thing simply happened all the time during the Ming dynasty; it's a historical wonder.
Marriage fraud tragedy: married princess's virgin dies
In the 8th year of Ming Hongzhi (1495), there was a big fan called Yuan Xiang. He bribed Inner Court eunuch Li Dang with the intention of asking Li Guang to help him marry a princess, so that he could climb the dragon and attach a phoenix. The royal family of the Ming dynasty had rules. Most of the princesses chose horses, and eunuchs and female officials were responsible for most of them. Therefore, Li Guang took advantage of every opportunity to strongly recommend Yuan Xiang to Emperor Hongzhi, touted him very much, and said all the beautiful things. Emperor Hongzhi trusted Li Guang and agreed to recruit Yuan Xiang as his son-in-law. Yuan Xiang got his wish and became a prospective horse. His family was also full of joy and joy. Emperor Koji also personally summoned Yuan Xiang. Feeling OK, he agreed with Yuan Xiang's parents on a date for the big wedding.
Unexpectedly, at this point, someone suddenly denounced Li Guang and Yuan Xiang's marriage fraud scheme. Emperor Hongzhi immediately sent someone to investigate. It was discovered that Li Guang had accepted many bribes from Yuan Xiang, yet the public did not evaluate Yuan Xiang highly; far from being as high, big, and complete as Li Guang said. At this point, Emperor Hongzhi suddenly realized that Long Yan was furious. However, Yuan Xiang and Princess Deqing have already set a marriage date; in terms of customs, it is already an iron plate and cannot be changed. But how could Emperor Koji tolerate marrying his daughter to a liar? As a result, he bucked the trend, overturned the marriage period, abolished Yuan Xiang's name as a horse, and chose a new horse for his daughter. Yuan Xiang deceived his marriage and failed, leaving Emperor Hong Zhi in a cold sweat. Emperor Hongzhi was alarmed. Fortunately, Princess Deqing was unscathed and did not suffer from the scammer's loss. At this point, he was much luckier than the later Emperor Jiajing.
In the 6th year of Jiajing (1527), the royal family selected a horse for Princess Yongchun. Through strong recommendations from eunuchs and female officials, the royal family chose a man named Chen Zhao and set a marriage date with the Chen family. Princess Yongchun will soon “get married.” Who knows, Tiangong is not beautiful; there are “little people” in the world. Some people complained to the royal family that Chen Zhao's family had severe illnesses for generations, and that her birth mother remarried and became someone else's concubine. Marrying the imposing Princess Daming to the son of a concubine, that is, the princess's future mother-in-law is a “double mother”, really humiliates the dignity of the royal family!
When Emperor Jiajing received the setbacks of the report, all of a sudden, it all went awry. After investigation, Chen Zhao's biological mother was actually a concubine. As a result, Emperor Jiajing said nothing and immediately repented to his family. However, the princess's marriage date has already been announced all over the country, and there must always be a reason to postpone the marriage period. If the truth were to be told, that the emperor's family had been deceived, the horse turned out to be the son of a “two-breasted girl,” wouldn't this have made the world laugh off his teeth? In order to save face for the royal family, Emperor Jiajing quickly ordered people to be selected in the national election and another round of recruitment. After many choices, I finally picked a man named Xie Zhao. This time, Emperor Jiajing didn't dare to take people's words lightly any longer; he had to personally examine the candidates. As a result, he met Xie Zhao. Who knows, seeing this with his own eyes, the Jiajing Emperor was furious. Turns out Xie Zhao was naturally bald! Obviously, this Xie Zhao is an ugly bastard! But I don't know how many methods he used to get through the mess.
It wasn't a big mistake to look ugly, and the marriage didn't wait for anyone. Emperor Jiajing had to swallow the bitter fruit and allow his daughter, Princess Yongchun, to marry Xie Zhao. On the day of the big wedding, the whole country was shocked. Princess Yongchun, who was like a flower, was actually married to a bald ugly monster. People of Good Deeds actually made up the folk song “10 Funny Things,” listing ten funny things at the time. The tenth one was that the royal family invited Xie Zhao to act as the horse, “10 Funny, Change the Horse to the World Report.”
When it comes to the princess's marriage, Emperor Jiajing's elder father-in-law is very worried. However, Xie Zhaochou returned to the ugly; it is said that he had a pretty good character; this could give Emperor Jiajing some comfort. Speaking of Emperor Wanli, he was much more troubled than Emperor Jiajing.
In the 10th year of Wanli (1582), Princess Yongning, the younger sister of Emperor Wanli, chose a horse. As soon as the wind came out, the whole country shook, and many men in the public were acting recklessly. There is a wealthy man surnamed Liang in Beijing. He decided that this was an opportunity to climb the dragon and support the phoenix, so he did his best to bribe Grand Eunuch Feng Bao and let Liang Bangrui, the son of the Leung family, participate in the election. Being rich can drive evil. After extensive scrutiny, Leung Bang-shui was selected under the operation of Grand Eunuch Fung Bao. However, in reality, Liang Bangrui is already seriously ill, and his illness has gone bad.
At the time of her big wedding, Leung Bang-sui made a big mistake. She had a huge nosebleed at the wedding site, and even her wedding gown was dyed red. However, the eunuchs, who had taken extensive bribes from the Leung family, saw that the matter was about to come to an end and were in a hurry, so they lied and said that seeing a fortune at the big wedding was a happy event for the newlywed. As a result, the crowd cheated, and finally pushed Princess Yongning of the Ming Empire to the front door of the Liang family.
Leung Bang-shui is already seriously ill. Naturally, it is impossible for anyone to walk around. It is only at this point that Princess Yongning knows it too late. After just one month of being married, Leung Yeong-ma was sickly. Princess Yongning also died of depression after being widowed for several years. An imperial princess, the emperor's sister, turned a man on a sheep's head selling dog meat into such a miserable situation! Emperor Wanli, who didn't know the truth, had nothing to do in the face of his sister's unhappy life; all he could do was sigh sadly.
Which of the above three horses is not described as a rare talent with good looks, a kind heart, good health, and a great personality? Actually, it doesn't live up to its name; “sell dog meat on sheep's head”! In the end, they got through the mess and cheated on the emperor's house, and those who didn't get it were retaliated; there were also crooks who succeeded, but ultimately harmed others and themselves, and even the royal family had nothing to do. This is a strange thing from ancient times to the present!
