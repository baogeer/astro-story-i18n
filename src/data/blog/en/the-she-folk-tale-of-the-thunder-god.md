 Lei Shen (Lei Shen): Commonly known as Lord Lei, it is the god responsible for thunder in ancient Chinese mythology. From “Mountain and Sea Sutra: East China”. Legend has it that the god Thor was born in ancient Leize (the former site is in present-day Heze, Shandong). He has a dragon body and thunder when he bulges his stomach.
Ancient people didn't really understand the natural phenomenon of thunder, so a series of related legends gradually evolved. In ancient China, the image of the god Thor evolved continuously. At first, people shaped it as a monster with a human head and dragon body, and when you hit it on its stomach, it made a thunder sound. Only later did it gradually change to the image of a sharp-billed monkey face, and it gradually took shape. In Taoist mythology, many numbers and levels of Thor are recorded. The most basic level is “Lord Thunder,” the upper level is an ordinary “Thor God,” and the upper level is “King Thunder.” Legend has it that Lei Wang was born on the Leizhou Peninsula in Guangdong Province. His name was Chen Wenyu, and later became an immortal. The highest-ranking god of thunder in Taoism is “nine days to respond to the sound of thunder and the popularization of Tianzun.” “Tianzun” is the highest rank among Taoist immortals, and “Puhua Tianzun” is the commander-in-chief of all Thor gods. According to Chinese ideas, Thor is the god of punishment for evil. If people do bad deeds or break their vows, they may be bombarded to death by thunder. Chinese people set Thor's birthday on June 24 of the lunar calendar, and a sacrifice ceremony will be held on this day. It pinned on the Chinese working people a beautiful wish to get rid of evil, avoid disasters, and pray for blessings.
Thor gods on the Leizhou Peninsula are all called Lei Shougong. Their birthday is the 28th of the Lunar New Year. On this day, every household has a big dance with fruit, cookies, offerings, and the lion dance troupe, dragon dance troupe, theatre, annual parade, etc., and people rejoice and pray for good weather and rain, and the safety of the people.

During the Southern Dynasties of China, there was an Orion named Chen Hong in Leizhou. He has a hunting dog with nine ears. Every time he hunts, he uses dog ears to tell fortune-telling: if one dog's ears beat, it can only hit one prey; if the dog's ears are beating, it can hit multiple prey; if the dog's ears don't move, then he shouldn't hunt. The next method works very well. One day, before Chen Hong went hunting, he saw that the dog's nine ears were moving. He couldn't help but rejoice: the trip would be very rewarding. The hunting dog walked into a field of thorns and kept screaming. As soon as Chen Hong took a look, it turned out to be a big paw! Chen Hong brought it home. Suddenly, there was a mix of ups and downs. As a thunderbolt blew up, the paw immediately burst, and a little boy jumped out of it. The word “thunder” in his left palm and the word “state” in his right palm. This is also the legend of the origin of the place name Leizhou. Since then, thunder has been heard over Chen's house every day. Entering the child's room is very regular. After a year, when the child was able to eat, the thunder over the Chen family stopped. As a result, Chen Hong named the child Chen Wenyu. The townspeople all called him “Thor.” When Chen Wenyu grew up, he acted as an assassin in Leizhou. He did a lot of good deeds for ordinary people, and his political voice spread far and wide. After his death, the townspeople set up a temple to sacrifice him. It was quite spiritual. The thunder was loud and loud, and it came from the hall.
During the Song dynasty, the imperial court learned about this god of thunder and named him “King Weide Zhaoxian”. The “Cantonese New Language” of the Qing dynasty describes the Temple of Lei Wang in this way: the god thunder is crowned, and there are twelve gods of thunder on both sides of the temple, corresponding to the twelve positions, as well as statues of Duke Lei, Electric Mother, Fengbo, and Master Yu. There is also a servant in the temple holding a round egg, alluding to the birth of the god Thor.
