 Ancient myths about aquariums mainly include pioneering the earth, creating the sun, moon, and stars, the origin of humans, and reflecting national struggles.
In the myth of Aquatic Pioneers, there is a fairy devil with great divine power and a fairy Gong Eun. They are the ancestors who pioneered the earth and created the human world. Before heaven and earth were formed, the entire universe was chaotic — this is the common imagination of the ancestors of all nationalities about the state of the universe at the beginning of time. In aquarium mythology, it was the fairy fairy witch who pioneered the world. She discerned heaven and earth with both hands and blew a sigh in the middle, so heaven and earth split. Half of the left side became the sky, and the right half became the earth. She then cast bronze and iron columns to support the sky, “holding it up the first time, 70,000 feet tall; holding it twice, the sky is soaring.” The distance between heaven and earth has been widened, creating an environment where everything grows. Gong Eun is a character who appeared after The Devil made the world. The myth of “Gong Giving and Dying Heng” is widely spread among aquarists. “Point” means pioneering, “constant” means worldly space, and “Gongen dianheng” means opening up the world. These myths say that after the Devil made heaven and earth, the earth was completely bare, and Gung Eun stepped on the ground with huge feet on crutches, creating mountains, rivers, lakes, and sea fields for every world. He first stepped on the south side. The earth couldn't bear the heavy pressure. It fell into the ocean, and his sweat dripped down and turned into seawater. He gently rubbed his foot, creating a wide flat dam; when he stepped on one foot east and one foot in the west, he stepped on the peaks of the mountains... Gongen's act of stepping on the earth had a clear purpose, which was to benefit everyone, so even though he stepped on it “soft on his feet,” he was tired and sweaty, but he did not stop. The groundbreaking achievements and extraordinary powers of the Tooth Witch and Gong Eun have made them heroes handed down from generation to generation.
Forging the Sun and the Moon. The myth of the origin of humans is in line with the myth of pioneering the earth, and they are all praises of the achievements of the devil. After pioneering the earth, in order to brighten the world and create conditions for everything to survive, the devil rose up his power and created the sun and moon. Her arm was sore due to hard work. As soon as she waved her arm, the baby in her hand was evacuated from the sky and turned into a twinkling star. The devil was too impatient. He created ten suns at once, and did bad deeds with good intentions. The ten suns hung high in the sky, causing endless disasters to the earth. The sunburned trees turned yellow, and the rocks melted, making it impossible for humans and animals to survive. The suffering of the earth made the tooth witch see her mistake, so she sent bronze arrows and iron arrows to humans so they could shoot down the excess sun, leaving only “one is the sun, and the other becomes the moon.” The myth of shooting the Sun and Moon is widely spread among all ethnic groups in the South, and the content is much the same. This is a record of ancestors of all nationalities exploring the mysteries of nature, and it is also a painful memory of ancient humans experiencing disasters. Much of the sun scorching the earth is clearly a reminder of the severe droughts, and the content of human origin where heavy floods recreate human smoke is unquestionably a reflection of the flood disaster.
Myths such as the Tooth Fairy made people, the twelve fairy eggs, the origin of humans, and dragons, thunder and tigers compete for the world all describe scenes of the origin of humans and their struggle for survival. There is a saying that the Tooth Witch created people to dominate the world. They first created short, thin people with breasts, and all were eaten by wild animals. She learned her lessons and recreated a sane person. Another explanation is that the Magus and the Aeolus spouses gave birth to 12 eggs and hatched 12 animals such as humans, thunder, dragons, and tigers. When people first find fire, they become owners of the earth. The latter statement is quite similar to the mythology of ethnic groups such as the Miao and Dong people, reflecting the primitive sense that the ancestors of aquariums were unable to separate themselves from nature. In human origins, when a brother and sister got married, the younger sister covered her head with a banana leaf due to her shyness and fear of offending the gods. This is probably the origin of the practice of today's aquarium marriage bride covering her head with an umbrella and repelling lightning. Dragons, thunder, and tigers compete for the world is a historical reflection of early human clan struggles. Among them, the depiction of the terrible power of thunder and the uneasy ending of “thunder is burned,” is a psychological reflection of the aquarium's dual feelings of adoration and dislike for thunder.
