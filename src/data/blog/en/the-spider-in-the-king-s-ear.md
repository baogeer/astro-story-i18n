 A mother and child live in the valley. Their lives depend entirely on their son selling firewood. One day, the teenager got tired of cutting firewood and lay down on a stone to rest. He looked up and saw a tree hanging a fruit he had never seen before. The teenager took it off and ate it. He only felt a hint of sweetness and fragrance in his mouth, and soon fell asleep.
When he woke up, he heard two people talking in the tree. One has a sharp voice, and the other has a mute voice. He said in a loud voice, “Zaka! Zaka! Dude, long time no see!” In a mute voice, he said, “Our king's ears hurt, knock! Many lamas have been invited to read the sutras, goo dong! A lot of 'knives' were thrown on the roof, knock on! We have a party every day, huh!” In a loud voice, he said again, “Zaka! Zaka! Is the king getting better? Zaka!” He smiled in a muffled voice and said, “These lamas are all big rice buckets, grunt! It only deceives the king. How do you know how to cure diseases, boo boo! Actually, the king's ear wasn't sick; he just got into a giant spider, grunting! As long as you are next to the king, set up a large water tank and tap the drum lightly. When the spider hears the sound of the drum and thinks it is spring thunder, it will crawl out; when they see the copper tank, they will draw a wire and tie the net. At this point, as long as the spider silk is cut off, the king's ears won't hurt, knock! Go-dong!”
The teenager felt very strange. How could these two people stand in a tree and talk? I opened my eyes and saw that there were no people in the tree at all. There was only a flower magpie and a black crow. The two had a very happy conversation. The teenager thought, “Can I understand the bird after eating that fruit? Anyway, I'm going to go to the city to see if the crow's story is real or fake?”
The teenager came home and said to his mother, “Grandma, I want to visit the city tomorrow.” Grandma said, “Kid, if you don't cut firewood for a day, we don't have any pickles for a day. Why are you running to the city?” The teenager said, “Grandma, don't get mad, you'll know when I get back!”
The next day, he got up early and rushed to the city with two chopsticks. Halfway through, I saw the man in front of me riding a mule and a little pony in the back. Little pony runs around town: “Grandma, grandma, wait for me!” The mule and horse also said while running, “Little pony! Come by yourself! There was an iron nail on the saddle. It hurt Grandma so much. I want to run home quickly.” The young man stopped the horse rider and told him what he had heard; the rider began to disbelieve and got off the horse and lifted the saddle. Sure enough, there was an iron nail. The teenager is also very happy because he can understand not only birds but also animals.
After entering the city, the boy went straight to the king's place of residence. When he saw that the palace gate was tightly closed, he hit it hard. It took a long time before the iron gate opened a finger-wide gap. The samurai yelled inside, “The king is seriously ill today, and no one will open the door.” The young man said, “Uncle, let me see the king's illness! Don't look at how young I am; maybe I can cure the king!”
The samurai brought the teenager to the king's hospital bed. He looked at the king's condition; indeed, it was exactly what the crow was talking about. He then ordered the maid to set up the water tank and knock on the drum. After less than a cup of tea, a big red spider crawled out of the king's ear. The teenager tore off the spider silk, put a little ghee on the king's ear, and stuffed it with a little wool. The king immediately felt completely at ease and was completely cured. He was very happy to say, “Kid, you are cutting firewood deep in the mountains. Why did you know that the king had ear pain? Why do you know I have a giant spider in my ear? Where did you learn how to attract spiders?”
The teenager thought: If the crow said it, the king would definitely not believe it. He said, “When I was cutting firewood in the mountains, two beautiful fairies told me.” The king was even happier and gave him lots of gold, silver, cloth, beef and sheep.
The young man returned home and handed over all of the king's rewards to his grandmother. At the same time, he recounted the history of treating the king's illness. Grandma complained, “Kid, this is a good thing, why are you hiding it from me?” The teenager said, “Grandma, don't you always say that you have to do things properly? Yesterday, I didn't know if the crow wasn't smart?”
