 Qin Hui, the traitor of the Southern Song Dynasty, killed Yue Fei for the crime of “being unnecessary,” and has always been hated by people throughout the ages. Yuefen in Hangzhou has an iron statue of Qin Hui and his wife kneeling. They are constantly being cursed, kicked, and spat on.
There is a very “stinky” legend about the iron statue of Qin Hui and his wife. In other words, there is a Zhejiang governor surnamed Qin. After taking office, he saw Qin Hui and his wife kneeling and being humiliated. They felt that they were “ashamed to have the surname Qin” and were disgraced, and wanted to remove the iron statue. In order not to incite public outrage, he ordered people to secretly remove the iron statue at night and throw it into West Lake. Unexpectedly, the lake water suddenly smelled bad the next day, and at the same time, the evil image of Takefun disappeared. People are calling for government investigations one after another. Soon, the iron statue actually floated from the bottom of the lake. The people picked up the iron statue and put it back in front of Yue Fen, and the lake stopped smelling right away. People all think that Qin cypress is so smelly that it has polluted the West Lake. The governor, whose surname is Qin, saw this situation and was helpless.
People throughout the ages didn't feel angry because they cast a kneeling statue of Qin Hui. Legend has it that they made the flour to look like Qin Hui and his wife, and then fry them together in oil, called “deep-fried rice” or “deep-fried Japanese cypress” (the same as Japanese cypress). Today, in China, “deep-fried Japanese cypress” is called “fried dumplings,” or simply called “deep-fried ghosts” locally, as if the Qin Hui couple were treated as demons. Regardless of the name, Qin Hui has already provided the Chinese people with a famous dish. This “credit” should also be attributed to him!
