 During the Daoguang years, Li Chengfu brought several pieces of newly unearthed bronze ware from Shaanxi to do business in Beijing. One day, he walked into Xuanwumen and arrived at Shijiama Street (now Beijing's New Culture Street). He happened to catch Keqin County Wang's return home. Pedestrians on the way had to avoid it. Li Chengfu didn't know there was such a rule. He found it both fresh and lively. He just watched the fun. Standing without a wall. The guards whipped him and beat him. He screamed “Unfair!” The king of Crooked County overheard it and ordered the bus to be stopped. He asked: Who is complaining? Bring him to me! As soon as Li Chengfu saw this situation, he was frightened and hurriedly knelt down and knocked his head. Grandpa Wang asked: What's wrong with you? He didn't dare to say that the guard had just whipped him and was afraid of being beaten again, so he rushed to say, “I have good bronze that I can't sell; I'm here to shout for the wrong treasure. Grandpa Wang laughed and shouted: Get up the car and go home! Bring this guy back to me!
Li Chengfu was taken to Wangfu, North Keqin County, Xitou Road, Shitouma Street, and presented him with several pieces of bronze. Instead, Grandpa Wang didn't look at him; instead, he showed him his collection of gold stones. King Keqin County asked Li Chengfu the names of several pieces of bronze ware. He answered fluently and expressed his opinion. Grandpa Wang really appreciated him.
Since then, Li Chengfu has been trading antiques for King Keqin County. He earned money to buy Jinshi Archeological Books to read, and worked hard on his own. Grandpa Wang was very appreciative of his good studies and gave him a look at his collection.
King Keqin is one of the Eight Iron Mats. The Eighth Iron Hat King mentioned here refers to the six princes and two county kings who had outstanding war exploits in the early Qing dynasty and were crowned “hereditary disregarded.” Wang Yuetuo of Keqin County, the first was the eldest son of Daizen, the second son of Nurhachi, the Emperor of the Qing dynasty. What generation was King Keqin from the time of Daoguang and Xianfeng, but the old antiques dealer can't tell. I only know that he was a gold stone collector, collecting three generations of Qin and Han bronze, with the largest collection of stone carvings, ancient paintings, calligraphy, and ancient jade and pottery tablets. The interior furnishings were all written and painted, and King Keqin was famous for his calligraphy.
King Keqin County also asked Li Chengfu to inspect the precious cultural relics in his collection and catalog them into a book. Since then, Li Chengfu's eyesight for appreciating the stone carvings and ancient jade pottery sculptures of the Jinshi Monument has been cultivated, and he has gained fame in the Jingshi Jinshi industry.
At the end of Daoguang, Li Chengfu returned to Xiangfen County, Shanxi, and a gentleman from his hometown raised funds and invited him to open an antique store at the Beijing Liuli Factory. In Beijing, he asked King Keqin County to write a plaque: “Deboshai Antique Store,” which was displayed in this ancient cultural market, and Deboshai officially opened. Afterwards, Grandpa Wang came forward to ask Xianfeng Xinke's champion Weng Tong and write Men Ying: “Derby Gui Zhang Ru is valuable; Baozi Yi Ding is an ancient disciple.” Grandpa Wang wrote a plaque. The champion wrote Men Ying. This is the only one in Beijing. This antique store has been in business for 94 years. It only closed in 1945. Unfortunately, both the plaque and the front door were missing later, which is amazing!
