 Emperor Kangxi went south and arrived in Hangzhou. He toured the mountains and water all around the West Lake, wrote poetry, and claimed to be a merry emperor.
One day, he will go to Ling Yin to have fun.
When the old monk at Lingyin Temple learned the news, he was really surprised and delighted. He rushed the bell and drummed to gather more than 300 monks from all over the temple. The monks wore brand-new coats, sandalwood incense on their heads, and sounded Nanwu Amitabha in their mouths. Everyone followed the old monk to Shilianting, three miles away, and brought Emperor Kangxi to Lingyin.
The old monk accompanied Emperor Kangxi and traveled back and forth between the front and back of the temple, up and down the mountain. Emperor Kangxi saw that Ling had high mountains, clear springs, shady trees on the mountain, and bright red flowers blooming underground. It was a really nice place! As soon as he was happy, he ordered to set up a table at the temple.
The emperor set up the table, it was very lively! Blowing, bouncing, and singing, it turned this Buddhist sanctuary into an emperor's house for a moment! Emperor Kangxi was wearing a goat beard in one hand, holding a wine bottle in the other, pouring yellow soup and singing poems.
The old monk had long heard that Emperor Kangxi liked the inscription of the poem. Seeing his head shaking his head at this point, I went to the local official who followed Emperor Kangxi and discussed: “Grandpa, I want to ask the emperor to give a plaque to the Mountain Temple. Can you guys see if you can?”
The Governor of Hangzhou said, “Good, good, if the emperor gave you a plaque for Lingyin Temple, even my entire Hangzhou province would be covered!”
Qiantang County officials also joined in: “The Emperor's wine is thriving. If you ask him for a plaque, I think he will definitely agree.”
Once the old monk settled in his heart, he stepped boldly and knelt down in front of Emperor Kangxi:
“Empress, look at the Buddha face of the Great Bodhisattva at Lingyin Temple, and let's enjoy the scenery as well!”
The old monk's request just scratched Emperor Kangxi's itch. He nodded, grabbed the brush a few times, and wrote the word “rain” crooked and skewed. On this day's light, he was already drunk, his wrists were shaking a little, and he was writing a little faster. The word “rain” actually took up most of the paper! The word “spirit” in Lingyin Temple, according to the old spelling, has three “mouths” and a “witch” under “rain”! Now there's only half of this small sheet of paper left, and you can't do anything about it. Let's rewrite it, how humiliating! Emperor Kangxi clutched the pen in one hand, and didn't pick his goat's whiskers with one hand, but there was nothing you could do about it. The officials surrounding them knew that Emperor Kangxi could not step down, but no one dared to say it clearly; they just stood by and worked in a hurry. Fortunately, a college student named Gaojiang Village came up with a solution. He first wrote the words “Yunlin” on the palm of his hand, then pretended to get close to Emperor Kangxi and secretly spread his hands. Emperor Kangxi took a look. Oops, these two characters really are life-saving King Bodhisattva! When I woke up half way up, I wrote down the four characters “Yunlin Temple” on the ground in Kiri Lake. When you've finished writing, throw the brush far away.
The old monk came over Zhang Zhang, that's not right! How is “Lingyin Temple” written as “Yunlin Temple”? He stuttered and asked:
“We call this place 'Lingyin Temple', not 'Yunlin Temple'! Did the emperor write it wrong?”
Emperor Kangxi listened, rolled his eyes, and shouted: “Speak loudly!” When the old monk dared to speak again, he had no choice but to stand beside him respectfully. Emperor Kangxi turned back and asked the officials:
“This place has clouds in the sky and forests underground. If you say, you call it 'Yunlin Temple', right?”
“Yes, yes, the emperor Sheng Ming!”
……
When the officials complimented him with all their rhetoric, Emperor Kangxi laughed so much that he ordered the plaque to be carved and hung.
As soon as the emperor said a word, the officials were busy. On the one hand, they asked someone to replace the original plaque at Lingyin Temple, and on the other hand, found a carver to carve the four large characters “Yunlin Temple” written by Emperor Kangxi on mahogany, with gold background, black lacquer, and embellished with two dragon beads, and hung them on the mountain gate on the spot.
Since then, the “Yunlin Temple” plaque, which lives up to its name, has been hanging at Lingyin Temple. However, ordinary people in Hangzhou are not selling his accounts; even though the “Yunlin Temple” plaque has been hanging for 300 years, people still call it “Lingyin Temple.”
