 Emperor Yan had a daughter, called a girl. The girl is very well-behaved. When Huangdi saw her, she couldn't help but praise her. Emperor Yan regarded the girl as a jewel in her pocket.
When Emperor Yan was not at home, the girl played alone. She really wanted her father to take her out and take a look at the East China Sea — the place where the sun rises. But it's because her father is busy with business: when the sun rises, he comes to the East China Sea until the sun sets; every day, I can't take her. On this day, without telling her father, the girl sailed alone in a small boat to the place where the sun was rising in the East China Sea. Unfortunately, strong winds and waves suddenly broke out at sea. The mountain-like waves knocked over the girl's boat. Unfortunately, the girl fell into the sea and was eventually engulfed by the merciless sea, and she was never able to return. Emperor Yan certainly misses his little daughter, but he was unable to use sunlight to irradiate her, causing her to die and come back to life; he was alone and saddened.
When the girl died, her spirit turned into a bird with a flower head, white beak, and red claws, and screamed “Jingwei, Jingwei,” so people called this bird “Jingwei.”
Jing Wei hates that the merciless sea has taken her young life, and she wants revenge. As a result, she continued to climb a small stone from Mount Hajito, where she lived, spread her wings and flew all the way to the East China Sea. She soared back on the choppy surface, screamed, and threw down stones and branches to fill the sea.
The sea roared, roared, and laughed at her: “Little bird, forget it, you've been doing this job for a million years, and you don't want to fill me up!”
Jing Wei answered the sea from a high altitude: “Even if it takes 10 million years, 10,000 years, until the end of the universe and the end of the world, I will fill you up!”
“Why do you hate me so much?”
“Because you took my young life, you will take many young innocent lives in the future. I'm going to keep working endlessly; one day I'll fill you up on the ground.”
Jingwei flew and screamed, left the sea, and flew back to Hatoyama to pick up stones and branches. She called it, throw it away. Over the years, she has flown back and forth, and never stops. Later, when a Haiyan flew over the East China Sea, he saw Jing Wei inadvertently. He was puzzled by her behavior, but after understanding the cause of the incident, Haiyan was moved by Jing Wei's fearless spirit, and formed a couple with them, giving birth to many small birds. The female was like Jing Wei, and the male was like Haiyan. Like their mother, Xiaojingwei also went to reclaim the sea. They are still doing this kind of work to this day.
Story inspiration: Jingwei's perseverance, kind wishes, and grand ambitions are respected by people. Tao Yuanming, a poet from the Jin Dynasty, wrote in his poem, “Jingwei's title, Weimu will fill the sea with it,” warmly praising Jingwei's sad fighting spirit of daring to fight against the sea. Later generations also often used “Jingwei to reclaim the sea” as a metaphor for the arduous and outstanding work carried out by people from time to time.
People sympathize with Jingwei and admire Jingwei. They call it the “Wicked Bird,” “Shidori,” and “Empress Sparrow,” and built a monument on the East China Sea, called the “Jingwei Pledge Water Site.”
