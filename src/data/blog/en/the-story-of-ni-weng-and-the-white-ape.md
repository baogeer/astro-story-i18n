 Legend has it that one night, Ni Weng was concentrating on his studies. Suddenly, he reached out a furry hand in a stone gap and asked him for something to eat.
Ni Weng knew this was a white monkey who had been practicing for a thousand years, so he picked up a pen and wrote the word “mountain” on the white ape's hand, like two mountains stacked on top of each other, pressing the white ape's hand so that it couldn't move.
Only then did Bai Ze learn that Ni Weng had no choice but to ask for forgiveness.
Ni Weng asked the white ape to agree to two conditions: first, they must change evil and do good in the future; second, the frogs in the rice fields are too loud to read quietly; they must drive away the frogs.
The white ape listened and quickly agreed. As a result, Ni Weng connected the stroke in the middle of the two “mountain” characters to form the word “out.”
Since then, of course, I can't hear the frog's cry here; Ni Weng has become good friends with the white ape.
