 It is said that during the Qianlong period of the Qing Dynasty, one day the emperor had a dream of a beautiful Uyghur girl in the remote western region of Gushule (present-day Kashgar), holding earthenware in her left hand and a jujube branch in her right hand, with a charming smile.
Emperor Qianlong was moved by her smile, and only woke up the next day to discover that it had turned out to be a dream.
Qianlong wanted to make his dream come true, so he sent a minister to Gushule in the western region to find this beautiful girl.
The ministers went through all the hardships to reach the western region, but they couldn't find the girl of Qianlong's dreams. Just when they were in trouble, they suddenly saw a Uighur girl standing on the wall of Tucheng. She was holding earthen pottery in her left hand and a jujube branch in her right hand.
I also saw that this girl was so good-looking and graceful that she was brought to Beijing and married in Qianlong, because her name was Iparhan, which means fragrant girl in the local language. In addition, she had a natural fragrance on her body. The Qianlong Emperor loved it so much that he called her Consort Xiang.
Legend has it that after her death, her body was transported back to her hometown of Kashgar and buried in her family tomb according to Muslim customs.
