 The horse head is a traditional musical instrument unique to the Mongolian people. It is named because a delicate horse head is carved on the top of the pole. It is the favorite musical instrument of Mongolian people, and almost every family has it. The sound of the horse head piano is very nice. Not only do people love to listen to it, but even camels won't walk away when they hear the sound of the piano. At dusk, the sunset slowly turns the prairie red, and the shepherds will sit in front of their yurts and play their favorite horse head piano. The round and rich sound of the piano is like telling a beautiful and moving story on the prairie.
Once upon a time, there was a herdsman named Su He on the prairie who depended on a smart white horse and was inseparable. One day, Hakuba suddenly became ill and died. Su He was very sad and always looked forward to Hakuba coming back. One night, he dreamed that Hakuba had come back and said to him, “Aren't you thinking about me? Then use my bones, tendons, and tail as a piano! That way we can be together every day.” The next day, Su He made a piano as she had in her dream and carefully engraved the head of a white horse on the top of the pole. The sound of the piano was as kind and beautiful as the cry of a white horse, and people later gave this piano a nice name — horse head piano.
