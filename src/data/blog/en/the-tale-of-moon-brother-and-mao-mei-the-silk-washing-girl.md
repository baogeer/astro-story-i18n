 Once, Ye Lang Wang went on a hunting trip and saw that Mao Mei, a lady from Wansha by the Liao River, had the appearance of falling for the country. Mao Mei swore to die because she and the Longjiao Woodman Moon Ge Ge Daredevil. Ye Lang Wang took Mao Mei to the royal palace and sent to persuade Brother Moon to abandon Mao Mei. At the same time, Xu Ye was a senior official. How could Brother Moon believe in this evil?
Ye Lang Wang then said to Brother Moon, “If you can reverse the Longjiao River within 7,749 days, I will allow the two of you to get married; otherwise, Mao Mei belongs to me.”
Later, with the help of Shang, Brother Moon was successful, but due to exhaustion, he was swept away by the waves when he brought water to the river to quench his thirst.
After learning, Mao Mei used the excuse of sacrificing Brother Moon to get married to Ye Lang Wang and went to a cliff and jumped into the Lujiangjiang River to be martyred.
Later generations changed the name of the Longjiao River, which flows east and west, to commemorate this pair of young people who are unswerving in love with each other, to “Moon River.”
In the dam on the south side of Maokou Street, there is a tall tomb. Locals call it Prince's Tomb. Legend has it that the prince can eat one bucket or two litres of rice in one meal and is extremely powerful. He can carry a cow to the river to bathe, be kidnapped in a battle, and buried here. Although the exact era of Wang Zi's tomb has yet to be verified, people believe it is related to Ye Lang, and there are even six scholars who believe that the legend of Wang Zi's tomb is the same as the one recorded by Chen Lishu Ye Lang in “Han Shu: The Legend of Southwest China”.
