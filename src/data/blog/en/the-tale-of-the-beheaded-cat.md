 Once upon a time, there lived in Kofuku-ji Temple, a high-ranking monk whose name was Huiku.
Master Hui Kong's dojo was not originally at Kofuku-ji Temple. It was only because of the successive droughts in those years, no grain was harvested, and the North China region was full of hunger and misery. The villagers didn't have to eat; they only had to pick wild vegetables. Once they had eaten all the vegetables, they ate tree bark and Guanyin soil. The imperial court said it had set aside hundreds of thousands of dollars for disaster relief, but the states and counties withheld everything, and there was very little money left to actually use for disaster relief. People starve to death every day, and people even eat people when they're out of their way. The temple has also already been taken over by a local scoundrel. Master Hui Kong couldn't bear to see the human tragedy of people eating people, so he left his hometown with his three disciples.
Master Hui and his entourage begged along the way and forged good relationships along the way. Because Master Hui learned medicine in his early years, saved the dead and helped the wounded along the way, plus Master Hui Kongshi was kind and proficient in Buddhism, people all revered him as a “living Bodhisattva.”
Traveling all the way south, Hui Kong Shi finally settled down at Kofuku-ji Temple. Since the south was not affected by the disaster, the situation was very different from the north; it was a bustling scene. Since then, Master Kei Kou has become a sutry-lecturing monk at Kofuku-ji Temple.
The teacher meditated and taught the sutras, and gradually became a well-known high priest in Kofuku-ji Temple. Many people admired him to seek relief from their hearts, and the teacher spoke in detail, leaving everyone satisfied. Since then, incense has flourished at Kofuku-ji Temple.
Furthermore, the abbot of Kofuku-ji Temple was an intolerant person. He was very jealous when he saw the rise in fame of Master Hui, so he harassed and maliciously slandered the Master. He was very dissatisfied with all of his disciples. He asked the teacher why he was so forgiving. He was right about the position of abbot of Dexing.
At this point, the teacher always smiles and doesn't speak; he just takes care to meditate and sit down.
One day, when all the monks went out to cut grass, a kitten appeared in the quiet mountain temple. This kitten's fur is shiny and supple, and its little eyes are spinning around and looking at the crowd with great curiosity. I saw it lick and lick its front claws from time to time, and sometimes lay on the ground to sleep. The monks all thought it was unusual and scrambled to catch the cat.
Although the temple was originally a place to pray and worship the Buddha, it is also divided into the 369 rank of high and low. It is also common for new monks to be bullied by their siblings. The abbot of Kofuku-ji Temple has a sibling. Originally, the position of abbot should have been Yuen Fen, but for some reason, when the last abbot died, he relinquished the position of abbot to Yuanzhen, who entered the temple a few years later. For this reason, although Yuanfan had no words on the surface, she secretly hated her teacher and brother. However, Yuan Zhen was not originally a person who went to the Buddha; he was forced to leave his home at Kofuku-ji Temple only because he murdered people in his hometown and was chased away by the government. Since he was originally a secular villain, he was able to speak well, served the abbot in the first place, and said a lot of bad things in front of the incumbent abbot, so after he became the abbot, even though he was ostensibly respectful to his brother, he secretly feigned each other's strength in private.
As a result, Kofuku-ji Temple was divided into east and west temples. Yuan Zhen's disciples lived in the East Temple, while Yuan Xuan's disciples lived in the West Hospital. Even when they met, the disciples from both temples didn't say hello and didn't interact.
Faced with such a small creature, the monks in both houses wanted to keep it for themselves. The dispute between the two sides continued, and the scene was completely chaotic.
Seeing this scene, Master Hui Kongshi grabbed the kitten's neck and mowed grass with a knife and said, “If you can say something in line with Buddhism, this cat will be saved; otherwise, I'll kill it.”
The crowd looked at each other and didn't answer in silence. Master Hui Kong immediately cut off the kitten and threw it away.
The monks were very surprised and rushed to report to the abbot.
Monk Yuanzhen was already jealous of Master Hui Kong. Seeing him kill in front of the monks, he asked with an iron face, “Master is a member of the family. Why did he break the killing ring?”
Huikong laughed and didn't answer.
Yuan Zhen also said, “Since the teacher is so determined, Lao Na can only follow the rules of this temple, take responsibility for 30, and then expel him from the mountain gate.” After speaking, they waved, and when the two monks walked out left and right, they wanted to take Master Hui.
Suddenly, a small Sami ran in from outside the temple and shouted, “Hugh can beat my master.”
When the monks looked back, it turned out that Huikong's youngest apprentice had a false sense of emptiness. Fushui asked the monks about the circumstances of the incident, wiped the sweat from their faces with their sleeves, offered a salute to the master, and also offered a bow to the abbots. I took off the earthy sandals under my feet, put them on my head, and said to Master Hui Kong, “Master, this is the disciple's answer.”
Master Hui laughed a bit, then sighed: “If you came back earlier, this kitten could have been saved from dying.”
The monks were puzzled.
Master Hui Kong said, “The family's disputes over this cat are all due to confusion and delusional thoughts in my heart. This kitten is the root of my thoughts, and cutting off the cat's neck cuts off all conflicts and antagonisms. False water wears sandals covered in dirt and despised by people on your head. That means you should treat everything with a normal sense of tolerance. If everyone had this kind of heart, wouldn't they have a grudge against a cat? Treat others with leniency; this is the way people who practice Buddhism should practice!”
The monks had an epiphany. Since then, the East and West hospitals have stopped complaining; people have more respect for Master Hui Kong, and a monument called “Cat Slicer” has been erected between the two hospitals.
Later, when Monk Yuanzhen died, he wanted to relinquish the position of abbot to Master Hui Kong. His teacher refused and recommended Fushui Shang. Since then, Kofuku-ji Temple, under the leadership of Hui Kong Master and Fushuisho, has flourished on a daily basis, and is known to everyone from all over the world.
