Since “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms” became popular, and since Luo Guanzhong had enough energy to eat milk and shake the flag and blow the horn for Zhu Geliang, the image of Zhuge Liang as the “chief think tank” in the hearts of the Chinese people is deeply entrenched in the hearts of the people, and it has been difficult to change it.
Zhu Geliang not only became the number one representative of the Three Kingdoms think tanks, but also almost became the spokesperson for the Three Kingdoms period of history. When it comes to the Three Kingdoms, the first reaction of many people is probably not Cao Cao or Liu Bei, but Zhu Geliang.
In fact, this “one generation to one hundred” phenomenon has existed since ancient times. In an excellent team or industry, there are many elite talents, but often only one or a few celebrities are well known to the public. For example, if you mention Tang poetry, you must say Li Du; if you mention Song lyrics, you must say Su Xin; etc.
In fact, Li Dusuxin is only a representative figure of their respective cultural eras. It cannot be explained that other poets and lyricists of the same generation are poor. Who dares to say that Wang Changling, Wang Wei, Bai Juyi, Xiao Li Du, Luo Yin, Da Yan, Liu Ou, Qin Lu, and Li Yue5c7 are not as good as Li Dusuxin?
Back to the topic of the Three Kingdoms, they all say that the Three Kingdoms advisers are like rain; this statement is true. We are already familiar with the elites in Wei Shu and Wu's three think tanks, such as Zhu Geliang, Xun Yu, Xun Yi, Pang Tong, Guo Jia, Fa Zheng, Lu Su, Liu Ye, etc., all of which are big names.
Because they work for Wei, Shu and Wu's three major political and military groups, these think tank elites have had a very high level of exposure on the historical stage. First-tier celebrities are often like this. The more popular they are, the more popular they are, the more popular they are. It is difficult for ordinary people to know about them. You can call them a mainstream think tank group in the three countries.
If there is mainstream, then of course there are non-mainstream. The non-mainstream here isn't about individuality, but rather about exposure on the historical stage. For various reasons, in addition to the big-name advisors who were highly exposed on the stage of Wei, Shu, and Wu, there were also several shining superstars scattered in the historical sky of the Three Kingdoms.
These non-mainstream superschemers are probably slightly less popular than people like Zhu Geliang and Xun Yu, but when it comes to strategy, they are worth the same amount as Zhuge Liang and others. For example, Jia Yi, Chen Gong, Chen Deng, Tian Feng, Yu Shu, etc. Let's talk about Jia Yi first.
Of these five people, Jia Xu is clearly very different. First, he is a super famous think tank under Cao Cao, and his net worth is by no means lower than Xun Yu. The reason why Jia Xu was not included in the “Cao Cao's Think Tank” article, and I'm talking about it here is because, although Jia Xu has big skills, he is not Cao Cao's henchman.
“They claim not to be their ancestors, but they have deep plans, are afraid of suspicion, keep their hands on themselves, and retreat without personal relationships.” They belong to the kind of tall people who are hidden in the DPRK. From this perspective, Jia Xu can be considered non-mainstream, just like Shu Han's “Little Jia Yi” Liu Ba.
In the first year of Emperor Han Huan Jianhe (AD 147), Jia Xu was born in Guyuan, Wuwei County, Liangzhou. Since the founding of the Eastern Han dynasty, the Eastern Han dynasty has fought a hundred-year war with various Qiang tribes scattered around Liangzhou. There has been a long period of military chaos here, and the complex and sinister living environment has just made Jia Xu extremely adaptable.
Jia Xu's level of strategy is taken into account in the entire group of Three Kingdoms advisers. They are all super heavyweight. None of the Three Kingdoms advisers has dared to say that their IQ is superior to Jia Xu. At the beginning, Jia Xu abandoned his official position and returned to his hometown. On the way, he met a group of rebels against the imperial court. Jia Xu and dozens of his companions were captured alive, and the rebels wanted to bury them alive.
Jia Xu is extremely adaptable. In order to save himself, he falsely claims that he is the nephew of Duan Yi, the captain guarding Qiang. At the time, Duan Yi was a member of the imperial court fighting the Qiang rebellion. He was very prestigious in Western Turkey. Plus, Jia Xu and Duan Yi were both from Guzang. Shi Bing knew about Duan Yi's weight; of course, he didn't dare to hurt Captain Duan's nephew, so he let Jia Xu go.
Jia Xu really had the skills. He bragged and lied, didn't blush, and laughed with the leader Yi Bing. He drank chicken blood, and worshipped his boss. After that, Jia Xu waved away in a big way, and the dozens of unlucky people who accompanied Jia Xu were all buried alive.
Of course, this is just a little clever, and the difficulty factor is not very high. People who respond quickly will say, “Whoever is my uncle, who is my second grandpa. What really shows a person's strategic intelligence is still in a big scene.
However, the first time Jia Xu made an official appearance on the historical stage, he left behind a long period of infamy. It can be said that Jia Xu was responsible for a large part of the turmoil in the early days of the Three Kingdoms. In the 3rd year of Chuping (AD 192), the giant thief Dong Zhuo was jointly killed by Wang Yun and Lu Bu. Dong Zhuo's three horses, Li Yi, Guo Yi, and Zhang Ji, saw no hope for their future. They were preparing to disperse their troops and return to their hometowns, and did not mix up in the official arena.
At this point, Jia Xu, who was eating mixed meals under Dong Zhuo's son-in-law Niu Fu, was full and had nothing to do. She wanted to show off her level of intelligence. Jia Xu emboldened Li Yi, saying that if we were to disperse, a small government office manager would be able to clean up us. Why don't the brothers join forces and head east to fight against Wang Yun. It was done. We became princes, and it wasn't too late to escape defeat.
These people, Li Yi, were originally powerful thieves from the river and lake. They were encouraged by Jia Xu. They certainly boosted their guts and sought revenge against Wang Yun. In the end, the clan destroyed Wang Yun and defeated Lu Bu. Various warlords were killed in one place. Bricks flew across, and the Central Plains region suffered unprecedented damage from the war. “White bones are in the wild, and no chicken sounds for a thousand miles.”
Jia Xu has been harshly criticized in history because he said something inappropriate. In fact, in addition to Jia Yi, there is also Wang Yun, who is really responsible for the chaos of Li Yi and Guo Yi. If Wang Yun didn't want to kill Li Yi and Guo Yi, refuse Lee and Guo surrender to the imperial court, and force the two to lose their way, things wouldn't have reached such an unmanageable point.
Jia Xu is a non-mainstream genius that is free from the mainstream social value and moral system. The value of Jia Xu's existence may not be required using general standards such as patriotism and love for the people. In a troubled world, human survival comes first. If people's self-worth is demonstrated, the whole society will progress along with it. After all, society is made up of countless individuals.
As far as Jia Xu is concerned, it is impossible for Li Yi and Guo Yi to provide a good stage for Jia Xu to achieve personal values. How much profit can he make with the bandits? However, there was a scuffle between the heroes at the time, and Yuan Shao and Cao Cao were both in the early stages of their careers. Jia Xu didn't necessarily have much in-depth knowledge about them. Coupled with the distance, it is clear that they were not the right buyers for Jia Xu.
On Jia Xu's selection menu, Zhang Ji's nephew Zhang Xiu was a good candidate, and he is based in the Nanyang area and has a relatively stable income. Jia Xu felt that it would be safer to eat at Zhang Xiu's home for the time being, so she secretly frowned at Zhang Xiu. Sure enough, they hooked Zhang Xiu's Seven Souls and Six Spirits, and sent someone to ask Jia Xu to become a military commander.
Although Zhang Xiu's stage isn't very big, it's enough for Jia Xu to stretch out his magical talents. The reason why Jia Xu was able to rise to fame in the Three Kingdoms is largely because he is in Nanyang? That was an amazing show. Along with Jia Xu's wonderful performance, Luye is Cao Cao, the top hero of the Three Kingdoms.
It was the spring of the 3rd year of Jian'an (198 AD). Because Zhang Xiu formed an alliance with Mu Liu Biao in Jingzhou, Cao Cao felt the pressure on the southern front. Coupled with Zhang Xiu's defeat to Cao Cao in Nanyang the year before, Cao Cao's eldest son, Cao Ang, and General Dianwei died in battle. Cao Cao wanted to avenge his revenge, and Cao Cao marched south in a big way.
However, not long after Cao Jun arrived in Nanyang, news came from the northern front that Tian Feng advised Yuan Shao to secretly attack Xudu. Cao Cao didn't dare to make fun of his home, and immediately withdrew his troops. Of course, Zhang Xiu didn't want to let go of Cao Cao, a big fish. With his brothers, he shook his head and tail. The one with a red head and raised his face wanted to “say goodbye” to Cao Cao.
Jia Xu stopped Zhang Xiu and advised Zhang Xiu not to pursue him; if he chased him, he would lose. Zhang Xiu glared at Jia Yi and checked whether Jia Xu had been kicked in the head by a donkey. Why wouldn't he want such a good chance to get rich? Zhang Xiu didn't listen. The wind rushed out, but soon Zhang Xiu was defeated by Cao Cao's ambush and almost lost her underwear.
After Jia Xu waited for Zhang Xiu to return, he immediately advised Zhang Xiu to go after Cao Cao again, and he would definitely win big. Zhang Xiu believed Jia Xu a bit this time, and stormed out with slabs. Sure enough, he beat Cao Cao with a blue nose and swollen face. The more Zhang Xiu looks at Jia Xu, the less he looks like an individual. How can he be so picky? Cao Cao was almost determined by his recruitment.
Zhang Xiu asked sincerely, and Jia Xu laughed: “This is very simple. Cao Cao retreated when he first arrived. It must have been that Xu Du was threatened by Yuan Shao, and only then hurriedly withdrew north. Cao Cao used his army carefully; it was anticipated that the general would follow up later, so Cao Cao must have set up an ambush halfway; the general certainly succeeded. As for catching back and winning, since Cao Cao repulsed the general's pursuit, they thought we wouldn't pursue them again, so they let their guard down on us.”
Now Zhang Xiu is completely convinced. Jia Xu can see the world so thoroughly. There aren't many people in the world today; it's just Guo Jia, Cheng Yu, and Fa Zheng. Of course, if you look at it from Zhu Geliang's point of view after the incident, Jia Xu's ingenious plan really isn't unusual, but the problem is that “Ma An Aftermath” is worth nothing. If everything “happens after the fact,” then there is no need for history to exist.
A counselor of the rank of Jia Xu really offended Zhang Xiu by squatting in the small temple, just as little shrimp like Zhang Lu couldn't use the right method. Zhang Xiu is also just a springboard for Jia Xu. Jia Xu is bound to be unable to follow Zhang Xiu for the rest of his life.
Later, Jia Xu eventually followed Cao Cao, but because of his past grudges with Cao Cao, Jia Xu still had a certain distance between him and Cao Cao. This also affected Jia Xu's intellectual performance with Cao Cao.
However, Jia Xu was born with mixed scenes. He was always able to change the course of history intentionally or not. His only few beautiful handwriting left a deep mark on history. Cao Pi, the later Emperor of Wei Wen, probably had the most experience with this.
As Cao Cao grew older, there was no time to delay in determining the heir to the empire, but Cao Cao was always hesitating between Cao Pi and Cao Zhi. Had it not been for Jia Xu's words, “I think the father and son of Yuan Banchu and Liu Jingsheng.” Cao Cao didn't necessarily choose Cao Pi. It can be said that without Jia Yi, there would be no future for Cao Pi. Cao Pi thanked Jia Xu for the rest of his life. The Jia family lived rich and prosperous until the end of the Wei dynasty.
In a person's life, to be alive is to be alive. Whether it's a temple battle, a shopping mall fight, or a grassroots subsistence, it's just a process that proves that it once existed. Jia Xu was like this. After following Cao Cao, Jia Xu made up his mind to return to seclusion.
