 Yang Se-yan was one of the four leading lawyers in Jiangnan in the late Ming and early Qing Dynasties.
On this day, Zhang Laosan, a lonely old man, wanted to sell two houses at a market price of 102 silver. The landlord bully wanted to buy these two houses, but he only wanted to pay 52 silver and said, “If you don't sell it, you have to sell it!”
Zhang Laosan sought Yang Se-yan's idea; Yang Se-yan wanted him to follow Wang's bullies first.
The next day, when Wang Bully heard that Zhang Lao was willing to sell the house, he invited Yang Seyan as a mediator. Yang Seyan wrote down a copy of the contract on the spot: “Zhang Laosan voluntarily sold two houses, sold rafters, beams, bricks, and tiles, and sold the foundation. The bargain price is 52 silver. Oral statements are unfounded; this evidence is established as evidence.” Both parties signed and stamped, and both money and goods were settled.
Four months later, Zhang Laosan found Wang's bully and asked him to demolish the house and free up the foundation. As soon as the king bully heard about the fire, he immediately offered a contract to get Yang Se-yan to testify.
Yang Seyan slowly and rationally thought about the contract. Wang Bully didn't hear it right. How did the “selling the foundation” at the time of signing the contract become “not selling the foundation”? After taking a look at the contract, it clearly says “Don't sell the foundation.” The king bully was speechless; he had to pay another 52 silver dollars to buy the foundation.
Afterwards, Zhang Laosan asked Yang Seyan, how did this word change? Yang Se-yan smiled and held out her left thumb. Only then did the old man wake up. It turns out that before Yang Se-yan wrote the contract, she put a light layer of ink on her left thumb. After receiving the certificate, she lightly pressed the word “down” and it became the word “no.”
