JIANGSU NETWORK, SEPTEMBER 22: Compared to Yang Guang, the emperor of Sui Yang, who is highly controversial, Empress Xiao is unknown in history. In books and novels of later generations, Hou Xiao was implicated by Emperor Sui Yang and depicted as a “female scourge” of ignorance, immorality, and disorderly. However, in real history, Hou Hsiao is a traditional woman with a gentle temperament and a high level of cultural cultivation. She is neither a blessing nor a powerful heroine.
Hou Hsiao is the daughter of Emperor Liang Ming of the Southern Dynasty. Since she was born in February, Jiangnan customs regarded it as ominous, she was placed in foster care by her uncle and uncle, and lived a period of poverty. Emperor Sui Wen wanted to choose a concubine for Yang Guang from the nobility of the Liang dynasty. After the divination, many women had only her as a good sign, so she was listed as the queen. In “The Book of Sui” and “Northern History,” they all claim that Hou Xiao had a gentle and docile personality, was intelligent and insightful, had literary cultivation, and knew the art of divination. Before succeeding to the throne, Yang Guang was inseparable from Hou Xiao in order to please her mother, Empress Dugu, and was inseparable from Hou Xiao. After taking the throne, he always had a very good relationship with Hou Xiao, and he always took Hou Xiao by his side when he toured the Sifang region.
As Empress Yang Guang, Empress Xiao has a strong sense of worry. She once wrote “Shu Zhifu” to euphemistically persuade Emperor Yang. There is no shortage of articles saying, “If you go deep and walk thin, your heart will be terrified. An aphorism like “If a husband is tall, he must be dangerous; his worries are full of worries to prevent spillover.” When Emperor Sui Yang toured the capital of Xing Jiang, peasant revolts raged, and the Prohibition Army also plotted a rebellion, but Emperor Sui Yi kept his eyes closed and instead executed those who accused the crime. When others wanted to make accusations in the future, Hou Xiao stopped them. The general situation in the world could no longer be saved; talking too much only made the emperor worried. From then on, the husband and wife sat in Troubled City until Emperor Yang was overtaken by Yu Culture. Hou Xiao buried Emperor Yang with a lady from the palace, and was kidnapped all the way north by Yu Wenhua and kidnapped. Zhong Tuyu Wenhua was killed by Dou Jiande, and later became a prisoner after Xiao. At this point, Emperor Sui Yang's younger sister, Princess Yicheng, who was married to the Turks, sent someone to welcome Xiao, and she spent another 11 years in northern Serbia, until the 4th year of Tang Zhenguan (630), when the Tang army was defeated by the Turks, and did not return to Chang'an after Xiao. Seventeen years later, Hou Xiao passed away, and members of the Tang Taizong sect sent her body to Yangzhou for joint burial with Emperor Yang. They gave her the nickname “”, which meant pity and sorrow.
In the latter half of her life, Xiao drifted back and forth, and became material for wild history and fiction to demonize her “One Woman Marries the Five Emperors,” but none of this has any historical basis. In particular, when she returned to Chang'an, she was over 60 years old and the aunt of Tang Taizong. There is almost no chance that a glamorous incident will happen between the two.
In 2013, the joint burial tomb of Emperor Sui Yang and Hou Xiao was discovered in Yangzhou. Emperor Sui Yang left only two teeth, but the post-Xiao skeleton still survives. It was determined that Hou Xiao was about 1.5 meters tall. Phoenix crowns, chimes, braids, terracotta figures, and porcelain were also unearthed in the tomb.
