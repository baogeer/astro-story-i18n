 The TV drama “Yang Guifei” starring Yin Tao and Huang Qiusheng was a hit on Hunan TV. Yang Guifei's ambiguous relationship with Li Bai in the drama became a major highlight and subject of controversy. So do Yang Guifei and Li Bai actually have a relationship with Li Shangshang? The author checked the data and found that Yang Guifei was born in 719 AD and Li Bai was born in 701 AD. The age difference between the two is 18 years, which is similar to the age in the TV series. Therefore, it is not too exaggerated to imagine that the talented beautiful woman once had a romantic history. So what is the historical relationship between the two? What kind of relationship does Li Bai have with Yang Guifei?
In August of the first year of Tianbao (742), Tang Xuanzong ordered the conscription of Li Bai into Beijing. Li Bai received an order, thinking that he could get the most out of his career, stop living in disgrace, and “go out laughing” and set off from Yanzhou, Shandong to Chang'an. Shortly after arriving in Chang'an, I was greeted solemnly by Xuanzong at the Jinluan Temple. Although Lee Lung-gi did not arrange any official positions for Lee Bai; he only left him to wait for Han Lin, but this temporary, alternate status gave Lee Bai an opportunity to get close to Xuanzong and Princess Yang Guifei.
As can be seen from Li Bai's poems, every time Xuanzong has played with Yang Guifei, he likes to let Li Bai follow him around and sing songs with Zuo Xing. In October of the first year of Tianbao, Xuanzong took Yang Guifei to Lishan to soak in hot springs. Li Bai followed him, and wrote poems such as “The Soldier's Guest at the Palace”; in the early spring of the following year, Xuanzong had entertainment in the palace, and Li Bai wrote “Ten Song from the Palace” (only eight of them can be seen today); in mid-spring, Xuanzong visited Yichun Yuan, and Li Bai also went to Xing Xing Gong to enjoy the fragrance. Peony, Xuanzong wanted to listen to a song with new lyrics and ordered Li Bai to write “Three Songs from Qingping”; in the summer, Xuanzong paddled the White Lotus Pond, and Li Bai wrote “The Beginning of White Lotus”; in addition Poems such as “Spring Trip” and “Yangchun Song” are also probably works created to accompany the waiters. It's easy to imagine that Li Bai, the “immortal man,” entered the palace and breathed a breath of fresh air into the luxurious and dull life of the court. When Xuanzong saw Li Bai, he must have felt fresh and interesting. For a while, Hyeon-jong treated Lee Bai unusually well, and it was entirely possible. Historical records of feudal recipes, nobles holding inkstones, and wrestlers taking off their boots were not necessarily made out of thin air by future generations.
However, the good times didn't last long. In the spring and summer of the second year of Tianbao, Li Bai began to express his disappointment in poems such as “Seeing the End of Nanshan Mountain and Sending Zigou to the Hermit”, “Xiajing Nanshan Mountain, People Staying Home for Wine”, and “Heavenly Retreat in Dongxi Gong”. Beginning in the fall of that year, he wrote a number of works showing fear and grievances, such as “The Jade Step Grudge”, “Ancient Style 44: Greed and Lavish”, “Song of Grudge”, “Desperate for Life”, and “Two Songs About the Long Door”. In the spring of Tianbao's three years, Li Bai left the imperial court and left Chang'an — Li Bai did not leave Chang'an voluntarily; he was banished.
Li Bai spent more than a year as a literary attendant in the imperial court, spending time with Xuanzong and Yang Guifei everywhere. Based on this, it can be inferred that Li Bai has seen Yang Guifei's beauty and singing and dance talents — according to the history book, Yang Guifei is “highly qualified”, “good at singing and dancing, and has a sharp sense of rhythm”, and plays the pipa very well. In the late spring of Tianbao's 2nd year, Xuanzong and Yang Guifei enjoyed peonies at the Chenxiang Pavilion at Xingqing Palace. Li Bai Fengzhao wrote “Three Songs in Qingping”, “The Cloud Wants to Wear Flowers,” “Poor Feiyan Relies on New Makeup,” and “Famous Flowers Love the Country, They Look Like a King with a Smile”. It is likely that Yang Guifei wrote a scene where Li Longji and Yang Guifei fell in love with each other. It would be unreasonable to say that Yang Guifei, a beautiful woman who is good at singing and dancing and is proficient in vocals, is indifferent to Li Bai, who is talented in poetry. A talented person met a beautiful woman. Although there were no rumors of scandals (Yang Guifei's boyfriend at the time was An Lushan), it is only in the imagination that they should have felt sorry for each other.
Therefore, I deeply doubt what Li Baichuan said in “The New Tang Book” that Li Bai was not appointed by Xuanzong and was evicted from Chang'an. The root cause is Yang Guifei's repeated “frustration.” At that time, Li Bai's status was nothing more than a “servant of Han Lin”; to put it bluntly, he was an “entertainer,” and used poetry to entertain the emperor and his concubine. Collix and Yang Guifei still couldn't compare him to it. Furthermore, Collix was a lieutenant who was very loyal to Xuanzong and obeyed his words. To be happy with Xuanzong's play, he tried his best to set it up but it was too late. Where else would he go to demolish the foot of the wall? He took it humiliated because Colliers once wrote a poem in front of Xuanzong and others when Li Bai was drunk and made him take his shoes off, then said bad things about Li Bai in front of Yang Guifei, leaving Li Bai out. Inevitably, this also underestimates Collix's stamina, and too much bravado about Collix — there's no such measure of entertainment, so how can you be a villain by Xuanzong's side? Li Bai was probably Xuanzong's pistachio at the time; he actually wanted to crowd him out. A eunuch, did he eat bear heart and leopard gall? More importantly, although Hyun-jong Lee Yong-gi at the time was addicted to love, he still did not lose his iron fist style in governing the country; he did not allow his subordinates to make love or slander others in front of him. For example, there is an article in the “Miscellaneous Records of the Ming Dynasty” (“An Lushan”) where An Lushan secretly bribed Yang Guifei, hoping to take the post of prime minister, and Xuanzong did not agree; Ma Zhang Yi thought that Xuanzong would appoint himself prime minister after visiting his private house once, but he was slow to be appointed. He complained privately to An Lushan, and An Lushan told Xuan Zong again. As a result, Xuanzong was furious. Xuanzong loved Yang Guifei. “Three thousand loves go hand in hand” is a famous story from time immemorial. However, he did not allow Yang Guifei to act in government. Yang Guifei was evicted from the harem twice, and her status as a queen was almost abolished. It also shows that Xuanzong was not overwhelmed by love. Yang Guifei hardly dared to casually say bad things about Li Bai in front of him to stop Li Bai's career.
Song Renren Hong Mai extracted the plot of Yang Guifei from Li Bai's poem using Zhao Feiyan's innuendo of Yang Guifei's sentence in the “New Tang Book”, and cited Li Bai's “Snow Scream Poem”, which satirizes women in troubled politics in history, as an example. He said that Li Bai was hinting at and revealing the obscene secrets of Yang Guifei and An Lushan (“Essays by Rong Zhai”, vol. 3). This statement is also unbelievable. Although Li Bai was very dissatisfied with what happened to him, he did not make such direct and blatant insinuations and disclosures in his poems. If this were true, I'm afraid what Li Bai received would not be a “money refund” treatment.
There are other explanations as to why Li Bai was given back the money. Wei Hao's “Li Hanlin Collection” says “(Xuanzong) Xu Zhong Shushe chased the Haidai room;” Li Yangbing's “Thatched House Collection” said “the same class of ugliness, the harmful power is slanderous, the motto doesn't fit in, and the emperor uses negligence”; Liu Quanbai's “The Chronicles of Tang Han Lin's Master Li Junjie” says “Being slandered and ordered back to the mountain”. Although these people are all family and friends that Li Bai had relationships with during his lifetime, the reasons mentioned are not necessarily entirely credible — they all have their concerns. Li Bai's devotee Han Lin is still not an official civil servant. He doesn't have much to risk “slandering” Li Bai; people in the same class may be jealous of Li Bai's talent and the emperor's grand encounter, but according to Xuanzong's personality and handling of affairs, they don't necessarily dare to say anything bad about Li Bai in front of him. What I think is more believable is what Fan Chuanzheng said in “Tang Zuo's Legacy of Han Lin, Master Li Gongxin”, “... Xuanzong loved his talents, or thought about entering and leaving the province through drunkenness, not to mention greenhouse trees, fearing to save the future, and go with it.” In other words, the main reason for Li Bai's departure from the imperial court and Chang'an was Tang Xuanzong himself: he was worried that Li Bai, who was often drunk, would divulge secrets about the palace. This is Xuanzong's most taboo thing. It is well known that the emperors of the Tang dynasty learned from the Han dynasty in many ways. Under the Han dynasty law, spreading the word between North Korea and China was a serious crime. For example, Xia Hou Sheng left the imperial court once and told outsiders what Emperor Xuan had told him was harshly reprimanded by Emperor Xuan and was afraid to say it again; Beijing told Emperor Han Yuan what he said to Master Zheng Jun, and Zheng Jun spoke to Zhang Bo again. Zhang Bo quietly wrote it down, and was later killed as a result. In fact, I'm afraid not only the Han dynasty or the Tang dynasty was like this; any dynasty kept the inside story of the imperial court strictly confidential. The main reason Xuanzong dispelled the idea of appointing Li Bai as the person of the Chinese Bookstore is probably because Li Bai loves to drink too much, gets drunk too easily, and lacks a handle on his mouth after getting drunk.

Originally from Yongle, Puzhou (present-day Yongji, Shanxi). Born on June 1, 719 AD in Shu County (present-day Chengdu, Sichuan). Yang Guifei is naturally beautiful. Li Bai (February 8, 701-762), character Taibai, called Master Qinglian. A poet from the Tang dynasty in China, known as a “fairy of poetry” and a “poet.” “Li Taibai Collection” has been handed down. Most of his poems are written when drunk. His most famous works include “Wanglu Mountain Falls,” “Hard Walking,” “The Hard Way in Shu Dao,” “Will Drink,” “Liang Fu Yin,” and “Early Departure to Baidicheng.” Later, there was Li Bai, the prototype of “Li Xia” in the movie “The Radio Waves That Never Disappear”; Li Bihua, the author of many novels such as “The Rouge Buckle,” was originally named Li Bai.
