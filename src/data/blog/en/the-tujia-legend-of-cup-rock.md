 In ancient times, Mount Emei in Sichuan and Mount Bamen in Hunan each lived an immortal with excellent magic skills. In a day, the immortals of Mount Emei said, “I fell a rock from Mount Emei to Mount Bamian. This rock is three feet three taller than Mount Bamian.” The fairy of Bamian Mountain said, “The rock you dropped was only three feet three feet short, and you can't get into Mount Bamian.” The fighting began, and the immortal of Emei threw a giant stone towards Bamian Mountain. The fairy from Bamian Mountain performed a spell, blocking it one foot away from Bamian Mountain and pressing it six feet six feet three feet higher than Bamian Mountain. It was three feet three feet shorter than Bamian Mountain, and was shaped like a woman carrying a child. This is now Beizi Rock at the junction of Tiantang Village and Chongqing.
During the Jiahe era of the Qing dynasty, large local owners mobilized nearby people to raise funds to build a stone bridge. This bridge is 3.5 meters long and 1.5 meters wide. After the construction of the stone bridge, a temple was built on Beizi Rock. Good men and women came one after another, and incense was very strong, until the 1960s of the last century.
In 1989, the Minister of Armed Forces of Shatian Township (located under Beiziyan) in Sichuan Province led five militias to blow up the stone bridge on the grounds of breaking feudal superstitions. The sky cleared thousands of miles that day. After Ishibashi was blown up, there was an instant storm and torrential rain, flying sand and stones. Three days after the armed forces returned home, they became seriously ill and died.
