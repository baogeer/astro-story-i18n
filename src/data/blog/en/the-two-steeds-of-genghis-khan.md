 One day, the Ermog white horse in the Genghis Khan group gave birth to two strong little ponies. Khan was very happy to see them, praised them greatly, and severely punished them. Genghis Khan then rode them and led the crowd to hunt, and the two horses chased down hordes of gray wolves and sheep. However, no one praised them both. The two horses were very sad. In particular, the little young horse was unwilling to endure this kind of humiliation and discrimination, decided to flee and seek the opinions of the older brother, the big horse. However, the big horse had many concerns, was willing to endure pain, couldn't bear to abandon the Holy Lord, family and friends, and dissuaded the little brother in all kinds of ways.
However, the little horse did not listen to the dissuasion and resolutely ran away alone. Afterwards, the big horse followed in its footsteps and fled together.
When Genghis Khan dreamed that the two horses had escaped, he immediately led an army of 100,000 to hunt them down and ordered that if they catch up, they would not be allowed to damage the arrows, capture them as much as possible, and promised to give half of all his livestock to the captors.
Due to the outstanding performance of the two horses and their rapid pace, none of them were able to catch up. Genghis Khan had no choice but to abandon his hunt plan and return home full of sorrow and tears.
The two horses fled to the Altai Mountains, where they spent four years. Meanwhile, the little horse ate a lot of meat and fat because it had no thoughts, no burdens, no merit, and no worries, while the big horse was left skinny because he missed the Holy Lord, his beloved mother, family and friends, and his native land.
Looking at his brother's poor face, the little horse had to follow his old path and return to his hometown.
Genghis Khan dreamed that the two horses had returned to the group. He was so happy that he went to visit despite their unruly clothes, and took the initiative to ask the two horses why they had left.
In the fall, the two horses once again participated in a grand hunt and were appreciated by the crowd. Genghis Khan named the little horse a “divine horse,” and the two horses have lived a “happy and happy life” since then.
“Genghis Khan's Two Horses” imparts more cultural connotations than a typical narrative poem. It is an outstanding expression of Mongolian traditional culture. It contains all aspects of Mongolian mentality, beliefs, and value orientation. It has special value in anthropology, ethnology, and folklore research, and has received attention from academia at home and abroad.
Oral transmission in Mongolian is one of the basic characteristics of “Genghis Khan's Two Horses”. As an epic poem, “Genghis Khan's Two Horses” should have been read aloud, but in reality, it is often passed on and promoted national culture in the form of folk songs and dances.
There are many place names in Ottok named after the story “The Two Horses of Genghis Khan”, such as Bayin Aobao, Tujiliang, and Zabqi.
The Mongolian oral narrative poem “Genghis Khan's Two Horses” was announced by the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region People's Government as the fifth batch of representative intangible cultural heritage projects at the autonomous region level in 2015.
The Mongolian oral narrative poem “Genghis Khan's Two Horses” was announced by the Ordos Municipal People's Government as the fourth batch of representative municipal intangible cultural heritage projects in 2015.
