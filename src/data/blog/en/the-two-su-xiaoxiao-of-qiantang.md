 When visiting West Lake and walking to Xiling Bridge, I saw many visitors taking pictures in front of the small tomb and Mucai Pavilion. I couldn't help but think that Qiantang once had two Su Xiaoxiao. Unfortunately, all visitors only knew about this one, and not the other Su Xiao.
At the time of the Southern Dynasty, Qi Su was small. He was known as a “beautiful green building and a talented air fighter”. He died at a time when he was young. Later, there was Sima Cai Zhong. Because he was very fond of Su Xiao Xiao, West Lake sought his tomb to worship him, and dreamed of sleeping with him. He died in Hangzhou a few years later and was buried next to a small tomb. See “The Chronicles of Chunzhu” for the incident. This is what is now widely known as Su Xiaoxiao, buried beside the Xiling Bridge.
The other, Su Xiaoxiao, is from the Song dynasty. According to Lang Renbao's “Seven Types of Drafts,” Su Xiaoxiao is a famous prostitute from Qiantang. There is a poem: “You live in Yangyang as a concubine in Wu, and no lover sends love letters. If you were visiting back then, did you have anything to do with Scuba Diving?” The meaning of the last two sentences is that if you visited me back then (like your brother was in love with my sister), how could I get involved in this silk lawsuit in Qianxian County. According to a poem by Zhang Guangbi from the Yuan Dynasty, it is known that Song Su was buried before the administration of Jiaxing County after his death.
Not only are there many tourists and readers today who have heard nothing about Song Su Xiao Xiao, but they are also unknown to some famous writers in the past. For example, Zhu Yizun mistakenly used the small tomb of Su in Jiaxing as the small tomb of Su in southern Qi because he did not know that Song also had Su Xiaoxiao, and thought that the West Lake Little Tomb was nothing more than an attachment.
