 Author: Ding Qizhen
Not long ago, I wrote a text introducing five talented women from the Tang Dynasty. After I posted it on my blog, it caused a lot of discussion. Some netizens criticized me, saying that my introduction was not detailed enough. In order to satisfy the wishes of this group of netizens, here are the two talented women I named as first (winner) and third (flower exploration), namely Li Jilan and Xue Tao, and talk about them in a bit more detail. Blog text is different from academic papers (I often have friends criticize my blog text using the standards of academic papers; that is a sign that they have high hopes for me; it's not that they don't understand the characteristics of online writing), so I'll try to keep the topic as easy as possible: just talk about the fluency of the two of them.
The main reason for singling out these two talented women is that they are both talented women who can be called “the artist of color.”
Li Jilan, “The Biography of Tang Caizi,” says that she has a “beautiful posture and a poor look.” It can be seen that she is unmistakably beautiful. This beautiful woman is not only good at writing poetry, but she is also good at playing the piano. How good are Lee Ji Lan's poems? Her contemporaries, Liu Changqing, who claims to be the “Great Wall of Five Words,” and Gao Zhongwu, a famous critic who edited “ZTE Interlude”, have praised her highly. Liu called her a “powerful woman,” and Gao praised her “from under Bao Zhao, which is rare.”
Li Jilan's flirtatious writing is mainly reflected in his dealings with a group of male poets. After Li Jilan left home, she traveled to and from Shaoxing and had close relationships with the hermit Lu Yu and the monk Jiaoran. At one point, it was clear that in a poem in response to Li Jilan, there were the following four verses: “A celestial girl will try to dye her clothes. The Zen spirit is unbelievable; I still brought back my old flowers.” As you can imagine, they talked and acted quite casually when they were together.
More casual was when I was with Liu Changqing and others. Once at Kaiyuan Temple in Wucheng (today Wu Xing), Li Jilan learned that Liu Changqing had a “severe illness,” or hernia disease, so he made fun of Tao Yuanming's poem “Mountain gas is good day and evening.” Of course, Liu Changqing is not an ignorant person. In response, he answered, “The birds are happy and trusted.” It's also Tao Yuanming's poem. [I'm afraid some particularly pure friends won't understand it. I'll explain: Li Jilan uses the synonym “mountain gas” to point directly to the most intimate part of Liu Changqing's body; Liu Changqing didn't shy away. Using “bird” (niao) to refer to “diao” (diao) indicates that scrotal enlargement is also useful — older brother has some support.] To use Tao Yuanming's verses, they are elegant; they refer to hidden parts of the body, vulgarity. Using elegance to portray folklore is mercy. It is said that the effect at the time was “sit up and laugh” and “two commentators are beautiful.” In other words, their merry was recognized by people at the time.
Xue Tao is a famous prostitute. There is a record in the literature that “she has a very beautiful manners; in between words, she gets paid” (He Guangyuan's “Precautions” vol. 10). Xue Tao is undoubtedly a multi-talented woman. As we all know, in addition to writing poems and playing music, she also invented the technology to make small pamphlets — named “Xue Tao Jian.” Furthermore, according to “The Book of Xuanhe”, she is also very good at calligraphy. “Her writing is not feminine, her handwriting skills are strong, and her writing skills are quite remarkable.” During the Northern Song Dynasty, there were also people who collected the original poem “Daylily Cao” by Xue Tao.
Xue Tao's poetry is widely known. I've already introduced it in my article. Let's talk about her eloquence.
In the social arena, Xue Tao was described by the ancients in eight words: “alert, relaxed, and full of conversation and laughter.” Xue Tao played a game called “A Thousand Characters” with an assassin at the feast. The “Thousand Characters” sentence he recited must include a word for birds, fish, birds, and animals. The assassin first said, “There is Yu Tao Tang,” and all the other guests at the table held back their laughter, and no punishment was given to him. When it was Xue Tao's turn, she said the phrase “Zoshi Ah Heng.” This assassin immediately pointed out that there were no words such as fish or birds in Xue Tao's sentences; Xue Tao must be punished. Xue Tao smiled and said, “There is also a small caviar in the scale, so that Jun (called the governor) doesn't actually have a fish character in your sentence 'You Yu Tao Tang'.” Seemingly, this assassin is an idiot. He doesn't know the Chinese character — he uses “fear” as a “fish.” Another time, Xue Tao once played a “one-character command” game with Gao Xian, the supreme governor of the Chengdu region (ambassador Nishikawa Setsu) and a poet. The method was to suggest a pictorial character and then use a rhyming phrase to explain it. Gao Bian first said, “Mouth, it seems like Liang Dou is there.” Beams and handles too. Without hesitation, Xue Tao said, “Chuan is like a trifecta.” Gao Bian asked why the rivers are not just one curved but three? Xue Tao answered him, “You are an imposing Nishikawa Setsudo, and you also use a bucket (measuring instrument) without a handle. A poor drinking girl like me has three rafters at home. One of them is curved. What's so strange?” She cleverly converted the word “chuan”, which means river, into the word “(roof)” through homonym.
Tales and jokes from talented women are certainly difficult to rise to the Hall of Elegance; they are not enough to teach. However, in response to their affluent character and the self-confident and generous mentality revealed by the Tang dynasty people, we still might as well yearn for it and even learn from time to time. I'm afraid this is also in line with the intention of the country's leaders to build a harmonious society.
