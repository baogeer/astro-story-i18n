 There is a legend in the Yao family: “Pangu opened the sky, Luban built the wall, He Wang sent He, and the Niu Wang cultivated the fields.” The ancient Yao books “The Big Song of Pantian” and “Roller Mountain List” say, “After two years of heavy flooding in Yin Mao, everything in the world was flooded. Only the Fuxi brothers and sisters were left hidden in the gourd. Only then was it possible to continue as a human being. Later, Pan Wang ascended the temple and gave the people of Yao the world's mountains to be cut down and allowed to live and work peacefully. However, in the Yaoshan region, poor mountains and bad water, despite people's hard work, they are still uncertain. One year, Taibai Baixing visited the public situation and saw that the Yao family had lived in Junling Mountain for generations, cultivating fires, and had a difficult life, so they reported to Heaven. Emperor Jade then sent King He to send Rice to the world, and King Cow came down to cultivate the fields. Since King Niu came to the world, the Yao family has been cultivating fields and planting crops. The weather and rain have been smooth year by year, and grains are abundant. In order to thank King Niu, the Yao family used the day King Cow went down a fan (April 8 of the lunar calendar) as the traditional holiday Ox's birthday.
The Yao family has a folk song, “April 8, throw the plow; in July and a half, the barn is full. Take back 10,000 tons of food, all with the help of cows.” On the first day of April 8, the Yao family takes care of cows the most. They use cows as gods to worship and worship, and let them leave their rakes and rest for a whole day. On this day, no one is allowed to whip cows, fight bulls, let alone kill cows, or even curse cows. On the first day, every household had to bathe the cow once, brush the whole body clean, clean and tidy the cow fence, lay out fresh grass once, draw on red paper or cut a Buddha charm and stick it on the cow fence to exorcise evil and scare the plague, and protect the cow's health. On this day, what the cows ate was even more unique and exciting. Early in the morning, people scrambled to release the cows to eat the dew grass. The sooner the better, “stealing the head.” In the morning, use glutinous rice lees to cook eggs for cows to eat. At Yao's family, children have a habit of eating eggs on their birthdays, so you can see that they worship cows. There is a saying, “If you live, eat carrots; if cows live, eat bitter ginseng.” In the afternoon, boil loach with bitter ginseng to feed the cows to keep them healthy and live a long time. At night, the Yao family also had to select the best and strongest cows for the party. The Yao family dressed in festive attire, surrounded by a roaring bonfire, knocked on long drums, and danced with cheerful songs.
