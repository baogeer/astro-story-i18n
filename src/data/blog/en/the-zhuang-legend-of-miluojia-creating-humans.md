 Milo Jia (meaning mother in the Zhuang language), the goddess of creation in the Zhuang people, is said to be the wife of Buddha, and the other is the mother of Buddha. It is also translated as “Mloka” or “Mroka” in Chinese. Murojia Goddess Gorge in Liujia Town, Guangxi is named after this.
Legend has it that Mrikka was a goddess who made heaven and earth, humans, and everything. As soon as she took a breath, it rose to the top and it became the sky; the sky broke, and when she grabbed cotton to fix it, it became a white cloud. The sky caused it. She discovered that the sky was too big to cover, so she sewed the ground with sewing thread. Finally, as soon as she tapped the thread, the ground shrunk, and the sky was able to cover it. However, the ground is uneven again. All the edges of the earth are wrinkled. The ones that stand out high are mountains, and the ones that stand low become rivers, lakes, and seas. Due to the extreme importance of their own reproduction to primitive humans, the ancestors of the Zhuangdong-speaking nation worshipped Murojia as the god of fertility and worshiped her as their Virgin. With the development of society, Mlojia also became the mother god of the flower mother and flower queen, who was in charge of childbearing, and became an idol for married women seeking children. There are related myths and legends among the various ethnic groups in the Zhuangdong language family.
