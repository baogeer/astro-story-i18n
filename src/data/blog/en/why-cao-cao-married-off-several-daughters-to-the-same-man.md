 Author: Kanetou Zeming
It is an indisputable fact that Cao Cao had many children, but there is no clear record in history books how many daughters he had in his life. However, history books still cover Cao Cao's use of his daughter's marriage to maintain and secure his power and marry many of his daughters to Minister Wang Gong. Chen Shou's “The Chronicles of the Three Kingdoms” and Fan Ye's “Book from Later Han” all provided us with sporadic information. They were at least seven daughters recorded by Cao Cao. They were Cao Xian, Cao Jie, Cao Hua, Princess Qinghe, Princess Anyang, Princess Jinxiang, and Princess Linfen. Among them, the most shocking one is the story of Cao Cao's three daughters “working together as a husband.”
Historically, Cao Cao's daughters have never been taken seriously by historians. However, during Cao Cao's political career, the fate of his daughters was always controlled by him, and the daughters' marriage seemed to be a means for him to stabilize and expand his power.
According to historical records, Cao Cao has seven daughters. In addition to the two daughters Cao Jie and Cao Hua, who were born with Mrs. Bian, there are also Cao Xian, Princess Qinghe Chang, Princess Anyang, Princess Jinxiang, and Princess Linfen. In the 18th year of Jian'an (213), Cao Cao gave three daughters, Cao Xian, Cao Jie, and Cao Hua, all to Emperor Han Xian and Liu Xie as concubines. Fan Ye's “Book from the Later Han Dynasty: The Chronicles of Empress” records the wedding ceremony given by Liu Xie when she married Cao Cao's daughter: “To give Empress Mu Cao's secret festival, Wei Gong is also the daughter of Cao Cao. In the 18th year of Jianan, three women, Xie, and the wife of Huawei, were hired to hire 50,000 animals. The young ones stayed with the country.” It can be seen from this that Cao Cao's “Three Female Servants and One Husband” did indeed happen. What's more, in addition to allocating his three adult daughters, Xu Xie, to Emperor Han Xian, Cao also arranged a “baby family” with Liu Xie for the time being, and promised not to let them into the palace for the time being, stay in his own country, wait until they can get married, and then send them to the palace. Cao Cao actually did not hesitate to use the marriage and happiness of his own daughter in exchange for his position and influence in the imperial court to control the emperor. It can really be described as having good intentions and hardships.
When the three sisters Cao Xian, Cao Jie, and Cao Hua first entered the palace, they were all positioned as “ladies,” but the following year, the three sisters were promoted together as “nobles.” Emperor Han Xian Liu Xie's original empress was Fu Shou. Because she wasn't used to Cao Cao's authoritarian behavior in the DPRK and the harem, she wrote a secret letter to her father, Fu Wan, hoping that her father would be able to get rid of Cao Cao's fate. Unexpectedly, the incident unexpectedly leaked out, and Cao Cao forced Emperor Han Xian Liu Xie to abolish Empress Fu and put her in the Cold Palace. As a result, Fu Shou died in isolation in the Cold Palace. Later, Cao Cao took the lead and asked Emperor Han Xian to elect a new empress. According to the principle of not avoiding relatives, Cao Cao chose his second daughter, Cao Jie. On the first day of the first New Year's Day in the 20th year of Jian'an (215), Liu Xie listed Cao Cao's second daughter, Cao Gui as the empress of the Imperial Palace. Faced with Cao Cao's actions, the military and military officials didn't dare say anything. Cao Jie is known as “Empress Xian Mu” in history.
There aren't many records in history about Emperor Han Xian Liu Xie's three wives, surnamed Cao Jie. The most famous of them was named Empress Cao Jie. Cao Jie is an ethical traditional woman. She has a different nature from her father. She is a weak person, and she has completely sided with her husband since she married Liu Xie.
Everything changed after Cao Cao died. Cao Pi inherited the throne from his father, Cao Cao, and began threatening Emperor Han Xian Liu Xie to “zen concession” and set up a “zen surrender platform” in Xuchang so that he could accept the position of the emperor in accordance with his name. All matters have been completed; all that is needed is to hand over and hand over the national jade seal. However, Yu Xi was at Empress Cao Jie, and Cao Jie did not hand it over even though Cao Pi sent someone to ask for it many times. According to “The Book of the Later Han Dynasty: The Chronicles of Empress”, Cao Pi instructed Hua Xin to ask for the national seal. After hearing it, Cao Jie was furious. He didn't give anything he said, and blocked everyone who came outside the door. In desperation, they asked for it five times and three times. In desperation, Cao Jie was only able to let Cao Pi's people come in and pick up the Yu Xi. However, in front of the visitors, she began counting down her older brother and threw the Chuanguo seal under the steps and tearfully scolded, “Heaven is not good,” so that the messengers were afraid to look up at her; they just kept their head down and carefully picked up the jade seal and ran away.
From her first marriage to Emperor Han Xian Liu Xie to her final abandonment, Cao Jie became an empress for a total of 7 years. When Cao Pi claimed emperor, he did not exterminate Emperor Han Xian, but instead named Liu Xie as Duke of Shanyang and Cao Jie as Lady of Shanyang. It also gave Liu Xie and his grandchildren enough rewards to live a comfortable life, and they ate 10,000 households. Liu Xiedang died of illness 14 years later (234). He died at the age of 54. Cao Pi buried him in the Zen Mausoleum according to the etiquette of Tianzi of the Han family. Twenty-six years after Liu Xie's death, Cao Jie died of illness in the first year of Jingyuan (260). He was also buried in the Zen Mausoleum according to the etiquette of Empress Han, and was buried with Liu Xie, the “Duke of Shanyang.”
However, there are no records in history books about Cao Cao's other two daughters, how Cao Xian and Cao Hua actually ended. However, in the 1970s, Cao Xian's graveyard was discovered in Cao Cao's family graveyard in Bozhou, Anhui. This is another surprising thing. Why was the daughter she married buried in the Cao family's graveyard?
