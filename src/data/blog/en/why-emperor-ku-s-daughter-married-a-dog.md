 According to legend, Emperor Gong (sound “Ku”) was a very important emperor after Emperor Huangdi.
Legend has it that the emperor is the great-grandson of Emperor Huangdi. He is known as the Gao Xin clan because he has a fiefdom in Gaoxin. At the age of 30, he took over from Zhuang Yi and became the leader of the Huaxia Tribal League.
The reason why Emperor Yu is important is because the later Shang and Zhou dynasties all came to him to find their roots. The Emperor's Palace was simply the emperor's treasure trove. Once the founders of later dynasties couldn't figure out where their family came from, they had to visit him. Of course, they're not looking for Emperor Gong, because in that case, they have the same roots; what they are looking for is Emperor Yu's wife.
Like Emperor Huangdi, the Emperor also had many wives. The emperor's former concubine is Kang (sound “Hara”). According to “Historical Records: Zhou Benji”, the legend of Jiang is the daughter of the Tang (“Tai”) tribe, and her son abandoned the ancestor of the Zhou dynasty. However, with regard to the abandoned birth, his father, Emperor Yi, did nothing to help. Legend has it that while playing in the countryside, Kang accidentally stepped on the footprints of a giant. When she got home, she became pregnant and then gave birth to a son. Jiang, who “had children before marriage,” abandoned her baby several times, and the place where she left it was extremely immoral. It was either in a deserted country or in ice and snow. However, this child lived a long life, and in the end, birds and animals were actually fed. That's why his name is “abandoned” — abandoned baby. Later, they abandoned their position as the official in charge of agriculture, “Houji” (meaning “Ji”), and the Zhou family returned their abandoned mother, Jiang, to the family.
The emperor's second wife is Jandy. According to “Historical Records: Yin Benji”, Jane Dee was a woman of the (“Song”) clan, and the son she gave birth to was an ancestor of the Shang dynasty monarch. However, this contract had nothing to do with his father, Emperor Yi. It is said that on a spring day, Jandy took her two younger sisters to the river to bathe, and it just so happened that swallows that had returned from spring flew by. One of them, a swallow holding a bird's egg, was probably frightened by three women in the river, and the bird egg fell all of a sudden. By being the wife of a tribe leader, Jane Dee dominated this bird egg. She probably thought this bird egg was as nutritious as an egg, but in the end she swallowed it. Emperor Yu's third wife was even better. She is the daughter of Chen Feng's. Her name is Qingdu. Qingdu directly gave birth to the saint Emperor Yao. Qingdu not only conceived her own children, but also gave birth and brought children of her own. According to “Imperial Century,” Yao did not follow the surname Ji from his father, Emperor, but his surname was Yi. This is because when he was born and raised in Qingdu, he did not live with the emperor; instead, he lived south of Sanya and lived with a family named Yi Changru. The other second wife of the Emperor, named Chang Yi, is not very famous, but her experience of having a son was also quite amazing. It is said that the emperor is also very fond of women of a different race. Emperor Yi heard that the women of the Zoutu clan all had unique abilities. They could walk without stepping on the ground or swimming without getting in the water. Unbeknownst to me, this concubine's childbearing abilities were even more amazing than the original four emperor. In addition to having so many famous and amazing wives, Emperor Yu also had an amazing daughter. “The Book of the Later Han Dynasty: The Legend of Nanman” records this strange legend.
When the emperor was in power, the Chinese were surrounded by ethnic minorities that had not yet been enlightened. Among them, the Inuebisu tribe, located in the western part of the Central Plains, often invaded the east. The Emperor led an army several times, but they were always unable to fight. In desperation, Emperor Wei had no choice but to recruit talented people from all over the world, promising that whoever would take over General Wu, the leader of Inujung, would reward him with 1,000 pounds of gold, 10,000 households in Fengyi, and a wife.
It is said that the emperor raised a sacred dog, with multicolored fur, named Panjiao (sound “Fu”). Panping not only understood but also understood people's languages. Hearing that they were highly praised, they immediately rushed to Inubisu's camp and stayed with General Wu to go undercover. After General Wu let his guard down, he took the opportunity to bite off his head and return to Emperor Yi to ask for merit.
No matter where the ministers close to the emperor had seen such amazing things, they were all foolish. Examine that person carefully; it is indeed General Wu's. The Emperor had no idea at this point. If things were to be done according to the contract, how could a dog be crowned, let alone marry a big girl with a yellow flower. So, let's just prepare to feed it something tasty.
Unexpectedly, a daughter who doesn't know which emperor's concubine was born heard about this incident. She thought that since the emperor made a promise, she should not regret it. Now that no woman wants to marry a dog, she is willing to set a precedent for “marry a chicken, marry a dog.” The emperor had no other tricks, and she probably felt that the concubines and daughters around her were not in a normal state of mind, so she gave her daughter Xu to Pan Yi. This is what “The Book of the Later Han Dynasty” says.
Another way of saying is that when the emperor repented, it suddenly made a human voice saying, “If you put me in the Golden Bell, I can become a person after seven days and seven nights (one week in the Gregorian calendar)!” The Emperor placed the pan in the Admiralty as required. However, Emperor Yi had a daughter who was very curious; before it had been seven days, she ran over to see it. At this point, Pan Yi's body had changed into a human shape, but her head hadn't turned into a “human model”; it was still “like a dog.” The princess broke a big deal and had to get married to a “human face” fan.
After Panyu got this princess, she fled to the mountains of Chongshan Mountain south of the Central Plains on her back. The places where this amazing couple did their activities were all unvisited, and where they stayed was in a cave. In such an environment, there was no need to be dressed up, so the princess also took off her clothes and ran wild and frantically in the mountains all day, naked, and became the first woman to pursue environmentalism in the process of world civilization.
The emperor misses his daughter so much that he has sent people to the mountains to search for her many times. However, as soon as the mountain search team is dispatched, either in the wind or rain, or the ground shakes, it is impossible to get in at all.
The daughter of the emperor had excellent fertility. “After three years,” the “Book of the Later Han Dynasty” says, “After three years, she gave birth to 12 children. “Six men and six women”. If a princess is considered a human, then she can only give birth three times at most in three years, which means she has to have an average of quadruplets every time she gives birth. This is no joke.
After Pan Yi's death, there were still no outsiders in the place where his descendants lived, so the siblings of six men and six women had no choice but to “reproduce very closely.” They cut wood bark, weave weeds for clothing, and use colored plants to dye these materials in a variety of colors. These people all leave a tail on their finished clothes. It's probably a dog tail on a commemorative plate.
Later, Emperor Yi, the crazy and insane daughter, went back to the Central Plains and told her father about her difficult life. The emperor immediately sent someone to pick up these grandchildren and granddaughters. However, the descendants of Panjiu, who are dressed in strange clothes, are completely different from the Huaxia people because they have lived in a closed environment since childhood. They are used to climbing mountains and crossing mountains, yet they don't walk around the river, and even the language they communicate is completely different. There was no choice but to respect their living habits and give them a large mountainous lake south of the Central Plains. These people have since multiplied there and are known as barbarians.
Although these things recorded in history books are absurd, the purpose is to show that the various ethnic minorities in southern China are actually descendants of the emperor's daughter.
Until now, legends about Panyi are still circulating among ethnic minorities such as Yao and Yao (meaning “luxury”) in southern China. They have preserved “Ancestral Map” (that is, Gu Huang Shi Tu) and “Gou Huang Song”. They believe in “dogs” as their ancestors, and “stone dogs” have become totem symbols.
