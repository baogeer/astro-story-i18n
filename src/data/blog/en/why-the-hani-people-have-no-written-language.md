 There are two folklore legends about the reason the Hani people don't have words. First, the Hani people didn't have words in the first place; later, a brave young man followed Han and Dai youth to search for words. After finding the text, the Han people wrote the text on paper, the Dai people wrote the text on Baye, and the young Hani wrote the text on cowhide. On their way home, they experienced floods and rivers skyrocketed. When crossing the river, the paper, bay leaves, and cowhide with the letters on them were all soaked, so the 3 people burned the fire and baked. As a result, the paper and bay leaves were dried, but the cowhide was cooked. The 3 people filled their stomachs with cowhide, so the Hani characters were eaten. Second, the Hani people originally had words, but they were set on fire by a villain, so the Hani script did not spread.
In addition, there are also many fables, life stories, myths, legends, and mythological stories circulating among the Hani people, which reflect from different aspects the will and desire of the Hani ancestors to understand, understand, and accumulate experience with natural phenomena, conquer nature, and promote social development during production struggles and social development in ancient society.
