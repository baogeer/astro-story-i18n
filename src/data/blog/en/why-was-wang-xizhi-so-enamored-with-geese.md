 In the history of calligraphy in China, “calligrapher” Wang Xizhi has learned many talents, studied his posture, and introduced new ideas. He changed the simple style of calligraphy from the Han and Wei period. The real books were compact in shape, thick and well-folded, and the handwriting was beautiful and energetic. He also created a beautiful and dynamic new form, which was admired by writers of all time.
Wang Xizhi is particularly fond of geese, and the story “Writing Sutras for Goose” is widely read. Legend has it that a Taoist in Sanin raised many good geese. One day, Wang Xizhi passed by the Taoist and saw a group of geese floating leisurely on the surface of the water. Their snow-white feathers reflected the high red roof, which was very appealing, so he asked the Taoist to sell the geese to him. But the Taoist smiled and said, “My geese are not for sale.” If you could write a copy of “Huang Ting Jing” for me, then you don't need a penny; all of these geese are yours. Wang Xizhi gladly promised. It took half a day to write “Huang Ting Jing,” and finally returned home with a goose.
Similar stories are also recorded in “Jin Shu: The Biography of Wang Xi” and “Taiping Imperial Tour.” Why does Wang Xizhi love geese so much? There are historical opinions that Wang Xizhi was born into a Taoist family. As a Taoist, he “practiced and ate with the Taoist Xu Mai, and collected medicinal stones a thousand miles away” in order to seek immortality. However, geese have the function of relieving erysipelas in traditional Chinese medicine, so they have always been taken seriously by Taoists. Wang Xizhi's “Writing Sutras for Geese” wanted to eat geese to nourish his body, and this was also the purpose of the Sanin Taoist raising geese.
However, many scholars studying calligraphy throughout the ages have pointed out that the real reason Wang Xizhi loved geese was to observe the goose's movements. Because of the goose's beautiful shape and posture, it greatly inspired his writing and handling methods, as well as the formation of his calligraphy style. Bao Shichen, a famous calligrapher from the Qing dynasty, analyzed in “The Art Boat Double Song”: “It must be written. The index finger must have a high hook. The big finger is placed between the index finger and middle finger to make the index finger curved like a goose; the middle finger is attached to the outer distance of the ring finger, such as the one with two hands of a goose that repels water. Therefore, the Right Army (Wang Xizhi's former official worshiped General Youjun, therefore also known as 'Wang Youjun') loves geese and is playing with their two hands on the water.”
In fact, Wang Xizhi was able to figure out the meaning of running the pen every minute through the beautiful posture of a goose swimming in the water, and appreciate the beauty of the lines in calligraphy, such as when writing, like a goose head holding a fine song, and pouring water with the palm of a goose when writing. Take “The Order of the Lanting Collection” as an example. The writing style of the 21 “Zhi” characters evolved according to the posture of a goose. As a result, Bao Shichen summed up the relationship between Wang Xizhi's studies and raising geese in a poem: “If you put all your energy to the extreme, you will be safe for both feet first. I realized that it would be difficult to do all five fingers when I realized that it would be difficult to do the same thing as a group of geese.” Based on this, posterity also summed up the calligraphy song tips for “Goose Xiang Shu, the pen is soft; the pen is amazing; the goose sword sounds, the pen is amazing; the goose song, the pen is amazing; the goose song, the pen's wisdom...”
This explanation of why Wang Xizhi loves geese is now widely accepted. Therefore, Wang Xizhi's calligraphy, “drifting like a floating cloud, like a dragon leaping at the Tianmen Gate, the tiger and the phoenix,” is smooth and chic, and has reached the level of independence and perfection. Not only is he good at drawing on the essence of Wei Jin's calligraphy, getting rid of the pattern of writing, painstaking research, and being brave in innovation, it is also inseparable from his careful observation and understanding of the goose's position and behavior. (Zhou Huibin)
