 Wu Chengen, Zi Ruzhong, is a Sheyang Ju Shi, from Shanyang County, Huai'an Province (present-day Huai'an District, Huai'an City, Jiangsu Province). Hu Shi and Lu Xun examined him as the author of “Journey to the West.” And Wu Cheng'en's roots with Beijing are probably not well known — he has visited Beijing three times to “apply for a job.”
In the 8th year of Jiajing (1529), Wu Chengen went to study at Longxi Academy in Huai'an to seek fame, but his academic performance was repeatedly thwarted. It wasn't until the 28th year of Jiajing (1549) that he was recommended by Jiangnan University Administration to study at Nanjing Guozijian. The following year (1550), he obtained “Gong Sheng” (second ranking), so he went to Beijing to “ask for election” (go to the Ministry of Public Affairs to be elected to the post).
At the beginning of May, Wu Chengen boarded a boat from Xiagu Town in Huai'an to Beijing along the Grand Canal. This is his first time in Beijing. Wu Chengen walked and stopped along the way, enjoying the scenery along the way, climbing Mount Taishan in Shandong, visiting Yangliuqing in Tianjin, and visiting Tongzhou City in Shuntianfu before finally arriving in Beijing. From time to time, friends such as Li Chunfang and Shen Kun entertain me. Li Chunfang joined Jiajing in the 26th year (1547) and has been in a relationship with Wu Chengen for a long time. Shen Kun and Wu Chengen are fellow countrymen and friends, and were in the 20th year of Jiajing (1541).
At the beginning of June, after Wu Cheng-en entered Beijing, “seeking election” did not go very well. After all, only “Gong Sheng's” “education” was very different from the “education” of “Gong Shi” and “Jinshi,” so there were no results for two months.
In August of this year, the “Gengxu Incident” occurred in Beijing. That is, the remnant forces of the Yuan dynasty defeated in northern Moke. Under the leadership of Tatar's Tumut leader, more than 100,000 iron riders drove straight in and attacked Gubeikou from Jizhen on the 16th. As a result, they invaded Beijing Division, approached Anding Gate, and besieged Beijing for three days. Master of the Ming dynasty, Qiu Luan, adopted a “strong wall” policy and left it up to me to plunder the Beijing suburbs. I answered that Khan grew up after being plundered. This brought a crisis to Beijing, and also had a great impact on Wu Chengen's search for a job.
It was so easy to wait until things got a bit of an eye, and Wu Cheng-en suddenly received a letter from his hometown, making him feel like a thunder — his 15-year-old son Wu Feng Mao died. Wu Cheng-en was a single person for three generations. The attack made it unbearable for him, so he needed to return to his hometown of Huai'an as soon as possible.
Before leaving, he wrote the poem “Master Geng Xu Yu Beijing is forced to return to his intention to present one or two confidants”:
The origin of the world is ready to be tasted, and Ou Xinning has returned to his travels.
Even though Somirong Fang Shuo is not necessarily an elephant's length.
There was a shocking amount of traffic dust in June, and the village was surrounded by dream mountains for a long time.
The Pearl was gifted without any return; I never forget it when wearing a jubu.
In the 41st year of Jiajing (1562), Wu Chengen entered Beijing for the second time to “stand for election”, but he has always been “elected” by the Ministry of Government. My friend Li Chunfang's parents came to Beijing in the spring of this year to coincide with Li Chunfang's father's 70th birthday. Wu Chengen attended Li Chunfang's birthday party for his father and wrote “Ode to the Yuan Shou” to celebrate his birthday. However, for Wu Cheng-en, the most important matter during his time in Beijing was to go to the Ministry of Public Affairs to explore “seeking election.” The Ministry of Public Affairs is one of the “six departments” of the central administration of the Ming and Qing Dynasties. Its former site is on the south side of East Chang'an Street, roughly the location of the National Museum of China today.
According to rumor, while in Beijing, Oh Seung-eun visited Gyeonggi many times, visited Seosan Daikaksa Temple, Sifangpuksa Temple (now Hyangsan Wofo Temple), and also visited Zen at Yushenggyo Temple in Tongzhou.
Yushengjiao Temple, commonly known as “Pagan”, is located in the northern part of Tongzhou, on the west bank of the Beijing-Hangzhou Grand Canal. It is famous for its lantern-burning Buddha tower, and is an iconic building in the Beijing section of the Grand Canal. The tower was built during the Northern Zhou period (577-581), and was rebuilt in the 7th year of the Tang Dynasty (633). Since then, large-scale repairs were carried out in the 20th year of Ming and Chenghua (1484), and exquisite brick carvings are placed around the seat of the Sumi seat, and round statues of the Rexus are placed at each corner. Some are armored and wield swords, some have good intentions, and others are vicious. Each statue is beautifully crafted. Among them is a statue of a pig. It has fat ears, a huge mouth, and a large nose. The image is very realistic, reminiscent of the pig Bajie from “Journey to the West.” There have always been many opinions about the origin of the image of Pig Bajie in “Journey to the West.” In ancient Chinese mythology, pigs are one of the important totems; in Buddhism, Morishitan's mount is a wild boar; in the Taoist system, “Marshal Tianpeng” evolved from the “Nine Gods of the Beidou” appeared during the Tang and Song dynasties. This statue of a pig burning a lighthouse can also provide a reference for us to explore Pig Hakkai.
After being stranded in Beijing for a period of time, Wu Cheng-en was increasingly struggling and had no choice but to leave Beijing.
Wu Chengen visited Beijing for the third time in the year 43 (1564), and stayed in Beijing for over a year. After initial examination and reexamination, Wu Chengen was finally appointed by the imperial court in the 45th year of Jiajing (1566), he was honored with famous scholars and academics at the time. He was the sole governor of Changxing County (now Huzhou City, Zhejiang Province), and Wu Chengen served as the mayor of Changxing County (Zheng Bapin, assisting Zhi County and managing matters relating to food management and Malaysian taxation), so he left the southern part of Beijing to take office. At this point, Wu Cheng-en was already old, and the years of running back and forth between north and south finally had a relatively happy ending. (Torikihei)
