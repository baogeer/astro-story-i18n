 Author: Liu Jixing
Above the history of youth, there are no surprises.
One day during the Guangxu years in the late Qing Dynasty, an unclothed beggar begged along the street in Tangyi County, Shandong Province. I saw that he had words in his mouth, rapping and singing, sometimes dressed up as a pig or dog, and sometimes learned to bark. The beautiful performance and playful vocals made onlookers burst into laughter. There is a sentence like this in his lyrics: “I beg, I save money, learn to teach myself to be poor.” Many onlookers shook their heads: “Beggars still want to practice philanthropy. Isn't that an idiot's dream?”
Viewers should be aware that this simple but thrilling scene is not from a martial arts novel, nor is this beggar a master helper like Prince Hong Qigong of Bei Ga. His name was Wu Xun. He was an ordinary beggar at the bottom of Chinese society at the time. However, it was this very common beggar, who was feared by future generations for his extraordinary life, and had a great influence on modern Chinese culture and education.
This beggar, named Wu Xun, relied on begging. After more than 30 years of unremitting efforts, he built three schools, purchased more than 300 acres of school fields, and accumulated 10,000 yuan of capital to run schools. This is a unique event in the history of education in China and the world. The “Qing Historical Manuscripts” proclaim the original records, “I have not declared the facts of the Yigai martial arts training to the Historical Museum in Dangyi, in order to accumulate and study.” The story of Wu Xun was later compiled into the “Qing History Manuscripts” section 286, section on filial piety. In Chinese history, the only person recorded in official history as a beggar was Wu Xun.
Wu Xun is a native of Wuzhuang (present-day Guan County) in Tangyi County, Shandong Province in the late Qing Dynasty. Like Ah Q written by Lu Xun, they are all poor people “so poor that they don't even have a name”. They are famous for ranking 7th among siblings in the same family, so they are famous for being Wu Seventh. The name Wu Xun was actually a given name. When he was old, the imperial court gave him the name “Xun” in order to reward him for his acts of righteous education.
Mr. Leung Kai-chiu once wrote “An Outline for the Development of Learning” for Wu Xun. The Gijuku which praises Wu Xun “practiced for decades, and the number of disciples who graduated is innumerable”, and he always “lived for the rest of his life with two dollars of steamed buns a day.” General Feng Yuxiang praised martial arts as “a problem for a long time.” Feng Yuxiang also called for “running a large number of free schools; urgent priority is paramount,” and founded 15 martial arts elementary schools in Shandong between 1932 and 1935.
Tao Xingzhi founded Yucai School and Zhang Boling founded Nankai School all had a lot to do with the influence of the martial arts spirit. Mr. Tao Xingzhi's short poem “Ode to Wu Xun” summarizes Wu Xun's life. The poem says, “At dusk, be happy and happy. Throughout my life, I've been running all over the place. For the sake of a poor child, be a camel. It's beneficial to people, and cows do it too. Gong has no backbone, and he doesn't have many friends. Uneducated, overtaken by the top spot. The crowd knelt down, and the stubborn stone turned the steering wheel. Don't buy property, don't marry a wife. Learn, learn, learn for the sake of a big deal.” Martial arts training programs not only have a high reputation at home, but they also have a certain influence abroad. He is included in the “World Education Dictionary” and because he has no culture, he is called a “silent educator” and a “civilian educator.”
After suffering from illiteracy, I started the idea of running a charity school
In the 18th year of Qing Daoguang (1838), Wu Xun was born in Wuzhuang, Tangyi County, Shandong (now Guan County). His family was poor since childhood. His father died when he was seven, making his life even more difficult. As a young child, he begged for a living with his mother. Although Wu Xun is young, he is very filial to his mother. Whenever he wants clean and tasty dry food, he always takes it back to his mother to eat. He never refuses to eat it himself. He is very sensible.
Every time he passes by the school with his mother, young Wu Qi has to stop for a long time. He is always deeply attracted to the loud reading inside. How eager he is to be able to read. However, at the time, due to his family circumstances, going to school was just an unattainable dream. One day, he summoned up the courage to break into the academy and asked a private school teacher to allow him to study for free. The private school teacher not only disliked him, but instead abused him and kicked him out of the house.
At the age of 15, Wu Xun came to work at his aunt, Boss Zhang's house. My unscrupulous aunt didn't give him minor preferential treatment because they were relatives. Instead, he made him work harder, but never gave him any wages, and often bullied him when everything was fine. Little Wu Xun put up with all this. At the age of 17, Wu Xun went to Li Juren's family to work as a permanent worker. One day, my sister sent a letter with a few dollars. Li Juren bullied Wu Xun and was illiterate, sent the letter to him, and swallowed up the money. After the Wu Xun, I learned that the question had been raised. Li Juren not only categorically denied it, but also scolded Wu Xun. While feeding pigs once, Wu Xun accidentally spilled the pig's food on the ground, and he was also beaten all over his body. On New Year's Eve of the year, Wu Xun posted Spring Festival couplets to his host. Because he was illiterate, he put the Spring Festival couplets upside down. The owner thought it was very unlucky. He punched and kicked, and made noise and scolding. He was not allowed to eat, punished him for not sleeping all night, and stood in the yard all night in the wind, snow, and cold.
Wu Xun worked at Li Juren's family for three years, and Li Juren never paid him a salary. Once, Wu Xun's mother got sick and was so helpless that he asked his owner for a salary. Unexpectedly, Li Juren came up with a fake ledger and insisted that his wages had already been paid. Wu Xun was illiterate, dazed, and angry. Instead, he was falsely accused by Li Juren of intentional blackmail. In the end, Wu Xun was beaten to the head by Li Juren's family and swept away from the ground.
This incident hit him too hard. After being hurt, Wu Xun slept in a small temple in Zhuangzi for three days. When he woke up, he was pained and understood that the reason he had been so humiliated was because he was illiterate. However, there are still many poor people like him around him. If they don't study, they will always be bullied for no reason. As a result, he developed the idea of starting a free school, so that people can read and read, and no longer be deceived.
Walk and sing, and be happy to raise funds
However, running a free school in abject poverty has never been heard of; the difficulty is imaginable. However, Wu Xun is very confident in this, and he is determined to achieve this great dream through a lifetime of hard work and perseverance. In 1859, at age 21, Wu Xun began begging to raise funds, opening a new page in his life. He covered his body with a bronze spoon with his hands, sang while walking, begged everywhere, and traveled all over Shandong, Hebei, Henan, Jiangsu, etc. This beggar, who will later be famous in the history of education for many years, began an extraordinary life of begging and raising funds to run a school.
While begging, he sings songs he wrote himself. The lyrics are like poetry, but they have positive content and a certain charm. All of the content is related to the establishment of philology. No matter what others ask or laugh at, he sings a song; whether at work or at rest, he sings happily. If he takes life and is being deceived, it's better to ask for food with himself. Don't watch me ask for food, and go to a liberal arts college in the morning and evening.
He sold his labor everywhere, worked hard and rushed to work, and lived a cowardly lifestyle with the aim of saving money to run a school. In the hard work done by livestock, martial arts training did not take suffering; they also happily sang:
Dung out, weed, and pull a fork to find it; it doesn't matter how much money it costs.
Give me the money, I'll save the field, and it won't be difficult to study a free school.
Being a mule and being a cow, don't worry about learning righteousness.
Who knows, the kind martial arts training will be deceived again. A year later, Wu Xun's hard-earned money was defrauded by his brother-in-law. Wu Xun was so angry that he couldn't get enough water or rice, and he was heartbroken. A few days later, he calmed down again and sang in style: I only saw good people building tall buildings, no bullies to the end.
Some people mocked him for harming him for being an “unscrupulous person,” and he sang bluntly and answered, “I have met people, paid respect, rewarded money, saved my life, and been unable to move for ten thousand years.”
When I want to eat, I inevitably run into people who are stingy and don't give anything. Wu Xun sings in a polite manner: “Don't give it to me, I don't complain; I have good people to take care of my meals.” When he was harshly abused, he didn't get angry and sang a song to the effect: “Don't get angry, grandpa and uncle, when are you not angry; I'll leave at what time.”
Wu Xun saved up all the money he wanted, sold all the dry food he wanted, and saved it for money. I only eat poor, moldy food, vegetable roots, sweet potato sticks, etc., and sing my voice with small songs:
If you eat groceries, you can use it as a meal, and save money to study at a charity school.
Eating well is not good; studying philology is good.
I don't know how much suffering Wu Xun has suffered during those years. He enjoyed himself from day to night, tirelessly doing those tiring tasks that others were unwilling or disdainful of. Such as grinding, grinding rice, cutting wheat for others, etc. In addition, they also clean the huts early in the morning and use fertilizer after drying the feces. Sometimes they also help people pick up water to water the garden, pick food, pick heavy things, etc., and calculate rewards based on distance and weight. The income is quite impressive. In this way, they accumulate the funds needed to run a free school day by day.
In order to earn some money to run a charity school, he sometimes goes to various temple fairs and bazaars like a juggler to get money. They perform “carrying a tripod” by standing upside down with their hands and feet as “scorpion climbing,” turning over and jumping on the “wheel,” lying on the ground to make horses and horses for children, and even eating crow, snakes, and scorpions, and swallowing stones and rubble. It's really not easy to practice one's own body like this; everything is for the purpose of starting a free school! He also cut off his braids, left only a small braid on the corner of his forehead, and dressed up like a clown in the movie to get alms from others.
In addition, he also works as a mediator, works as a postman, picks up trash, rolls cotton, spun threads, etc. Wu Xun wandered all over the place, worked, wanted food, and adrift. At night, I slept in someone's mill, stove, or broken temple. Every day late at night, he twists the rope under a bean-like light, getting tangled up in twists and turns. While merit, he sang:
Pick up the thread, wrap the egg, and work hard to build a charity school;
Don't worry about twisting the eggs, connecting the wires, and fixing the science.
At the age of 29, Wu Xun had already saved up some savings. He used these savings to buy 45 acres of cheap low-lying saline-alkali land and happily sang:
As long as it is up to me to learn, I'm not afraid to buy alkali sand when buying land; the alkali will recede, the sand will scrape, and there will be no alkali sand after three years.
As long as I should learn, I'm not afraid of getting a big pit if I want the land; the water is flowing, the soil is wet, and the pit will be leveled after three years.
When Wu Xun was 38 years old, there was a major drought in Shandong, and quite a few people starved to death. Wu Xun used his own money to buy 40 tons of sorghum to help the people. Wu Xun's older brother doesn't do his job properly. He often borrows money from him. Some relatives and friends also come to him to ask for funding. Wu Xun refused all of them. He answered correctly: regardless of his family, whatever the circumstances, he studied for me several times.
A couple of lonely mothers-in-law and daughters-in-law in the village made a living by demanding food, but their kind-hearted martial arts training generously gave them ten acres of land, and also sang:
This person is good, this person is nice. It's not enough to give her ten acres.
This person's filial piety, this man's filial piety, gave her ten acres for her old age.
I have never been married in my whole life, and I have set up three charity schools
As the saying goes: Gather your armpits into a bow, and gather sand into a tower. After years of hard work, martial arts training has finally accumulated a small amount of money and has saved a considerable amount of money. He heard that there is a person named Yang Shufang in this county (Tangyi County, Shandong). He is honest, has a good reputation, and is trustworthy. He wanted to keep his money in Yang's family, so he went to Yangfu to find out. Since he was a beggar, the owner refused to see him, so he knelt down at the gate for two days, and finally moved Yang Juren. Wu Xun tearfully recounted the original story of begging to save money and Xing Yi Xue. Yang Shufang was deeply moved by this. Not only did he promise to help him save money, but he also said he wanted to help him run a school and fulfill his long-cherished wish.
In the 12th year of Guangxu (1886), Wu Xun was 49 years old. He had already built 230 acres of land and accumulated more than 3,800 cranes, and decided to establish a free school. In the 13th year of Guangxu (1887), two enlightened landowners admired martial arts training and jointly donated land for free use in martial arts training. Wu Xun began to go to various places to buy bricks, tiles, and wood, and personally escort them. After construction began, Wu Xun woke up early and went to bed late every day, moved bricks to fetch water on the construction site, and worked together with the workers.
In the 14th year of Guangxu (1888), Wu Xun spent more than 4,000 dollars to build the first charity school outside the East Gate in Liulin Town, Tangyi County, and was named “Chongxian Gijuku”. Wu Xun spent a full 30 years to achieve his ideals. In these 30 years, he suffered all the hardships, but he always steadfastly moved towards his goals step by step. After the “Chongxian Gijuku” was built, Wu Xun personally invited learned mentors and others to teach, and asked Yang Shufang to act as student director, preside over the Gijuku, and kneel and ask poor people to send their children to school. More than 50 students were enrolled in the same year, divided into separate classes and economics classes, and there were no tuition fees. On the first day of school, a rich feast was prepared to entertain the students, teachers, and the townspeople, but Wu Xun himself bowed his head and thanked the guests. He resolutely refused to attend; he only ate some leftover soup to fill his stomach after the banquet.
After the establishment of Gijuku, Wu Xun realized his wish, but he still begged for a living and still lived in a broken temple. The students collectively begged him to stay at Gijuku, but he refused, saying, “I don't feel any pain in my life. As long as you study hard, I'm happier than anything else.” There was a strong wind one day, and the tiles from the temple house came off and fell on Wu Xun's head, breaking his head and bloodshed, but he leisurely sang, “Break my head, catch fire, learn the cause.”
Wu Xun, the founder of Yixue, is also very concerned about the study situation of the students in Yixue. From time to time, Wu Xun often kneels down to thank the teachers who are diligent in teaching; for students who are playful and don't study seriously, he cries and exhorts: “Studying is useless; I don't have a face when I go home to see my parents and siblings.” Early one morning, all the students had arrived, yet the teachers hadn't woken up yet. Wu Xun quietly walked into the teacher's bedroom, kneeling silently in front of his bed, and couldn't stop crying. After the teacher woke up, Wu Xun said, “The teacher is sleeping. The students are making a fuss. I'm kneeling and asking. It's been 100 years.” Another school teacher took a leave of absence and went home after the due date. Wu Xun walked 60 miles to the teacher's house and waited alone outside the door all night. The teachers of the school were so ashamed that they didn't dare to exceed the deadline. The teachers and students were moved by the sincerity of Wu Xun. None of them had a moment of negligence or slack. The academic style was very good, and the teaching was excellent as a result.
Zhang Yao, who was the governor of Shandong at the time, heard about Wu Xun's acts of charity and specifically summoned them. Wu Xun walked to Jinan Province in a ragged manner. At the time of the meeting, Wu Xun laughed and talked with Zhang Guan while constantly twisting the line. The governor was greatly moved by his honesty and simplicity. He ordered an exemption from the military, money, food, and military service, and donated an additional two hundred and two silver dollars. At the same time, he asked Emperor Guangxu to present the “Good Kindness and Good Deeds” plaque. Qing Ting awarded him the title “Yi Xuezheng” and admired the yellow horse coat. This was a supreme honor, but in the face of Chincha, Wu Xun was unwilling to kneel down and thank goodness, nor did he want to wear a yellow coat, saying, “Yi Xuezheng, no need to seal it. It's useless to wear a yellow coat. You can't move after studying righteousness for ten thousand years.”
In the 16th year of Guangxu (1890), Wu Xun funded the certified monk 230 yuan, and founded the second charity school in Yangerzhuang, which is now Linqing City.
In the 19th year of Guangxu (1893), Wu Xun collected and purchased a large number of useful books, and set up a reading club for people who had no money to buy books to freely borrow them. Sometimes he also brings books to the village and town's bazaars and temple fairs for the townspeople to read. A large number of simple study articles and books are also reprinted and distributed free of charge to farmers. In the same year, imperial court officials, undergraduate servant Yude, visited Shandong, and Wu Xun stopped cars on the street to raise money. Yude donated 200 dollars to him.
In the 22nd year of Guangxu (1896), Wu Xun spent 3,000 yuan to set up a third charity school in Linqing Yushi Alley, named “Yushi Alley Yijiu” (that is, today's “Wu Xun Experimental Primary School” in Linqing, a model school in Shandong Province).
Wu Xun set up charity school wholeheartedly. In order to avoid the burdens of his wife's office, he never married a wife or bought a family, and lived a hard life. He couldn't bear to spend any money on himself, and gave everything to run a charity school. He became famous in his later years and won wide respect.
Shortly after the “Imperial History Alley Gijuku” was built, Wu Xun accumulated work and became seriously ill, but he refused to take up his room and was resting under the roof of Gijuku. Half a month later, on April 23, the 22nd year of Guangxu (1896), Wu Xun passed away laughing while reading a book. At the age of 58, he was buried next to Liulin Sohyeon Gijuku in accordance with his will. On the day of the funeral, people from various villages 60 miles along the road spontaneously set up a memorial service, and tens of thousands of people were automatically sent to the funeral. There was a crowd of spectators along the way. The teachers and students were crying, and ordinary people burst into tears.
Ten years after Wu Xun's death, Qing Ting proclaimed his achievements to the National History Museum and built tombs, temples, and monuments for him. Wu Xun's performance was admired by the world. Many famous people wrote inscriptions, and Liu Jixing examined it. At that time, there were many schools named after martial arts training across the country. In the Republic of China period, Mr. He Siyuan, then director of the Shandong Education Department, funded the reconstruction of the martial arts training hall to commemorate the martial arts training of the famous “civilian educator” and “Yi Xuezheng” in the late Qing dynasty. He Siyuan also erected a white marble statue of Zun Wu Xun at the Wu Xun Temple. This statue is slightly larger than a real person. According to local people who have seen martial arts training after liberation, all said that this statue was similar to Wu Xun himself. Unfortunately, the statue was destroyed by the Red Guards during the Cultural Revolution. In 1932, the then Chairman of Shandong Province Han Fuyi built the “Wu Gong Memorial Hall” and built two “Wu Gong Memorial Hall” on both sides of the memorial to commemorate the martial arts training promoted in Linqing. A teacher training school in Nantong, Jiangsu also juxtaposed the statue of Wu Xun with the statue of Confucius. People in Shandong even call him a “martial artist.”
The “Xinhua Daily” hosted by the Communist Party of China also published articles praising martial arts. On December 1, 1945, Guo Moruo wrote the title of Wu Xun in the “Xinhua Daily” special issue commemorating martial arts training: “Wu Xun is China's Pei Stolozi, and the Chinese people should put up bronze statues for him everywhere.” To commemorate martial arts training, the Communist Party of China's Jilu and Yubian District Government during the war of resistance ordered that Wu Xun's hometown, Tangyi County, be renamed “Wuxun County,” and Liulin Town in Tangyi County was renamed “Wuxun Town,” and various commemorative events were held on the anniversary of Wu Xun's birth. In the same year, the Southern Hebei Administration of the Communist Party of China also established martial arts training teachers in Liulin Town to commemorate this pioneer who made outstanding contributions to education in China.
Guo Moruo called Wu Xun “China's Fistéloch”. This Fistéloch is a European from almost the same generation as Wu Xun. He was born in Switzerland, which was still very poor and backward at the time. His grandfather used to be a missionary. Excellent church traditions taught him how to selflessly love people with sincerity and kindness from an early age. He has worked in a church orphanage all his life. Like Wu Xun, he is a lower-class person, and he also “has a great, loving heart.” He said, “I've always been a snubbed, weak-willed junior teacher, driving a book with only basic common sense, an empty unicycle, and unexpectedly getting involved in a career, including starting an orphanage, a teacher college, and a boarding school. It takes a lot of money to do these things the first year, but even one-tenth of that amount is hard for me to get”.
This beggar-like Fisterloch eventually popularized civilian education in Switzerland thanks to his lifelong efforts. Educational success made this poor and backward mountainous country become Europe's top educational superpower in the late 18th century and early 19th century. Switzerland's success has led education experts and senior politicians from various countries to study advanced educational experiences in Switzerland. Later, the West called Fisteroch a “Holy Spirit,” and praised him as a “Sacred Heart” and “Holy Virtue” for his noble spirit of devotion to education. Campier, a famous French educational historian, praised, “He was the earliest model in the development of human education to call for and practice 'education of love'.”
In 1951, the film “The Legend of Wu Xun” was wrongly criticized. Its tomb was destroyed during the “Cultural Revolution.” After settling the chaos, Wu Xun's grievances were finally brought to light: in 1986, the General Office of the State Council made a decision to restore the reputation of Wu Xun. In Guanxian County and Linqing in northwest Lu, there are quite a few schools named after “martial arts training”: Wu Xun High School in Guanxian County, Wu Xun School in Liulin Town, Guan County, and Wu Xun Experimental Primary School in Linqing. The land of China is blessed. People who have gone through the hardships of martial arts and are determined to dedicate themselves to education are immortal in the spirit of heaven and earth, shining with the sun and moon, and will always inspire future generations. (Liu Jixing)
