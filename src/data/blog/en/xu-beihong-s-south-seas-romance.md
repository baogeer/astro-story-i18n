 As we all know, during the War of Resistance against Japan, Xu Beihong visited Nanyang several times to hold exhibitions and raise funds in order to support China's war of resistance. In 1941, Xu Beihong traveled all over New Zealand and Malaysia to sell his works for charity. This period was also Xu Beihong's most creative stage, and many outstanding works were completed during this period.
Xu Beihong mainly stayed in Penang during this period. He stayed at the Nanguo Hotel in the city. Because of its moderate location and good conditions, the guests at the Nanguo Hotel were full of visitors. Also, according to the interview notes of Ouyang Xingyi from Singapore with Fang Jiaxun, who worked as a junior secretary in the hotel during his time, Fang recalled that Xu Beihong was very kind. He woke up very early every day and started painting after washing his face and brushing his teeth. After I finished drawing, I used the remaining ink to practice calligraphy. Xu Beihong likes red bean paste from Penang and also loves hot pot, so he never gets tired of it. After the meal, Xu Beihong was always in a hurry to pay. He always said, “He just sold a painting; of course, he invited guests.
Also, according to the book “Bei Hong in the Stars” recently published by Ouyang Xingyi, it also records Xu Beihong's little-known love affair in Nanyang.
“While in Penang, a 17-year-old girl from Penang broke into Xu Beihong's world, and the artillery fire of the Pacific War destroyed this relationship, leaving nothing but endless emotion.
When Xu Beihong came to Xinma, her relationship with Jiang Biwei had already broken down, and her marriage had failed. Inevitably, she felt a bit lost. Although he had a strong foundation in his heart at the time — first, he started an art exhibition to raise funds to support China's war of resistance — but human feelings were complicated, and in his heart, in addition to fiery patriotism, he could also accommodate romance between men and women. He cares about and cares for his friends, and also feels he should have someone as his partner to take care of his daily life.
Several seniors who had close relationships with Xu Beihong back then said that Xu Beihong also had his own considerations. At the time, he was invited by the US China Aid Association to hold an art exhibition and donate the proceeds from painting sales to the Chinese people in the war of resistance and refugee relief. Xu Beihong is fluent in French, but he doesn't speak English. He has language problems in the US. Therefore, he really needed someone who is fluent in English to be accompanied by him to act as his personal secretary and interpreter.
Later, a 17-year-old girl from Penang was introduced to Xu Beihong by a friend. She was educated in English since she was a child. Since her family members are educated in Chinese, they are also fluent in Chinese, can speak Chinese, and can also speak Minnan dialect, which is common in Singapore and Malaysia.
After meeting this young lady from Penang, Xu Beihong has been to many places with her. The relationship between the two is growing day by day, to the point where they are discussing marriage.
Several interviewees also said that Xu Beihong intended to marry this young girl from Penang and then settle down in Penang. But then war broke out, and all plans were disrupted. At the time, someone also advised Xu Beihong, saying that the two are very far apart in age and that they should carefully consider whether it is appropriate to form a husband and wife. However, this should be considered a “love” left by Master Xu in Penang.
This young girl, who once had a relationship with Xu Beihong, later married a businesswoman and has always lived in Penang. She is now a “grandmother's level” old woman. Looking back on my old dream, I don't want to talk to others about this “the song doesn't end up before it ends” old feelings.
There is more to this story. In May 1959, Jiang Biwei traveled from Taiwan to Penang and Singapore and stayed at Wong Manshi's home for about 10 days. According to her recollection, Wong Manshi's wife told her at the time that Xu Beihong met an overseas Chinese lady in Penang. Mr. Xu had been engaged to her and brought her to the Huang family. Both Mansi and his wife had met. The girl was both ordinary and unbeautiful, so Mrs. Wong urged Mr. Xu to cancel the original plan. Whether what Ms. Jiang said is true is probably difficult to confirm now.”
Some elders with close relationships with Xu Beihong provided more information about this relationship.
For example, Han Shan-won's “An Anecdote to Beihong Penang” and Chen Genji's “The Malaysian Poet Laureate Goes Through the Needle and Seo Beihong's Marriage” records the memories of Sun Shaoqing, a Malaysian poet and educator in his 80s: “When Beihong stayed at the Penang Nanguo Hotel in 1941, he often loved to chant Wang Wei's verses:
Red beans are raw in southern countries,
Sow a few branches in spring.
May you harvest more,
This is the most thoughtful thing.
Sun Shaoqing understood how Bei Hong's relationship had broken down at the time. With the help of her cousin, she arranged for Beihong to go on a blind date with a Miss Deng. When they met, Sun Shaoqing, his wife, Miss Deng, and her mother were there.
The two parties agreed and soon got engaged in a Buddhist ceremony at Gokuraku Temple in Penang. Guan Zhenmin, a literature and history teacher at Zhong Ling Middle School, acted as a witness and presided over the engagement ceremony. The abbot of Gokuraku Temple, the old monk Zhi Kun, presided over the Buddha ceremony to bless the couple, using a plain feast. The ceremony was attended by Sun Shaoqing, Luo Qingquan, and Miss Tang's parents. Wong Meng-gyu also arrived from Singapore. Xu Beihong used a valuable diamond ring as a token to prepare for an art exhibition in the US and then return to Penang to get married to Miss Deng.
Beihong and Miss Tang visited Singapore together to meet Wong Meng-gyu and Wong Man-shi. In Penang, Beihong also painted three horses in one day to thank Sun Shaoqing, Luo Qingquan, and Principals Zheng Jincun, and gave Sun Shaoqing two couplets:
Dinghies are perfect for solitude; big names often talk about it.
The past goes back and forth, and it has gone awry.
However, after the Pacific War broke out, Xu Beihong returned to China and wrote a letter to Luo Qingquan in Guilin saying that he was no longer able to return to Penang, so he decided to break the marriage contract.
Miss Deng's father, Deng Lao Bo, risked his life to hide Beihong's picture box on the ceiling of his home during the Japanese rule, and still hoped that Beihong would return to Penang.
