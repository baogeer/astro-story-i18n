 Xu Da, a native of Haozhou Zhong Li (present-day Fengyang, Anhui), was the founding military commander of the Ming dynasty, and one of the 24 generals in Huaixi.
Originally from a farmhouse, Xu Da was cautious and good at governing the military. Since joining the rebellion led by Zhu Yuanzhang, he spent his whole life defeating Chen Youliang, eliminating Zhang Shicheng, and overthrowing the rule of the Yuan dynasty, establishing immortal merit for the Ming dynasty. He can be called the founding hero of the Ming dynasty.
Major Achievements: As of the 28th year (AD 1)
(368), Xu Dahui's division marched from Shandong to Henan and captured Bianliang. In April, advancing west from Hulaoguan (northwest Xingyang, Henan), they destroyed 50,000 yuan troops led by Toin Temur in one fell swoop at Taerwan (border with Yanshuai County, Henan), and forced Yuanliang Tualuwen to surrender in Luoyang. Subsequently, Xu Da and the Spring Division often occupied the provinces of Song (Song County, Henan), Xu (Xuchang, Henan), Chen (Huaiyang, Henan), and You (Linru, Henan). Li Siqi and Zhang Sidao, the late Guards of Tongguan (Northeast Tongguan, Shaanxi), fled in unison. Xu Da and others sent troops to Tongguan and moved west to occupy Huazhou (Huaxian County, Shaanxi).
In May, Zhu Yuanzhang arrived to oversee Bian Liang to discuss the next battle plan with General Zhu. Based on the military situation where Yuan Ting's foreign aid has been exhausted, Xu Da suggested taking advantage of the momentum to directly take Yuandu. Zhu Yuanzhang agreed. In July, Xu Da led an army across the Yellow River from Zhongjiu (southwest Fengqiu, Henan), and conquered Weihui (Jixian, Henan), and Cizhou (Cixian, Hebei), and turned to Linqing to meet with the various Ming armies in Shandong. They continued north along the canal, defeating more than 10,000 people from the Yuan Army in Hexi (Wuqing, Tianjin) and Tongzhou (Tongxian, Beijing), and invaded Dadu. Emperor Yuan Shun heard the news and led his wife, children, wife, wife, and attendants to secretly open the Jiandemun Gate in most cities on the night of the 27th, and fled in the direction of Juyong Pass and fled to the “Shangdu.” On August 2, the Ming army occupied the capital, and the Yuan dynasty was overthrown. The Yuan dynasty has since completely withdrawn from the historical stage.
