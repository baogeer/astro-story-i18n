 Yongtai Temple is located at the foot of Mount Taimuro, about 11 km northwest of Dengfeng city in Henan Province. Facing Shaolin Temple, it is backed by the imposing Wangdu Peak (named because you can see Luoyang, the ancient capital of the nine dynasties), the beautiful Zijin Peak in the north (named because Zhou Ling Wang Taizjin lived here), and Shaolin Reservoir with ten thousand valleys in the south. It is surrounded by mountains, verdant mountains, lush trees, streams linger, pagodas, and the scenery is quiet. The Tengyan Tourist Highway in front of the temple is 1 km southbound and connected to National Highway 207, so transportation is convenient. Eitai-ji Temple was the first temple for female monks built after the introduction of Zen Buddhism into China, and it is also the earliest existing Buddhist temple for nuns in China. It is the first batch of provincial key cultural relics protection units in Henan Province.
Yongtai Temple was founded in Northern Wei. It was originally known as Minglian Temple; it changed its name to Yongtai Temple in the Tang Dynasty; it changed its name to Eizen-ji Temple in the Jin Dynasty. After the Yuan Dynasty, it was also renamed Yongtai Temple and became Yongtai Temple. There are also people who call Eitai-ji Temple Eitai-an or Eitai-in Temple. So how did the name Eitaiji come about? What's the difference between a temple and a hermitage?
In the 2nd year of Zhengguang in Northern Wei (521), Princess Yongtai, the daughter of Emperor Xuanwu, “wanted to become a nun” in Songshan, Zhongyue. In his history, Emperor Xiaoming claimed to have built a nun temple next to the Minglian Pagoda under Zijin Peak, so that his younger sister, Princess Yongtai, serve the Buddha and preach the Dharma, and rejoice over 100 women in the family of ordinary people. The temple is named Minglian Temple because it was built next to the tower of Princess Lian, the daughter of Emperor Liang Wu of the southern dynasty and one of the four disciples of Dharma, the founder of Chinese Buddhism.
The name Eitai-ji Temple first appeared on July 25, the 2nd year (706) of Tang Zhongzong Li Xianshen, when the Dowina of Songyue Temple hated the Supreme Court of Daoying, indicating that Renji Temple “has mountains and water, is superior in shape and quiet; it is easy to live without multiple workers.” After the renovation was completed, Myourenji Temple went through a total of 185 years from its establishment until it was renamed Eitai-ji Temple because “Heaven is the first one for Princess Yongtai, so there are 27 monks named after Yongtai...”
The Jin dynasty, Eitai-ji Temple, or Eizen-ji Temple, is a sign that the monks flaunted themselves as the true line of Buddhist Zen. Since the Northern Song Dynasty, people were deeply influenced by Cheng Zhuli studies and the social culture of “men are superior to women” gradually formed. Buddhist monks also say that female monks are “second monks” (meaning lower people are first class). This is an evil result of gender discrimination. The nuns at Eitai-ji Temple changed the name of the temple to Eizen-ji Temple as a way to fight against this prejudice. After the death of the famous monk Gyun-an of Eitai-ji Temple in the Golden Age, his disciples built a pagoda for him. The title of the pagoda was “Song Mountain Eizenji Temple Junan Master Pagoda Chronicles”.
After the Yuan and Ming dynasties, Eizen-ji Temple was also renamed Eitai-ji Temple. The lineage of Eitaiji nuns during this period was also mostly ranked according to Shaolin Temple's “70 character generation” order starting with Fu Yu in the first year of the Yuan Dynasty. Therefore, at this time, some nuns at Eitai-ji Temple claimed that Eitai-ji Temple was the lower house of Shaolin Temple, and called Eitai-ji Temple Eitai-in or Eitai-an. After the nuns died, they were also buried and built a pagoda in Taling Shaolin Temple. For example, in the 4th year of Yuan Zhi Zheng (1344), the pagoda was built in the Talin of Shaolin Temple. The tomb tower still exists today. The tomb tower still exists today.
During the Ming and Qing Dynasties, the name Eitai-in Temple or Yongtaian never appeared in monuments or tomb towers; they are all still used under the name Eitai-ji Temple or Yongtaizen-ji Temple.
According to folklore, Buddhist monks are “male monks residing in a temple and female monks staying in an hermitage.” Influenced by this, local people also often refer to Eitai-ji Temple as Eitai-an. Actually, the statement “male monks live in a temple, female monks live in an hermitage” is incorrect. So what's the difference between them?
Both the temple and the hermitage are places where Buddhist monks preach the law and live. Both male and female monks can live in historic temples and hermitages, and there are no strict boundaries. The so-called “hermitage”, in the literal sense of the word, is a small temple where monks live. However, in ancient times, it was a monk's humble statement about where they lived, which meant that no matter how luxurious or humble the house the monk lived in, when it came to outsiders, they humbly called it a maoan or pukan. Over time, the hermitage formed a fixed name. Men have lived historically, such as Chuso-an, Niso-an, Sanzoan, and Guanghui-an in the Songshan area. Therefore, “male monks live in a temple, female monks stay at a temple,” there is no basic saying; it is just a popular name.
Regarding the title of a female monk, it is commonly known as a female monk as a nun. Chemical Buddhism refers to female monks who have become priests as Bichouni; female disciples who believe in the Buddha but have not left the family, and who also have a legal name are youyi. The “Datang Zhongyue Yongtaiji Monument” says that Princess Yongtai “went out of his way to become a nun,” and that Eitai-ji Temple is a “nun temple.” This shows that in the Tang Dynasty, Eitai-ji Temple was already quite famous as a nunnery.
In history, Eitai-ji Temple has had two princesses, Ming Lian and Yongtai, born here. Its high standard is second to none in the history of Chinese Buddhism. In feudal society, it was not uncommon for members of the royal family to go out to serve the Buddha. For example, there were 10 empresses in Northern Zhou, all of whom believed in Buddhism. Among them, there are 6 people who are commonly known as Nepalese. Empress Li Qiu of Emperor Yu Wenyong, Empress Zhu of Emperor Xuan, the birth mother of Emperor Yu Wenyan, and Emperor Xuan's Empress Chen, Empress Yuan, and Empress Wei Chi all walked out of the Imperial Palace one after another and went to the monastery for Nepal. Since Princess Minglian and Princess Yongtai are the “children of the royal family,” there is no need to humbly claim in front of others that the temple they have lived in is a thatched cottage. Eitai-ji Temple has a history of more than 1,480 years since it was founded. They all claim to be a “temple,” and the Buddhist monks who have lived here for generations have all been Bichouni. This is clearly stated in historical documents relating to Eitai-ji Temple.
