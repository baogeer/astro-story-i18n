 In the Kokurayama area of Nanjing, Jiangsu, there used to be a famous garden to accompany the garden. It is bordered by Yuhuatai in the south, Mochou Lake in the southwest, Zhong Mountain on the north, and Chaotian Palace on the east. “In the meantime, the landscape is beautiful, the stairs are beautiful, the flowers and trees are rich, and the prayers are numerous. I can't remember them enough, let alone in poetry.”
The owner of Suiyuan called himself an old man of Suiyuan and wrote the famous “Suiyuan Poetry”. After his death, he was also buried in Suiyuan. His tomb is located on the 100step slope of Suijiacura in Nanling, Mount Kokura. He is Yuan Mei, a famous poet and essayist from the Qing Dynasty.
Yuan Mei (1716-1798), Zicai, a native of Qiantang, Zhejiang (present-day Hangzhou). He is gifted and intelligent. He entered the county school at age 12. In the fourth year of Qianlong (1739), Yuan Mei, 24, entered the exam, ranked 2nd and 5th. He was selected as a Shu Ji Ji of the Hanlin Academy, and became famous in Beijing for a while. After the “dissolution” of the Hanlin Institute, Yuan Mei left Beijing as an official and served as the prefectures of Lishui, Jiangpu, Shuyang, and Jiangning in Jiangsu.
Yuan Mei's status as a poet and essayist is so famous that people forget that he also had an official experience. Although Yuan Mei served as Zhi Xian for not long, she served the government with integrity, was diligent and conscientious, sensitive and capable, and did not shy away from the powerful. He knows that if one person or family goes through lawsuits for many years, it will inevitably lead to family setbacks. Therefore, regardless of whether the lawsuit is large or small, Yuan Mei will try the case as soon as it is, and the case will be judged immediately. He never delays and strives to be impartial and selfless, so as to save the people from being burdened by rhetoric.
Outside of Fangsanxi Cave in Jiangning, two families have been filing lawsuits for many years over the ownership of a plot of land. Because there is no land title, the ownership is unknown, and the dispute has not come to an end for many years, and the papers in the courts have already piled up like mountains. In order to save the two families from being burdened with lawsuits, Yuan Meibing of Zhi County in Jiangning at the time ruled that all previous judgments were rescinded, ordered people to re-measure the land, and also issued land deeds so that they could reclaim and cultivate their own land, and resolve the long-standing backlog of cases.
In order to clean up popular customs, Yuan Mei often summoned the village elders to inquire about the names of people with criminal records and stubborn people in the country, publicize them after verifying that everything was correct, and promised that if there were no misdeeds within three years, the names could be removed from the list. This practice certainly worked. As a result, “all adulterers have gone back down”. People who committed adultery were able to correct their past mistakes, and the social climate improved markedly.
Yuan Mei is an official and strict self-discipline. He believes that as an official, people around him should be strictly restrained. Only then can the public and the public get along well, and many official and social ills will naturally be eliminated. Yuan Mei's talent was appreciated by Liangjiang Governor Yin Jishan. The Qing dynasty scholar Lu Yiyi praised in “Miscellaneous Knowledge of Leng Lu”: “When he was slaughtered, he was diligent and clear, and he deserved to follow the law.”
Yuan Mei loves the people, and the people love Yuan Mei. He records in volume 10 of “An Addendum to the Garden Poems”: He knew Lishui when he was 27 years old. When he left office, “the people cried out. Some people covered themselves with '10,000 people's clothing'. They were brilliant, and they all had the names of people from the county.” Yuan Mei was moved by the farewell scene of “A Thousand Sons and Daughters Embroider Clothes All the Way”.
In the 10th year of Qianlong (1745), he was transferred from Shuyang to Zhi County in Jiangning. The local people also treated each other affectionately with “five steps to one glass of wine, ten steps to fold the willow.” Many years later, Yuan Mei still laments, “Gai Xian regards the people as a family; he is an official and cannot forget the place, nor can he forget it.” In other words, if officials treat ordinary people as their own loved ones, ordinary people will naturally also treat him as their loved ones, and they will miss him for a long time.
Yuan Mei spent her whole life being indifferent to fame and fortune, and moved forward and backward freely. In the 13th year of Qianlong (1748), he bought an abandoned garden in Nanjing and changed the name to “Suiyuan”. After that, Yuan Mei left his job and went back to seclude, settled in Nanjing, carefully built a garden, and lived here for half a century.
“The shepherd rides a scalper and sings loudly. Wanting to catch the cicadas, they suddenly shut up.” “Daytime is nowhere; youth comes on its own. Moss flowers are as small as rice, so learn how to grow peonies.” These beautiful and beautiful verses are all written by Yuan Mei. In the inconspicuous moss, he saw its desire and persistence for life in full bloom. Some people say that the moss flowers drawn by Yuan Mei are no less than the famous peony flowers.
As a poetic leader at the time of Qian Jia, Yuan Mei was an expert in poetry and writing. He advocated “sexual and spiritual discourse,” advocated direct expression of his feelings, wrote personal “temperament experiences,” and left works such as “Kokura Yamabusa's Collection of Poems” and “Suiyuan Poems” for future generations. His talent, wisdom, and liberation, his true temperament and infatuation have long been integrated into the context of the ancient capital of Jinling.
