 Zeng Yi, Zi Gu, was a writer, historian, and politician during the Northern Song Dynasty, known as Mr. Nan Feng. In June of the 4th year of Xining in the Northern Song Dynasty (1071), he served as the governor of Qizhou (present-day Jinan, Shandong) and became the parent and official of Qi. During his tenure in Qizhou, Zeng Gong worked for more than two years and three months, then transferred to Zhizhou in Xiangzhou. Although he did not work for a long time in Qizhou, Zeng Gong practiced good governance and was loved by the people. His story is brilliantly recorded in Fang Zhi's “Qi Sheng” from the Yuan Dynasty, as well as in Fang Zhi Kangxi's “Jinan Fu Zhi” and Daoguang's “Jinan Fu Zhi” from the Qing Dynasty.
(1)
Zeng Gong is a jealous person. His brother, Zeng Zhao, said that he was “courageous and straightforward” and “did not appease the Israeli and Arab world in order to correct falsification. There is righteousness; even though the rulers don't give in, it's not good; even though the whole world follows it, it's not easy to compare it with it”. In charge of Qi Zhou Zhan, Zeng Gong's unafraid character of rape was fully demonstrated. There is a family named Zhou in Qudi, Qizhou. They are a famous local family. Zhou Gao, a son of the Zhou family, relied on his family position to commit abuses in the village, scolding and harming civilians, which has become a major local scourge. State and county officials at all levels knew about this but were afraid to handle it, because the Zhou family also had greater support from the powerful, and those forces were enough to cause state and county officials to drop their posts. Zeng Gong, who first arrived in Qizhou, didn't worry about this. After learning about the situation, he resolutely took Zhou Gao and “brought it to law,” yet the Zhou family didn't dare to go back to work again due to self-knowledge.
During the Northern Song dynasty, the people in Qizhou were strong, and bandits were infested from time to time. Licheng and Zhangqiu have a “Overlord Society” that makes people fearful. They have been rampant in the village for many years, stealing property, robbing cars and taking prisoners, and doing nothing evil. Zeng Gong is determined to eradicate the “Overlord Society” and protect people's lives. He first used thunder to arrest and exile 31 of them, then organized villagers to form a military force to jointly inspect the remaining bandits. Whenever thieves showed up, they drummed to spread the news, and formed a siege in solidarity with each other. As a result, people are arrested every time the pirates act. One of them, called Ge You, surrendered voluntarily to the government after suffering the hunt. Zeng Gong Lue and Shi Xiaojie quickly dismantled the “Overlord Club” through Ge You: he arranged for Ge You a few “companions” to parade the village in a smartly dressed horse-drawn carriage. The rest of the bandits also surrendered themselves one after another, and the “Overlord Society” did not attack. Under Zeng Gong's rectification, Qizhou “did not dare to move with restraint; the government was cleared, the state department was cleared, there were no drumming police, no private homes were closed, and no roads were left behind.”
(2)
Zeng Gong treated thieves and at the same time leniency the public. He put down lawsuits, clarified customs, examined people's lives, and solved problems for the people. At the time, at a time when the imperial court was implementing the Wang Anshi Amendment Act, Zeng Gong implemented it according to the actual situation and minimized disturbance to the people. From Hebei, the Minjun River was transferred to another route. Qizhou needed 20,000 migrant workers. Judging from the registered population, it should have been three men and one husband. However, with his many years of experience in politics, Zeng Gong believes that there are concealments and omissions in the register, which is not the actual household registration situation in Qizhou. After an investigation, those false records were removed, and Zeng Gong finally decided that Jiuding from Qizhou had left the husband. This decision not only saved the imperial court a great deal of money, but also reduced the burden of military service, and was deeply praised by the people.
On some waterways in Qizhou, people travel by raft. This method not only runs slowly, but also charges money for the ferry, making it even impassable in case of rain or snow. Zeng Gong exempted nameless ferries and built bridges over some rivers to make it easier for people to get to and from. He also moved the location of the original house according to the topography, and changed the post road from Changqing to Bozhou and then to Weizhou. In this way, 6 post stations were eliminated, and people all felt that it was more convenient.
(3)
The city of Qizhou during the Northern Song Dynasty was often flooded. The topography of the city of Qizhou is high in the south and low in the north. There are mountains on the south side and rivers on the north. The springs in the city are scattered all over the place, and they converge into a series of canals. During the rainy season, spring water surges and converges northward, and the northern water system also floods into the city, so the area around the northern gate of the city is often flooded. After examining the hydrological situation in Qizhou City, Zeng Gong carried out the renovation and expansion of the water conservancy project on the basis of the original flood control facilities in the northern part of the city, and built a large sluice gate called the “North Water Gate.” With this barrier, northern Qizhou no longer has to worry about flooding.
At the same time, Zeng Gong organized the people to dredge Daming Lake and used lake sand to build a long embankment that runs through the north and south of the lake — Baihuadi. Horses can be planted on the embankment, and willow trees were planted on both sides. The trees were shady, making it very beautiful. He built small bridges such as Magehua, Shuixi, and Huxi around Daming Lake, connecting the lake and spring streams to form a beautiful, poetic landscape with rippling water waves, adding a strong cultural charm to the natural landscape. Kang Xi's “The History of Jinan Province” contains a poem by Zeng Gong Yong Que Huaqiao: “If you take a trip to Xi Chi, you can't let go of West Lake (that is, Daming Lake). Since then, Shichihashi Kaze and Moon, the dream has grown to a magnolia boat.” Just as Su Dongpo defended Hangzhou, Zeng Gongzhi also made this paradise of “one city, mountains, half city lake” and “hear the sound of springs in the scent of purple lotus flowers” more poetic.
In Qizhou during the Xining years of the Northern Song Dynasty, there were no houses for ambassadors. If there were guests, they built temporary houses. It was laborious, and the houses were rudimentary, but after the guests left, they became abandoned houses. As a result, Zeng Gong built two guest houses on the shores of the pleasant Baotu Spring to entertain guests: Yuelishantang on the north and Yueyuantang on the south. According to Fang Zhi's “Qi Sheng” written by Yu Qin in the Yuan Dynasty, “Nan Feng learned about Qi Zhou Day and built this for visitors.” According to Zeng Gong, taking care of the embassy building is Taishou's duty, and examining the geography and cultural features of mountains and rivers is also Taishou's duty. He examined in detail the events of Liangzhuang and Qizhou: First, the source of Baotu Spring was not Wangwu Mountain, as the ancients said, in the mountainous area of the south of the city, and Baotu Spring was the source of Luo Water; second, Lishan (or Qianfoshan) in the south of the city was the place where Dashun worked. As a result, Zeng Gong named the buildings on the shores of Baotu Spring “Lishan Hall” and “Luoyuan Hall,” and wrote the famous “Two Churches of Qizhou” and “The Chronicles of North Water Gate in Qizhou.” Zeng Gong's investigation was extremely significant for the city of Qizhou and later Jinan.
In September of the 6th year of Xining (1073), Zeng Gong was transferred to the post of Yangzhou. People in Qizhou loved Zeng Gong and didn't want him to leave; instead, they “closed the bridge” to keep him. Zeng Gong left quietly in the middle of the night, with admiration and affection for the people of Qi. Many years later, people built the Zenggong Temple in Qianfoshan to miss this diligent and pragmatic state. During the Daoguang years of the Qing Dynasty, Zenggong Temple was rebuilt on the northwest bank of Daming Lake and renamed “Nan Fung Temple”. To this day, the “Nan Feng Temple” still stands in the shade of Yiyiliu on the shores of Daming Lake. It is an important cultural landscape in Quancheng Jinan. (Zhang Peng)
