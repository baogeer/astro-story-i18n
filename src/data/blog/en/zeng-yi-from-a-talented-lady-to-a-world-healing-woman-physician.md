 Zeng Yi is a famous female poet in the late Qing Dynasty. As a member of the “Huanhua Poetry Club” in Chengdu, she co-wrote poems with her sisters under the auspices of her mother Zuo Xijia, and became famous for a while. Unlike other girlfriends who have been passed down through poetry, a febrile illness in her year also made Zeng Yi bond with traditional Chinese medicine. She was exhibiting medical books on her sickbed. After getting married to Yuan Xuechang at age 20, she also traveled to various southeast provinces. At that time, the Qingjiang River was under the sun in the evening, and it was full of devastation, and I felt a sense of emptiness. In her later years, Zeng Yi was an expert in medicine and wrote “Women's Studies” and “Medicine” to explore the way of “hygiene.” From a talented girl in a boudoir to a female doctor in the world, behind Zeng Yi's perception and writing about the disease is a shift in status from a poet to a healer, and it also contains clues about changes in women's consciousness since modern times.
Cherish spring and sorrow in Akihara is a common theme in boudoir poetry. There is no shortage of words describing the state of mind of the four seasons in Zeng Yi's “Ancient Love Room Poetry Collection”. It's just that these feelings are entangled with illness, and are also mixed with sorrow from time to time. Zeng Yi was born in the 2nd year of Xianfeng (1852). At age 10, her father Zeng Yong died due to military service. Her mother, Zuo Xijia, traveled west from Poyang, Jiangxi to return to Huayang, Sichuan with her children. Since then, Zeng Yi and her family have settled down by Huan Hua Creek, next to Du Fu's Thatched Cottage. Although Zeng Yi's “The Collection of Raccoons”, which she wrote before her marriage, often described beautiful scenes at 4 o'clock when life was happy, such as “Spring Twilight” by “The Dragon Has Become Bamboo, Fresh Green Sees Through the Window” and “Autumn Night” by “Qiuxing is so exciting that I can't sleep, I can't sleep when I smell banana leaves.” However, as the disease invaded, her feelings about the four seasons also changed. Because she couldn't help but return to the Jiangjin University Department, she wrote, “Helping the sick, and nurture your true nature. Autumn flowers look like me; Crescent Moon is thinner than others. Sentences such as “Refining Medicine Burns Autumn Leaves, Burning Incense and Relying on Green Yun” (“Remembering Ji Shuo's Five Sisters After Illness”). The sick person's thoughts about their closest loved ones were mixed with a slight sense of weakness, and the fun of the old autumn scenery also dissipated with the prolonged smell of medicine. Similar poems appear frequently, such as “Work, Poetry, and Good Illness, People Are Thin as Flowers” (“Autumn Girl Song”), “Yakuji is written year after year, and the cold night never sleeps” (“In the midst of a cold night sickness, Ji Shuo's five sisters and sent Xu's second brother to Kyoto”), and in “Huan Yue Song”, “Dongfeng is already green in the west, and the soul of the poem struggles to separate.” “Good Scenery, Sunny Days, and Sad Years” (“Bodhisattva Man: Sending Uncle Jun's Four Sisters Shou Chun”). In her thoughts, the sick Huanhua female poet laments “Whom do you want to send the book of Huan Tears”.
After Zeng Yi left the cabinet, she traveled with her husband, from Sichuan to Min, from Fujian to Xian, and along the Wanjiang River. During this period, there were quite a few works inspired by ancient times. Because she is often sick, Zeng Yi writes poems during her illness. For example, “Apricot Blossoms Are Rainy and Cheaper, Spring and Night Are Stronger”, “Strong, Bones Don't Know the Heat, May Tides Deep in Autumn,” and “When Sick Is Painful, Flaming, Depressed, and Unwell.” Following the death of her third and fifth sisters, Zeng Yi's poems about illness added to the rotation of the four seasons a sense that something was wrong with others. The current situation was turbulent, and what she saw and heard during the trip made her consider other ways to face her illness.
Zeng Yi experienced fever four times in her life and became medicated for a long time. In addition, she had great experience in diagnosing typhoid fever. In addition, following places such as Guangde and Guoyang over the years, quack doctors often stuck to ancient methods and misled people's lives, so she wrote “Medical Chapter” — “This is why I wish for a long time.” In “An Debate on Cold Disease, Typhoid Fever,” Zeng Yi described Wu Jutong's “The Case for Treating Warmth Disease” by Wu Jutong during her illness, “treating it like nourishing the yin, sweet, and cooling, and being cured by the method”. This makes it easy to understand that the “Medical Edition” mainly uses the temperate school of medicine. However, Zeng Yi was not an ancient person. When discussing the cause of typhoid fever, she thought “the lungs are covered by two tubes, one is the esophagus, which supports the diet; one is the trachea to breathe”, apparently drawing on the views of Western medicine's anatomy. She further pointed out that “Western methods of treating diseases are not as good as those in China, but there are actually better treatments than Chinese ones,” and has repeatedly promoted vaccination of children against cowpox, using both Chinese and Western medicine. The second volume of “Medicine” was published in 1906 and published the following year, using warm sickness as a clue, and a prescription for healing. It can be seen as Zeng Yi's attempt to respond to illness with medicine. However, “Women's Studies” was written in 1905. Although it follows the traditional division of labor between women and men, it is also influenced by the Restoration ideology, which emphasizes “making men fulfill their duties is shared with women; all rights enjoyed by men are shared with women.” At the same time, in the first chapter, “Marriage,” the book suggests “you must pay attention to choosing your spouse's physique”, reflecting the simple ancient idea of eugenics; in chapter 9, “Health,” it calls on women to “not only focus on hygiene, but also to study medicine, so that if a family is strong, the country is strong; if the country is strong, the race is also strong because of it.” It can be said that “Women's Studies” and “Medicine” explain Zeng Yi's “hygiene” approach from different perspectives. The former treats chaos in the female world, and the latter treats fever and typhoid fever. Although parts of these two books seem debatable now, Zeng Yi's remarks seem unusual in an age where women's access is relatively limited and the status of a healer is not high. No wonder she laughed at “Women's Studies” in the preface, “Even those who keep up the old hate exaggerations; those who restore them look down on triviality. There are those who agree with neutrality, and good will be accepted with joy”.
Academics often regard texts written by Zeng Yi in the two different roles of a poet and healer. The former relates to literary aesthetics, while the latter focuses on social history and gender studies. However, in reality, whether it is poetry, medical books, or theories, they are all Zeng Yi's unique perceptions and expressions in the face of illness. The three complement each other to create this unique writer. When writing the preface to “Women's Studies”, Zhang Baixi claimed that Zeng Yi “put aside poetry, calligraphy, and painting to be of no benefit to the world during the difficult days, so she abandoned her poetry, calligraphy, and painting to be of no benefit to the world. As far as practicing on weekdays, she can be reserved as a female scholar and write the second volume of “Women's Studies”. Also, medicine is still rampant, and the plagiarism of Western healers is often superficial, and there are many murders due to fear that true interpretation will be lost... “Medical Chapter 2”. This explanation seems complete, but it is too brief.
In fact, Zeng Yi did not completely abandon the tradition of being a talented woman. The deeply moving passages in “Women's Studies” and “Medicine” have both literary aesthetic value, apparently thanks to her experience in literary creation. For example, “Medicine Edition” notes the heartbreaking “Death of the Three Sisters” -- the third sister, Zhong Yi, was weak and had bleeding and fainted when giving birth to her third child. Zeng Yi, a first-time medical practitioner, had not left the court at the time. Seeing that the three sisters were in danger, the doctor stuck to the ancient recipe and “mixed half a cup of the biochemical soup that I used when I was about to give birth”, which finally saved the lives of the three sisters. Unexpectedly, the three sisters gave birth one after another, and they lost even more energy. Finally, when they gave birth to their sixth child, they lost their lives. Unauthorized drug exchange is the secret of young girl Zeng Yi. She didn't tell anyone. Although luck saved the lives of the three sisters, she was still uneasy to know that the sisters would continue to give birth in the future. Until the third sister died due to childbirth, and Zeng Yi was already married at the time. She had no chance to revive at the last moment, nor did she have a chance to see the last side of the three sisters. The words in this narrative are bloody, and the feeling of regret is hard to contain. Now it seems that it is not only a tragedy of the three sisters, but also the tragedy of countless women who have no right to speak out about childbearing. In “On the Benefits and Losses of Foot Stalking”, Zeng Yi also used her own experience to persuade women to give up stalking her feet. She recounted that she came home from school when she was young and saw her brother fishing for flowers, “had unlimited freedom and fun,” and “she consciously carried a thousand forces, enough to be shackled, and wept every time she caressed them.” There are only a few words that have already drawn the image of a footstalking girl who secretly cries, touching readers' compassion. Judging from the tradition of “medical literature exchange,” Shen Kuo from the Northern Song dynasty wrote “Writing Tales from Mengxi,” and famous doctors from the Qing Dynasty such as Xue Xue had “A Punch of Poems” and Xu Dachun had “Huixi Daoqing,” but there is no doubt that Zeng Yi is also an example of learning all medical literature. Those highly literary passages in her medical books highlight and continue her literary talent.
Zeng Yi's choice to “abandon literature and practice medicine” is not an exception; what is behind this was the social transformation of a group of young women in the late Qing dynasty. Talented women in the late Qing dynasty were forced out of their girlfriends due to war and chaos. When they were in exile, their perception of illness was no longer simply worrying about self-pity, cherishing spring and autumn, but was closely linked to the social practice of “outside the world.” As missionaries in the late Qing dynasty introduced Western medicine to China, the traditional Chinese medicine system, which originally adheres to Confucian traditions, was impacted. Coupled with women taking the initiative or being forced to intervene more deeply in social reality, women such as Zeng Yi, Gu Shuzhao, and Sun Xitai were known to the world as “healers” rather than “talented women.” At the same time, the female academic system based on the modern knowledge structure is becoming more and more perfect, and the influence of traditional female education regulations is weakening. What was originally a small group of female traditional Chinese medicine practitioners gradually evolved into a certain number of Chinese and Western female medical teams in the late Qing dynasty. If most of the female doctors of the same generation as Zeng Yi were girlfriends, and some also had family roots. For example, Gu Shuzhao's father was the famous doctor Gu Xi, and Wang Heng's father was the famous doctor Wang Zhu, then most of the female doctors after that were female medical students, and female medical practitioners officially embarked on the path of professionalization. The group of female doctors in Guangzhou, represented by Luo Xiuyun and Zhang Zhujun, is a product of modern Western medicine education derived from Boji Hospital. However, as female doctors from the science class entered the historical stage one after another, the tradition of writing articles that girlfriends were good at gradually disappeared.
Looking back, Zeng Yi's life was not only an elegy for the last generation of girlfriends, but also the “origin” for modern female intellectuals to intervene in the public sphere. Her practice of medicine rediscovering disease and depicting the center of the disease in her poetry are two parallel paths to achieve self-worth. Without a humanistic map written by a talented woman, it would be difficult for her to have the spirituality and tension in her medical theory about sickness and awe of life and death. This kind of sensitivity and talent that comes from the cultivation of poetry is superior to depicting the “vibrations” of life. No matter what era, it can inspire deeper medical and humanistic care.
