 “Wuhou Temple: 1700 Years of Meditation” was first published in “People's Daily” (overseas edition) in December 1990. It's been over 30 years. This is not a very popular subject, but in 2002 it was included in the second volume of the 7th grade standard educational experimental textbook by the People's Education Press, and since then it has continued to be included in textbooks for many publishing houses and books for various students. This seems hard to understand; in fact, it's because of Zhu Geliang.
There are many famous people in Chinese history, but most of them are in certain circles. For example, Li Bai and Su Dongpo are in the literary circle, famous ministers like Wei Zhi and Zuo Tsung-tang are in the political circle, etc. However, there are few well-known figures such as Zhu Geliang, who have both achievements and articles, and can be accepted by the public and the public. In feudal politics where Confucian thought was orthodox, he was an example of loyalty and diligence. In the real life of folk chai, rice, oil and salt, he was the personification of wisdom and wisdom.
I visited Chengdu in the fall of 1990 to see the Wuhou Temple. What I can't think of is the famous Wuhou Temple, which everyone talks about. Its official name is not Wuhou Temple; the giant plaque hanging above the gate is “Han Zhaolie Temple.” Zhaolie was Liu Bei's nickname after his death. After Zhu Geliang's death, ordinary people wanted to build a temple to commemorate it. The imperial court did not allow it to build the first temple in Dingjun Mountain, where he died in the line of duty, until 30 years later. It wasn't until Western Jin that they were allowed to build a temple next to Liu Bei Temple in Chengdu. I don't want this to break down. Separated by a wall, the incense at the Wuhou Temple surpassed that of Liu Bei Temple, and the prestige of the courtier surpassed that of the emperor. In the Kangxi era of the Qing Dynasty, in order to avoid this kind of embarrassment, there was no choice but to use Junchen Temple. In the same compound, Liu Bei was behind Zhu Geliang, and only Zhaolie Temple was hung on the large plaque at the front door. However, people still call it the Wuhou Temple, and outsiders in particular don't even mention visiting Chengdu to worship any Zhaolie Temple.
Strangely enough, Zhu Geliang died in 234 AD, and the Shu State came to an end in 263. Supposedly, the matter between Zhu Geliang and Liu Shu's small imperial court had already come to an end at this time, and the dispute over whether the temple should have been built between the people and the imperial court continued until the Kangxi years of the Qing dynasty, which lasted more than a thousand years. This only shows the cruelty of feudal etiquette. It also shows the tenacity of the people. After all, the common people won, and in the end, the imperial court barely kept a sign of shame. However, no matter what kind of Zhaolie temple, the public always directly calls the Wuhou Temple and only recognizes the Wuhou Temple.
I was currently preparing another article, “Big or Big,” which immediately reminded me of Zhou Enlai's “Gang of Four” in power when he died. The authorities were not allowed to mourn, yet Tiananmen Square was still filled with wreaths, and the people sending souls on Shiliang Street couldn't see it. The government hasn't built any memorial for Zhou Enlai for a long time, but the first monument commemorating Zhou Enlai has appeared in Japan, and memorials outside of Beijing have sprung up one by one, which really backfires with the hearts of the people. This historical association deepened my understanding of Zhou Enlai. It wasn't until 8 years later, when I published “Big Big Is Zhou Enlai,” I compared the two of them in the article, and achieved a personal resonance in the air.
Any classic thing, whether a person or an object, can always give life unlimited associations and use it to have new thoughts and actions. Zhu Geliang is a politician with strategic thinking, a loyal servant who works hard, and a senior official with integrity and self-discipline. He single-handedly helped Liu Bei establish the Shu and Han administration, and led to the establishment of the Three Kingdoms. His “Order of Success” and “The Book of Commandments” have been passed down through the ages, whether it be a meritorious person, or an official. Mao Zedong said that in Chinese history, there was no shortage of people who built meritorious careers, and there was also no shortage of people who influenced future generations with their ideas and conduct. The former were such as Zhu Geliang and Fan Zhongquan, and the latter, such as Kong Meng, and others. You need to know that in Mao Zedong's eyes, “Qin Emperor Han Wu” was only good enough to “slightly lose in literature” the previous one. It can be seen that he respects Zhu Geliang.
Unfortunately, Zhu Geliang died early and failed to achieve much. This is yet another tragedy. All of this has made him a prism of history. It can reflect all kinds of brilliance in his body, and many people for humanity have also used him to spit out their hearts. Over the past thousand years, everyone who has passed through the Wuhou Temple has sighed and written poems, so ancient and modern times have intersected, and the gods have influenced it, and the Wuhou Temple has become a historical museum with traditional culture and various feelings. For example, in Yue Pilot's book, Du Fu's famous phrase: “If you don't succeed, the hero will burst into tears.” Until now, Chen Yi's inscription: “People admire Kong Ming's victory over Zhaorei. Why is it that Yu Yi Kong left a legacy.” The more love you leave, the more you miss it. Zhuge Liang, along with the Wu Hou Temple commemorating him, has become a carrier of traditional culture and a coordinate in a historical voyage.
But Zhu Geliang is also a bit different from other celebrities; he is deeply rooted in the public. From his hometown of Shandong to Nanyang, where he marched, to Wuzhangyuan, where he died in the line of duty, or even places he never visited, I don't know how many temples and shrines Zhuge Liang are there. There are two main reasons for this. One is due to the sculptural rendering of “The Romance of the Three Kingdoms,” where he has become the personification of wisdom. People also need such an example in their lives; second, as Chen Yi said in his inscription, he really has love for his subordinates and the people.
In recent years, I've been running a lot in Yunguichuan in the southwest because I'm drawing ancient trees. This was Zhu Geliang's main activity area back then. During the Shu and Han dynasties, this place was mostly deserted; Zhu Geliang's painstaking management really left a lot of love behind. On the roadside of Jianmen Guanxia, there is a type of shrub with red fruits. It is still bright and pleasant to eat in early winter. Locals call it “Zhuge Military Food.” There is an “Elephant Temple” in Guizhou. The legend of “Elephant” is the younger brother of “Shun”. When the stubborn prodigal son returned, Zhu Geliang built a temple for him to enlighten the local people. Wang Yangming once wrote “The Legend of the Elephant Temple” and left a famous quote: “With good human nature, there are no indestructible people in the world.” There was a lot of miasma in the clouds, and Zhu Geliang taught people to drink tea to prevent illness. Today, the Xishuangbanna area still worships him as the tea god, and the Zhuge Liang Temple has been built and worshiped every year. Although these are legends, I believe they still have some historical basis. This is still a bit different from people who label Guan Yu as the god of fortune. Guan Yu didn't do business, and his life had nothing to do with getting rich. People probably respected his loyalty and needed to take advantage of his power. And Zhu Geliang created a great cause, left behind true love, and is both a human being and a god.
Things like gods have always been shaped by ordinary people choosing character prototypes according to their own wishes. In my article, I said, “There are two types of people throughout the ages. One type of person lived for the present, worked hard to enjoy, and died; one type of person was born for ideals, worked hard, and died. No matter how old he is, a person always has to be reduced to a human; no matter how long he lives, he always becomes a ghost; yet only a very few people are lucky enough to be selected by the people, promoted as gods by history, to enjoy the four-hour worship, and become eternal.”
Gods that have left more traces of real people are also more affectionate and respectable.
